## The question this volume opens on, inherited and unanswered

**Why does weather memory threaten a region the Registry claims is stable?** — in three parts, none of them about the eleven streets:

1. **Why a region entered as stable is on a forecast of weather-driven seam events at all**, on the stated ground that there is no maintained seam, no recorded loss, no resident witness and no schedule.
2. **Why there is no second outlook**, who would print one, and whether the absence is a decision or an oversight.
3. **What it is about an instrument a person cannot produce which makes the thing it cannot produce the most important thing in a district.** — half answered by a punch roll, a yard man's flag and book, and a month spent in a field book, with a fourth part underneath that nobody has been down to.

**The volume's answer to part one is the volume's whole engine, and it is not a mystery and it is not a creature:** *a place is not stable because nothing has happened in it; a place is stable because there is nobody writing down what happens in it.* The eleven streets have a gang, a foreman, a weekly sheet, a rack, two tins, a condition column and a reason column. **Mireward has a man of fifty-eight, forty-one years of a pad in a drawer in Crown Heights, and nothing else.** They are the same kind of place. One of them has stationery.

## The region

**Mireward** is the wet fenland south of the city: raised paths, drainage canals, seasonal markets, villages with no legal address during high water. Residents navigate by landmarks, smells, bell calls and **the condition of a bridge**. The district is on the city's returns because a return list is printed from a list of districts that is a hundred and forty years old and has never been printed from a list of offices. **There is still no district office and there will not be one in this volume.**

The working places of the volume: **Cobb Lock**, a village with no legal address and a schoolroom of nineteen children; **the old ford**, where the seam is; **the three posts**, where the water marks are; **the landing**, which is not a rateable thing; **the council drain**, which is maintained and is not a route.

## The people of Volume 03, and the rule about them

Ordinary people with real jobs, registered before they are used, and none of them a device, a faction, an antagonist, or a person who is thanked.

- **Percy**, fifty-eight, has gone out into Mireward on the first dry week of every summer since 1974 with a rod and a chain and written four lines about about nine feet of drain into a pad in a drawer in Crown Heights. **Nobody has ever asked him for one. He is not an office and does not become one.** He is Volume 03's first working day.
- **Garn Ollerton**, thirty-seven, ferry pilot, twenty-six years, four landings, knows the water by what it did last time it was here. **She is the first person in the series who has written a route backwards, from where you are going to be.** She walked Milo in and out of the tenth crossing and was not asked and is not thanked.
- **Coll Sarrowe**, forty-four, ditch keeper, thirty-one years, holds the water down and is on no paper at all. He is Percy's other half: **Percy writes down what he finds and nobody reads it; Coll holds the water and nothing is written at all.** Neither of them is a district.
- **Sabra Trewin**, thirty-four, nine years teaching nineteen children in a schoolroom with no address. **A roll in a place with no address is a piece of paper in a head, and she keeps it in her head on purpose, and she held the count of the tenth crossing.**

## Escalation sequence

### Chapters 131–140 — *Somebody Goes There First*
The card with a hand on it. The man who has gone for forty-one years and has never been asked. The bridge whose condition is a route. The eighth seventh column, which wants a name and there is nobody to give one. A schoolroom with no address. The tenth crossing. **The pairs move for the first time since the twenty-fifth of April, and they move because he was asleep six nights in a building with a door that shuts.** The sentence is reprinted by a machine and is now a number.

### Chapters 141–150 — *The Order Is Not A Weather Report*
The Registry's proposal arrives as a printed instrument and it is **not a weather report**: it is a land instrument wearing a weather coat. Mara Quill finds the second use in about nine seconds in a building about a mile from where the first one was drafted. **Mara's Volume 03 thread begins here and she does not soften it.** First named document of the volume to carry the office of the First Surveyor as a *signing block* rather than an issuing line — and it is a signature block and not a man, and no person is produced behind it.

### Chapters 151–160 — *Four Copies Of A Bell*
The Rainway's cue and its demand begin to be legible as a **practice**, not a mood: a bell rung for a death that was not entered anywhere, rung again the following year, and again. The volume's first working grammar other than the Row's Keepsake is **Weather with Cadence leaking**, named on the page, and it may not be solved by knowing the category.

### Chapters 161–170 — the volume midpoint, *What The Rain Is Carrying*
**The midpoint reversal, and it is the series' structural hinge:** the Rainway is carrying **a public mourning that was displaced from the Quieting**. It is not a weapon designed by anybody. **The Registry's refusal to acknowledge it is what has made it worse**, and the refusal is not villainy — it is a department that cannot record a loss it has no form for, doing the correct thing with the forms it has. **The First Atlas, the Singular Mode and the Quitting are named in this block and not before it, and not in a way that explains anything that came earlier.**

### Chapters 171–180 — *Two Locks And A Choice*
The proposal hardens. The eleven streets are named in a schedule as **not to be maintained**, which is the first time the district has appeared on a printed side of anything since the fourteenth of April, and it appears as a thing to be withdrawn. **Milo must choose between a village and the central archive**, and the choice is set up here and paid in Batch 0006.

### Chapters 181–190 — the climax, *The Village Or The Archive*
**He chooses the village.** The choice causes a difficult flood — the central gates take water they were not built to take, on an ordinary afternoon, in front of people — and a **permanent loss of reliable route data**: the district's own remembered-route material for the south bank is gone, and the loss is real and is not a stage and is not recovered. The concrete resolution begins.

### Chapters 191–200 — the volume close, *Somebody Goes There First*
Mireward receives protected routes and a **temporary representative**, and the representative is a person and not an office and has a term and a name on a paper with a date on it. **Alderquay's central flood gates fail in a way that proves distributed responsibility matters** — not catastrophically, but on a Tuesday, in a way that four offices each of which was correct had to share. The volume's final image is written here and only here: **a village school paints its route on the wall in several colours, none of them official.**

## Concrete resolution, and what is not resolved

Mireward receives protected routes and a temporary representative. Alderquay's central flood gates fail. **Nothing in the eleven streets is restored, discharged, varied, withdrawn, re-authored or put back on any list, and no Volume 02 thread is resolved in this volume.** The plate stays in the grass. The round stays one man short. A foreman is months past four months and is not going to be asked. The sixth line of fifty-four words is not read. Tern is not found. The matter stands over, is not adjourned, has no date and is on no list.

## Relationship milestone 3 — public risk

**They disagree about whether to save a remote village or protect the central archive. They choose the village and disagree afterward.** Milestone 3 is a **disagreement that is correct on both sides and is not repaired in this volume.** The romance's rule is unchanged and is not waived: *nobody gets to save anyone by deciding for them* — and in this volume that rule is applied against him, by her, about a village, and he loses.

- **Atlas residue: at most one appearance in ten chapters, and one is not required.** The seven inherited sentences may not be repeated.

- **An instrument a person cannot produce stays that way.** A punch roll is nine inches of paper. A pad is a pad. A roll in a teacher's head is a roll in a teacher's head. None of them is a return and none of them is a record, and no batch may answer a return.
