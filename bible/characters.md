# Characters

## Milo Rook — protagonist

- **Age:** thirty at the beginning of the story.
- **Role:** field surveyor for the Alderquay Civic Atlas Office; later an independent cartographer and organizer of the distributed Atlas.
- **Immediate situation:** He is good at his job, underpaid for the risk, and still officially assigned to ordinary utility corrections. He carries a brass route-token stamped **Lark Street**.
- **External want:** Find the source of Lark Street’s disappearance, identify what happened to his mother, and force the city to acknowledge the neighborhood and the people erased with it.
- **Where he is, after Chapter 40:** Alderquay, and he asks, in writing, each morning, who is taking him where. He is across **six** times in Volume 1 — the lane on the 4th of Sazur, the loop on the 6th, the false route on the 18th, the drill on the 21st, the return mark that failed on the 26th, and the sixth on the 10th of the next month at 14.06, on a count of nine from the path head held by Rue and walked by Verrick Assal — and he reads all six out loud in a yard because Iona Sen made him.
- **Stage:** 1 — trace-sight. He can identify a likely seam, draw a rough threshold and return route, and make a short-lived crossing with a witness. He cannot stabilise a seam, identify a grammar with certainty, or hold a place open by drawing.
- **Cost paid so far:** the **orientation blank**, first stage only, and it has widened to a list of **eight** remembered routes: the route to the Civic Atlas Office, a corridor he has used for six years, his own front door, four hundred metres of his own street, a route taught to him in a clinic on the sixteenth, the way from the ferry steps up to the chandler’s yard, the two hundred and fourteen metres of the route card he wrote himself on the sixteenth for a nurse to walk, and **the two hundred metres from the works gate to the end of the disused platform, which came back the next morning in chalk on a gate post.** They return only when another person marks them. The **Field Book is intact** and the written route home is complete, correct, and legible to anyone else; it simply no longer tells him which way to turn. The written-line loss belongs to Chapters 51–53 and must not be pre-empted. No sensory failure, personal blank, seam-scar, record blank, or authorial blank has appeared.
- **The second method change, earned in Chapter 25.** Iona Sen examined him after the fifth crossing, read out that all four of his signed conditions had been satisfied, and told him that a condition is a floor and he has been treating it as a ceiling. Her finding, which is **not a condition and cannot be signed**, is that he is **not to be left alone with a piece of paper about a person** — not once, not ever, for as long as he is in this district. He broke it on the 26th by writing a turn onto a landing from a name a woman had struck out of a rota in front of eleven people, and he has written that finding down nowhere.
- **The method change, earned in Chapters 11–20.** He attempted a private single continuous route, anchored it on two people's labour without asking either, knew it had no count in it and drew for two hours more, and it put a woman through a wall. He filed it as false, with a page and a half of his own confession attached. He now writes directions in the reader's units, understands that Ada Fenn's door-check and a good route card are the same discipline, and has signed four conditions with Iona Sen: a named person outside holding a count, stated minutes, walked in and walked out by someone who can stop the work, and the clinic the morning after. **The rule he has adopted is that a map has to be legible to someone other than himself.** The flaw is not cured: he disclosed the Lark token to Mara Quill and then refused her offer to file the disclosure, and she wrote the refusal down with the date on it.
- **Internal need:** Milo must learn that remembering a place is not the same as owning its future. He needs to become a custodian of other people’s ability to choose rather than the sole author of a recovered past.
- **Central flaw:** He turns uncertainty into control. When frightened, he hoards information, makes a private map without asking permission, and treats people who disagree with him as obstacles to be corrected. He is generous with effort and stingy with trust.
- **Central fear:** He will repeat his father’s failure by reducing living people to a route, and he will discover that he loved an image of his mother more than the people who actually survived her absence.
- **Strengths:** precise observation; physical courage; patience with frightened witnesses; good at finding practical compromises; remembers municipal procedures and old repair work.
- **Limits:** he can become tunnel-visioned, overworks when frightened, and mistakes a clean route for a just one. He cannot read a room, speak publicly without becoming defensive, or stabilize a seam alone.
- **Starting habit:** He keeps his own **Field Book** — a paper route-work book that is not Civic Atlas Office property and never appears in an office inventory — and writes down the route home after every survey. The later absence of that route is an early sign of his power’s cost. In his own speech he sometimes calls it “the book,” never a second notebook.
- **Protected sensory memory:** The sound of Neris’s voice giving the Lark tram route is the one private sensory memory he trusts when other memories fail. It is real, but it is not proof that her person survives.
- **Arc:** He begins as a man trying to restore one family’s address. He becomes a man who can hold several incompatible truths, organizes a network of custodians, and finally gives up the fantasy that one person can restore everything without asking anyone else. His final growth is a change in authorship, not a larger magical blast radius.
- **Romance:** One slow-burn relationship with Mara Quill. Attraction grows through shared work, professional disagreement, and practical dependence. It does not erase Milo’s flaws or make Mara a reward for his progress.

## Mara Quill — continuity advocate and romantic partner

- **Age:** thirty-one at the beginning.
- **Role:** a trained continuity advocate in the Registry’s **Continuity Records** division. She can challenge an order, identify a lawful appeal, and translate civic procedure into a sentence a grieving resident can use.
- **External want:** Keep Alderquay from a citywide Greywake and prevent the Registry from turning emergency powers into a permanent machine. She wants institutional reform, not the destruction of the institution itself.
- **Internal need:** Mara must accept that safety is not the same as control, and that a person can be responsible for a system without becoming obedient to it.
- **Flaw:** She uses procedure as armor. When frightened, she chooses the defensible answer over the honest one and can turn personal concern into a legal argument.
- **Fear:** becoming complicit in another person’s disappearance after telling herself that every order prevented a worse outcome.
- **Strengths:** memory for law, negotiation, conflict de-escalation, and a rare ability to admit when an institution is wrong without making the admission useless.
- **Limits:** she is not a combatant or a supernatural powerhouse. Her influence is local, and the Registry can remove her access, records, and credibility more easily than it can defeat Milo in a fight.
- **Starting relationship with Milo:** She is assigned to review his unauthorized survey of a seam. She suspects he is chasing a private grievance and intends to close the file. He resents her authority because it makes his private loss legible to strangers.
- **The Volume 1 milestone, paid in Chapters 11–20, is disclosure rather than affection.** She is told about the brass token and the district being his mother's; she offers to file it as a disclosure that would protect his measurements; **he refuses, and she records the refusal, signed and dated, in her own hand, and then goes on working the case.** She discloses the counterpart: her own block capitals on a six-year-old intake routing slip, a second set of initials in the annex box that she has read four times and cannot read aloud, and six years of working this case off her own hours in a records alcove without telling anybody. Neither of them has said anything about how they feel, and the vocabulary is work, disclosure, disagreement in front of danger, and repair.
- **Arc:** Mara moves from regulatory opponent to reluctant witness, then to a co-author of counter-maps. She refuses Milo’s habit of hiding costs, and he must learn to trust her with information he considers dangerous to his plan. Their final relationship is a chosen partnership with separate homes, work, and public roles—not a rescue ending.
- **Boundary:** The romance remains secondary to her professional and moral arc. She does not become a passive ally, and she does not give up her career when they become partners.

## Pell Harrow — courier and Streetkeeper

- **Age:** seventeen.
- **Role:** bicycle courier who knows informal paths, rents, warning signals, and which residents will open a door for a wounded stranger.
- **External want:** Find his older brother **Tern**, who entered a seam in Mireward and never returned through the ordinary roads.
- **Internal need:** Stop treating every disappearance as a puzzle that can be solved by the first person clever enough to look. Accept that some people choose routes he cannot follow.
- **Strengths:** physical stamina, local knowledge, quick improvisational thinking, and an instinct for when a street is being watched.
- **Flaw:** He lies about his age and rushes into danger to prove that he is not a child.
- **Function:** Pell keeps Milo close to the cost of a map. He is the first person to insist that a restored street must have a place for a teenager to sleep, not only a monument to its dead.
- **Arc:** He becomes a trained route-keeper. In Volume 8 he finds Tern alive inside the moving community from Volume 7; Tern chooses continued contact on his own terms rather than returning to the old neighborhood. Pell learns that love can be a route between people without requiring either person to move back.

## Dr. Iona Sen — seam physician and mentor

- **Age:** fifty-four.
- **Role:** physician who treats bodies and minds damaged by seam crossings; formerly a Thread Warden and a technician in the Atlas maintenance system.
- **External want:** Make medical care available to people the Registry calls unlocatable, including residents of remnant districts.
- **Internal need:** Stop treating her own complicity as a private shame and publish what she knows about the Atlas’s early failures.
- **Flaw:** She believes competence justifies controlled disclosure. She keeps dangerous knowledge from patients when she thinks a truth would be used against them.
- **Strengths:** diagnosis, calm under pressure, ethical memory, and a practical understanding of how seam injuries differ from ordinary neurological damage.
- **Arc:** Iona becomes the independent medical authority for counter-map clinics. Her testimony helps the final public case, but she does not become a magical administrator. She continues to treat individual patients after the victory.

## Tarin Kest — inspector and reform ally

- **Age:** twenty-eight.
- **Role:** Registry field inspector who understands enforcement, evidence, and the practical limits of legal permission.
- **External want:** Prove that the Registry can correct itself before someone decides the entire institution must be dismantled.
- **Internal need:** Separate reform from personal loyalty to his superiors.
- **Flaw:** He believes a well-placed signature can solve a political problem and underestimates the damage caused by delay.
- **Arc:** He becomes the administrative bridge between Streetkeepers and reformists. He may testify against Halden and later help create a public continuity office. His loyalty changes, but he never becomes a hidden powerful mage.
- **Where he is after Chapter 40:** he has read the file, written his paragraph, and gone back to a desk in Crown Heights. **He is competent, he is wrong about one document in about ninety seconds, and he is on the page as an adversary whose competence is on display.** He is not a friend, is not a magical administrator, and **must not become a friend inside ten chapters or inside Volume 1.** His first purchase is the **Filewell precedent** and a third Crown Heights consultation reference; his first cost is **a variation application in his own name that came back with *no person available* in the box**, which he put in his coat rather than a folder because he has spent nine days watching Milo Rook put his name on a document about somebody else's district.

## Sel Iri — Open-Door Choir leader

- **Age:** thirty-nine.
- **Role:** charismatic organizer who wakes sealed streets and exposes the harm of continuity orders.
- **External want:** Return every erased district to public reach, even if the return destabilizes the city.
- **Internal need:** Accept that liberation can harm people who live in the overlap, and that grief is not a mandate.
- **Flaw:** She treats people as evidence of an injustice and underestimates the need for consent.
- **Function:** Ideological foil to both Milo and Mara. She is often right about the Registry and wrong about timing, risk, and the difference between opening a door and choosing what happens after entry.
- **Arc:** She becomes a difficult ally who accepts responsibility for a reckless mass awakening and helps defend the final distributed network. She is not absolved by helping at the climax.

## Halden Vey — Prefect of the Registry

- **Age:** fifty-eight.
- **Role:** institutional antagonist and head of the Registry of Continuance.
- **External want:** Prevent a Greywake by preserving the Common Atlas, even if the Registry must enforce a single version of public safety.
- **Internal need:** Admit that his fear is not proof of necessity and that a system can be dangerous without deserving unconditional obedience.
- **Flaw:** He treats dissent as a public-safety failure before asking who benefits from the silence.
- **Strengths:** genuine expertise, political ability, and the willingness to make hard decisions.
- **Arc:** Halden becomes a reluctant witness against the One Survey and helps testify about the Atlas’s failures. He does not receive a full redemption; he lives with the knowledge that his safeguards enabled later abuses.

## Orrin Sable — first-volume local antagonist

- **Age:** forty-four.
- **Role:** district continuity officer responsible for the Bellwether Lane order.
- **External want:** Complete a flood-control project and keep the blame for an earlier worker’s death out of public record.
- **Internal need:** Accept that a job built on practical pressure still requires personal accountability.
- **Function:** A small-scale version of the larger system. He is pressured by superiors, makes a dangerous choice with local knowledge, and later must testify about the order rather than become the final villain.
- **Where he is after Chapter 40:** the works, a regrade running on a soft lens that will take two more feet before November, a plate on the only door in the flood-control wall, a foreman he did not stand down and did not defend, three pumps on a street his own plate put under water, and **a Bench in October. His argument is right, it has been proved twice more, and nothing in Chapters 31–40 answers it or makes him wrong.** He is named once in the joint finding, in a list of persons who were present, **because the finding is about Milo's level and not about his order. He is not suspended here and is not to be suspended before Chapters 58–60.**

## Soren Rook — father, First Surveyor, and final antagonist

- **Age:** fifty-seven at the beginning.
- **Role:** former Surveyor-General, designer of the modern Atlas safeguard, and the current human custodian of the Common Index.
- **Physical state:** Soren is alive in a sealed maintenance chamber beneath Crown Heights, bound to the Index by years of deliberate self-surrender. He is not a ghost: he can be injured, can refuse commands, and ages with the Atlas, but his voice and handwriting travel through route-stones and copied survey records. Releasing the Index’s sole authorship breaks that bond and kills him.
- **External want:** Finish the **One Survey**, making every route in Alderquay consistent enough to prevent another Quieting and Greywake.
- **Internal need:** Accept that a city cannot be made safe by making every person answerable to one map, and that his own fear of another mass disaster is not permission to choose everyone’s memories.
- **Flaw:** He responds to disagreement by making it a hazard. He has spent so long surrendering his personal history to the Atlas that he can no longer distinguish protection from authorship.
- **Fear:** A plural, revisable city will produce another catastrophic disagreement, and he will be responsible for millions of deaths.
- **Method:** He plans the One Survey as a corrected version of the Long Rain, not a repetition of it, and he says so plainly to anyone who will listen. He holds a complete eighteen-year inventory of the excluded routes, including Lark and including names the Registry has never published. He intends the operation to be bounded, to run a published shutdown after the evacuation, and to store the suspended routes in a sealed residual layer instead of casting them out of the instrument. Each element is defensible. He does not offer a distributed alternative because he has calculated that one cannot be assembled inside the window he believes is coming, and because a singular Index is also the configuration in which he survives; he does not count that as a motive, and the prose must not excuse it for him.
- **History:** Soren approved the Lark Street order after concluding that severing the district was the only way to stop an immediate seamquake. He then withdrew from public life and entered the Atlas maintenance system, becoming its First Surveyor. He used the emergency to erase Lark’s residents’ claims and suppress an inquiry, so the order was both a necessary lesser loss and a deliberate political erasure. He knows the order harmed people, but he has never accepted that a smaller loss cannot be justified.
- **Where he is wrong:** He is right about the bookkeeping and wrong about holding. A bounded singularization still needs a plural world to switch back to, and pressure that is stored is not contained, only deferred. The ending defeats his plan on both counts, so the failure is not presented as simple carelessness.
- **Appearance strategy:** He is known through old records, a recurring signature, and indirect messages before he appears in person. His presence should be foreshadowed from the beginning so that the final antagonist is part of the original mystery rather than a late substitute threat.
- **Ending function:** Soren is defeated by the loss of sole authorship, not by a hidden stronger power. His final choice is to maintain the Index for a few minutes so the city can evacuate, then release it when Milo and the communities provide an alternative. Soren dies when the Index loses its single center; he does not regain control or receive a restoration.

## Neris Rook — Milo’s mother and absent presence

- **Age:** thirty-eight when she was lost, eighteen years before the opening.
- **Role:** Lark Street tram conductor, route-maker, and practical organizer of the evacuation.
- **Want:** Get the people beneath Lark Street out without turning any one group into an expendable route.
- **What she leaves:** A brass route-token, a marked tram turnaround, and a final written sentence distributed through several survivors rather than one intact recorded message.
- **Mystery truth:** Neris did not become a person trapped inside a street. She died during the Lark seamquake. Her memory pressure remains as a role, route, and set of choices shared by the people who followed her instructions. The remnant’s apparent “Neris” is a composite grammar, not a soul preserved outside death.
- **Emotional function:** Milo’s final acceptance must not require forgetting her. It requires understanding that the people who survived her choices are not substitutes for her, and that they are entitled to make new choices.

## Relationship network at the opening

- **Milo and Mara:** professional opposition with immediate mutual respect.
- **Milo and Pell:** cautious mentorship that becomes chosen partnership.
- **Milo and Iona:** mentor relationship complicated by her history with Soren.
- **Milo and Tarin:** adversarial professional relationship with a potential for future cooperation.
- **Mara and Halden:** loyalty built from shared competence, then broken by evidence that Halden’s safeguards conceal crimes.
- **Mara and Sel Iri:** ideological adversaries who each want to protect people the other is willing to endanger.
- **Soren and Milo:** absent father, inherited method, and unresolved grief. The relationship is a central conflict, not a surprise parent reveal.
- **Neris and Milo:** a living bond in the first half; after the truth emerges, a bond represented by a route, a choice, and other people’s memories.

## Cast rules

- Supporting characters have wants, routines, and disagreements that exist without Milo.
- No character becomes a passive explanation device.
- Romantic and family relationships do not erase professional accountability.
- Death, when it occurs, changes the route, work, and obligations of the people left behind.
- No new major antagonist may be introduced after the series outline without updating the antagonist ladder and flagging the change.

### Milo Rook, after Chapter 50

- **Six crossings and no seventh. This batch adds none**, and the reason is on the page in six words: on the tenth of the month after he was under an awning with nothing in his hands and the door was shut. **Eight remembered routes and no ninth**, and he has walked four of them this month without losing any.
- **The fifth finding is on paper for the first time and has not helped.** Iona Sen made him write it in his own hand, dated the sixteenth of Sazur and signed, took the page for a locked drawer, and then said out loud in a yard in the rain that writing it down has made it worse, because there is now a piece of paper about a person and he is the man holding it. **It is on her, she would do it again, and it is not a cure and he is not cured.**
- **He failed at the thing he is good at.** He had a copy of the district's turn sheet in his hands for about six minutes, read the head, the date, the reduction, the columns, the rule and the route in the head, **and did not read the column of names**, because he has been told not to be alone with a piece of paper about a person. Rue read them aloud. It cost a woman of sixty who had kept off every list for nine years and a man of forty-one who has no record of existing anywhere.
- **He refused, in public, to be the author of the thing he could have built** — the columns, the rule, the versions, the arithmetic and the refusal are his, and he is not the holder, the clerk, the custodian, or the only pen. He was told in a yard that being right is worth about half of being useful, and he accepted that.
- **He was made to give away even the design.** Mara Quill decided, in a yard, that the promise he made to her stands and does not get wider, and that what widens instead is his authorship, because a document is a document by the method it was made in and there are now three copies of his method in the district and one has six names on it that four people did not agree to. He argued for nine seconds and accepted it.
- **The written line home is intact, correct, and he walked it twice in the week ending the fifteenth.** The written-line loss remains a Chapters 51–53 event and is not hinted at.

### Mara Quill, after Chapter 50

- **The Volume 1 milestone this batch is a loss and not a gain.** She decided, out loud and in a yard, that the promise of the twenty-ninth of Sazur stands, is hers, and does not get wider, because she cannot carry thirty people, and because the day she promises a woman of sixty that nothing about her reaches her in writing is the day she has become a second Registry. **What she took instead was the design**, and she built a fifth version of the turn sheet herself, on Registry paper she is not supposed to use, with columns that are not his, and holds the nail on alternate weeks so that **the rule at the foot is in her hand and not his**.
- **She refused to rescue him twice in this batch**, and both refusals are on the page: she would not look for a fifth Thursday in any form, and she refused to let him be a person who could be leaned on. **The Lark disclosure is a third refusal and it is not one of these** — it is offered and refused in Chapters 29–30, it is recorded in `state/continuity.md`, and the string *Lark* does not appear anywhere in Chapters 41–50, where the token is only cold on the thirteenth. Do not credit this batch with it.
- **She disclosed the intake routing slip's fourth set of initials in a yard for the first time and still did not read them aloud**, and named that as the thing she is carrying into the room on the third of next month. Her own-conduct review sits on the **third of next month**.
- **Nothing romantic has happened and nothing is going to.** Her vocabulary in this batch was procedure, disclosure, disagreement in front of about twelve people, and refusal.

### Pell Harrow, after Chapter 50

- **He is back on the round and he has not seen Tern.** He asked one question he will not write down and got a true answer, and would rather have had a lie.
- **He supplied the design of the deliberate blank in a sentence** — *a name with nobody behind it you can go and knock on; a space nobody knows what is supposed to go in it, and that is an invitation; so it is not left because nobody has come, it is left because it says so* — and it is the design decision the volume guardrail needed and nobody else in the district had.
- **He refused two things**: to make the round into a service, and to go back and look at what he found in Wragg Street, *because the day I go back and look is the day I am a man with a list instead of a courier with a round, and I know which one I am and I have known since I was fourteen.*
- **He is not Milo's magical assistant and has no power of any kind.** His mark holds for a working shift and has not been asked to hold one overnight, and that limit is still not tested.

### Milo Rook, after Chapter 60 — the end of Volume 01

- **Eight crossings, and the two added in this batch each cost a route.** The seventh, on Sunday the seventh of December, in at 16.06 and out at 16.35, twenty-nine minutes inside, the low end of the reach, with Hettie Brann's count from the path head and Doreen Sankey walking him in and out; the eighth, on Monday the eighth, in at 09.06 and out at 09.33, with Pell Harrow's count and a nurse's card for the fourth condition. **Both read out in a yard with Iona Sen's tally, which now has eight lines on it, and she makes him say the number out loud.**
- **Ten remembered routes, and the original eight intact.** The ninth is the hundred and one steps of the stair tower at the interchange, on a card from Ola Petrossian; the tenth is the walk of the eighteenth of October with a lamp and a cat in a laundry bag, marked on a card by a woman of sixty from the second landing because Rue would not do it herself and that woman has not been on a list since November. **All ten are marked by somebody who is not him and he has written down that this is the whole of the condition and not a favour.**
- **The written line home went on the twenty-fourth of November and it is the second stage of the cost and it is not the first.** Nine lines, his own hand, the oldest entry in the book, gone clean, the paper and the ink on either side intact. **It is not persistent inside Volume 1 and he can still find home when it is marked.** On the fifteenth of December it is twenty-two days old and still blank; **he asked two people to write it for him in the last hour of the last chapter and both refused him and both were right**, and he wrote down that being refused by this district has been the correct thing twice in two months and that he does not want to get used to it. He cannot tell whether it is a cost, a bad memory or a page that needs another person's mark, has written all three down in that order, and has decided none of them. **Persistence and Mara Quill's return route are Volume 2.**
- **The Lark token was warm once, for about four minutes, on the reach, and he did not use it.** The negative space lined up with a hundred and forty feet of made ground exactly. **The reason he wrote on the facing page is the volume's whole argument**, and it is worth quoting in any later file that touches him: *if a street is open because of something I can take out of my coat, then the three answers in this district stop being three answers the moment one of them is a thing I can take out of my pocket and put back, and if I open that with this then every bit of giving it away was a way of keeping the key.* It was cold again in about ninety seconds and it has been cold on every other day of the batch.
- **He entered the overlap with a map he did not draw, and he read the rule at the foot out loud before he used it.** The instrument was Rue's two-column sheet with the rule in her own hand, and his contribution on the reach was three things called in daylight in front of about four hundred people — **the order's public safety claim, the missing worker record, and the current residents' presence while the crossing was open** — and a fourth that Doreen Sankey made him say, which was his own motive, in public, in his own hand, once: *I am not going in as a man with a piece of paper in my hand and I am not going in for Lark Street and I am not going to be the reason this place is kept, and if this works today then it works because there are about nine people holding it up and I am one of the nine and I am the one who can be replaced.*
- **He is not cured, he is not fixed, and the fifth finding has not been healed.** Dr Sen corrected it out loud in a yard in the last week: the condition is not *do not be alone with a document about a person*, it never helped him once; **the condition is that he gets walked to a door and walked out of a door, every time, by a person who is not you, and says so in advance, and if they do not come you do not go** — and that it took a line going out of a book to make a man ask for it, and that this is not a magic cost and not a doctor's opinion, it is just what happened.
- **He must not be written in Volume 2 as Volume 1's author.** His own last sentence: *I am the man who wrote the columns and did the arithmetic and is not the author, and is not the holder, and is not the clerk, and is not the custodian, and has been in a corridor for ninety minutes this afternoon while a woman of about thirty-four put her name in a box in a hall. That is the whole of what I did in three months, and I have written it down, and I am not going to be able to add a line to it, and I am not going to want to.*

### Mara Quill, after Chapter 60 — the end of Volume 01

- **She paid, and she paid deliberately, and she said so in a corridor.** Her own-conduct review on the third of December found six years of off-hours work and an undeclared fourth set of initials; **she lost preferred access, has a two-year note, appealed in one line, and it is not stayed.** On the fifteenth she certified a head count with a declaration of interest at the top of it, which is the third thing in a file about her own hand, and filed nineteen paragraphs and an attachment she knows will be produced against her for the rest of her working life — *a document in a file is a thing somebody can produce, and I have read it, and I would rather be the person who put it there.*
- **The empty box is hers and it is the mechanism.** On the fourth of December she refused the department's request for a schedule of persons resident at the withdrawn alignment, and put the refusal on the file in her own hand with her own date, and said in it that she does not know that no persons are resident there and knows the schedule is short by twenty-one. **The appeal of the eighth of December therefore went in with a form box that said nothing, in her own hand, and about forty people in her own district had seen her say it, and that is the only reason she could stand in a room and ask for an order to be suspended.**
- **She refused, in this batch, to be kind to him about the book**, to speak for a household, to produce the turn sheet in any room, to call an unsworn mechanic as a man rather than as a document, to go to a Bench before the event, and **to ask a clerk in a hall of thirty people to look up a file number.** She has not been thanked for any of it and did not ask to be.
- **The relationship milestone for Volume 1 is mutual competence, and the last step of it is standing next to him and not being able to do anything about it.** On the fifteenth, in a yard of about eleven, she said the thing she had not said: *on the twenty-ninth of Sazur a man promised me that nothing of his bearing on this file would reach me in writing with a date on it, and I held that for forty-four days and then I made him give away the design instead, and he gave it away in about nine seconds and he did not complain, and that is the most decent thing anybody has done for me in six years and I have not said so to him and I am not going to.* About nine people looked at him and he looked at the ground. **Nothing romantic has happened and nothing is going to. She is not going to write his return route for him in Volume 1 and it was never hers to write, and he has not asked, and the promise stands and is hers and does not get wider.** She remains a Continuity Records advocate, in the Registry, damaged, and not his protector.

### Pell Harrow, after Chapter 60 — the end of Volume 01
