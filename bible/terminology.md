# Terminology

Use these terms consistently in prose and outlines. Capitalization is intentional where a term names an institution, a formal category, or a named historical event. Avoid adding game-like synonyms for the same idea.

## Core magical terms

**Remnant street** — A real route or place that persists in the Margin after its public name, usable routes, and shared witnesses have been forcibly separated. It is a pressure-pattern of human use, not a ghost copy or an alternate reality. Unless a text says **thin remnant**, *remnant* means the full kind, with a cue, a service, a demand, and a failure.

**The triad** — The three supports every inhabited place rests on: **name**, **route**, and **witness**. A full remnant forms only when all three are withdrawn. Severing one support leaves a ruin, a false address, or a rumor; severing two leaves an unstable **thin remnant**; severing all three leaves a full remnant. Use these three words rather than alternatives such as body, address, or witness community when describing how a place was erased.

**Thin remnant** — The unstable residue of a partial erasure, in which one of the three supports survives. It can offer one service at most, cannot maintain its own witness, and is the usual site of a small seamquake. Thin remnants are common, cheap to dismiss, and frequently mislabeled as ordinary unsafe ground. A half-completed erasure can convert a thin remnant into a disproportionately coherent full remnant, because the one surviving support is left carrying pressure it was never built for.

**Seam** — A point or short boundary where a remnant street overlaps the present city. A seam can be a door, drain, platform, shadow, sound corridor, or ordinary-looking street corner.

**Memory grammar** — The dominant rule by which a remnant interprets movement, exchange, roles, care, feeling, debt, or destination. Canonical grammars are Cadence, Keepsake, Kinship, Mercy, Ledger, Weather, and Threshold. There are no others, but the list is a vocabulary and not a solution: each street combines a dominant grammar, a leaking secondary grammar, its own disguised cue, its own demand, and its own failure condition, so two streets of the same grammar can require opposite handling.

**Margin** — The shared pressure archive of routes, names, and habits that the official map cannot reconcile. It is not sentient, not an afterlife, and not a literal underground city.

**Atlas residue** — The limited, sometimes distorted rule-warning that appears in a Field Book near a strong seam. It gives a short prompt, not a solution.

**Field Book** — Milo Rook’s paper route-work book. It holds measurements, hand-drawn maps, consent notes, and occasional Atlas residue. It is not a modern dashboard or automated guide.

**Threshold** — The first mark of a viable map: the physical place where a route enters or changes state. “Threshold” is also a memory grammar concerned with doors, boundaries, and destinations.

**Turn** — The second mark of a viable map: the meaningful action, obstacle, or relationship that makes a route move.

**Witness** — The third mark of a viable map: a living person, maintained object, or repeated routine that proves the place’s use continues. A witness can refuse, change their answer, or set a boundary.

**Return route** — A marked route back to the present that another person can recognize. It is not an automatic escape and can fail if its witness loses trust.

**Blank** — A deliberate gap in a field map reserved for current life, ambiguity, and future change. A blank is not a missing power or a puzzle waiting to be filled. An **orientation blank** is a lost sense of route, a **personal blank** is a lost connection to a place, relationship, or sensory detail, a **record blank** is a failure of official documentation, and an **authorial blank** is Milo’s relinquishment of sole Atlas authorship. None is a generic resource or automatic skill upgrade.

**Restoration** — A negotiated return of a remnant place to enough coherence for living people to inhabit, govern, and change it. Restoration does not guarantee a historical replica.

**Claimed place** — A remnant that has filled its blank with an old identity and displaced current residents into inherited roles or obligations. It is a dangerous failure state, not a legitimate traditional claim.

**Counter-map** — A shared map assembled from separately authored routes, with conflicts preserved as boundaries and alternatives rather than erased to make one clean line.

## Institutions and history

**Alderquay** — The river-delta city where the primary story takes place.

**Continuance Charter** — Alderquay’s public civic charter. It promises equal access to roads, water, schools, and relief while granting the Registry emergency authority over unstable seams.

**Civic Atlas Office** — The public mapping and utility-survey bureau where Milo works.

**Registry of Continuance** — The city authority responsible for sealing unsafe seams, managing continuity orders, relocating displaced residents, and maintaining the legal authority and central Index of the official Atlas. The Civic Atlas Office maintains the public-facing map.

**Continuance order** — A legal and practical instruction to close, redirect, rename, or remove a place from the public record. An order can be lifesaving, corrupt, or both.

**Continuity Records** — The Registry division that manages names, land records, relocation papers, continuity orders, and public appeals. Mara Quill works in this division.

**Common Atlas** — The distributed civic instrument that records routes, names, and obligations across Alderquay. It is made of route-stones, paper records, trained custodians, living workers, and a central index.

**Common Index** — The authoritative, singular layer of the Atlas housed beneath Crown Heights. It was meant to coordinate movement and memory but is vulnerable to forced agreement.

**Quieting** — The catastrophe forty-one years before the present in which the First Atlas’s Singular Mode was still engaged after the emergency ended, so the two incompatible accounts of a citywide disaster were forced into one and the other was suspended with nowhere to go. The plural design itself was not at fault. The event led to the first mass erasures and the Registry’s creation.

**Long Rain** — The forty-one-year-old storm sequence that struck the Alder Fault and prompted the emergency construction of the Common Atlas.

**Alder Fault** — The unstable river-delta fault beneath and around Alderquay where the Long Rain, seamquakes, and major seam activity concentrate.

**First Atlas** — The incomplete original design for the Common Atlas, built during the Long Rain response and later studied as the moral and technical starting point of the Registry. It was plural by design: several named routes, distributed custody, and deliberate gaps left wherever accounts disagreed.

**Singular Mode** — The First Atlas’s emergency setting, added during the Long Rain, which reconciled conflicting accounts by forcing one of them to become the Index’s version and suspending the others outside the instrument. It was built to be temporary and was logged as temporary. The Quieting happened when it was still engaged after the crisis ended and its output was treated as the record. The Registry later rebuilt it as a standard instrument. Soren Rook’s One Survey is its citywide, deliberate re-run, published and scheduled as bounded work that turns out to have no reachable end. Capitalize the term when naming the instrument; characters may call it “the Mode” or “the setting” in speech.

**Seamquake** — A physical disaster caused by incompatible routes or memories occupying the same space. It can collapse streets, displace crowds, or make a district claim several destinations.

**Greywake** — A citywide or regional cascade in which many sealed or excluded routes begin to overlap the present at once. It can follow accumulated erasures, a sudden release, or a singular synchronization that forces incompatible pressure through one index. Greywake is the escalating form of seamquake, not a separate world-ending monster.

**First Surveyor** — The office and person responsible for maintaining the Atlas’s central authorship. The current holder is Soren Rook, Milo’s father.

**Thread Wardens** — An old guild of surveyors, archivists, nurses, and route-keepers. Their original purpose was to preserve multiple accounts of a place.

**Open-Door Choir** — A radical movement that wakes remnant streets and rejects continuity orders. Its members disagree about whether every street should be restored immediately.

**Streetkeepers** — Residents who maintain informal routes, warnings, shared rooms, and civic memory outside official institutions.

**Quay Companies** — The shipping, construction, insurance, and transit firms whose contracts make continuity decisions economically real.

## Named places

**Lark Street** — Milo’s family neighborhood, officially erased eighteen years before the story. It is a Cadence and Kinship remnant, and its full history is not revealed in the first volume.

**Bellwether Lane** — A missing working-class lane in the river core whose evacuation bell organizes a dangerous Cadence loop. It is the first sustained case in the story. **As fixed on the page in Chapter 30 of Volume 01:** the three calls are the works' shift calls sounded from the pump-house gantry at ten to two; the first two moved people toward the old service court; the third is a check, a person saying a space is clear before the next turn; and the continuity order interrupted the sequence while residents were still being moved. It is a partially witnessed evacuation broken by the order, not a massacre echo, and the worker's death did not create its grammar.

**Saltmarket Row** — A Keepsake remnant tied to food, tools, and trade. Its doors open for shared work, not theft.

**Mercy Ward** — A former hospital court whose Mercy grammar can stabilize a patient while a living person tends them.

**Rainway** — A Weather remnant in Mireward that carries deferred collective grief and can alter rain, pressure, and public mood.

**Doorstep Cut** — A Threshold passage used by commuters; it reaches different destinations depending on the destination honestly named.

**Widow’s Landing** — A Kinship remnant near the river docks that recognizes people by the care work they actually perform.

**Ledger Street** — A Ledger remnant where concealed obligations can be seen, but careless touching can transfer a phantom debt.

**Hall of Wards** — The Registry’s public hearing and inspection building in Crown Heights, where continuity orders can be challenged.

**Thread Hall** — A hidden exchange and school for field cartographers beneath Crown Heights. It is not a wizard academy; it is a trade institution with old records and unsafe experiments.

**Crown Heights** — The northern terrace containing the Registry archive, old survey schools, and foundations of the First Atlas.

**Mireward** — Wet fenland south of Alderquay, where routes shift with water and residents use landmarks rather than fixed coordinates.

**South Salt** — Industrial district of salt works, pump houses, worker housing, redevelopment, and contested blank lots.

**Outer Reach** — The service roads, orchards, depots, pilgrimage paths, and camps beyond Alderquay’s built districts; later volumes use it to show how the Margin reaches beyond the city.

## Grammar names in prose

Use the grammar names as ordinary technical labels used by trained cartographers, not as spoken battle cries:

- **Cadence:** sequence and timing.
- **Keepsake:** object, sensation, and routine.
- **Kinship:** roles, relationship, and recognition.
- **Mercy:** care, shelter, and repair.
- **Ledger:** debt, exchange, and property.
- **Weather:** collective feeling and season.
- **Threshold:** boundary, passage, and destination.

A character may call a street “a Keepsake place” after learning its rule. Do not use “Keepsake grammar” as a replacement for every noun; vary natural language in dialogue.

## Usage rules

- Do not call a remnant a ghost, shade, dungeon, pocket dimension, or simulated level unless a character is using inaccurate folk language.
- Do not describe restoration as recovery of a perfect original. The current residents’ changes are part of the restored place.
- Do not make “memory” a universal resource that can be drained from anyone at will. Milo pays with his own orientation, sensory access, autobiographical connection, and ability to remain fixed in one map.
- Do not import modern technical jargon into the Field Book or the Atlas. The setting uses paper, instruments, guilds, civic law, and physical routes.
- Do not introduce a new category of grammar, faction, or final enemy in later volumes without recording it in the bible and continuity state.

It is entered on a per-head additional schedule, **not** the street index, **not** a rate book, **not** a rateable place, so a compensation assessed on displacement from a named place is untouched; and **a Continuity referral for a school place asks for a person**, so the same signature opens the intake. **The consequence, stated aloud in a hall on the fifteenth of December: the per-head additional schedule for the household is charged to the named head and not to the district rate, at two and four a head a week, for every person entered, week by week, in advance, with no term, ended only by the household ceasing to exist or by the district producing a rateable basis for a block that has no street.** **In Volume 1 this is paid by Lena Marr, and it is not curable, and the person who signed it had two questions written down and showed them to a woman of sixty-three on a step first.** The rule underneath it is the volume's: **a rate is built on a property, a person in a room is not a property, and a rate cannot be built on a door — and if the district cannot build a rate on a door then somebody has to stand at the door.**

**Ola Petrossian** — clinic nurse, twenty-three, four months in the job, never been past the tram steps. **Gave the ninth of the ten remembered routes on a card at a door with a date on it, on the seventh of December at ten to four**, and satisfied the fourth of Iona Sen's four conditions on the eighth crossing at a quarter past eight without being asked. She is not a device and she has no power and she has not been thanked.

**Cass Ardery** — Survey Enforcement field officer, promoted in this batch from a named instrument to a working one. **He withdrew his own classification of the eleventh in ink across his own paragraph, dated it and initialled it, wrote the word *pinned* into the box that says *nature of hazard*, and said that *minor* was true in a different ground and is not true in this one.** He wrote the triad accounting into a report on the back of a works truck and **refused the word *triad* in it**, because he has read it twice in the file and cannot define it and *a term in a report that nobody in the report can define is a term that gets removed by the next man.* He **asked in writing to be second-in-command on the investigation of a thing he classified**, and it was granted, and his reason is that *a department that cannot have the man who made the mistake in the room is a department that has already decided.* He is not a friend and not an ally and not a final antagonist.

**The written line home** — nine lines in Milo's own hand, with the loop he cannot stop making on the capital L, the oldest surviving entry in the Field Book, written from the door of a building with no number through the fourth loose step, the second stair not the first, the dead lamp on the third step, past the chandler and the gate and the wall with the paint on it and along by the water: *nine minutes and I have done it since I was nineteen.* **It went on Monday the twenty-fourth of November at about ten past eight in the morning. The page is there, the hand is there, the ink on either side is the same ink, and it is clean to the bottom of the line, and it is not fading.** It is the second stage of the orientation cost, it is separate from the remembered-route cost, and **it is not persistent inside Volume 1: he can still find home when it is marked, and the marking comes from somebody who is not him.** **It is still blank on the fifteenth of December, twenty-two days in, and he has asked two people to write it for him and been refused twice by people who had no reason to refuse him.** Volume 2 is where a crossing makes it persistent and Mara Quill writes his return route, and **neither of them may be asked for it here and neither has asked.**

**The seventh and eighth crossings** — **Sunday the seventh of December, in at 16.06 and out at 16.35, twenty-nine minutes inside, from the low end of the reach, count held by Hettie Brann from the path head, walked in and out by Doreen Sankey; and Monday the eighth, in at 09.06 and out at 09.33, twenty-seven minutes inside, count held by Pell Harrow with a counter, walked by Mr Sankey, clinic at a quarter past eight.** All four of the signed conditions satisfied in both. **Both read out in a yard with Dr Iona Sen's tally present, which now has eight lines on it.** Each cost one remembered route — **the hundred and one steps of the stair tower, and the walk of the eighteenth of October with a lamp and a cat in a laundry bag** — and **the second-stage cost was not paid twice for the same passage, because it had already been paid, without a crossing, on the twenty-fourth of November.** **Neither is a page of counting — the counts are on Iona Sen's tally, which has eight lines on it, and the book carries the two passages as dated time entries — and Milo Rook is across eight times in Volume 1.**

**The token, once** — on the seventh of December, on the reach, **warm for about four minutes**, and the negative space in the front of it lined up with a hundred and forty feet of made ground exactly. **He did not use it.** The reason is on the facing page and it is the volume's argument: **if a street is open because of something he can take out of his coat, then the three answers in the district stop being three answers the moment one of them is a thing he can put back; and if I open that with this then every bit of giving it away was a way of keeping the key.** The token opened nothing, summoned nobody, replaced no witness, and made the old lane into no replica, and it was cold again within about ninety seconds.

**The four hundred answers** — at the third call on the sixth of December at about ten to two, with the gate at full flow in the sight of eleven people and a crew on the reach. **Not a chorus:** a check went out on a reach with about four hundred people on and around it, and **a space with a person in it has been described as clear by the person in it, in about four hundred separate and entirely ordinary ways, in under two seconds**, by people who were on a platform, or in a trench, or on a fill, or in a doorway four streets away, and one of them said it twice. **A woman of sixty-eight was not in the district on the sixth of December and did not say anything on the sixth of December, and nobody went up three flights of stairs about her.** Then **the reach took the answer and used it**: eleven inches out of true over forty feet, in one movement, and it did not go down.

**The mark that holds for a shift and not for a night** — Idar's double arrow with a date, on the reach at about eleven on Saturday the sixth of December, gone by about two in the morning on the seventh. **The Streetkeepers and the Streetkeeping practice can hold a return mark for a working shift, on the way out and on the way back in, and cannot hold one overnight, and may not be asked to.** Nobody asked Pell. He said it was him and refused to do it again, unprompted, and the rule is on the page in Rue's words.

**A crew is what keeps a road** — Tomas Rusk's sentence, given on a telephone at seven in the morning on the seventh of December with a clock on the table, and it is the mechanism of the whole case: **on the second of that June the crew came off the frontage to the pump house, and from the moment the crew came off, that length of ground was not a road, it was a gap, and a gap is not a road and it is not a hazard and it is not a street, it is a place where a road used to be, and the difference between those is a crew.** And on the sixth of December at ten to two there was a crew on that reach, and the gate was at full flow, and the form says the aperture goes back to twenty-four inches at fourteen minutes past two. **A road is kept by somebody. A road with people on it at ten to two is a crew.** He said it once and would not say it again.

**The carbon relocation schedule** — found inside the reopened order on the twelfth of December and filed by Advocate Quill on the fifteenth as **the second attachment to her formal objection**, a copy of a relocation schedule **copied off an older survey book and not off a district register**, listing four routes, three of them in the river core, and the fourth naming **a destination that is real, dated out of use, unplaceable, and about an hour and twenty minutes from Alder Quay on an entirely ordinary road.** **It carries one mark in the margin, in a hand, and it is a question about the destination and not about the order, and it is not one of the two questions on the other carbon, and it is not a third pair — a pair is two marks in two hands on one page and this is one mark in one hand.** **Nobody in Chapters 51–60 joined it to the copied turn sheet or to the copying tick, and Milo Rook wrote the reason down twice.** It is the next volume's question and it is answered nowhere in this one. **It does not touch the Quieting, the First Atlas, or the One Survey.**

**The resolution's cost ledger, as it stands at the end of Volume 01:** a woman of about thirty-four on a per-head schedule for a building with no number, weekly, in advance, with no term; a mechanic of sixty-one's name on a drainage record and in a Crown Heights file forever; a conductor of thirty in a printed rule book and off that reach for good; a foreman of thirty years reinstated twice and certifying a level he has said he cannot stand behind; an advocate of thirty-one with a two-year note, nineteen paragraphs, and an attachment that will be produced against her for the rest of her working life; a woman of sixty-eight on nothing, asked for once and not asked for; a man of forty-one behind a boatyard on no list at all; a woman of fifty-four with a plot number and no bill in six years; and a surveyor with a blank nine lines long in his own book that two people have refused to fill. **None of it is curable and none of it is a punishment, and every item was signed or said or chalked by the person it belongs to.**

**The woman on the fourth floor of Cedar Court** — **not named on the page, and she is Milo's own floor.** She came down on the Monday the tenth of the month at about four in the afternoon on her own, because there was nothing on the nail, and told a yard of about eleven that a column with a turn in it and no name in it is not a mistake anybody made at school; **she did two on the Friday because she was standing there and it was raining and there was nobody else, and she has said that if anybody writes it down as two turns then she has had two turns.** She wrote the line in herself, in the porch, in the empty space at the foot of the sheet, and it was not a name. She came down again on the Sunday the seventh at about eight, before anybody was on the path, and **walked the frontage once, front to back, ninety minutes, in daylight, at about two miles an hour, with about thirty people behind her at eleven feet and not one of them touching her arm; she did not go in and has not gone up since.** She is the second of the three answers, a memorial route rather than a home. **Her age is given once, in her own mouth, on the seventh: *because I am sixty-eight*. No other chapter gives it and no state layer may give it a second value.**

**She is not Ada Fenn, and the two may never be folded together.** **Ada Fenn is on the third floor** — she went up it with a lamp on the fourth of Sazur and brought four people down, she has said in a yard that she has been up those stairs at six o'clock every evening since the closure, and *nobody has ever walked up three flights of stairs to ask an old woman whether she would mind stopping* — and **the two residents who are sixty-eight in Volume 01 are on different floors of the same building and are referred to in the same breath exactly once, in Chapter 57, on the seventh of December.** A review pass of the volume close read *the woman on the fourth floor* as Ada Fenn in five chapters and proposed to move her down a floor; the manuscript is right and the reading was wrong, and the quotable lines are Chapter 18's *the third floor took forty minutes, because Ada Fenn did it her way and at her speed* and Chapter 30's *the third floor of that building has got four people on it and two of them are over seventy*. **No later batch may give Ada Fenn a fourth floor, and no later batch may give this woman a third.**

# Volume 02 local register (Chapters 61–70)

Cast and places introduced by the opening block of Volume 02, and the municipal machinery it uses. **Later batches must reuse these names, roles and wants rather than renaming or replacing them, and must not convert any of them into a device.** Every entry is quotable from a chapter in `chapters/volume-02/`.

## The disappearance

- **Car 214** — Line 3, the 07:52 off Crown Heights; due Alder Quay interchange **08.06**, water stairs **08.19**, shed road at Alder Quay depot **08.31**; on the shed road again at **16.41 on Monday the 22nd of December**. **Thirty-one passengers on the manifest, a conductor and a driver: thirty-three people.** The yard noticed at **08.41**; the authority's notice outside the depot went up at **10.10** and says the car **was not late**.
- **The back length** — about **600 yards** of double track between the Alder Quay interchange and the water stairs, comprising the down loop and the quay service access, withdrawn from the running diagram on **Monday the 13th of October** and therefore from the public Atlas sheet, the district street index and five route readers in the same week. **It is not a street, not a gap, and not a road: it is a length**, which is the third category of place and the one nobody has a word for. The gang calls it *the back*. **It has no name in the city and cannot be given one.**
- **Service amendment 12/44** — the authority's running-diagram series: *the twelfth amendment of the forty-fourth year*. **The number is a series and not a date.** It removed the back length; it is printed, dated, and in an amendment list any competent person may read; the *requested by* and *instructing officer* lines are both blank; the justification box contains a filing number and not a reason.
- **11/D/6** — a standing instruction of the office of the Prefect. **It is on the works' notice of the 16th of November, on service amendment 12/44, and — as a standing instruction without a number — on the district continuity order of six years back.** Three documents, one reference. A count of three is a count of three.
- **PW/Q/4** — the quay section maintenance schedule, one of a series by length. **Its third edition, dated the eleventh of September, has an empty line against the back length; its fourth, dated the twenty-second of November, has it full, with a margin note in the clerk's hand: *Length added to sched. per amd. 12/44. C.S.*** The document that justified the change did not contain the thing it was justifying, and six weeks later it did, and nobody lied.
- **The routing slip** — about four inches by six inches, with a destination box and a purpose box and two signature columns. **The purpose box on the one that matters says *for index*, dispatched and received the 10th of October.** A filing reason in a justification box is a reason, because that is what a box is.
- **The automatic box** — registers a car at the terminus and the interchange and nothing else. **The shed is a loop with a flag and not a box.** A count on the back length was taken by an automatic function because the length is not on the diagram, so there was no box in it, and the only instrument that could have said whether a car was on that length did not know the length existed. The automatic *fare* register is a different machine and counts and does not name.
- **The four turns** — the north end signal (**not a turn, and a box, working by itself at two intervals an hour since the sixth of June six years ago**); the down loop; the wall at the south end; the water stairs end with its buffer.

## Saltmarket Row

- **Saltmarket Row** — a hundred and ten yards off Cardew Street between a cold store that used to be a fruit warehouse and a net-loft shuttered since before the war; **eleven pitches down the north side, nine brick and two a hoarding**; the wholesale fish and vegetable market of the district **until nineteen years ago**; **not a street, not on the street name index, and it cannot be given an address.** **A Keepsake remnant that preserves the habits of a market and not the people in it, held up by about eleven habits and about four people**, of which five were identified in four hours and the other six are somebody else's Saturday. It is the first thing in Volume 02 that preserves people as practices.
- **The five identified habits:** a coffee man of about sixty who puts his hand on the corner of the same wall at half past five every day in his own time; a fishmonger of about forty-five who calls the lot prices from a doorway with no fish, *not for anybody*; six flat irons on a rail in an order, put there by somebody nobody may make a map of; a man of about forty who sweeps the Row badly at about six; and the scale.
- **Doreen Sankey's line about it, which is the whole of the method in one sentence: the Row is not a thing that happened, it is a habit that has got two doors.**
