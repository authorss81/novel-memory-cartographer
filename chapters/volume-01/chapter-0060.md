# Chapter 60: What the Route Looks Like Now

It is a hundred and forty feet of frontage between the foot of the embankment and the low end, a stop-screw through the stem and a padlock on the wheel, a fence with a settlement plate on it and a date on the plate, and a gravel path about nine feet wide with a lamp bracket on the wall above it that has had a dead bulb on it since before the closure and that four people have said ought to be mended and that nobody has money for.

It is called **Bridge Reach** in the district's own drainage book, in a hand four inches high, with a date against it, and it is a *works frontage* on the contractor's own maintenance schedule with a fortnightly inspection and a gang against it, and it is entered on a diversion panel in a timetable clerk's hand as *Bridge Reach (works frontage)*, and it is not a street and it is not on the street name index and it is not rateable and it cannot be given an address, and a man of forty-one who lives behind a boatyard has said, in a yard, that a works frontage is the only kind of street anybody in this district is ever going to get and that he is not complaining.

It is a **supervised passage** on named dates, in daylight, with a named person outside the reach holding a count and a named person walking in and out, and it is on about forty people, and it has a rule at the foot of a sheet of accountancy paper in Rue's hand, and it has a deliberate space at the foot of it that is still empty and has a line under it in the same pen that says *This line is left empty. It is not an oversight. It is for a person who was not here*, and it went through the seventh and the eighth of December empty and nobody filled it and nobody has said why and about four people have checked it.

It is a **temporary stay** against demolition and against forced relocation, until the conclusion of an investigation, and not thereafter unless the investigation recommends it, and it is not a permanent anything, and nobody in the river core has said the word permanent about anything.

It is **access**, to every household, to its own housing file and its own compensation file, on application, in person, at a counter — and it is not a schedule of persons, and Advocate Quill's answer of the fourth of December is on the file with her name and her own date under it, and the box is still empty and it is going to stay empty.

It is a **hearing**, in the Hall of Wards, on a date to be set, to which the district is entitled as of right and not as of favour, and it has not been set, and the reporting officer is to be a person other than any person named in the Prefect's letter or in the order.

And it is a **name**. Bellwether Lane is on the community and memorial register, marked *contested*, and is not on the street name index, and is not a rateable place, and is capable of being produced against any person who uses it, and the woman of sixty from the second landing has read that sentence four times and has said, and it is on the page, *I asked for a street for nine years and I have been given a thing that can be produced against me, and I would like the register to know that I know that, and I am going to use it anyway, and I am not going to be one of the women who waits for the perfect thing.*

---

The things that changed and are not going to change back are these, and they are on the page, and a person in this district who wants to know what happened in December can be told four sentences and can be shown a fence.

The **western culvert** is at full flow and is not going back to eighteen inches. The aperture is twenty-four inches with a stop-screw through the stem and a padlock on the wheel, the barrel is silted to about a third of its diameter from the low end, and it was re-aligned under a separate instruction over two days by nine men. **It is not the culvert that was there before the order and it is not going to be.** It is gated, and it is silted, and it is re-aligned, and every one of those three words is a change somebody made on purpose.

The **disused platform** is out of service and its nameboards are in the water, and it came out of that in the first week of November by an accident rather than by a programme, and there is a fence round it and a note on the fence.

The **stair tower** at the interchange is a hundred and one steps on a new pier with a note in the works file about differential founding, and it is on the public map, and Milo Rook cannot find it unless a nurse of twenty-three has written a number on a card.

The **four-inch steel plate** is on a bench in the works compound with a hole in it where a fixing was, and the door behind it is shut and stays shut, and the reason it is on a bench is that the works found it and there was nowhere else for it, and nobody has proposed to refit it.

The **reach** is out of true by eleven inches over forty feet and is fenced from the works gate to the north path, and there is a settlement plate on it with a date on it, and the rail that came up four feet is cut off and is in a skip on the Terrace.

**Orrin Sable** is suspended from duty in this district and is required to give account, and the order is opened for investigation into the grounds of its making and into the classification of the eleventh of the current year and into the withdrawal of a sentence from a report six years old. **That is not a finding and does not imply one.** The classification is withdrawn and the department has noted in writing that it was made correctly on the day it was made and that its withdrawal is not a criticism of the officer who made it, and Cass Ardery has said in a portacabin that he wrote it and would unwrite it in his own hand, and has, across his own paragraph, in ink, dated and initialled. He has not been suspended and he has not been praised and he is going to be the reporting officer's second-in-command on an investigation of a thing he classified, and he asked for that in writing and it was granted, and he has said that he asked for it because a department that cannot have the man who made the mistake in the room is a department that has already decided.

And the wall behind the district did not fail. That is in the file and it is true and it was the only thing the man wanted.

---

**Mara Quill** has a two-year note on her conduct, and an appeal against it lodged on the fourth in one line, and is not stayed, and has lost preferred access, and has a formal objection of nineteen paragraphs in a Crown Heights envelope with a division mark on it, and a review on the third of December that found that she had worked a file off her own hours for six years in a records alcove without telling her review panel, and that the fourth set of initials in the annex box of an intake routing slip of the sixth year back — which she has read four times since the fourth of Sazur and has still not read aloud to anybody, including him — was a matter she ought to have declared and did not.

She read that finding out in a corridor to about nine people on the third of December and about six of them did not understand a word of it and two of them understood all of it, and she has not been a favourite person in this district since the fourth, and on the fifteenth she certified a head count in her own hand with a declaration of interest at the top of it, which is the third thing in a file about her own hand, and she said in a yard of about eleven people on the Wednesday that she would not be doing any more of this for a while.

"I have got two things left that I have not spent," she said, "and I am going to spend one of them this afternoon and I am not telling anybody which, and you may all work it out and be wrong, and I would rather that than the truth, because the truth is that I am about thirty-one years old and I have a two-year note and a hearing that is not set and a file in Crown Heights with my name in it four times, and on the twenty-ninth of Sazur a man promised me that nothing of his bearing on this file would reach me in writing with a date on it, whether I could file it or not, and I held that for forty-four days and then I made him give away the design instead, and he gave it away in about nine seconds, and he did not complain, and that is the most decent thing anybody has done for me in six years and I have not said so to him and I am not going to."

Nobody in the yard said anything, and about nine of them looked at Milo Rook, and he looked at the ground, and Rue said afterwards that it was the only time in eleven weeks she had watched two people in this district be in the same place and be unable to do anything about it, and that it was also the first time she had wanted to.

Nothing romantic has happened and nothing is going to. **She is not going to write his return route for him in Volume 1 and it was never hers to write**, and he has not asked, and the promise of the twenty-ninth of Sazur stands and is hers and does not get wider, and neither of them has said anything about how either of them feels, and neither of them is going to, and if a person in this district were to say so in a yard he would find about nine people looking at him.

---

The Field Book is a foolscap book with a limp spine and it has two hundred and eleven entries in the left-hand side going up the page and it is the oldest surviving thing in the world as far as he is concerned, and on the fifteenth of December, at about half past four in the afternoon, in the route room of the Civic Atlas Office, on the second of the two tables under the north window, it is open at a page in the middle, and the page is clean.

Dilley Farr was on the other table and had been on it for about two hours and had said four words to him in that time, and one of them was *sit*, and Milo Rook was sitting, and the book was open, and the entry is nine lines long in a hand of his own from a Monday in a year that does not matter, and it starts with the loop he has never been able to stop making on the capital L, and then it is clean to the bottom of the page, and the next entry starts on the Monday after, in the same ink, with the same nib.

*Out the door. Left. Eleven steps to the hall, the fourth one loose, take the second stair not the first. Down to the front. The lamp on the third step goes on the left and you wait four seconds for it. Along the front to the corner, do not cut the corner. Past the chandler, and the gate, and then the wall with the paint on it, and along by the water, and it is nine minutes and I have done it since I was nineteen.*

Three chalk arrows on the way home. One on the outside of the building at about five feet, at the foot of the stairs, pointing up, with the fourteenth of December under it. One on the inside of his own front door at about four feet, with the twenty-eighth of November under it. One on the wall of the second-floor landing, with no date, because he did not ask, and it was there on the tenth and it was still there on the fourteenth and it is on the landing of a building with no number and he is not going to ask the woman on the second floor who put it there because she is sixty and has been off every list for nine years and is not going to be on one now, and that was the price.

The Route Room has three atlases on the wall and a repair schedule on a board and about four hundred sheets of paper in a plan chest with a district seal on it, and none of it has Bellwether Lane on it, and the **community and memorial register** is in a different building in a different part of the city and is a bound book with a contested entry in it, and the **street name index** has a line against the reach that says *no rateable place — works frontage — see Bridge Reach*, and somebody in the district index section wrote *no rateable place* in a box that exists for a rating, and nobody has taken it out, and Milo Rook asked Dilley Farr whether the box was the right one and Dilley Farr said it was the only one there was and that he had looked for about a minute, and that a box that says *no rateable place* in the district's own index is the most useful thing anybody in that building has written in six years and he was not going to have it tidied.

"Sit down," said Dilley Farr.

"I am sitting."

"You have been on that page for the best part of an hour and you have not turned it over and you have not asked me anything, and I have been in this room for thirty years and I do not know a single thing about a seam and I do not want to, and I want to say one thing to you and then I am going to go and finish a schedule." He did not look up. "I have watched three men in this room lose a route in eleven years. Two of them had a wife. Neither of them wrote it down, and both of them stopped coming in, and the department let them go and the department was right to, because a surveyor who cannot find a door is not a surveyor. And you have written yours down, in your own hand, with a date, and you have written the rule at the foot of somebody else's sheet and you have given the thing away in a porch in front of thirty people, and I want you to know that I have signed no deletion in this room for six weeks and the reason is not principle, it is that there has been nothing in front of me worth signing a deletion for."

"Then why," said Milo Rook.

"Because of you," said Dilley Farr. "You have made four things in six weeks that I can sign a deletion about. Go and finish your page. I will not read it and I will not be asked and if anybody in this building asks me what is in it I will say that it is a piece of paper, because there is a woman on a counter in your district who has been saying that to a badge for six weeks and it has begun."

---

He asked at about ten past five.

He asked Rue in the porch at about ten past five, first, because Rue is the person and because she had said in a yard on the third of December that the whole of what she does is that she is not the one who writes a thing down, and she heard the whole of the question and said no to it in about four words.

"No. That is mine, and mine is three streets and a chandler's yard and a pie shop, and I have walked it since I was sixteen and I do not know a step of yours and I am not going to learn nine steps in a hall and then have them in my mouth in your kitchen." She put the satchel over her shoulder. "And it would be me. And the whole point of every one of these three months is that the thing that is written on this street is not written by any one person. Write it down. Ask somebody who has to write it down."

He asked Doreen Sankey at about twenty past five, at the gate, in the rain, and Doreen Sankey listened to the whole of it and shook his head.

"No," he said. "No, and I will tell you why, and then you will not ask me again, and I am right. I have been asked for that route in about four houses this month and I have said no in all four and one of them was a man with a good coat and one of them was a man with a worse coat, and if I walk it once to write it down then in a month somebody will be able to say the boatyard man keeps the surveyor's way home, and the second it is written down it is a job, and I know what a job is, because in a house in Wragh Street a woman has kept a sheet for nineteen years and her husband is dead four years and there is a tick in it she cannot account for." He got his coat open against the rain. "Ask a person who has to be there. Not a person who is passing."

Nobody filled it in.

At about ten to six on the fifteenth of December, in a route room in the Civic Atlas Office, with the light going and about four people in the building, Milo Rook took out the pencil and wrote nine lines at the bottom of a page in the back of the book, in his own hand, and dated them, and it is the last entry in Volume 1 and it is the only one in two hundred and twelve that is an entry about a thing that is not there.

*Fifteenth of next month, 17.50. Twenty-second day since the twenty-fourth of last month. I asked two people in the yard and at the gate and both of them said no and both of them were right and I have written down who and why, and it is the second time in two months that being refused by this district has been the correct thing and I am not going to get used to it and I do not want to.*

*Ten remembered routes. Eight from the fourth of Sazur and none of them gone and all of them marked by somebody who is not me. The ninth is a hundred and one steps, a card, a nurse of twenty-three, the seventh of this month. The tenth is the walk of the eighteenth of October with a lamp and a cat in a laundry bag, ground-floor door, coal hatch side, along the path, out the works gate, round by the gate-man's stool, to the tram steps, and back — marked on the ninth, in Rue's hand, on a card, with a date, and she would not do it herself, she made the woman on the second landing do it because that woman has not been on a list since the fourth of Sazur and was on a list on the third and would not have it again. So it is marked. Ten is ten and I can walk all ten and the man who wrote this sentence on the twenty-fourth of November would not have believed the paragraph above it.*

*And the line home is still a blank, and it has been a blank for twenty-two days, and I asked for it this afternoon and I was refused twice by two people who had no reason to refuse me and who gave me their reasons, and it is still a blank.*

*I cannot tell what it is. I have written down three things and I want them on the page in this order because I have been wrong about the order of things in this case for eleven weeks. It might be a cost, and it might be a bad memory, and it might be a page that needs another person's mark. And the honest state of the matter is that I do not know which, and I have been in this trade for eleven years and I have never once in my life written a road down and been unable to find it, and I cannot now, and I am going to be in this district in January with a piece of paper with a blank on it in it, and I am not going to be the man who fills it in.*

*The Lark token was cold on the fourteenth and on the fifteenth and I did not take it out. It was warm once, on the seventh, for about four minutes, on the reach, and I did not use it, and the reason is on the facing page of the sixth and it is the only thing in this book I have ever been frightened of getting right.*

*The book is silent on the ninth, the tenth and the eleventh and it wrote on the twelfth and on the fifteenth. The ninth and the eleventh are three days. I have written that pair down. The twenty-fifth, the twenty-sixth and the twenty-seventh of last month were silent and the twenty-eighth was not, and that is three days, and that is another one, and I have written that down as well, and there are seven pairs in this book now — six days, nine days, three days, three days, two days, three days, three days — and I am not going to join any of them to any other, and I am not going to collapse the two threes into the threes, and I am not going to write down what I think seven of a thing means, and there is an eighth one of a single day in the middle of this month that I looked at on the tenth and did not write, and it stays looked at.*

*A street is a thing people keep. I am not going to write that in this book. It wrote it on the eighth and it wrote it about a road and it does not say who keeps one, and I have had a brass token in my coat for six weeks that will open something, and the one thing I know about this place is that the person who can open it is the one person on it who must not.*

*So there is a hundred and forty feet of frontage with a name in a drainage book, and a fence, and a settlement plate, and a rule at the foot of a sheet of accountancy paper in Rue's hand, and a space at the bottom of it that is empty on purpose, and a woman of sixty with a contested name on a register that can be produced against her, and a conductor in a rule book, and a mechanic on a page with his name at the foot of it and no oath on it, and a man of sixty-eight who is on nothing and has not been asked, and a woman of fifty-four with a plot number in her own hand, and a boy of seventeen with chalk. And I am the man who wrote the columns and did the arithmetic and is not the author, and is not the holder, and is not the clerk, and is not the custodian, and has been in a corridor for ninety minutes this afternoon while a woman of about thirty-four put her name in a box in a hall.*

*That is the whole of what I did in three months, and I have written it down, and I am not going to be able to add a line to it, and I am not going to want to.*

He wrote the times down after that, because he writes times down, and he put the date at the top of a clean page, and the Field Book wrote nothing for the rest of the evening and he has not decided what that is either, and has not written it down.

---

Across the city, at about a quarter to five on the afternoon of Monday the fifteenth of December, Advocate Quill put a parcel on a counter in a corridor of the Registry of Continuance and it is nineteen paragraphs and it is in her own hand and it is a formal objection and a public safety appeal against the order of the sixth year back, and it is against a classification made correctly on the eleventh of the current year, and it is against a sentence taken out of a report six years ago, and it is against six years, and it is against a dead man whose name is on a card index in a district office with his wife's address on the other side of it.

She knows exactly what it will do to her, because it will be produced against her in every room she enters for the rest of her working life, and about four people in this district know that, and one of them is a man in a route room who cannot find his own front door and who will not be told anything about how anybody feels about any of it.

The parcel has three attachments. The first is the order of the sixth year back, complete, nine boxes and a bound volume and a card. The second is the **carbon copy of a relocation schedule**, copied off an older survey book and not off a district register, listing four routes, three of which are in the river core, and the fourth of which names a destination that is not on the street name index and is not in the six-year-old reconstruction, and which has a bus service that stopped going there in a year that can be named, and which is about an hour and twenty minutes from Alder Quay on a road that is entirely ordinary, and which nobody in this case has ever stood in.

On the back of that carbon, in Advocate Quill's own hand, in biro, is four lines, and it is the only thing she has written about it anywhere, and it is filed with the attachment and not in a file of her own, and it reads:

> *This is a copy of a document and I have not traced it and I am not able to place the fourth destination and I have not been able to in two evenings.*
> *I am not saying what it means. I am saying that a copy of an old survey book names a place this city cannot point at, and that it was in the order, and that nobody has produced it in six years, including me, and I have been working off my own hours on this file for six years and I have read nine boxes in one afternoon.*
> *There is a mark in the margin of it. One mark. It is a question and it is about the destination and not about the order, and it is not one of the two questions on the other carbon, and I am not going to call it a third pair of marks because a pair is two marks in two hands on one page and this is one mark in one hand and I do not know that a second is coming.*
> *I am filing it because a document in a file is a thing somebody can produce, and I have read it, and I would rather be the person who put it there.*

The clerk at the counter stamped it and put it in a tray and it went up to a division in a building in Crown Heights, and about nine people in the river core were at that moment standing in a yard or a porch or a room with a fence round a hundred and forty feet of frontage, and none of them knew about it, and one of them was in a route room under the north window with a Field Book open at a page with nine lines gone clean out of it in his own hand, and there was a chalk arrow on the outside of his own building at about five feet with the fourteenth of December under it, and it was the arrow that had got him up his own stairs the night before, and there was a second one on the inside of his own front door with the twenty-eighth of November under it, and there was a third on the wall of the second-floor landing with no date on it at all, and nobody had ever asked who put that one there, and nobody was going to.

The third attachment was the community and memorial register, and the entry in it is four lines, and the last line is this:

> *Entered as a contested community and memorial name. Not a street. Not a rateable place. Not an address. Capable of being produced against any person who uses it. Requested in part by a resident of nine years who asked for a street and has been given a thing that can be used against her, and who has asked that this be noted, and it is noted, and the name is entered.*
