# Chapter 50: The Fifteenth

There was no door.

The works' notice on the hoarding said the coal hatch side access would be closed from the ninth of this month to the nineteenth inclusive, and on Thursday the thirteenth Lena Marr stood at the north door of the ground floor of Cedar Court at ten past one in the afternoon in the rain with Rue holding a count, and the strip of broken concrete between the flank of the building and the foot of the embankment was on the other side of a line of steel fencing with a notice wired to it, and there was a trench open in it, eight feet wide, four foot deep, and about forty feet away there were two men in the trench of the soft-lens reach and a man in a waterproof standing on the fill with a radio that was not working.

The gantry is on the far side of the embankment and the call came up out of the canyon, and it came at ten to two, and the interval went, and Milo Rook — who was under the north awning with nothing in his hands and no business there except that he had asked in writing on the Monday and had been told no and had come anyway — counted twenty-nine seconds and then thirty-one and then forty-five.

Nothing asked. Nothing asked the way it had asked on the twenty-third and the thirtieth and the sixth, and Lena Marr said *clear* anyway, at the door, into the rain, to about nine people and a trench, and the plant on the far side of the embankment did not stop, and the two men in the trench did not put their hands down, and the man in the waterproof did not look up.

"Right," she said. "That is the fourth. I am writing it down as not done, and I am going to say why in front of you and then I am never going to talk about it again, and I am not going to be asked."

"Say why."

"I was not at the threshold. The application said the threshold of the withdrawn alignment and the minute was got on the strength of the application, and I have been paid for four Thursdays at a door and there has been a door on three of them, and on the fourth there is a fence and a trench, and I have said a word at a door forty feet from the wrong side of a fence." She took a card out of her coat. "And the other thing is that a man in that trench said *nobody told us* on the twelfth and he has been saying it every working day since, and he said it again on Thursday, and I am not going to stand in the rain and be paid for a check that a man with a shovel has just told me does not exist."

She wrote four lines on the card in her own hand and signed it and gave it to Hettie Brann, who came out of the counter for it, who read it, and who said one sentence: "I am not reading this and I am taking it, and I am going to hand it to the clerk at the gate, and if a clerk asks me what it is I am going to say it is a piece of paper."

---

The instrument expired at noon on Friday the fourteenth of the month and it did so in a way that nine people came to watch, which nobody had planned and which is the only honest account of it.

They came at a quarter to twelve, and the woman at the door of the Board school came, and she came with the list against her chest, and she said the thing she had come to say while she was still at the gate and before anybody had said anything else, which was that she had asked Mara Quill in a corridor on the Thursday whether there was anything that could be done in a room, and that Mara Quill had said no, and had said why, and that the why had been that a thing which is only ever four weeks is the only instrument anybody has made in this case that could not be turned into a form.

"That is what I am told," said the woman at the door, "and I would like it said that I think that is a horrible thing to be told by the only person in this case who has ever been able to do anything, and I am going to have to go back to a list that closes at noon and there are nine children on it and two of them are not coming back in the spring and I am not going to ask anybody in this yard to make that better, because nobody can and everybody in here knows it."

At ten minutes to twelve Mara Quill read the minute aloud in the yard to about nine people, including the fourth paragraph, which is the paragraph the chair wrote into it herself on the twenty-first of this month, and which says in terms that the instrument does not suspend the order, does not restore the public name, makes no variation of the order, protects nobody and restores nothing, and cannot be extended by anybody including the chair of the Bench, and that there is no form for extending it because there is no form for a thing that is only ever four weeks.

"Everybody has read it," she said. "I am not going to say anything about it and I am not going to stand here and be sad at you. It stops at noon and the next thing that happens is that nothing happens, and I have said in a yard this week that I am not going to put a fifth Thursday anywhere and I am not going to, and if anybody in this district has a better idea than mine then it is not a room and I cannot help you with it."

Pell Harrow said, from the porch, in the flat voice he uses when he is about to say something he has written out first:

"I've been on the round since the twenty-ninth of Sazur and I have been doing it every day of it, I have been asked to make it into a service and I am not going to. A service is a thing with a timetable and a man who can be fined, and this round is a man on a bicycle going to seven doors because a woman on the ground floor went out at eight this morning and could not get to the low end, and it is that, and it is that on the worst day of the month, and it is nobody's business. You are not going to be able to make that into a route and you are not going to be able to make it into a form, and the only thing I can tell you about the fifteenth is that it is a Saturday and I will be here at eight and at four like I am here every day, and that is not an answer, and I know it is not an answer, and I have said it out loud so that it is on the page that I said it."

At noon it expired. There was nothing to see. Nobody rang anything and no clerk came and no letter was sent, and Milo Rook wrote the time down because he writes times down, and the Field Book was silent.

---

They had eleven people in the yard on Saturday the fifteenth at about eleven o'clock, which is the fewest that yard has held since the third of this month, and Hettie Brann said in the queue that it was because the rain and Nell Crale said it was not the rain.

Then the woman at the door of the Board school came down from the front of the building with a piece of paper in her hand that was not a list, and she was holding it the way you hold something that has been in a house you do not live in.

"This came in a letter box in the office this morning," she said. "It is not addressed to me. It is addressed to *the person keeping the street* and it came in with four other letters."

Milo Rook took it, and then handed it straight to Rue, who took it and read it out loud, because she is the person who reads things out.

It was a copy of the fourth version of the sheet. It was clean, it was legible, it was not forty per cent, it had the works' head on it at the top, and it had the four columns, and it had the rule at the foot, and the last line of the rule had been cut off in the copying and the space under it had been filled.

The space under it was not empty.

The space had a name in it, and Rue read the name out loud in the yard, and about nine people heard it, and the yard did not make a noise at first and then did.

It said **Owen Fitch**.

It said, in the second line of the space, **third floor**, in the same hand, and there were no words beside it. Not *not asked to*. Not anything. There was a name and a floor and the space that had been left empty on purpose had been filled in on a clean copy in a good hand in about eleven seconds, and the copy had come into a school office in a letter box addressed to *the person keeping the street*, and it was the fifth one anybody in this district had seen.

"There is also one of these in a fourth-floor flat in Wragg Street," said the woman at the door, "and a man who lets rooms has had one and shown it to two people, and I am not telling you the names of either of them and I would like it noticed that I have had a list in my hand for nine years and I have never once given it to anybody, and that is not the same thing as this and I know it is not the same thing."

Owen Fitch came down at about ten past eleven, on his own, from the outside stairs, in his coat, and he did not come into the porch and he stood where he has stood twice this month, and he had a stick, which is not usual, and he did not ask anybody to explain anything.

"Read it again," he said.

Rue read it again.

"Right," said Owen Fitch. "Then here is the whole of it and I am going to say it once and then I am going up and I am not going to be asked and I am not going to be argued with, and I would like it noticed that Lena Marr has not said a word, which is the first time in a fortnight that woman has not said a word in a yard, and that is not a criticism, she is doing the thing I asked her to do, which is not to pick."

He put his stick against the wall.

"You cannot take my name off it. I have been three weeks waiting for somebody to try and everybody has been too polite to, and I want the yard to understand what I am about to say and I do not want it softened afterwards, because I will not be coming down again about it. To take my name off that piece of paper, somebody has to stand up in a room and say that it was never ours. Not that I did not agree. Not that there is a rule at the foot. That it was never ours."

He looked at the sheet on the trestle table.

"And the minute on the twenty-first says that the public name is not restored and that no variation of the order is made, and it says that this arrangement protects nobody, and it is a minute of a Bench and it is the only thing any of us has, and if a document that was never ours has got my name and a foreman's head on it and a rule at the foot, then either it is ours — and then the woman of sixty's crossed line is a line in a district document, and the man behind the boatyard's four words are four words in a district document, and the woman on the fourth floor who came down twice this month is a name nobody can say in a district document — or it was never ours, and then it comes off the nail, and on the fifteenth there is nothing."

He looked at Lena Marr then, and she was standing at the back with her hands in her coat, and she had not said anything, and she said nothing.

"I have been the man who stands at a turn on that street since before there was an order," he said, "and I have told this yard twice this month not to put me on anything, and I have not been thanked for it and I have not wanted to be, and I have read that rule at the foot four times and it is my rule and it is the only thing in this district that has held, and it is on a piece of paper in five places with my name on one of them and no rule at the foot of that one." He picked up his stick. "So somebody in this city is writing down the people who keep this street, and it is not us, and we cannot stop it by being right, and I am not going to be on it, and I am not going to be on anything, and I would like somebody to explain to me how a man takes his own name off a thing he is not allowed to say was never his."

Nobody explained it to him.

---

Milo Rook got the sheet back at about half past twelve, and then stood in the yard and did not look at it for a while, and then did, and read the whole of it, and it took him about a minute and a half, and there is no version of this that does not record that he read the whole of it, in the open, in front of about eleven people, and read it aloud afterwards, and that he read the name on the fifth line before he read the rule at the foot.

*Fifteenth of the month, 21.20.*

*Four Thursdays are finished. One named person, four dates, three workings, and a fourth that the named person has recorded herself as not worked because she was not at the threshold, in her own hand, and I was there and she is right and I could have argued and I did not, and that is the only thing I have done this month that I would want read back to me in a room.*

*The instrument stops at noon today. It cannot be extended by anybody and there is no form for it and the chair of the Bench wrote that into the minute herself in words I have read out loud twice. Nobody asked me to try. Mara Quill has said in a yard that she is not going to look for a fifth Thursday and I have written that down because in three weeks there will be thirty people in this district who will assume that somebody is working on it.*

*And the thing that came instead is a piece of paper with a contractor's head on it and a man's name and a floor number in a space I left empty on purpose on Tuesday, in a good hand, in a clean copy, in five places, in a letter box addressed to the person keeping the street. Owen Fitch came down and said the thing that ends my month: you cannot take a name off a document without saying the document was never yours, and the rule at the foot of the page is mine, and a man who has spent six weeks refusing to be on anything has been put on a thing by his own district's arithmetic, and the arithmetic is mine.*

*Three sources of witnessing now and all three of them are documents with my method in them. The rota is Rue's and has been since the seventeenth of Sazur and the promise at the foot of it is Owen's and it is the only one that holds. The sheet is designed by me, revised by Advocate Quill on alternate weeks, held by a woman who will not read it, and has a space in it that a woman on a floor nobody can name can fill and empty at will, and is a good deal stronger than the rota and a good deal more dangerous, and I do not know which of those two facts I would rather have.*

*And the copy is the fifth and it is not the fifth of a document and it is a piece of paper that somebody has copied out of a thing this district made and has carried to a school office and to a flat in Wragg Street, and it is a list of the people who keep a street that is not on a map, and there is a man in a fenland village who asked a boy of seventeen that exact question on the thirteenth of this month, in a card, and has had a list a long time, and I am not going to write those two things in the same paragraph again, and I wrote it once and then wrote underneath it that I would not, and I have not, and the reason is that joining them would be me deciding that they are the same, and a man who decides two things are the same in a book is a man who has started making the case instead of the record.*

*What I have instead is a name in a space, and a fence, and a woman on the fourth floor who came down twice, and a mechanic's sentence that is true and unsigned with one thing attached to it that he will tell me and not a man in a coat, and a man on half rate who has certified a level over a trench he has said he cannot stand behind, and a plate on a door, and a street under six inches of water, and a Bench on the twenty-first of October that I sat in and did not speak at, and a Bench after that which I am not going to be in, and the sixth of next month, which is twenty-one days away, in a yard and on a hoarding and in a mouth.*

*Eight remembered routes. There has been no ninth this month and I have been to four of the eight and lost none of them, and I do not know what that means and I am not going to write down what it might mean. The line home is in the book in my own hand and I have walked it twice this week and it is correct and any person asked to walk it can walk it.*

*The token was cold again on Thursday and I did not take it out. The book was silent on the ninth, the tenth and the eleventh, it wrote five words on the twelfth, and it was silent on the thirteenth, the fourteenth and the fifteenth, and I opened it at about ten o'clock this morning and it wrote nothing, and I have paired the thirteenth with the fifteenth, which is two days, and written it down. There are five pairs in this book now: six days, nine days, three days, three days, and two. I am not going to make anything out of them and I am not going to collapse them either, and there is a pair of one day in this month that I looked at for a long time and did not write down, and that is the last thing I am going to write this month that is about the book.*
