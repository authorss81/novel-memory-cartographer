# Chapter 58: Quiet, Not Healed

The public safety appeal was lodged at ten past nine in the morning on Monday the eighth of next month, on the eighth day of a month in which a reach had gone out of true twice and a plate was in the water, and it is four pages, and it is the only document in this case that anybody in this district would have chosen to be the last thing they ever wrote, and about eleven of them were in a corridor in a building in Crown Heights at a quarter to nine in the morning and got it into a box.

The box was empty.

That is the whole mechanism and it is on the page in Advocate Quill's own hand and it is the only thing in eleven weeks that she built for herself, on the fourth of the month, in advance, at a cost of about nineteen days.

> *Advocate Quill's answer, given on the fourth day of the current month: NO. The district cannot produce a schedule of persons resident at the withdrawn alignment. I represent that the district is unable to compile such a schedule; that the request is in my opinion incapable of satisfaction, in that a schedule of persons resident at a place which is not a rateable place and has no address and no street is a document with no rateable unit against which any amount can be assessed; and that no schedule, list, return, or roll of persons resident at that place has been made by the district, the works, or any party. This representation is made knowingly and against my own interest as an officer, in that a nil return is capable of being read as a representation that no persons are resident there. I do not know that no persons are resident there. I know that I have been unable to establish a number and that the number on the district's schedule is short by twenty-one and that thirty-three rooms are on no list at all.*
> *M.Q., fourth of the month, own hand, own date.*

The department put the appeal in a queue at ten past nine and it is a statutory instrument with a clock on it, and the clock is six working days, and on the eighth working day the answer may be a hearing or it may be a refusal, and Mara Quill said in a corridor at about twenty past nine on the Monday morning, to about eleven people, that she had done the arithmetic on the calendar herself and that the sixth working day was the **fifteenth of next month**, and that she had not told the yard that, and that she would tell them on the Thursday, and that the reason she was telling them on the Thursday was that if she said it on the Monday then about forty people in the river core would spend the whole week in a state, and a state is a thing this case has been ruined by twice.

---

What came back in the six days is what always comes back, and Milo Rook has said since that the machinery did not fail and did not triumph, it ran, and the only thing it needed was somebody to put a date on it.

The Crown Heights letter of the twenty-ninth of last month was produced inside two hours of the appeal being lodged, by the department, voluntarily, as a matter considered, and it was in four paragraphs, and the fourth of them said that the department was aware that no similar arrangement was proposed after the fourteenth of last month.

"Read it out," said Milo Rook.

"It has been read out," said Mara Quill, "in a corridor, twice, by me, to two men, in the tone of voice I use for things I have already lost, and I did it on the Monday and I did it again on the Wednesday, and the second time one of them said, *that is a matter for the department to consider, Advocate*, and I said, *it is, and here it is, and I would like it noted that I have now volunteered it twice*."

"That is not going to be enough," said Rue.

"It was never going to be enough and it was not meant to be enough," said Mara Quill. "It was meant to be *on the record twice, on my initiative*, and it is, and that is the only difference between a document that ambushes you and a document you put on the table yourself, and the whole of my trade is about four seconds of that."

The works' filing came on the Tuesday afternoon. The tram authority's came on the Wednesday, and it is the shortest document in this case and it is four lines and it is signed by the traffic superintendent, and it is printed, and it is in a rule book, and it is the thing Jo Lask asked for on the twenty-first of November in a yard with about nine people in it.

> *Line 7 — standing instruction, reach of the Alder Quay–Bridge Street. Issued: the third of the current month. Officer issuing: District Traffic Superintendent. Conductor called out: J. LASK, conductor, Alder Quay depot.*
> *One. Where a count is taken on this reach it is recorded as a count, being a number given by a person to a person, and is not recorded as a signal.*
> *Two. A count taken at the Ferry Road turn or the Alder Quay interchange may not be replaced by an automatic function on this reach without the running time being restored in full on the day it is replaced.*
> *Three. No box on any sheet of this authority is filled by a person on this reach except under this instruction, which is published, and which names the officer who issued it.*

He read it in a yard on the Wednesday evening, in about twenty seconds, to about thirty-one people, and gave it back, and said one sentence, which was: "That is my name in a printed book and I have known since the twenty-first of November that it was going to be, and the cost of it is that I will not be conducting on this reach again and I have been on it every day since the fourth of October, and I would like that in the yard as well because it is the only part of this that cost me anything."

Nobody argued about it and a woman of sixty-three said, "Say that again about the reach," and he said it again.

---

The Prefect's office wrote on the Thursday the eleventh of next month at about four in the afternoon, and it is one page, and it is on the letterhead of the office of the Prefect, and it is signed by the Prefect's office in a hand that is not a hand and has a name at the bottom of it, and that name is **Halden Vey**, and it is the only place in the whole of this case in which that name has appeared, and he does not appear in person, and a man in a car collected the letter at a quarter to five and would not come to the yard.

> *From the office of the Prefect, Registry of Continuance.*
> *1. The district continuity order of the sixth year back (ref. CO/6/BELL) is **suspended** with effect from ten o'clock on Friday the twelfth of the current month, and its schedule is suspended, and the order is not varied, withdrawn, or discharged, and the public name is not restored by this suspension.*
> *2. The order is **opened for investigation** into the grounds of its making, into the classification of the site dated the eleventh of the current year, and into the removal from the original safety report of the sentence *no evidence was found of any hazard to persons at the service court*, the withdrawal reference against which is not a district reference and is held in the office of the First Surveyor.*
> *3. The officer who made the order, Mr O. Sable, district continuity officer, is **suspended from duty in this district** with effect from the same hour. He is required to give account. This suspension is not a finding and does not imply one. It is made because an officer may not investigate his own order.*
> *4. The investigation will be conducted by Survey Enforcement and reported to this office, and the reporting officer will be a person other than any person named in the present letter or in the order.*
> *5. The classification of the eleventh of the current year is withdrawn and is not to be relied on by any party, and the department notes that it was made correctly on the day it was made and that its withdrawal is not a criticism of the officer who made it.*
> *6. The order having been suspended, all timetables, gates, notices, classifications, drains, and records of the district and of any department are to be re-cut to the extent that they point at the withdrawn alignment, and are to cease to point at it. Re-cutting is to be certified by a person familiar with the route withdrawn.*

That last paragraph is the sixth paragraph of one page and it is the only paragraph in eleven weeks that nobody in this district was asked for, and it is in the order of a Prefect and not in the order of a district, and it is what unpinning means, and nobody in this district has said that out loud.

Paragraph one was read out in a yard on the Thursday evening at about six o'clock to about forty people by Rue, from a copy that the man in the car had left with Lena Marr, and about nine people cried and nobody has ever written down which nine, and Owen Fitch was not among the forty and came down afterwards and said one thing.

"Read me paragraph six," he said.

"Mr Rook, read him paragraph six."

"Upon the suspension of the order, all timetables, gates, notices, classifications, drains, and records of the district and of any department are to be re-cut to the extent that they point at the withdrawn alignment, and are to cease to point at it. Re-cutting is to be certified by a person familiar with the route withdrawn." Milo Rook read it twice, as he was asked. "And that last four words are a variation requirement, and I have got a letter in a file in Crown Heights that says there is no person familiar with the route withdrawn, and it was refused on the twenty-ninth of last month, and it was the correct refusal, and it is the correct refusal, and it is going to be the correct answer to this for about another three days, and then it is not going to be, and I know exactly which part of the sentence is going to change and I am telling this yard now so that nobody can say afterwards that the district was ambushed by a form."

---

The re-cut took two days and it is three documents and a shovel, and it is the best piece of work in the case and almost nobody in it.

The first is the **drainage record**. The district's own 1904 book for the low streets carries, in a hand four inches high, a length of frontage between the pump house and the low end with a name against it, and the name is **Bridge Reach**, and the entry has been struck through once in a pencil that is not a district hand and has been like that for six years, and on the Thursday morning a man from the district drainage section came out to the low end with a book and a piece of chalk and unstruck it, and wrote the date beside it, and wrote under it, in the book's own columns, *maintained frontage, 140 ft, reverting on the suspension of CO/6/BELL*, and then went to the coal hatch side and said four words to Rue, who was standing there.

"Requested by a mechanic."

He would not say more than that and nobody asked him, and the entry is in the district's drainage book, in the book's own hand, with a date, and the request is in a second book as well because Tomas Rusk asked for it in writing on the eighth of December, on one page, with his name at the foot of it and no oath on it, and that page is in the file of the reopened order and is the last thing he ever put his name to and he has said so in a yard and has not been thanked and was not thanked.

The second is the **works maintenance schedule**. The reach goes back on it as a frontage with a name and a reach, and a gang, and a fortnightly inspection, and it is on the contractor's own schedule under the employer's head, and it cost a man in a portacabin about four hundred words with a works clerk and a piece of chalk, and the words were mostly about insurance.

The third is **Barrow's amendment**, and it is the smallest and it is the one nobody in the river core wanted, and he laid it himself on the Friday morning at about eleven o'clock in a room in Crown Heights with a man from the timetable division, and it is a one-paragraph amendment, and it does not reinstate anything.

> *Line 7 service amendment, twelfth of the current month. The Alder Quay–Bridge Street loop is not reinstated. The diversion is not amended. The withdrawn alignment is recorded on the diversion panel as *Bridge Reach (works frontage)*, and the extra Bellwether stop is not printed, and the diversion panel carries no name of a street, and the running time at the Alder Quay interchange is not further amended.*

"You have put it on the panel," said Milo Rook.

"I have put it on the panel," said Barrow, "and I want it written down that I was asked twice today not to, and that both times it was by a man in my own division, and that I have done it anyway, and that it is the correct entry and that it is not a name, and that a *works frontage* is a thing that appears on about four hundred sheets in this authority's files and has a maintenance schedule behind it and a man to inspect it, and that is the only reason a works frontage is different from a street, and if anybody ever asks me in five years why I put a name on a diversion panel that does not belong there, the answer is that the order was suspended and the works are keeping a road."

---

At ten o'clock on Friday the twelfth of next month the order was suspended, and at about ten to two in the afternoon the third call went out of the canyon from the gantry for the first time in six years on a reach that was not named on anything, and it asked, and one person answered it, and Lena Marr had said in a yard on the Thursday evening that she was not going to stand at a door again and had not been asked to and was not going to be asked, and she was at the ground-floor door of Cedar Court on the coal hatch side anyway at about ten to two on the Friday, on her own, at about ten past two on the Thursday, and she said the word once, and it went.

And then it did not ask again.

That is on the page, and it is six days, from the thirteenth to the eighteenth, and it is the only thing in eleven weeks that this district got for nothing.

It did not ask on the Saturday, or the Sunday, or the Monday, or the Tuesday, or the Wednesday, or the Thursday. On the Friday the nineteenth of next month it asked, once, at ten to two, and a woman of about seventy from Wragg Street who has never been asked to do anything in her life said it at the north door, and it did not ask on the twentieth.

"It is not mended," said Dr Iona Sen, in a yard of about nine people on the Monday morning, when somebody said the word *fixed*. "I want to say that once and I want it said in a room rather than a yard, because I have been doing this for twenty-two years and the words people use afterwards are the ones that get them killed. It is not fixed. It is quiet. There is a difference and the difference is that a fixed thing is one you stop watching, and this is a thing you will have to watch every day for as long as there is a district here, and the day somebody in this district stops watching it is the day it starts again, and it will not start with a noise. It will start with a schedule."

Nobody wrote that down and it was written down in about four places over the following week and none of them was by him.

The reach is out of true by **eleven inches over forty feet** and it is fenced, and there is a settlement plate on it with a date on it, and the rail that came up four feet is cut off and is in a skip on the Terrace, and the four-inch steel plate is on the wall of the works compound, on a bench, with a hole in it where a fixing was, and nobody has proposed to refit it and it will not be refitted and the door behind it is eleven inches above the low point of the old alignment with fourteen feet of clay behind it, and the door is shut and it stays shut, and the reason the plate is on a bench is that the works found it and there was nowhere else for it.

The culvert is at full flow and is not returning to eighteen inches. That was the arrangement and the arrangement has changed: the form says *returned to a working aperture on completion and left silted, re-aligned and gated, and the re-alignment to be carried out under a separate instruction*, and the re-alignment was done on the Thursday and the Friday of the following week by nine men under a new instruction, and the aperture is now **twenty-four inches with a stop-screw through the stem and a padlock on the wheel**, and the barrel is silted to about a third of its diameter from the low end, and it is not the culvert that was there before the order and it will not be. It ends gated, silted and re-aligned, and every one of those three words is a change somebody made on purpose, and none of them is a restoration.

And it is on the page that nobody in the district has said out loud what everybody is thinking, which is that on the eighth of December a person wrote a street down for four hundred people in one and a half seconds, and on the twelfth of December somebody in Crown Heights made an order stop, and a hundred and forty feet of frontage has been quiet since, and nobody in this district knows which of those two things did it, and **both of them were on the same side.**

And it is on the page that Milo Rook wrote the first half of that down on the evening of the eighth of December, in a yard, with about eleven people in it, out of his own book and not out of anybody else's, and that on the Friday morning, when the order stopped and the reach went quiet, he read it back in about four minutes and added the other half of it, and wrote underneath it: *I would rather be wrong about this one. I have been right about the mechanism for eleven weeks and it has cost this district about four people and two school places and a man's own door, and being right is not the part of it that was useful.*

And the twelfth of December at about half past four in the afternoon, in a room in a building in Crown Heights, with a caution on him and a solicitor he chose himself, Orrin Sable began to give account, and got three sentences out of the ten he had set himself, and stopped.

Milo Rook was not in the room. Nobody from the district was. What is on the page about it is what Orrin Sable said to about six men in a portacabin at about ten o'clock that evening, out loud, in a yard that had emptied, and Milo Rook wrote it down at about eleven that night.

"Three sentences out of ten and I am not going to say the rest of it and I want it on the record that I have not said it and I am not going to in December," said Orrin Sable. "I will say three things. The first is that the safety need was real and it is still real and the wall behind this district did not fail on the sixth of this month and that is the only thing I have ever wanted to be true. The second is that the concealment was not required by the safety problem and I have known that since the first week and I did it with a document and not with a lie, which is worse, and I put the true sentence in the box that only takes it, and I have had a filing clerk in a building in Crown Heights read that box back to me over the telephone last Thursday, and she read it and then she was quiet for about four seconds and then she said *that is a very unusual thing to find in an alternative considered box*, and I said yes, and she said nothing else, and I am sixty-two and I have been an officer for nineteen years and I have never in nineteen years put a true sentence in a box that only takes it and I do not know why I did it and I have thought about it every day since the tenth of last month."

"And the third," said Rue.

"The third is that there is a file in this district that I have never been able to get at, and I have stopped trying, and I am telling about nine people about it in a yard because there is a dead man in it and his name is on a ledger in a works yard with the word *pending* against it, and I am not going to say the rest of it in December and I am not going to say it here, and I want the two words *not* and *not* noticed, and I am aware that I have used them, and I am aware of what I am doing."

Nobody asked him.

And it is on the page that nobody asked him, and it is on the page that Rue did not write it down, and that Milo Rook did not write it down either until eleven o'clock that night, when he wrote two lines and did not put them in the same paragraph as anything about the road.

*Twelfth of next month, 23.10. The order is suspended and the name is not restored and the seam is quiet and not mended and the third call asked once, at ten to two, and has not asked since. Two hundred and fifty-two people are on a list in a book in a drainage section and a man of sixty-one has his name at the foot of a page and a conductor has his name in a printed rule book and a woman of sixty has a street on a sheet of paper she has not got yet and a man of forty-nine has been counted from outside and a woman on a fourth floor walked ninety minutes and would not be thanked.*

*And the thing that came out of the reopened file this afternoon is on the page in the state layer and I have not decided what it is, and I am going to write it down here in my own book because there is a rule in this district now that a thing that cannot be used is written down, and a thing that can be used is minuted, and this cannot be used yet.*

*Inside the order there is a carbon copy. It is a relocation schedule, and it is a copy, and the copy is off an older survey book and not off a district register, and it lists four routes, and three of the four are in this district and I could place them in about four minutes on a Sunday. The fourth names a destination. It is a real name with real streets either side of it and a bus that stopped going there in a year I can name, and it is not on the street name index and it is not in the six-year-old reconstruction, and I have established all of that in about an hour and I cannot place it and I have not tried to place it tonight.*

*And there is one mark in the margin. One. It is in a hand and it is a question and it is a question about the destination and not about the order, and it is not one of the two questions that are on the other carbon, and there are two documents in this case with a pair of marks on them and this is not a third pair, because a pair is two marks in two hands on one page and this is one mark in one hand and I do not know that a second is coming. I have written that down so that nobody in Crown Heights can say I added one, and because Kest has not matched the strings and I am not going to be the man who matches them either.*

*I am not joining it to the piece of paper in five houses with my arithmetic in it. I am not joining it to a tick in a fourth column in six houses in four streets. I have written that on this page once already and I am writing it again because the temptation on the ninth and tenth of this month was very strong and the reason is always the same and it is a bad one: because it would make a case instead of a record.*
