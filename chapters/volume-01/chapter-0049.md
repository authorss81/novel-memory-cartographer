# Chapter 49: A Promise Made to One Person

She asked him for it in the porch at about ten past twelve on Wednesday the twelfth of the month with about twelve people in the yard, and she did not ask him for anything else, and the asking took four minutes and the answer took nine.

"Advocate Quill wants the columns," said Rue, at the back, which is how things come in.

"Four versions of a sheet, dated, tied with a piece of twine, in a hand that everybody in this porch could identify from about four feet away, and a set of columns that are yours," said Mara Quill. "And a reduction at forty per cent that is legible to nobody over eleven in daylight, which is your finding and it is a good finding and it is on the record of this sheet whether you are in the room or not. I want to know how many of those copies there are."

"Three that we know of," said Milo Rook. "One went to a letting agent's office in Wragg Street. One went into a house on the Terrace and a woman of sixty read her own name on it in a kitchen. One has not come back."

"And one came out of a man who lets four rooms behind a boatyard using a machine in a works office that he paid a shilling to use, and nobody in that building is going to be disciplined for it, and the man is not a criminal and has not been one." She had the bundle of four versions in her hand; nobody had given it to her and she had asked for it in front of twelve people. "I am going to say the whole of it and then I am going to stop, and I am not going to be fair about it, because I have a review on the third of next month and it is about my own hand."

"You have told me it is on the third."

"I have told you it is on the third and I have not told you what is in it, and I am going to tell you what is in it now because you are going to find out in a corridor in three weeks and I would rather you had it in a yard." She turned the bundle over. "There is an intake routing slip for the Bellwether file. It is six years old. It has four sets of initials in it, one per block, and three of them belong to people who have left, and the fourth is mine, in the box that means *I have seen that an address is affected and passed it on.* I have read that box four times since the fourth of Sazur and I have not read it aloud to anybody, including you, and I am not going to read it aloud to anybody now, and that is a thing I am carrying into a room on the third and it is a thing that has nothing to do with you."

Nobody said anything about that and she did not invite anybody to.

"Now the thing I came for." She held up the bundle. "On the twenty-ninth of Sazur you promised me that nothing of your bearing on this file would reach me in writing, with a date on it, whether I could file it or not. I have held that for forty-four days. I have held it through a Bench, a plate, a bridge and four accounts, and it has been the most useful thing anybody has handed me in six years, and I have never once been able to use it, because everything you have sent me describes what you did and not what the district did."

"That was the point of it."

"That was the point of it and it has just run out." She put the bundle down on the trestle table. "It was made to me. One person, in a corridor, in a fortnight, and I can hold it, and I can be the only person who holds it. And you have now got about thirty hands in it. There is a woman of sixty on the second landing whose name a man read off a school list into a stranger's machine, and a man of forty-one behind a boatyard who has a line of his own hand on a piece of paper with a company's head on it, and a woman on the fourth floor whose name I have never been told who has written a day in a space and taken it out and written another, and a mechanic downstairs who gave you a sentence this district has never had, and about eight more, and every single one of them is now inside something that I made out of your arithmetic, and I cannot write to any of them and I cannot be cross-examined on any of them and I cannot go into a room and say *I know these people*, and I was in one on the twenty-first of October and I did not say it, and I have had three weeks since to work out why, and the answer is that I know their arithmetic."

"I know they are not on your schedule—"

"You know four of them by name and you know one of them by a floor and you have written down that you have not asked, and that is *exactly* the discipline that has caused every disaster in this case for six years." She was not loud. "A document that says a person is there, on the authority of a person who can be cross-examined. I said that in a yard on the eleventh of this month and I have not been able to build one and you have built a worse one and I am not going to call it worse because I have been inside it for a week and it is working."

They stood in the porch.

"So the answer," said Mara Quill, "is that the promise stands and it is mine and it is not going to be widened to thirty people, because I will not be able to carry thirty people, and because the day I promise a woman of sixty that nothing about her reaches me in writing is the day I have become a second Registry and I have got a review about my own hand in three weeks' time."

"Then what do I do."

"You give it away." She picked the bundle up again. "Not the promise. The design. The columns are yours and the rule at the foot is yours and the forty per cent is yours, and a document is a document by the method it was made in, and you are the method. There are now three copies of your design in this district and one of them has six names on it that four people did not agree to, and it is not going to be fixed by you going round explaining the intent."

She took a sheet of paper out of her folder and put it on the trestle table.

"That is a fifth version. It is typed on my office's paper, which is Registry paper, which I am not supposed to use for this, and I have used it anyway, and I have told my clerk I have used it, and she has written the word *unauthorised* in a book about it and initialled it, and I have told her to keep it. It has my columns and not yours and they are not the same columns. It is a sheet of *turns* and a sheet of *hours somebody will not be there*, and the difference between those two things is the whole of what has happened in this porch this month and it has taken me four days to see it and you have been in it for four days and did not see it, and I am not going to make a thing of that."

"I saw it on the ninth," said Milo Rook, "and I did not write it down for two days because I did not want to be the man who said it."

"That," said Mara Quill, "is the single most honest sentence anybody has said to me in this district, and it is worthless, and I am going to use it anyway."

She squared the sheet.

"Here is what I have decided, and I am saying it in a yard because I am not going to do it in a corridor and I have spent a career learning that the difference is the whole of the job. The four Thursdays expire at noon on Friday the fourteenth and I am not going to do one thing about it. I am not going to apply to extend it, I am not going to minutely minute it again, I am not going to have it converted, and I am not going to have it attached to a schedule, and if anybody in this yard asks me on Monday morning whether there is anything I can do in a room I am going to say the same thing I am going to say to the woman at the school door, which is that there is not, and that a thing which is only ever four weeks is the only instrument I have ever made that could not be turned into a form, and I am not going to be the woman who finds out whether that was true."

"And the sheet."

"The sheet is yours to give away and it is mine to hold, and I am going to hold it on alternate weeks with Hettie Brann, who will not read it and who has already told you she will not read it, and whose not reading it is now the most valuable thing in this building." She looked at about twelve people. "And on the week it is on my side of the nail, the rule at the foot is in my hand and not in his, and that is the only difference and it is the whole difference, and I would like somebody to write that sentence down."

Rue wrote it down. It is the only sentence in the fourth version of anything in this district that is in Advocate Quill's handwriting, and it is on the wall in a yard, and it is not a record.

---

Tomas Rusk came down on the Wednesday afternoon at about two o'clock in a taxi and it cost him three and six again, and Mara Quill had asked for his sentence in a kitchen at nine o'clock that morning by telephone, which is how she asked him, because he is sixty-one and has a knee and cannot be asked in a doorway.

She asked for it in a kitchen with a kettle on and two other people in it and she wrote it down and read it back and he corrected one word, and then she said the thing that was the point of the morning.

"You have not sworn it. I know you are not going to swear it, and it is going to the next Bench in her hand, unsigned, dated, with your name at the foot of it, and it is worse evidence than a sworn one and I told you that on the second and you have been finding out what worse means for four weeks." She put the pencil down. "And I am going to ask you for the sentence. The one you will not put in writing. The one about the road. Not for a Bench. For a woman of forty-one in a room behind a boatyard who is on a piece of paper this week and who has never been on one."

He was quiet for about a minute.

"Nobody has asked me for that," said Tomas Rusk.

"Nobody has needed it until now," said Mara Quill. "I am asking. I have no authority to ask and I am asking."

"Right." He put his hands on the table. "Then here it is and it is one sentence and I have not written it down in nine years and I am not going to now. On the night of the closure the road shut in the middle of a run. Not at the end of it. In the middle. The crew came off the frontage to the pump house and the reach emptied, and a frontage with nobody on it is a road that is not being kept, and I have said that in about forty yards in this district and four of them were the district's own. That is the sentence. It is true. It is not sworn. You can have it in a room and you can have it in the street and if you want it in a book you can have it in a book and it will still not be sworn, because I am not signing anything until this pension has been in my hand for four years, and the sentence does not need me. The sentence needs a road."

He took his hand off the table and put it on his stick.

"There is one thing I am going to want," he said, "attached to the account itself, and I am not telling you what it is today, and I have not decided it, and I have told you now so that nobody can say I sprang it on you afterwards, and I will not be asked about it twice, and I would like it noticed that I have said the sentence and held the condition in the same conversation and that is the whole of my character."

"Noted," said Mara Quill, "and I am not going to ask you about it twice either, and when you have decided it you will tell me and not a Bench and not a man in a coat, and if you tell me this side of Christmas I will have some weeks in hand and if you tell me in the week it is wanted I will have none, and either of those is a thing you get to choose and not a thing I get."

---

Dr Sen was in the yard at about four o'clock on the Wednesday and did not come under the awning, which she has not done since the thirtieth of Sazur, and stood at the edge of it in the rain the way she stood at the edge of it on the thirtieth.

"Take out the book."

"There is nothing in it about a person."

"There is a bundle of four versions in your coat with four dates on it in your own hand and a set of columns that six people have used, and you are the man who made it, and you are the reason it went round, and you have had that bundle in your inside pocket for four days." She held out her hand. "Give me the notebook. Not the book. The one you write in the evenings."

He gave her the notebook.

She turned to a blank page and gave it back with a pen and stood there in the rain and did not go under the awning.

"Write it down," she said. "The thing I said to you in the water clinic on the sixteenth of Sazur with a pad behind a pad, that you have never written down in six weeks, and that I have never once asked you to write down, and that I have watched you break twice. Write the words of it and the date and put your name at the foot, and then give it back to me, and I will put it in a drawer and I will not give it to you again."

He wrote it down.

*Sixteenth of Sazur. Iona Sen: you are not to be left alone with a piece of paper about a person, not once, not ever, for as long as you are in this district. — M.R.*

"Well," said Iona Sen, from the edge of the awning, in the rain. "Now you have written it down, which means there is a piece of paper about a person and you are the man holding it, and I have made it worse, and I want that on the record in the only place either of us can put it, which is out loud, in a yard, at four o'clock, in the rain." She took the page and folded it once and put it in her own coat. "And it will not help. I want you to know that it will not help, because I have been a doctor for a long time and I know exactly what a piece of paper does and I have just handed a man who cannot be alone with one a piece of paper about himself. That is on me and I would do it again and I am not going to be forgiven for it and I am not going to apologise for it either."

---

At the office at half past ten that night he wrote three lines, and the third one is the reason this chapter exists.

*Twelfth of the month, 22.30. Three things.*

*One. She has decided what a promise made to one person means when the work has thirty hands on it, and she has decided it in a yard with twelve people in it, and the answer is that the promise stands and it is hers and it does not get wider, and that what gets given away is my design, and on the week it is on her side of the nail the rule at the foot is in her hand. I argued for about nine seconds and then I accepted it and I did not like it and there is nothing in it for me.*

*Two. I have been a surveyor's answer to a question that is not a surveyor's question since the fourth of Sazur, and the evidence is a sheet of paper that I had in my hands for six minutes on Tuesday of last week and did not read the names on, and a man of forty-one who lives behind a boatyard has been in a file, and a woman of sixty has been in a kitchen, and a mechanic of sixty-one gave a sentence to an advocate's pen this afternoon that he has not given anybody in nine years, and a woman on a fourth floor nobody in this district can name wrote a day in a space on a nail and has taken it out and put another, and I was in the building both times and did not have the paper in my hand either time.*

*Three, and it is the one I am going to read back in June. A doctor made me write down in my own hand that I am not to be left alone with a piece of paper about a person, and I have done it, and it is in her coat, and it has made it worse and she said so, and she was right, and I am not going to write in this book tonight that she was right about everything, because she is not. She is right about me. And the Field Book has been silent for four days and has written five words on the twelfth and I have paired the ninth with the twelfth and the tenth with the thirteenth, which are three days each, and I have written both of them down and I have not joined them to the six-day and the nine-day, and there are four pairs in this book now and I do not know what a pair is and I am not going to find out this month.*
