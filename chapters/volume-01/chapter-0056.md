# Chapter 56: A Map With a Rule at the Foot

He asked it in writing on Sunday the seventh of next month at about half past three in the afternoon, at the trestle table, in front of about thirty people, and it is four lines and it is the fifth time in eleven weeks he has asked it and the fifth time the answer has been longer than the question.

> *To Rue. I am asking to go into the reach at the low end this afternoon, at about half past four, for about nine minutes.*
> *Count: Rue, outside, from the north path, as on the twenty-sixth of Sazur.*
> *Walker in and out: Doreen Sankey, who is not my chalk, who can stop it, and who is told now that if he says the word it stops and nobody is to argue with him on the way back, including me.*
> *And the other one, which is not about the reach: I am going to have to be walked to the front door of this building and up my own stairs afterwards, by somebody, because I cannot find it, and I have written that down and I am asking for it the same way I ask for the rest of it, and if it is refused I will not come in.*

Rue read it twice.

"The count is me and the walker is him and the nine minutes is nine minutes and the answer to the last part is yes and I am not going to argue about it because you have written it down and I have been waiting for you to write it down since the twenty-fourth." She gave the sheet back. "But I am not holding the count from the north path today. I have got a thing at half past two and I cannot do it, and I have not said so in advance and I am saying so now at the table with thirty people in it, and if I had said so on Friday you would have arranged your day around it and I am not doing that to you."

"Then who is the count."

"That is not mine to give," said Rue. "That is the count and it has to be outside and it has to be somebody who is not going to be affected by what is in there, and there are about nine of us who can count and about forty of us who are wet to the waist, and you are going to have to ask, and I am not going to be the woman who decides who watches the clock while a man goes into a hole."

It took nine minutes and it ended with **Hettie Brann**, who is the water clinic counter and has been on it nineteen years and who has not read a sheet in this district in six weeks, and who said no three times before she said a different yes.

"I will count," she said. "I will not read. I have not read it and I am not going to and I will not come in and I will not be asked anything about anybody who comes out. Nine minutes from the path head and I shall say *and* and then the number and nothing else, and if I hear anything that is not a person speaking then I am going to write the time on the back of my hand and I am going to hold on to it, and that is all I have got and it is a great deal and I am not doing it for him, I am doing it because the day this district needs a count and there is nobody in it to keep one, that is the day it stops being a district, and I have been on that counter nineteen years and I have watched it stop twice and I am not watching it stop a third time while I am standing up."

The count was Hettie Brann's and the tally was hers too, in her own hand, on the back of a form, and she had it in her apron pocket before anybody had asked her to have it.

---

**Four twenty-eight. The north path.** The written request says half past four and the four conditions he signed in October say nine minutes and he was in at **six minutes past four**, two minutes early, and wrote both times down, and nobody in this district has ever made him late and he has made himself early four times.

Ola Petrossian walked him to the ground-floor door of Cedar Court at ten to four and did not come in and did not go up, and she is twenty-three and has been in the job four months and has never been past the tram steps, and she said one thing at the door.

"There is a hundred and one steps in the tower at the interchange and it is a new tower on a new pier and it is the same hundred and one, and I have counted them, and I am writing it on a card with today's date on it because you are going to lose that and not the pier, and I do not know how I know that and you are not going to tell me."

It was in his coat pocket before he was through the door.

The reach is a hundred and forty feet of made ground between the foot of the embankment and the low end, and on the sixth of December at about four o'clock in the afternoon it is a strip of new fill with a rail up through it at the low end and a fence on the north side and about nine inches of water standing in the wheel ruts, and a hundred and forty feet is not a long way. He knows that. It has been in four documents and a man has been killed in it and it is a hundred and forty feet and there is a lamp bracket on the flank of the building with a dead bulb on it from before the closure.

Doreen Sankey was nine feet behind him and he could hear him, and Doreen Sankey had not agreed to this on Saturday and had agreed to it on Sunday morning at about eleven o'clock in about eleven words, which were: *You have a man on a platform and I have no place to be, and you want somebody who is not your chalk, and I am the only one in this district who can turn round and come back up this and I do it eleven times a day and I will do it in front of four hundred people and then nobody can say a man from a boatyard stood in a hole.*

The token was not cold.

That is the first thing and it is the only thing he wrote about it for four days, and he wrote it in the book at twenty past nine that night and dated it, and the paragraph is four lines and it is the fourth line that is the cost.

*It was warm. For about four minutes it was the temperature of a thing that has been in a coat pocket against a man for eleven weeks and has been taken out and put back, and it was warm, and I have not had a temperature out of it since the fourth of Sazur. And at about four minutes past the ground went quiet on that reach, the way a room goes quiet, and the negative space in the front of it lined up with the made ground from the pump house to the low end, a hundred and forty feet, exactly, and I have measured it a dozen times and it is a hundred and forty feet in four documents.*

*I did not use it. I want the whole of the record: I did not use it, and I had about four minutes in which I could have, and I did not, and I did not say why out loud to anyone, and there is one reason and it is not a rule.*

*If I open a thing with a key out of my own coat, then there is a street in this district that is open because of something I own, and every one of the twenty-six people on Rue's sheet and every one of the forty who came out of that reach this morning and the man of forty-one who is standing nine feet behind me are standing in a place that a man's mother opened, and the three answers in this district are a street, a way to walk and a road with flowers on it, and they are three answers because they are three people, and they stop being three answers the moment one of them is a thing I can take out of my pocket and put back. I have spent six weeks giving away a sheet of paper and four versions of it and a rule at the foot and the design itself, and I was right about all of it, and if I open that with this then every bit of it was a way of keeping the key. I would rather have a district that argues with me about a name on an index than a district that stands in a place because of a brass token with LARK STREET on it.*

*I put it back in my coat and it went cold in about ninety seconds, which is the temperature it has been at since the sixth of Sazur, and I have written that down because in a month somebody is going to tell me the token was the reason, and it was not, and there is a man of forty-nine on this reach who got his hand up a fifth of a second before a rail went over and he did not decide to, and he is not a token.*

---

He had the sheet in his left hand. It is a sheet of accountancy paper with a heading and two columns and a rule at the foot in Rue's hand, and the two columns are **TURN** and **DONE**, and there is a date at the top of it and nothing in either column, and the rule at the foot is the rule at the foot of everything this district has built since the third of last month, and it is legible to anybody over about eleven years old and it is the only map in eleven weeks that Milo Rook has not been the author of.

He did not draw anything. He read the rule out loud, on the reach, at about four minutes past five, in a voice that carried about forty feet, and Doreen Sankey heard it and was the only other person in the district who heard all of it, and he read it because the whole of the argument of six weeks is that a map with no rule at the foot is an invitation to whoever has got a pen.

Then he did the thing that is in three documents and one hearing transcript, and it took about eleven minutes in all, and it is the only time in this case that anybody has said the three things on a page in a row.

The first was the order's public safety claim, and he gave it as it is on the page and not as he would put it.

"There is an order of the sixth year back. It is in force. It is supported by a finding of the works of the first week — *no evidence was found of any hazard to persons at the service court* — which is not in the file any more, because a replacement page was filed eleven days after the report it replaced, and the page it replaced is in a bound volume in a building in Crown Heights, and the department has told us in writing that the withdrawal reference on it is not a district reference. And in the same first week there is another paragraph, and the paragraph is still in the file and it says the three calls are not a signal to persons and indicate no entry and no refusal, and that the frontage is at all times a service access and not a public route. Both of those are true. I have never said in this district that either of them is a lie, and the second one is why there are forty people in a reach with no way out of it, and the first one is why a woman is still asking a bench in a fortnight whether her husband is dead."

The second was the missing worker record, and this one was four sentences and it was the hardest.

"There is a dead maintenance worker called Jonas Venn. One loss recorded. No cause of death, no next of kin, on the district's own ledger, with a relocation due against his name and the word *pending* on it. He died in a second-stage main in flood season in a real accident, and the accident is not in this reach, and he is not in this reach, and there is not one thing anybody has ever said in this district that puts him in this reach, including me, in eleven weeks. I want that said on the reach in daylight while there are about four hundred people within a quarter of a mile of me, because in about a fortnight a clerk is going to produce a piece of paper and the piece of paper is going to say that a hazard at the frontage and nobody at the frontage were the same sentence, and they were not, and the cost of six years of this has fallen on a man of forty-one behind a boatyard and a woman of sixty on a landing and a mechanic of sixty-one with a knee, and none of those three people was owed anything by Jonas Venn and all three of them have paid."

And the third was the current residents' presence, and he gave it as a number and he gave the method.

"Two hundred and fifty-two people, in fifty-five rooms, in eleven streets, on the thirty-first of the month and the first of this month, counted on doorsteps without knocking by six people whose names I am not going to read out because I have been told by a doctor not to be left alone with a piece of paper about a person and I am not going to start on the thirteenth of December. That is not a schedule and it is not a list and it does not go into an office. It is a count, and it is the fourth one, and it is twenty-one more than the schedule of two hundred and thirty-one that this district is being paid to collect at two and four a head a week, and there are thirty-three rooms in it that are on no list at all, and one of those rooms is behind a boatyard on the Ferry Road side, and there is a man of forty-one asleep on a third floor in that building who is the last registered head of household for the whole of it and who is not going to be asked to sign anything, and the count is the only thing anybody has got."

"And the fourth thing is me," said Doreen Sankey, from about nine feet behind him, on a reach, out loud, and it was the first and last time in eleven weeks that anybody in this district wrote down a thing he said that was not a number and a time. "Say the fourth thing or don't, because you have said three and there is a fourth and it is the only one about you and you have been not saying it for eleven weeks."

Milo Rook said it.

"A cartographer went over the low end of this street on the eighteenth of October with a lamp and a piece of paper and a woman who was carried down in a laundry bag, and there is a version of that route on a piece of paper in five houses in this district with a flood-control contractor's head on it, and four of the five are copies somebody made and one of them is a copy of a copy, and I have never been able to take my name off it, and I cannot get at it, and I wrote the columns on it and I did the arithmetic on it and I have been on six crossings of this reach and this is the seventh, and I am not going in as a man with a piece of paper in his hand and I am not going in for Lark Street and I am not going to be the reason this place is kept, and if this works today then it works because there are about nine people holding it up and I am one of the nine and I am the one who can be replaced."

He came out at **four thirty-five**, six minutes early, and Doreen Sankey came out with him, and Hettie Brann said, from the path head, in about two words, "*And* four thirty-five," and wrote it on the back of her hand, and the whole of the count is on the back of a clinic counter form in a hand that has not read a sheet of paper in this district in six weeks.

---

**Six o'clock, the yard, about thirty-one people.**

He read it out because Iona Sen makes him and has made him five times, and she was at the trestle table with the tally in her hand, and she did not read it for him and did not prompt him and did not look at it.

"Seventh. Sunday the seventh of next month, in at 16.06 and out at 16.35, twenty-nine minutes inside, from the low end of the reach, walked in and walked out by Mr Sankey, count held by Mrs Brann from the path head, nine minutes stated, four conditions signed in October all of them satisfied, and the fourth condition is the clinic in the morning and I have not missed one in eleven weeks and I am not going to miss one now." He put his hand in his coat. "And the cost. I have got nine remembered routes and there were eight this morning. The hundred and one steps of the stair tower at the Alder Quay interchange are the ninth. I have climbed them since the fourth of October, and I went down them a fortnight ago with a foreman on the new pier, and I could not have told you this morning which way the tower turned."

Dr Sen wrote one word on the tally and looked up.

"Name of the person who marked it."

"Ola Petrossian. This afternoon. On a card, with a date, at the door, and she gave it to me and would not come in."

"Then it is marked and it is nine and I am not going to say anything else about it, because you do not want me to and because in a fortnight you are going to want to know whether it is a cost or a habit and it will still not be knowable, and there is a word for a page you keep re-reading and it is not *cost*." She put the tally in her coat. "Go to the clinic in the morning. And say the ninth out loud in a yard tomorrow, in daylight, when it is not raining, because a thing that is only ever said at eight o'clock at night in the rain is a thing that gets better."

---

What is in the Field Book for the seventh of next month, and it is one line and the line is:

*Nothing on the reach. Nothing in the building. Nothing on the way out.*

He looked at it for about a minute and a half, at the trestle table, with about thirty-one people about him and a foreman in a compound two hundred yards away and a woman of sixty-three sitting on a wall on the north path with a whistle, and then he took the pencil out and put the word back in, and dated it, which is what he does.

*Nothing on the reach. Nothing in the building. Nothing on the way out. The token was warm for about four minutes and I did not use it, and there is a line in this book about it on the facing page with the reason, and the reason is the same reason as everything else I have given away since the fourth of this month and it took me eleven weeks and a brass token to be able to write it down: a place is kept by people and a document is what the people agree on, and a document that one man can open in his coat is not a document, it is a habit with a lid on it.*

*And then nothing for eleven days, and I have written six dates down and I have not paired them, and I am not going to. Eleven days is not a pair. I looked at it twice.*
