# Chapter 54: Full Flow

The gate came out to full flow at twenty past six on Saturday morning the sixth of next month and Milo Rook was in the works compound at ten to six, standing on the north path with his hands in his pockets, because nobody had asked him to be there and because the coal hatch side had been open again since the twentieth of November and a man who cannot find a door is allowed to stand near forty others.

The western culvert is a brick structure the width of a cart, built into the foot of the embankment in 1889 and rebuilt in 1931, with a sluice gate on a screw stem that takes four men and about an hour and a half if they are fresh and three hours if they are not. It has been wound down to **eighteen inches of sill** since the sixth of Sazur, by hand, in front of witnesses, because a full seal would back water into the works zone and into the low streets behind it, and it has been in that state for sixty-one days, and the district has spent most of those days being told by a man it liked that it had been shut.

There is a form for taking a culvert out for a test. Danner filled it in on the Friday evening in his own hand and it is on the file, and the aperture before is **eighteen inches** and the aperture after is **eleven feet four inches, clear**, and the reason for the aperture is *final test, reach flood-control installation, gate to full flow for the duration and returned to a working aperture on the same day*, and the box for *alternative considered* is filled in his own hand because he will not leave a space on a district form, and what is in it is: *Full flow required on the flood side to the head of the test. No alternative. Return aperture to be twenty-four inches on completion and the culvert to be left silted and re-aligned and gated, and the re-alignment to be carried out under a separate instruction.*

Danner read that box out to about eleven men at ten to six in the morning in the cold and did not read the rest of the form, and Petts — who is on that contract eleven years and who has said *nobody told us* on the twelfth of last month and every working day since — said the three words again, out loud, on his own initiative, and Danner said, "I have heard you," and did not ask him to stop, and Milo Rook wrote that down and has written down twice that he did not ask him to stop.

The four men on the stem went down at ten to six and the level came up about eleven inches over the first half hour and then stopped coming up, which Danner said was correct, and at twenty past six they locked the stem and Danner wrote the time in a box on the form, and Milo Rook wrote the time in his own book, and *the same time in two hands* is a thing he has written down about nine times in eleven weeks and has never once been able to use.

---

The test is at nine. It is not a spectacle. It is a crew of nineteen and a sluice and a board of four questions, and what it establishes is whether the wall behind this district can take a river, and the answer at about twenty past nine in the morning on the sixth of December was yes.

Danner said so to a works clerk at about half past nine and Milo Rook heard it and wrote it down, and the clay held, and there was no water in the works zone, and the low point came up on a schedule level, and the third pump on Bridge Street could be taken off at about eleven, which nobody in this district had been told and which means, and Orrin Sable said it in exactly those words to about six men in a portacabin with the door open, that about forty gallons a minute had been coming off the district's own drainage for eleven weeks by means of a fire engine with a man volunteering on it, and that the man had been on shift every working day since the twelfth of October and had not been paid for it, and that the works had paid for it out of a petty float and had not had the authority to do that and had not told anybody.

His name is Rennick and he said it out loud and it is the first thing anybody in this district has liked about him.

At about ten past eleven the deck moved.

It was not a lot and it was not sudden and there were about thirty people in that part of the compound and nine of them were on the made reach. The west side of the Alder Quay interchange went **out of true**, about **nine inches over about thirty feet of street**, in one movement, the way it had gone on the nineteenth of the month in October, except that on the nineteenth a stair tower had come down across the rails and this time nothing came down, because the tower had been rebuilt in November on a new pier with a note in the works file about differential founding, and the note had been written by a man who is not a cartographer and had read a joint finding.

Nine inches again. About nine hundred people were in that interchange at five minutes to twelve on a Saturday and Jo Lask was driving through it and came out of it with nobody hurt and stopped the car and got out and stood in the middle of it for about a minute and a half with his arms down, and the reason he got out is that he has been in the middle of four things this year and he is the man who stands in them.

Dr Sen had four people through her treatment room between noon and half past two, and the four were Petts, and a man of thirty-one off the second gang with a wrist, and a tram driver of forty-four called something Milo Rook did not catch and has not written down, and a woman of twenty-six who had been standing on the platform and had gone down eleven steps of a stair and had been brought back up them by two men and a conductor, and the third of the four is one of the four from the nineteenth of October, which is a different set of four, and Milo Rook has written both sets down in the same line in his own book because the book is not a place for tidying.

He did not go in the treatment room and was not asked to and Iona Sen came out into the yard at about two o'clock and said one sentence to him and went back in.

"You will want to be useful in about an hour," she said. "Do not be."

---

The interval came at about ten to two in the afternoon, out of the canyon from the gantry, and there were about four hundred people on and around the interchange at that moment and nobody in the district had counted them and nobody needed to, and the number four hundred is the number of people Milo Rook has used in every account he has given of that reach since the seventeenth of October and it is the number that matters.

Doreen Sankey was at the ground-floor door of Cedar Court on the coal hatch side at about ten to two, with Rue four seconds later at the north door, and about twenty-two of the twenty-six names on Rue's sheet were in or near the building, and the day had not gone as anybody expected and the sheet was not used for anything that day, and nobody has ever found out why not.

Danner's crew had the gate at full flow until about two o'clock and it was standing open in the sight of about eleven people including a cartographer and a foreman, and nobody had thought about that, and it is in the file that the aperture was returned to twenty-four inches at **14.06**, which is six minutes after the third call went.

The second call went at eight minutes to two. The plant stopped. Twenty-nine seconds, which is the shaft, and thirty-one, which is the street, and the two of them are both true and a mechanic of sixty-one gave the district that in November and has not been asked about it since.

It asked.

And this is the thing that is in the account and the account is short: it asked, and about four hundred people answered.

Not a chorus. That is the word everybody has used since and it is wrong and Milo Rook has said so in four rooms. What happened is that a check went out on a reach on which there were four hundred people, and a check is a person saying a space is clear before the next turn, and **there were people standing in spaces**, and a space with a person in it has been described as clear by the person in it, in about four hundred separate and entirely ordinary ways, in under two seconds, by people who were on a tram platform, or in a trench, or on a make-shift, or on a pile of fill, or in a doorway four streets away and one of them said it twice.

It is in the accounts that the woman of sixty said it at the coal hatch side and Rue said it at the north door, and it is in the accounts that a man of forty-one said it and then said it again to four people who had come out of the building, and it is in the accounts that a foreman said it into a radio he was holding, and it is in the accounts that a conductor said it three times on a diversion while a car was standing, and it is in the accounts that a woman of sixty-eight was not in this district on the sixth of December and did not say anything on the sixth of December, and nobody went up three flights of stairs about her on the sixth of December, and there are about nine people in this district who are going to say to the end of their lives that a woman on a third floor was the reason it did not go the other way, and she is sixty-eight and she has a fever coming and she was not asked.

And the ground answered, and it answered the way a road answers a count.

The made reach — a hundred and forty feet of it, between the pump house and the low end, laid on Tuesday the second, levelled on the third, proved on the fourth, and **certified at ten past four on Friday the fifth of December by a foreman of the district flood stage in the fourteenth day of a standard deviation in the readings at the trench, who has written on the form that he will not be able to say in a room that he stood behind it** — went out of true.

**Eleven inches over forty feet**, in one movement, between about two and about two, with four hundred people on and around it, and it did not go down. That is the part that is in the joint finding of the ninth of December, in four paragraphs, made by the works and Survey Enforcement together.

It did not go down.

The first two feet of settlement in ten weeks, the eighteen inches in one day and the nine inches before it, were all *going down*. They were a lens settling. They were a surface made level across a place which had been two surfaces for six years, and a man with a spirit level and a bubble in the middle had written *level* on a site sheet in his own hand and had been right about the bubble.

This was not that. This was the two surfaces becoming one and the one not being level, and it happened at the third call, in daylight, with a check going round a reach on which about four hundred people were standing in spaces, and **the reach did not take the check and it did not refuse the check; it took it and used it.** It is in four paragraphs of a joint finding made by the works and Survey Enforcement together on the ninth of this month, and the third paragraph is the sentence above, and the fourth is that a reach which has taken a check in this way **did not return to its former state within the period of the finding and was not expected to**, and that is the sentence that everything else after it hangs on.

A rail came up through the fill at the low end, four feet, in one movement, and went over. A man of forty-nine who had been standing eleven feet from it got a hand up about a fifth of a second before it did and has said since that he did not decide to.

The service door — four inches of steel, four bosses, cut for a door of two foot six, fitted at ten past six in the morning on the twelfth of last month in nine minutes by two of Danner's own, lawfully, on a correct form — came off its fixings at about two and a half minutes past two and went into the works zone with a sound that four people heard and that no person has described since in the same words. It is not on the ground. It is eleven feet from where it was, across the water, in the silt, on its side, with the four bosses up.

The plant restarted at about six minutes past two. The second call went at three minutes past two. The third call went at four minutes past two and it did not ask, and there has been no discussion in this district about why, and there is a mechanic of sixty-one in this district who has a possible answer and has not been asked for it and has said in a yard that he is not going to be asked for it.

Nobody drowned. Nobody was killed. Eleven people were hurt and four of them went through the treatment room, and two of those four are a different four from the four of the nineteenth of October, and a woman of twenty-six and a man of fifty-four were counted and are all right.

The low streets went under nine inches by about half past two and the district flood line is nine inches, and the works had four pumps on three streets it is not responsible for and the gully was coming up on the low end for the thirty-ninth day in a row in a month with under an inch of rain in it, and a woman of sixty-three got a hundredweight of ice out of a cellar that was four inches deep at one o'clock and had four in it by four, and she did it on her own and did not tell anybody until the Monday, and she is not in a yard and is not the face of anything.

And a crew of nineteen and a sluice and a board of four questions stood on the north side of the works and watched the wall behind the district not fail, and wrote the time in a box, and the wall did not fail. That is the whole of the engineering and it is true and it is in the file and it is the reason the seam went where it went.

Milo Rook came off the north path at about ten past four and did not go to the ground-floor door and did not go round the front of the building, and found the north door and went in by it, and Doreen Sankey was on the second landing at about ten past four in the afternoon of the sixth of December of this year and told him that he had gone in by the wrong door, and Milo Rook said, "I have known that for six weeks and I have been waiting for somebody to say it to me so that I could stop pretending it was a coincidence," and Doreen Sankey said, "It is a coincidence, and you are not to tell anybody I said anything about it, and go up to the fourth floor and be in your own flat by twenty past four, and I have to go and see whether the old woman at the second landing has got water in her rooms."

He got up his own stairs, the second stair and not the first, and there was a chalk arrow on the inside of his own front door at about four feet with the twenty-eighth of November under it, and it was five weeks old and it was the only thing in his own life that had not moved.

He wrote the times down at about nine o'clock that night, and the Field Book was silent, and there is a paragraph in the back of the book with no date on it at all, which is the only undated thing in two hundred and eleven entries, and it says:

*Sixth of next month. The gate came out to full flow at 06.20. The test began at nine and was answered at about half past nine. The deck went at 11.52. The gate went back to twenty-four inches at 14.06. Eleven hurt, four through the room.*

*And the thing that is in the account and is not in the engineer's account: the check went out at ten to two and about four hundred people answered it, and the reach took the answer. It is not a collapse. A collapse has a beginning and a middle and an end and everybody gets told where the end is. That has not got an end, and it has got worse since two o'clock, and the plant went on at 14.06 and the second call went at three and the third call went at four and did not ask, and Danner wrote the aperture in a box and Rennick wrote the time and about nine men wrote the time and a foreman who was brought back to full rate for five days wrote a level on a Friday afternoon that is now eleven inches out of true over forty feet in one afternoon, and every one of those is correct, and all of them are the same afternoon.*

*I am not going to be the man who explains this. I have got a book with a line gone out of it and I have got nine remembered routes and eight of them are still there and I cannot find my own front door unless a boy of seventeen is on a bicycle, and I am the custodian of a piece of paper in five houses and four of them are not mine. Tonight I am going to write down one word and one word only, and I am going to write it because I have never written it before and because nobody in this district has said it out loud and because it is mine and it belongs to the survey and not to the street, and if I am wrong then the word is wrong and I will take it back down in the morning and nobody will ever know it was there.*
