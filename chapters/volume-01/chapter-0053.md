# Chapter 53: In His Own Hand

He found it on Monday the twenty-fourth of the month at about ten past eight in the morning, standing in his own kitchen with the book open on the table, and it took him four attempts to understand that it had happened, because on the first attempt he thought he had opened to the wrong page.

The Field Book is a foolscap book with a limp spine and the entries are in date order going up the left-hand side and there are two hundred and eleven of them, because he has copied out a route after every survey since the week he was apprenticed at nineteen and he has never once missed and there is a fortnight of the Long Rain summer of his second year that he copied out four times because the first three were wrong.

The entry is on the left-hand side of the opening, on a Monday, dated the fifth of a month in a year that does not matter, and it is in his own hand, and it is a line.

> *Lark — no.*
> *Out the door. Left. Eleven steps to the hall, the fourth one loose, take the second stair not the first. Down to the front. The lamp on the third step goes on the left and you wait four seconds for it. Along the front to the corner, do not cut the corner. Past the chandler, and the gate, and then the wall with the paint on it, and along by the water, and it is nine minutes and I have done it since I was nineteen.*

It is nine lines. It is the oldest surviving thing in the book. It is the reason the book exists, in the sense that a man does not begin a habit on a Monday in his twentieth year of something he did in his first, and it has been the first line in the book for the whole of its life and it has never been crossed out and it has never been amended, and he had walked it twice in the week ending the fifteenth of the month, and he wrote down that week that it was correct and any person asked to walk it could walk it.

The page was there. His hand was there. The words were not.

He stood in his own kitchen and read it four times and on the fourth time he understood that the letters were not gone in a particular place, and that this mattered, because a thing that has faded has a shape and a thing that has gone does not.

The whole of the page was clean. Not white — the paper is the same paper it has been since he was nineteen, and there are two pencil marks on it from a survey in his second year and the corner is soft. The ink is there, the first line of the page, his own hand, the loop he has never been able to stop making on the capital L, and then nine lines of nothing, and then the next entry, the Monday after, in the same hand, the same pen, the same nib, in the same way.

He sat down on a chair he had not sat on for the purpose and then stood up again and did not go out of the door for fifty-one minutes, and at about ten o'clock he did one thing and it was the correct thing, and it is in the book in his own hand with the time on it.

*Twenty-fourth of the month, 10.02. The line home is gone. It is gone clean and it is nine lines and it is in my hand and the page is the page and the ink on either side of it is the same ink. I have read it four times. This is not fading. I am not going to write down what I think that is.*

*Twenty-fourth of the month, 10.05. This is not the route I lost on the fourth of Sazur. That was a thing I could not feel. This is a thing I wrote, in a hand that is mine, on a page that is mine, and it is not there, and the paper has not been touched.*

---

He did not tell anybody until the Tuesday, and he told Rue, and he told her in about nine seconds in a doorway and then not for another two hours, and she said nine words and then stood in a doorway and let him finish talking.

"Two things," said Rue. "One, I have not got time to be kind at you and I am not going to pretend I have, and you are going to be the worst man in this district for about a week and everybody is going to think you have lost your nerve because that is what it will look like from a yard. And two, do not say it to Mara Quill, because she has a review on her own hand on the third of next month and if she hears that the surveyor's route has gone she will spend it being kind to you instead of reading four paragraphs about her own initials."

"That is the wrong order."

"That is the right order and it is not my order, it is a woman's on a counter in Wragg Street who read a list off to a man and has not slept since the third of this month." Rue shifted the satchel. "Tell her on Wednesday. Tell Iona Sen before you tell her, because Iona Sen will not do a single thing about it that is not useful, and I am telling you this as a man who has watched you be helped by about nine people in eleven weeks and has not watched you be helped once."

He told Dr Sen on the Tuesday afternoon at the dispensary counter, in about two minutes, with four people in the queue behind him, and she did not put her hand on his arm and she did not say she was sorry.

"Sit down."

"I have got—"

"Sit down, and do not tell me how you have got, and do not tell the queue." She wrote two words on the back of a form. "I have had four people this year come in here and tell me they cannot find their own house, and two of them wanted me to say something kind, and the kind thing is the thing that will put you in a chair by Thursday. Here is the useful thing. You have got eight routes you cannot feel. That is the first cost and it has been with you since the fourth of Sazur and it has not moved and it has not got worse, and every one of the eight comes back when somebody marks it, and no person in this district has ever once asked you whether you could feel it, so the district does not know and cannot use it." She tore the form off the pad. "And now you have a ninth thing, and the ninth thing is in a book, which is worse, and I want you to understand exactly how much worse and I want you to hear the number, because you are a man who does sums and you will do a sum about this instead of feeling it and I would rather you did the sum now than in a fortnight at two in the morning."

"Nine," said Milo Rook.

"Nine. Not ten, because the written line is not a remembered route and it has not taken one, and the eight are all still there and none of them has gone and I want that written down by you in your own book in those words, because you will try to make them into one thing and they are not one thing and the difference is the only interesting thing about either of them." She put the form in the tray. "And there is no ninth remembered route. There are eight and there is a line, and if you start counting them together then in a month you will be standing in a room saying the ninth, and the ninth is a page."

"What is it, then."

She looked at him for about four seconds.

"It is a question, and I am not going to answer it, and neither are you, and I have watched two men in my career try to answer it and one of them got worse and one of them got better and neither of them can tell me which and I am not going to let you find out by accident on a Saturday." She put the pen down. "But I will tell you what I know about the shape of it, because you are a man who needs a rule or he will invent a subject. A remembered route comes back when somebody marks it for you, and a chalk arrow on a gate post has done it four times in this district and a woman of sixty did it once with a card and a pen. A written line does not come back that way, because there is nobody to mark it, because it is not on a wall, it is on a page you own, and the only hand that could mark it is the one that wrote it." She stopped. "So somebody else is going to have to write it. Not fill it in. Write it. From their own feet. In their own hand, on your page, in your book, with your name at the top of it and not theirs at the bottom."

"That is not possible."

"It is not possible for you and it is possible for a man on a bicycle and a woman with a satchel and it will be done badly," said Dr Sen, "and it will be done in about nine days, and I am telling you on the Tuesday of the twenty-fifth of November, in a dispensary, in front of four people, so that when it happens you cannot say afterwards that nobody told you it was coming and that you were not in a position to refuse it."

---

The plan for the sixth of December was built in four rooms over three days by nine people, and it was built around a man who cannot reliably find his own front door, and nobody pretended otherwise and nobody made a speech about it.

It came out of the Tuesday and it was Rue who put it on the trestle table on the Wednesday morning, in eleven words, and it is the sentence the district ran on for eleven days.

"Right," she said. "We do not ask him to remember anything. We walk him to the door and we walk him out of the door. Every time. Both ways. And it goes in the round and it is not a favour and it is not a rota and I am not writing it down, and if anybody finds him at the wrong door at the wrong time they are to say *come with me* and not *where were you*."

Milo Rook said, for about nine seconds, that he could manage a door.

"Nineteen years in this city," said Rue.

"Say the rest of it."

"You have walked into the wrong building in this district twice in six weeks, and you were not drunk either time, and both times you were on time and both times somebody was waiting for you." She put her thumb on the table. "And I have never once seen you do it and I have not said anything about it, because it is none of my business, and it is now my business, because on the sixth of December there is a man who is going to have to be at a ground-floor door in a building in the dark part of the afternoon and the only person who can be relied upon to be sure of it is me."

---

**Wednesday the twenty-sixth, about eleven in the morning, the yard.** The woman on the fourth floor came down on her own at about eleven, as she has twice, and there was nothing on the nail that week except the six lines, and she had not been asked and nobody went up for her, and Milo Rook was in the building and had come out of the front and was standing on the coal hatch side at about the wrong moment, which he established afterwards and wrote down.

She stopped, and looked at him, and looked at the wall of the flank, and then said, in about four seconds:

"You are at the wrong door."

"I am aware that I am at the wrong door."

"You have been at that door for about a minute and a half and you have not gone into it, and in about another minute you were going to." She did not soften it, and she did not explain it, and Milo Rook did not ask her a single question, and about twenty-two people were in the yard and heard the whole of it. "I am not going to say anything else about it and I am not going to do it again."

She went up at about five past eleven and Milo Rook did not follow her and did not write it down until the Wednesday night, and what he wrote down was: *She came down on her own. She said four seconds. She has not been asked, she did not say who told her, and she has not explained it, and I did not ask, and that is the third time in a month that the person who did the most useful thing in this district was somebody nobody had spoken to.*

Then he went round the front of the building and into his own at the other end of the yard, at about eleven fifteen, and got up his own stairs, and it was the third time in a fortnight and the difference was that on the first two occasions he had known that he was doing it wrong.

---

Mara Quill came to the yard on the Wednesday the twenty-sixth at about half past seven in the evening, and there were about fourteen people, and she had a folder and she was not going to talk about the book, and she said so in the first fifteen seconds.

"I am not going to ask you about it and I am not going to be kind to you about it, because you have had four people be kind to you in two days and you are going to go into a room on Monday week with a folder under your arm and if you go in there having been comforted you will lose it. I have watched it happen twice. That is my whole contribution to this and I have said it out loud so that it is on the page that I said it."

She put the folder on the trestle table.

"Now. Two things that are not about that, and I have got four minutes, and the second of them is the one I came about.

"The first is the seventh of next month. On the fourth I asked Mr Rook, on the authority of a letter from this division, to supply a schedule of persons resident at the withdrawn alignment during the week of the sixth of the month following. It is the ordinary request. It is a standard form, it has a box on it, and the box takes a name."

Nobody said anything.

"Advocate Quill's answer is *no*," said Mara Quill. "Not *not yet*. **No.** The form is a list of people, the district cannot produce a list of people in eleven streets, and any schedule I put in that box is a schedule I will be producing in a room in a year's time to a department that wants a list of people, and this department has had a list of people since the fourth of Sazur and has never once used it, and I am not going to hand it a second one. Mr Rook, if anybody in this yard has told you to give me a list of people for that form, they are wrong, and you are to refuse it and tell me that they told you, and I will deal with the person and not with you."

"And the second thing," said Milo Rook.

"The second thing is that on the fourth I was in a room and the authority of the letter was read out to me and I read it out to about nine people, and there is no longer any argument in this district about the fact that a letter has an authority, and the *content* of the letter is a refusal, and a refusal is a document, and it has been on the file since the fourth of the month, and I have not mentioned it in eleven days and I am mentioning it now because it is the trap." She looked at the yard. "Advocate Quill has refused to supply a schedule of persons, and the refusal is on the file, and it means that on Monday I am standing in a room with a form box in front of me that is empty, in my own hand, with my own name and my own date under it, and every single person in this yard knows that I did that, and that is the only reason I am going to be able to stand there and ask for the order to be suspended. I have spent twenty days with an empty box. If I had put three hundred names in it on the fourth I would have nothing to say on Monday at all."

Somebody at the back said, "So they've told you there's nobody there."

"They have been told by me that there is nobody on the face of that form," said Mara Quill, "and I have been told it by about forty people in a yard in front of my own district, and that is a representation of this district's position and not a fact about this district, and on Monday I am going to be careful to say the first one and not the second, and Mr Rook, you are going to be the man who asks me about it in the corridor on the Sunday, and you are going to ask me about it twice, and I am going to be angry, and it is going to be the best thing that has ever happened to this case."

---

The field work of the week was nine feet of trench through a made path, a trench that had to be under the coal hatch side and had been a fence with a notice wired to it for eleven days in the middle of it, and Milo Rook went into it four times and wrote down the readings in a hand he could still produce.

The soft lens did what the book said it would do. The rod went in nine feet and stopped, as it has done since the seventeenth of last month, and on the Friday the twenty-eighth of the month at about half past ten in the morning Danner had a reading off it and said one word, which was *there*, and then said the rest of it, which was that the two feet were all of it, and the ground had stopped, and he was not going to be the man who says a number like that twice.

Mr Rennick had asked him for eleven days in the middle of last month and brought him back for five, from Monday the first to the Friday, and the four-day window the reading needed was the second to the fifth, so the day he has been paid for and not used is the first, and it is the first day of a level on ground that stopped about four hours before anybody could get a rod into it, and Milo Rook wrote both of those things down in the same line and did not like doing it.

He came and found Milo Rook at the top of the trench and said the whole of it out loud, standing there, with nine men about them.

"I was on half rate from the twenty-second and my review is open and it has got no date, and Mr Rennick has put me back on for five days to certify the last reach, and that resets the review again, and it is the second reset in eight weeks." He had a form rolled under his arm. "I am going to tell you what that means, because you are going to hear it said in a room and I would rather you heard it from a foreman than from a chair. It means that in five weeks' time there is a file in which a foreman of the district flood stage has been reinstated twice for certification work and has never once been certified against anything that was not a certificate. That is the whole of it. It is not a lie. It is a form with my name on it and the form is true and the reading of the form is not up to me."

"You have said that in a yard before."

"I said a version of it on the tenth of the month and I have had it in an office drawer for eighteen days, and I am telling you on the Friday so that you have it four days early and I would like you to use it and I would like it noted that I have said the word *use* and I have not said it to a lawyer."

He reached for the form and then did not take it out.

"Here is the thing I have not written down, and you can have it now and not in December. On Monday the first of next month I can certify a level on that reach. On the Tuesday I can certify it. I want to write it on the first, on the first day, on ground that stopped about four hours ago, because a level certified on the first day of a stopped ground is a level certified on the facts, and a level certified on the fourth day is a level certified on a Friday afternoon by a man who is frightened of a Monday."

"And you are going to write it on the fourth," said Milo Rook.

"I am going to write it on the fourth or the fifth, and I have told you why in about a minute, and the why is my own neck." He unrolled it. "The readings in that trench have been a standard deviation for fourteen days. I am certifying on the fourth or the fifth of next month, and on that form, in the *alternative considered* box, in my own hand, I am going to write that I record the certification is made in the fourteenth day of a standard deviation in the readings at the trench, and that I will not be able to say in a room that I stood behind it, and that I am certifying it because the level has to be certified by a man and the only man is me."

"That is what you wrote on the tenth of last month."

"That is what I wrote on the tenth of last month, at ten past five in the afternoon on a Monday in the rain in a porch with about eleven people about, and the office took it, and the office initialled it, and the office did nothing with it at all," said Danner. "So I am going to write it again, on the form, on the fourth, and I will tell you why I am putting it in twice. The first time it was a thing said by a man in a porch. A thing said by a man is a thing a room can decide was said by a man who was upset. On the form it is a thing said by the certifying officer in the box that only takes it, and it goes into the file with the level, and it cannot be separated from the level, and anybody who takes the level out of that file has to take the sentence out with it."

He put the roll back under his arm.

"And that is my own neck. And I have been told by two people in six weeks that a document is a thing that can be produced, and I have decided that the correct answer to that, for a man who has to put his name on a level over four feet of water in a soft bank, is to produce it first, with the sentence attached, while I still have something to lose by doing it. Which I do not, and that is why this is free."

---

They had the check on Friday the twenty-eighth of the month, because the works' notice came off the coal hatch side on the Friday the twenty-first and Lena Marr had put out a notice of her own on the Thursday that said *the door is open on Friday, ten to two, anybody, and nobody has to be Lena Marr*, and the sixth of Sazur and the twenty-third of October and the thirtieth and the sixth and the thirteenth had all been a **named person**, and this one was the first that was not.

"I am not taking it," said Rue, on the Thursday night, to about nineteen people. "Rue is not a named person. I have been out at eight and in at four every day for eleven weeks and I have never once been the one that stands at a door, and if I stand at a door on Friday then for the rest of this district I am the woman who stood at a door, and the woman of sixty will be the woman who stood at a door on the sixth of next month with a man behind her, and I will have done the thing to her that was done to a woman of sixty in a school office in this month. So anybody."

Verrick Assal said, "Ask it again," and Rue said, "That is not an answer and I know it is not an answer," and Verrick said, "It is the only thing I have got, I have had it since the sixth of Sazur, and I am telling you the same way I told Mr Rook and you can all write it down again."

Doreen Sankey took it, and the count was **Rue's**, and the walking in and out was **Doreen Sankey's own**, and Rue has recorded the terms of it in the round, in her own hand, and they are that the person who goes to the door goes at ten to two and waits until the plant stops and then says the word, and the person who is with them says it again from the other side of the door, and nobody counts, because a mechanic of sixty-one has said in this yard that counting is counting a machine.

It asked, at the interval, at about ten to two on the twenty-eighth, and Doreen Sankey said it from the coal hatch side and Rue said it at the north door about four seconds later, and nothing else happened, and the plant started up again.

"That is five," said Lena Marr, to about nineteen people. "The twenty-third of October, worked. The thirtieth, did not ask. The sixth, worked, and a second voice off a landing. The thirteenth, no door. And the twenty-eighth of November, worked, and not a named person for the first time in six years, and a man of forty-one who came here in October and asked for a way to walk and has asked for nothing since." She did not make anything of it and neither did anybody else, and it is on the page because it is the fifth time a person has said a word at a door in eleven weeks, and the check is a check, and a check is a person saying a space is clear before the next turn, and nobody in this district has decided that again.

---

**Friday the twenty-eighth of the month, 22.40.** He walked home at about half past ten and up his own stairs, the second stair and not the first, and at the top of them he stood for about a minute and then went and got a piece of chalk out of his coat and put an arrow on the inside of his own front door, at about four feet, with a date under it, which is the practice of a gate-man in this district and is not his, and he has written that down.

*Twenty-eighth of the month, 22.40. Nine remembered routes, and eight of them were there this morning, and the ninth is a page and not a route, and I have been near to counting them as one thing twice today and both times I stopped.*

*Four attempts, and the fourth is a woman from a fourth floor who said four seconds to me at a door at eleven o'clock on a Wednesday and would not say another word about it, and a boy of seventeen who told me to go round the front and up the second stair and did not put his bicycle down and did not look back, and it is now the third time anybody in this district has marked a route for me and I have asked every one of them for nothing, and the mark is not a favour and it is not a debt and I have been told by a doctor that it is the ordinary condition of a man in my condition and by a woman with a satchel that it is nobody's business.*

*And the first stage is not worse. Eight is eight. I have been to six of the eight this week and lost none of them and I do not understand that and I am not going to write down what it might mean.*

*The second stage is a blank nine lines long in my own book and it is not persistent inside this month. I found my own house today on the second try on the back of a chalk arrow I put on my own door with a date under it, and I am not cured and nothing has been closed, and I am not going to be able to find it again on Thursday unless somebody is there, and I have written down that I do not know whether that is a magic cost or a page that needs another person's mark, and I am not going to decide that this month, and Dr Sen has told me in a dispensary that in a month I will be standing in a room saying the ninth, and the ninth is a page.*

*Six days, nine days, three days, three days, two days. Five pairs. The silence in this book since the fifteenth of the month is the longest it has been in eleven weeks and I have written six dates down and I have not paired them, and a set of four is not a pair and a set of six is a fortnight, and the fifteenth of this month to the twenty-fourth is nine days, and that is one pair and I am not writing it, because I have decided that I will not add a pair in a week in which I have lost the line home, and I would be a man arranging his own evidence, and I have been that man twice.*
