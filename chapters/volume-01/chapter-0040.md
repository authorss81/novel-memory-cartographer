# Chapter 40: What She Won

The Directions Bench listed the application at ten past two on the afternoon of Tuesday the twenty-first of the month and sat for fifty-five minutes, and the clerk who read the list had been in the room on the fifth of Sazur at dawn and had read the stay out then, and nobody asked him to, and he did it anyway because he said that was his job and that people should know what had been minuted in this room.

Mara Quill's application had nine paragraphs and asked for four things. It had been settled in a room with Dilley Farr's survey copier on the Thursday and on the Friday, and the four things were these, and they are set out here in the order she asked for them and not in the order they came out.

One: that the field report of the eleventh, in which Survey Enforcement determined the site to be a minor seam with no witness maintained and no persons affected, should not be relied on in the hearing of October except as to the condition of the works.

Two: that the works' daily return should record persons, and that the count of two hundred and forty-nine people in fifty-four rooms taken on doorsteps on the sixteenth and seventeenth should be entered on the record as a count and not as a rateable basis.

Three: that the schedule level certified under paragraph four of the amendment of the twenty-eighth of Sazur, and the joint finding of the twentieth, should go to the October Bench with the file.

Four: that one named person be permitted to walk the threshold of the withdrawn alignment once a week, in daylight, on a fixed day, for four weeks, with the consent of the works and with nothing recorded as a household route.

The chair of the Bench was a woman of about sixty called Perrins who had been in the room for the fourteenth of Sazur and had been in the room for the fifth and had not, as far as anybody could tell, been impressed by either of them.

They got one.

"I am going to say what this application is not," she said, after the first paragraph, "so that nobody spends the afternoon discovering it. This does not suspend the order. The order was upheld in full on the fourteenth of last month and it is in force and nothing said to me this afternoon alters a word of it. This does not restore a name to the street index, and I have been asked three times this morning by three different people whether it might, and it does not, and anybody who has come here this afternoon expecting a street name is in the wrong room. This does not lift the regrade, and the regrade is the district's own money and is running, and the plate on the door of the twelfth is lawful and stands, and if anybody in this room has come to have the plate taken off I would like them to say so now rather than at half past four."

Nobody said so.

"Nor does it do anything about the water," said the chair. "I have the return of the sixteenth in front of me and it says six inches and standing and it is the twentieth day and I am told there are three pumps on that street. Directions about drainage are a different matter and there is a form for them and it is not this one."

---

Mara Quill got the third thing in a form she had not asked for, and she made it smaller herself before anybody made it smaller for her, which was the single most impressive thing she did in fifty-five minutes and which Milo Rook noticed and did not admire, because admiration would have implied that he had expected otherwise.

The chair put it in this way: that the file go to October with the level certification and the joint finding attached, and that the Bench would take them *as documents* and would not take the surveyor's account of what they meant.

"They are in," said Perrins. "Mr Rook, you may not speak to them. You have a document and a document speaks for itself or it does not, and I have read the joint finding and it is four paragraphs and it does not name you in the second one except as the certifying officer of a level, which is a fact and not an opinion. Are you content to be silent on your own certification?"

"Yes."

"That was quick." She wrote for a while. "Then the classification and the return are refused, both of them, and I am going to give my reasons because I would rather this were in a minute than in a letter to somebody in Crown Heights. The classification is evidence. It is a document made by an officer with a duty, and it is relevant, and I am not going to exclude evidence in a room because a party thinks it is the wrong shape. If the district wants to argue that the fourth finding of the eleventh is wrong, the district can argue it in October with people in the room, and it will have to find somebody to say so."

Nobody in the room said so.

"And the return is a return. It is a daily form and it is made by a clerk from a day book and it is not evidence of the state of a street; it is evidence of what a day book contains, and on the sixteenth the day book was closed at half past six because the foreman was stood down, and that is a fact about the works and not about Bridge Street, and it goes into the file exactly as it is and the district is free to attack it in October in about eleven seconds if it wants to."

"And the count," said Mara Quill.

"The count of the sixteenth and seventeenth is entered on the record at the applicant's request, as a count, taken on doorsteps, without knocking, by six named persons, and I have read the form and the sum is two hundred and forty-nine in fifty-four rooms, and I am told the district's own figure in August was two hundred and thirty-one." She looked up. "It is entered, and it is entered as a count. It is not a rateable basis, and it is not a finding about anybody, and it does not alter the per-head schedule, which is a matter for a rates authority and not for me, and Advocate Quill tells me she has that before a rates authority and I have noted the date. Counsel, is there anything further?"

"No, ma'am."

"Then the fourth application." The chair turned two pages back. "A named person, once a week, in daylight, on a fixed day, for four weeks, on the threshold of a withdrawn alignment, with the consent of the works, recording no household route. Advocate Quill, who is the named person?"

Mara Quill put a sheet of paper face up on the table, and it was a sheet of typing paper, not a form, and it had one name on it.

"Lena Marr," she said. "Cedar Court. And she has not been named by me, because I have not asked her."

The room did a thing that rooms do.

"She has consented in writing," said Mara Quill, "on Monday the twentieth of this month, and the writing is in her own hand and in her words and I have read it out in this room and I am going to read it out again, because she asked me to and because an advocate who reads a witness's consent in her own words is doing the only thing an advocate can do. May I?"

"You may."

"'I am not doing this for the file and I am not doing it for October. I am doing it because there is a check that goes at six o'clock and nobody has stood at a door since a woman of sixty-eight decided to stop, and I have read forty-one names out loud at my own kitchen table since I was nineteen years old and I did not know until the third of this month that that was a rota, and it is a rota and it is four live lines and a half. So I am going to stand at a door once a week for four weeks and if the thing asks me for clear I am going to say it, and if it does not ask me for anything then I have lost four Thursdays and I will say so here and in the yard and I will not be praised for it. And I am putting my name on it because the moment somebody's name is on it the thing is real, and I have spent six years putting names in places where they did no good and I am going to do it once where it might. Do not tell me I have done a good thing. I have a boy who cannot come home on the route I wrote him.'" Mara Quill looked up from the sheet. "That is the whole of it and she will not come to this room and she has told me why."

"And what is the price of it," said the chair.

"She loses something at the school." Mara Quill did not look up. "She is a named person on a Registry instrument and a school is looking for a reason, and she has nine children out of a school until the spring intake, and two of the nine are not coming back in the spring, because their mother is a person on a paper in a building in Crown Heights. She told me that on Saturday in a kitchen and she told me that she had thought about it for two days and that she had decided the two children were worth it and that she wanted that in the minute."

"It is in the minute," said the chair.

"Thursday," said Perrins. "Once a week, in daylight, on the last Thursday of each of the next four weeks. The twenty-third of this month, the thirtieth, the sixth of next month and the thirteenth, and I am sorry that the first of them is the day after tomorrow, and I would have given you a month if a month could be got through this building in ten days, and it cannot, and four weeks is what I can do, and I have done it. The works has consented on the record, the foreman is not certifying returns at the moment, which I am told is a separate matter, and the consent is given by the site manager and is good. The person may stop at any point and the stopping needs no reason. Nothing is recorded as a household route. And it expires at noon on Friday the fourteenth of next month and it cannot be extended by anybody, including me, and there is no form for extending it, because there is no form for a thing that is only ever four weeks."

"It will not be extended," said Mara Quill.

"It will not be extended," said the chair, "and it is the smallest instrument I have made in nine years and I have made about four hundred, and I want the applicant to understand that it protects nobody, it restores nothing, and the district it is in is going to be under six inches of water until somebody with a bigger budget than this Bench looks at it."

"I understand," said Mara Quill.

"And neither," said the chair, "does it touch the order, and I have written that in, and I have written in that the public name is not restored and no variation of the order is made, and if it is produced in October as a victory for somebody I shall look at the minute and see what it says, which is four Thursdays and a name."

The minute was finished at ten past three and signed at half past three and it is, in its entirety, four lines long and it does exactly what a thing of that shape does, and nobody in this district has said out loud what it is worth.

---

Tarin Kest had an application of his own, and he lodged it himself, in his own name, in his own hand, on a Survey Enforcement form of the Registry's own making, at twenty past four on the Tuesday afternoon, in a corridor, and he did it where a clerk and about nine other people could see him do it.

Milo Rook was one of the nine.

It was an application to vary a continuity order in force in the interest of the public, on the ground that the precedent of the Filewell order of the fourth year back showed that an order of this class was capable of variation and lapse, and it cited a Crown Heights repository consultation reference, and it was four paragraphs long and every paragraph of it was correct.

The clerk looked at it for about eleven seconds.

"I can complete this," she said. "Section three requires a person familiar with the route withdrawn. There's a box."

"I know there's a box."

"There's no name."

"There's no name," said Tarin Kest, "and I have written *no person available* on it, and I would like to know whether you are going to refuse it or whether you are going to complete it with that entry in it, because I would rather have the completed form with the entry in it, and I will take it to my division with the entry in it, and a form with an entry in it is a document and a document is what a department can be made to do something with."

The clerk wrote *no person available* in the box and initialled it and gave it back.

"Thank you," said Tarin Kest.

"It will go nowhere," said the clerk.

"It is not going nowhere, it is going into a file in my division with a date on it and a name at the top of it, and in three weeks somebody in Crown Heights will have to read it and write a reason." He put it in the inside pocket of his coat, and it was the first time Milo Rook had seen him put a document anywhere other than a folder. "That is the whole of what I have learned in this division in nine days and it is not a technique, and I am not going to tell anybody at a yard about it, and I would be obliged if you did not either."

"I do not intend to."

"Good." He looked at Milo Rook. "You did not speak."

"I was told not to."

"You were told not to and you said yes in under a second, which is not a thing a man says when he wants to be in a room." He shut the folder. "I am going to exclude your interpretation of the level from October. I have already written it. I am not going to change it because a bridge came down, and I want you to know that now, on a day when you would be entitled to think otherwise, because if I change it after a bridge comes down then it is a reaction and not a decision, and in three weeks' time somebody will ask me which it was."

"It was a decision in August?"

"It was a decision on Tuesday," said Tarin Kest, "and on Tuesday I had read the file and not the joint finding, and there is a difference and I know what the difference is worth and I have written the paragraph."

---

He was in the corridor outside the Hall of Wards at four o'clock with a folder and a coat on and about nine days of something in his face, and Milo Rook said two things to him and one of them was about the four Thursdays.

"The classification stands and the level is in the file and the count is in the minute and your form is in your coat," he said, "and there is not one of those five things that the woman on the second floor had anything to do with."

"Then say it to me as though it were a complaint and get it out of your system, because I have been standing in a corridor for ten minutes and I have a report to write."

Milo Rook said it.

"I am not going to have a book that a stranger can be taken out of," he said. "In October a man in a coat is going to read four pages in which the only person with a signature on any of them is you, and he is going to take it out, and it will be lawful, and nobody will have to be crooked, and I want there to be a document in that file that somebody else wrote and that I am not in. That is the whole of what I want. I do not want it to be true. I want it to be *true and not mine*."

"You want four independent accounts of a thing you are one of the authors of."

"Yes."

"That is either the most or the least honest thing anybody has said to me this month," said Tarin Kest, "and I am going to have to decide which, and I am not going to decide it in a corridor." He looked at the coat pocket with the form in it. "If you ever get a document like that, I am the man who will read it twice and write *no* in a box, and I would like that on the record from you, in front of that marshal, so that when it happens I cannot pretend to be surprised."

"It will not be in front of the marshal."

"Then it will be in front of somebody," said Tarin Kest, "and Mr Rook, I have nine days left in this audit and then I go back to a desk in Crown Heights, and I am going to spend four months on that desk thinking about a plate of accountancy paper with a promise at the foot of it, and I have not got the faintest idea what I am supposed to do with that, and I am not going to tell you about it again, and I would be obliged if you never mentioned it to anyone."

---

They came out of the Hall of Wards at twenty past four and there were about twenty people in the yard at Cedar Court, because Lena Marr had put a notice up on the porch wall in the morning with the hour and the outcome, and the notice said *Directions Bench, 21st, half past three, four Thursdays* and nothing else, and twenty people had come to find out what four Thursdays was.

Lena Marr was on the coal hatch side and she had the sheet of typing paper in her hand with her own name on it, and she had not asked for a copy and had not been given one, and she said the thing about the two children first because she had said on Saturday that she wanted it in the minute and it was in the minute.

"Two of them are not coming back in the spring," she said, "and I told the school woman on Saturday and she did not cry, which I had expected her to, and she said *that is two, then*, and asked me whether I had told their mother, and I have not, and I am going to on Sunday, and I would like somebody in this yard to come with me and I am not going to ask."

Nobody volunteered, and she did not ask twice, and Milo Rook did not offer, and everybody understood that he had not offered on purpose.

"The other thing, and it is smaller and it has been sitting on me since the twenty-first of Sazur," she said. "I have not been back. I have said I would find out where he is buried and I have not gone, and I am not going to tell that yard tonight that I have run out of road, because I have not, I have simply discovered that I do not know the question any more. I have got a woman of fifty-four who mends other people's clothes and a daughter in Wragg Street and a grave in a register that was not kept after the closure, and every clerk I have found in four months has been entirely correct, and I have stopped being able to be angry at them, and I would like somebody to tell me what to do with a correct clerk."

"Come back on the Sunday," said Rue. "Not for an answer. For the walking."
"That is the first sensible thing anybody has said to me about it in four months," said Lena Marr, "and I am not going to say thank you, and I am going to do it."

Then she said the thing that turned the afternoon over, and she said it to about twenty people with a sheet of typing paper in her hand.

"Right. I have stood at a door once a week for four weeks from the twenty-third and the Bench has said so in writing, and that is four Thursdays, and I want to know what the district is doing on the fifteenth of next month, and I have asked that question in this yard four times this month and I have got the answer three times and it is the same answer, and the answer is: nothing, because a four-week thing stops."

She turned the sheet over.

"Four Thursdays is not a route. A route is a thing that is still there on the fifth Thursday, and there is no form for the fifth Thursday, and there is no form for the fifth Thursday because a form is made by somebody who can be asked whether the fifth Thursday is wanted, and nobody in this district can be asked that except people in a yard, and a yard is not a form.

"So somebody has to build the next one. And it is not a hearing, and it is not a hearing in October either, and it is not a petition and it is not a rota, because a rota is five lines and five names and one line through one of them and a promise at the foot that nobody else crosses anybody's name off, and I have looked at that promise for a fortnight and I have decided that it is the only piece of civic machinery in this district that works, and I am not going to wrap it in a form and hand it to a clerk in Crown Heights, and I would fight anybody who tried."

"Then what is it," said Rue, from the porch.

"It is a way back that more than four people can use, that is not a map and is not a list and is not a record, that somebody can be handed and somebody else can change, and that has a space left in it on purpose." Lena Marr was not looking at Milo Rook and did not appear to know that he was standing eleven feet away. "And it has to be somebody who was not there on the first crossing who can change it, and not a surveyor, and not a foreman, and not a woman from an office, and it has to be somebody who can be asked whether they want the job. And I have got a building with five floors and about forty people in it and eleven tenancies, and the answer is going to be the same in every room I walk into, and it is going to be *no*, and it is going to be no the way Owen says no, and I am going to have to sit there and take it from eleven households who have every reason to say it."

"Who is going to write it," said Tarin Kest, from the back, where nobody had noticed him either.

Nobody answered him for about four seconds, which was a long time in that yard.

"That is the question," said Lena Marr, "and it is the first question anybody has asked me in six weeks that I have not got an answer to, and I have got one day to get one, and I am going to bed."

---

He got it in about a week and it was no good, and it took him a fortnight, and it was never written, and here is what he wrote that night at the office with four forms on the desk in four piles that did not touch each other.

*Twenty-first of the month, 22.50. Directions Bench, ten past two to five past three. One thing got, out of four asked for.*

*It is four Thursdays, on a fixed day, in daylight, by one named person, with the works' consent, recording no household route, expiring at noon on Friday the fourteenth of next month, and it cannot be extended by anybody, and there is no form for extending it because there is no form for a thing that is only ever four weeks. The chair wrote in the minute that it does not suspend the order, that the public name is not restored, that no variation of the order is made, that it protects nobody and restores nothing, and that the district it is in will be under six inches of water until somebody with a bigger budget looks at it. That is the whole of what she won and it is the same shape as the stay of the tenth of Sazur, which expired in one line on the twenty-eighth and had never done anything else, and she got it in one hearing instead of losing it, and I have watched her work for four months and that is the best single piece of advocacy I have ever seen anybody do.*

*And it cost her the two children, and Lena Marr put that in a yard herself at twenty past four in the afternoon because she had said on Saturday that she wanted it in the minute.*

*Refused: the classification, on the ground that evidence is not excluded in a room for being the wrong shape, which is correct and which I have never thought of. The daily return, on the ground that it is evidence of a day book and not of a street, which is also correct. My account of the level, on the ground that a document speaks for itself, and the joint finding of the twentieth is in the file and it is four paragraphs and the second of them says a surface was made level across a place which had been two surfaces for six years, and the Bench will read it as a document and not as anything else, and that is the correct treatment of it and it is going to destroy me in October and I asked for it.*

*And Tarin Kest put a variation application in his own name in his own hand into a coat pocket in a corridor in front of nine people and it came back with* no person available *in the box in a clerk's hand, and he said thank you, and he said it will go nowhere, and then he said it is going into a file in his division with a date on it and a name at the top of it, and that is the whole of what he has learned in nine days, and he is going back to a desk in Crown Heights on Sunday and he is not going to be a friend of mine and he has never once pretended to be anything else.*

*The level is out of my file and it is going into the works' file under a foreman's signature, and Danner said yes on the Thursday in six words, and the six words were* Plate fixed. Six twenty-five. My plate. *and the price of the six words is that he will be asked to stand behind a level over a trench with four feet in it in a Bench in October, and he is not a man who will be able to, and he said so, and he said it while standing, and I have not written it down because he did not ask me to and I am not going to.*

*Crossings: six. Losses: eight, and the eighth is given back in chalk on a gate post by a man who was told by Rue to say he did not do it for me. The book is silent and has said nothing in the nine days since the tenth, and it said nothing on the sixth or on the thirtieth of Sazur either, and I have written the two pairs down and drawn nothing out of them. Field Book sound. The line home is in it in my own hand and any person asked to walk it can walk it. The token is cold and is in the pocket and was cold in the dark on Sunday on a step in a side street a hundred and fifty yards from a bridge.*

*And the new problem, and it is the only thing on this page that I would call that.*

*Four Thursdays expires at noon on the fourteenth of next month and the district has nothing for the fifteenth. I have been asked in a yard by a woman with a sheet of typing paper in her hand to build something that is not a map and is not a list and is not a record, that four people can use, that has a space left in it on purpose, and that somebody who was not there on the fourth of Sazur can change.*

*And I cannot build it. Not because of the four conditions and not because of the four crossings and not because of the orientation blank, and not because I do not know how a route is drawn, and I have got a book with eight lost routes in it and I could draw it in an hour and it would be beautiful and it would be mine.*

*Dr Sen's fifth condition is not a condition and cannot be signed, and it is that I am not to be left alone with a piece of paper about a person, not once, not ever, for as long as I am in this district, and I have never written it down anywhere, and on the tenth of this month I went into a court at four minutes past two with that sheet folded in a satchel in my head, and the lane asked the question it has asked twice now and the answer was the same both times, and it is going to go on asking it and I am going to go on going in, and every one of those crossings costs a route and buys me nothing, because the person who could answer the third call is not on the sheet and will not be, and the one who is on the sheet on the fifth floor has not come down and has written three words and said second and Thursday, and the only person who could draw the thing this district needs is a man who is standing on a page about a person with a pencil in his hand.*

*There is a building with five floors and about forty people in it and eleven tenancies, and the answer is going to be no in every room, and the answer is going to be no the way a woman on the fourth floor says it, with four words beside her name so that nobody can use her, and I am going to have to sit in eleven kitchens and be told no eleven times, and then somebody is going to have to write it anyway without me, and the first person that anybody thinks of is a person on the fifth floor of a converted pump works who has not come down since the third of the month, and nobody in this district is going to go up and ask, because I have been up and I have not asked, and I have written that down tonight so that the next person knows it has been thought about and left where it was, and it has.*

*Twenty-four days to noon on the fourteenth. And a Bench in October, and a mechanic's sentence that is going to be true and unsworn, and a plate on a door, and a street under six inches of water and rising, and four Thursdays.*
