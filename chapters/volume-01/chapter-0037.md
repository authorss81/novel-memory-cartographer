# Chapter 37: The Precedent

The return for the twelfth went up on the board outside the portacabin on Monday the thirteenth, and the return for the thirteenth went up on the same board on the Tuesday morning, and by the Tuesday afternoon it had been read by nobody in particular and by everybody in the district, because in Alderquay a return is a piece of paper on a board and a board is a fact, and Milo Rook read both of them standing up because he has learned that in this district you read things standing up and then you walk away from them before you decide what they mean.

**DISTRICT WORKS — DAILY RETURN**
*12th. Low streets, Bridge Street to the wall with the old paint on it: surface water 4 in, standing, not receding. Properties inspected: 11. Persons affected: nil. Subsidence: none observed. Alignment, low end: see variation of the 6th, plate fixed 12th.*

Then, on the Tuesday, on the same board, in the same hand, for the thirteenth: *surface water 5 in. Properties inspected: 11. Persons affected: nil. Subsidence: none observed.*

Milo Rook looked at the two of them side by side for a while and then went and found the Recovery officer in the awning.

"How many are on your schedule," he said.

"Two hundred and thirty-one."

"When were they counted."

"August. By the district, on a schedule, before any of this. I did not count them and I have said so in writing twice."

"And how many dwellings is that?"

"That," said the Recovery officer, "is a question I have been asking since Friday and I have not got an answer, and I would like you to understand that I have a relief schedule on this table with two and four a head on it and two hundred and thirty-one heads, and I have had it on this table since the fourteenth of Sazur, and I have watched the water come up Bridge Street on Sunday and I have not been able to make it go down by being right about anything."

She turned the schedule over. She always turned it over before she said anything else.

"You cannot charge a person for a room they are not in," she said. "And you cannot pay a person for a room you have never been in. And both of those are my problem and neither of them is the works' and neither of them is the Registry's, and I have been sitting under this awning since the fourteenth of Sazur discovering that out of the three of you it is me who has got the number in her own hand."

---

Tarin Kest had sent word at nine o'clock through the gate-man, and the message was four words on the back of a delivery docket, and the words were *District store, eleven, bring nothing.*

He had a table in the district file store with three boxes on it and no chair, because there is one chair and it was in the corridor. He had been there since half past eight. He had a pair of cotton gloves on, which he had not had when Milo Rook last saw him and which he put down when he shook hands.

"I am going to read this out to you," he said, "and then I am going to be asked in October why I read it out to the man it concerns, and I have thought about that for two days and the answer is that I found it at nine in the morning and I did not want to be the man who found out alone, and if you are going to be an adversary of mine you are going to be an adversary who knows what is in the room."

"Read it out."

Tarin Kest read out a file index.

"It is the district's own district store and it is the only district store that is allowed to hold a live order, and this one is not live, and it is the fourth year back." He turned the sheet round so the writing was the right way up for the first time. "Order in force, district continuity, river core. Site: a block of thirty-four dwellings at the low end of Filewell, and a yard behind it, and a public name withdrawn from the index in the ordinary way with a variation in six weeks. Order made under the standing instruction of the Prefect's office, the safety instruction, the one that says that a continuity order is to be reviewed at the first season end after the works that caused it are complete."

"Date it."

"Fourth year back. Fourteenth of the month. Lapsed on the fourteenth of the next month, on the review, on a variation, on the certificate of a person familiar with the route and the schedule of works complete and the district drainage restored." He put his finger on the index line. "And then the thing I am actually going to read out. The index to that file, against the paragraph the order-maker's report sits behind, carries a reference. It is four letters and two numbers, entered by a clerk, in a clerk's hand, in the form the repository uses when a division borrows a volume out of a store it does not own."

Milo Rook did not move and did not say anything for about four seconds.

"The Bellwether file," he said. "Page nine. The withdrawal reference. Same form."

"Same form, four letters and two numbers, entered by a clerk." Kest took a folded sheet out of the third box and did not open it. "I have not got a copy of the Bellwether index. I have got a piece of paper with a description on it and I am not going to pretend to you that I have matched the strings, because I have not and I could not today in this building without a disclosure I am not in a position to make. What I am telling you is that a similar reference is on a district order file in this store, in this city, in the fourth year back, and that the order it sits on was withdrawn from the district's own records on a Crown Heights instruction, and that the order was allowed to lapse, and that the person who allowed it to lapse is named on the certificate."

"Whose name."

"A works clerk's, and the works clerk has been dead for two years, and the certificate is signed by the foreman who fitted the gate, and the foreman is alive and is in this district and does not remember doing it, which I have checked, and which is the single most ordinary thing I have ever found in this job."

Kest squared the three boxes.

"Now the part I have thought hardest about," he said. "A variation like that one requires two things. A completed schedule of works. And a person familiar with the route. Not a surveyor. A person. And in the fourth year back the district had one, and the clerk who indexed it wrote his name in the box without hesitating, and that is the whole of the difference between that file and yours, and it is not a moral difference and it is not a difference in the quality of the two officers, and it is the only thing I have found in nine days that explains why your order looks the way it does without anybody in the order having behaved differently from the man who wrote the Filewell one."

"And what is that?"

"The Filewell district had thirty-four households in it and they were not rateable and they were in unlicensed rooms and they had a woman on the low end who had been there nineteen years and who the clerk could name because he had met her at a counter. Your district has one household in it, on five storeys, and the register's answer to who speaks for it is a man on the third floor who is asleep." Kest said it flatly, and he did not say a name, and he did not ask Milo Rook a single question about it, and he wrote a word on the corner of a sheet and turned the sheet round.

*No person available.*

"That is the third time that phrase has been on an instrument in this district since the twenty-third of Sazur," he said. "It is on the tram authority's certification box, and it is on this, and it will be on whatever Advocate Quill puts in front of a Bench in three weeks, and it is the correct entry every time, and I have begun to think that a correct entry in a box with nothing in it is the loudest sentence this Registry produces."

---

The room with a door was the works office at half past three on the Tuesday afternoon, and there were three of them in it, and Tarin Kest had asked for it, and Milo Rook had not been told why and had gone.

"You asked me on Saturday," said Kest, "in front of nine people, whether my figures were contaminated by a private grievance, and you said you would answer in writing, and you were right to and it is not an answer. So here is my paragraph, and I am reading it to you before it goes in, because I do not write reports about people behind their backs, and because you will want to know it is coming and I would rather you heard it from me than from a copy."

"Read it."

"The surveyor's record in this matter is excluded from the October hearing as to interpretation, construction and cause. Measurements of level, fall, settlement and aperture are admitted as measurements, unverified, on the surveyor's own signature, and are subject to the works' counter-evidence. The account of a coordinated evacuation interrupted by the order is not in issue on this file and I have not tested it, and I record that I have not tested it, and I do not accept that it establishes a hazard of any kind, and it is not the reason I am doing what I am doing."

"And the four accounts."

"Three sworn, one unsworn, and the unsworn one is a mechanic's writing in an advocate's hand and it is admissible and it is worse than a sworn one, and I have nothing whatever to say about it that would improve it, and I would like it observed that the man who decided not to swear has cost himself more than the man who decided to." He shut the folder. "I am not your friend. I have written a paragraph that will keep a sworn mechanic's sentence out of a finding, and I have done it under a standing instruction I have never read and do not expect to, and I have done it because the sentence would be true."

"Then I have no argument."

"No," said Tarin Kest. "You have an argument about your survey and I have read it and it is good, and I have got an instruction and a nine-day-old file and a man on a bench in October who is going to ask me why I let a man with a grievance certify the level of a made road over a district that is under water at the present moment. I am going to say the thing nobody in Crown Heights will say to me, which is that the level is your level and the water is his water and the sentence in your file that says *no person available* is doing the work of both."

---

Mara Quill was at the office on the ground floor of the Civic Atlas building at about ten past five on the Tuesday evening with a folder, and she had been upstairs for forty minutes with Dilley Farr, who had let her use the survey copy of a form because she had asked him for it by the name of the thing she wanted and not by her own name.

"Sit down," she said. "No. Don't sit down, I want you standing, because in about four minutes I am going to say something that is going to be unfair and I want you to have to look up at me."

He stood.

"I have drafted an application," she said, "under the Registry's own rule, to have the surveyor's record in the Bellwether matter struck from the file on the ground of a declared interest. On your application. Yours."

The Civic Atlas Office had four people in it and the corridor had a draught in it and Milo Rook heard the words and understood them and had to put one hand on the edge of the counter.

"Say the rest of it."

"The rest of it is what it costs and I have costed it the way you would." She turned the folder round. "Your certificate as surveyor of record, for the amendment and for the level. Your office, which is Dilley's office and which Dilley has kept open for six years by filing things and not signing things, and which will close. Your standing, which is the only thing you own that is worth anything. And the amendment's paragraph four, which goes back to the timetable division, which will be re-laid on a description of a bend by a man with a list, because that is what Barrow did once and would do again."

"And the measurements go with the certificate."

"The measurements go with the certificate. They are certified measurements. They exist because you signed them. And if you are struck out then the twenty-second of Sazur is a hole in a file where a fall and a settlement of nine inches over forty feet used to be, and Orrin Sable's mechanism is a mechanism that nobody in that room can demonstrate, and I have watched a mechanic demonstrate it with a rod and change the whole of his month."

Milo Rook stood at the counter of a survey office and had about eight seconds in which he could have been a different man.

"Then take it out," he said.

"Take what out."

"The measurement. Take it out and leave the accounts. There are four accounts and a paragraph six and a closure notice read whole and a report that says nobody is there, and none of that has my name on it and none of that can be struck out, and if I am out of the file then what is left is a district that argues without a surveyor, and I have been in this district for six weeks watching what happens when a street is defended by people who are not measurements."

"No," said Mara Quill.

"You have—"

"I have three reasons and I am going to give you all of them and you are going to hate the third one." She did not raise her voice. "One. The account that is going to decide this is a mechanic's account about a road that was not being kept, and if there is no level in the file then that account is a man saying a thing that nobody can check, and a man saying a thing nobody can check is exactly what a Bench cannot do anything with. Two. The classification that Ardery has signed says *no person available* and it says it on the strength of nobody having any standing, and the only thing on this file that has standing is a document, and documents are made out of signed measurements and sworn accounts, and there are three of the second. And three."

She stopped, and it was the third one that mattered and she took about four seconds over it.

"Three is that you are not going to be out of this file. I did not draft that application because I think you are contaminated. I drafted it because it was on the desk when I came in and because in seven days I have to put something in front of a Bench, and I want you to know exactly what kind of person I am, and I would rather you heard it from me on a Tuesday evening than found it in a minute in October. I drafted the instrument that would destroy you because I was looking for a way not to have to be the one who defends you in a room, and that is my own hand, and I have a review on the third of next month about a similar piece of work."

Milo Rook did not say anything for a while, and the draught moved up the corridor.

"There's another way," he said. "Is there."

"There is one and it is better and I have not slept about it. And it costs you the thing you have just offered me, so you are not going to want it, and I have decided that is not my problem."

"Go on."

"The level is a foreman's level." She put a second sheet on the counter, and it was a form and it was not a Registry form. "The plate is his machine and the plate is his fill and the schedule level and the date are set by the district works and the certification is his because he applied for it and he put *plate fixed* on it at twenty-five past six on Sunday morning with the door shut and a lamp on a hook. He has certified his own work nine times in eleven weeks on that site. He can certify a level. He is doing it to himself."

"He will be stood down on Thursday."

"He will be stood down on Thursday, or the day after, and he will be stood down for refusing to certify *subsidence: none observed*, and there is a trench on that site with four feet in it and a soft lens under it, and Danner wrote the truth in the box on the variation on the sixth of this month, and I am going to be somewhere near the man who reads it." She squared the two sheets. "If the level comes out of your file and goes into the works' file under a foreman's signature, then the surveyor's record can be excluded from October without a single thing being lost, because the level is a fact about a made surface and the man who made the surface can stand behind it. And everything else of yours — the fall, the aperture, the eleven inches, the alignment, the field notes — can be struck, and I will strike it, and I will do it without your consent, and I would like you to hear that sentence in advance."

"You are not going to ask me."

"I am not going to ask you," said Mara Quill, "because on the twenty-ninth of Sazur you made me a promise about everything that bears on this file and I have held it for fifteen days and it is the single most useful thing anybody has handed me in six years, and I am not going to hold it and then ask you for anything."

"Take it out," said Milo Rook. "The level goes to Danner and the rest goes and you strike it and I do not want to see the minute."

"You do not get to say that."

"Then I do not get to say it." He put his hand flat on the counter, which he did perhaps twice a year. "Take it out. It was never mine. I put a certificate on a box that says *no person available* and I left the use blank, and Danner applied for a plate on a form where the truth about the drainage had to go in a box because there was nowhere else for it, and you want to know who the real surveyor of record in this case is and it is a foreman on a district flood levy who cannot afford to lose a job in November."

"Wait," said Mara Quill, "before you go and be generous about it at him. I have not asked him."

"Have you asked Danner."

"No."

"Then it is not a plan," said Milo Rook, "it is a rescue you have designed for me, and if you take it to him on a Tuesday evening without asking him you will have done the thing to him that has been done to everybody in this district since the sixth of six years ago, which is decide what a man's duty is before anybody asks him what he thinks it is." He was not angry. It came out level, which was worse. "You are the advocate of record. You have a Bench in seven days. You have a review on your own hand on the third and a second set of initials in an annex box that you have read four times and cannot read aloud. Do not come and hand me a way out that you have not walked into a room with the man who is going to have to stand in it."

Mara Quill stood quite still for a moment.

"Tomorrow," she said.

"Tomorrow."

"And Rook. It is not a rescue. I did not design it for you and I want that said before you build a chapter out of it." She picked up both sheets. "I designed it because a file that one man can discredit is not a case, it is a witness, and I have spent seven days watching a witness instead of a case and I would like it on the record that I recognised the difference on the twenty-ninth of Sazur, in a corridor, in a conversation with a man who was holding a folder, and it took me fifteen days, and I am not going to apologise for the fifteen days because I do not think anybody could have done it in less."

They walked to the turnstiles together, which they had not done since September, and neither of them said anything about that, and at the north end of the corridor she said one more thing.

"You are not going to be able to be in a room with me on the twenty-first of October and I have known that since the twenty-eighth of Sazur, and you have known it since the fourteenth, and neither of us has said it, and I am not saying it now either. I am saying that the level goes to Danner and that on Thursday I am going to ask him, and that if he says no then I am going to do the thing you have just refused to let me do without asking, because it is seven days and I am the advocate of record and I am not going to let my own scruple be the reason this district loses a document."

He walked to the chandler's and up fourteen steps, and he did not try the route, because it was gone, and because it had been gone since the twenty-third of Sazur and he had stopped testing it, and he wrote the evening down in three lines and the third line was not about the level.

*Fourteenth of the month, 22.10. Two documents tonight and I have not signed either of them.*

*One: a precedent four years old in a district store, a continuity order on thirty-four dwellings at Filewell, varied and allowed to lapse on a certificate signed by a foreman who does not remember doing it, with a Crown Heights consultation reference on its index in the form of the one against page nine of ours. Nothing identified. No name. A clerk's hand and two references in one shape, and the resemblance is a resemblance and I have written it once and I am not going to write it twice.*

*Two: she drafted the instrument that would take me out of my own file and she told me she drafted it, and I have not been that angry since the fifteenth of Sazur, and what I was angry about was not the right thing. The right thing is that she designed a way for this file not to need me and I have been telling people since the twenty-second that it does not need me, and it turns out she believed me, and believed me in fifteen days, and I have taken six weeks and a plate.*

*And the third thing, which is the one I will read back in June: I said take it out and she said take it out, and neither of us asked the man whose signature it would be. That is the whole of what has gone wrong in this district since the sixth of six years ago, and it is in a corridor, and I have been the author of it four times, and the fifth time is on Thursday, and Thursday I am not in this case.*
