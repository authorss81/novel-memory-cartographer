# Chapter 47: The Sixth of November

The notice went up on the works-office hoarding on Saturday the eighth of next month at ten past nine in the morning, on the same hoarding as the plate notice, in the same typeface, under the same head, and Milo Rook read it standing up because he has learned that in this district you read things standing up and then you walk away from them before you decide what they mean.

**DISTRICT WORKS — NOTICE TO THE PUBLIC**
*Low streets reach, second stage. In order that the final reach of the withdrawn alignment at the low end may be brought to a schedule level and certified on ground that has finished settling, the coal hatch side access, from the ground-floor door of the temporary accommodation at the foot of the works to the works gate, will be closed to the public from the ninth of this month to the nineteenth of this month inclusive.*

*Access to the temporary accommodation will be by the path to the north. The path to the north is not a public route.*

*No alternative provision is made. Signed for the works: Mr R. Rennick, site manager.*

Milo Rook read the last line twice and then read the whole thing a third time, standing in the rain by a hoarding, and the two things he had in his hands were the second-last line and the second line, and the second-last line said the north path is not a public route, and the second line said eleven days, and the fourth Thursday of an instrument of a Bench was the thirteenth of this month, and the coal hatch door was the only door anybody had stood at four times in a month.

He wrote the time on the back of his hand and rubbed it out and wrote it properly later, and what he wrote was: *Eighth of the month, 09.25. Eleven days. The fourth Thursday is inside them. I have not said one word to anybody about this and I am going to go and see a man who is a great deal better at this than I am before I do.*

---

Orrin Sable was in the trench at the low end at half past ten that morning with the second gang, and he was in it deliberately, which he has not done once in eleven weeks and which cost him a coat and a pair of boots he bought in the last two days.

Danner was on half rate and under a review until the review, which has no date, and the last reach of the alignment — the reach that crosses the reach the district's own record says is a two-foot-six culvert laid in 1904 and which is in fact a lead lateral the size of a man's wrist, behind a works' dead and valved storm main — could not be brought to a schedule level and certified by anybody but a foreman of the works, and the ground under it was booked to take about two further feet of settlement across the reach **before the first of this month and then stop, and it stopped on the first, and the floodgate test is on the sixth, and the four days between those two dates are the whole of the margin there is.**

"A level is not a line," said Danner, from the bottom, with water in his boots. "A line is where you put it. A level is a thing that is true this afternoon and next month, and the whole of it is whether the ground underneath has finished, and it has not finished, and it is booked to finish on the first of next month, and a level laid on Thursday the first is out by two foot by Christmas, and a level laid after the sixth of next month is not a level at all, it is a line, because the floodgate test is on the sixth and the test wants a made surface on its flood side, and the window is the second, the third, the fourth and the fifth of next month. Four days and I will have put a foreman's name on a thing that is wrong by Christmas, and I have already told this district once this month that I cannot stand behind a level over a trench with four feet in it, and I am not going to be able to and I am going to have to do it anyway."

"Mr Rennick has asked you to come back," said Milo Rook, from the top of the trench, which he was allowed to be at and had asked in writing for on the Friday.

"Mr Rennick has asked me to come back at full rate for eleven days, which resets the review, and which means that in my file there is no longer a fortnight of half rate and a man in a good coat will say that I was stood down and came back, and there is a word for that and the word is *reinstated* and it is a soft word and it is not true." He got out of the trench and took a long time at it. "And I am going to do it, and I have written on the form that I am doing it for the certificate and not for the money, and Mr Rennick has initialled it, and Mr Rennick has said that is the first sensible thing a foreman has written on one of his forms in two years and has put it in his employer's file."

Petts was in the trench with him and came out second, and he is the man who said *nobody told us* at six o'clock on the twelfth, and he has not stopped saying it, and nobody has ever asked him to stop, and Milo Rook has not asked him to stop, and has noted that he has not asked him to stop.

---

They went up the ramp together at about ten past twelve, which is not something that happens, and it happened because the first gang had gone for the hour and the second gang were eating on the fill and Orrin Sable said, without turning round, "Twenty minutes, and you can ask me the thing."

"The path is closed for eleven days and the fourth Thursday is inside it."

"Yes."

"You put it up yesterday at ten past nine on a Saturday on the same hoarding as the plate."

"I put it up this morning at ten past nine on the same hoarding as the plate, and I put it up on a Saturday, and I want both of those facts in whatever you write, because in three weeks somebody is going to say I did it on a Friday afternoon after something happened in a yard and it will be easier for both of us if the sheet says Saturday at ten past nine." He came down the ramp. "And I am going to tell you why the path is shut, and then you are going to tell me whether you can think of a way round it, and I do not expect that you can, and I would like it noted that I asked."

He said the whole of it in about two hundred words and Milo Rook wrote none of it down while he was saying it, which is a thing he has never done before and which he was aware of doing.

"The ground under the last reach is eleven years of river silt with a lens in it about twenty foot across, and it has settled nine inches over forty feet since August and two foot more since the third of this month, and the book said two foot more across the reach before the first of this month and then it stops, and the book was right and it stopped on Monday, and I have got a man who can tell you why. A level is no use to anybody on ground that is still going down, and the floodgate test is on the sixth of next month, and the test wants a made surface on its flood side, and it is the only thing in this city that has ever tested whether the wall behind the district can take a river. The ground stops on the first, the certificate has to be made on ground that has stopped, and the test is on the sixth, and that leaves me the second, the third, the fourth and the fifth. Four days, and the laying of it means digging the trench through the path, and the trench is four foot down and about eight foot wide, and there is no way to lay a trench under a public path while people are walking on it, and if you shunt the path you shunt it onto the north side, and the north side has a twelve-inch fall over nine feet and it goes under the embankment in two places and it is not a path, it is a place where the fill goes." He stopped at the bottom of the ramp. "And the closing of the coal hatch side is not a measure about the district. It is not anybody punishing the district. It is that a trench four foot deep through the only made ground on that reach is a hole, and there are about nine people in that building who cannot use the north path at all, and one of them is sixty and one of them is a woman of sixty and one of them is a woman of sixty-three with a cellar, and I know that because your Recovery officer told me a number out loud in a yard and I have written it down."

"And the pumps," said Milo Rook. "The three you put on Bridge Street."

"Three pumps on Bridge Street, which is not my street, and I have no drainage division and no rateable and no schedule, and I took a man off the eastern works on the seventeenth of this month to run one of them, and there is one more at the Terrace and one at Sazur Row, and the gully is coming up on the low end for the eleventh day in a row in a month with under an inch of rain in it, and the district flood line is nine inches and we were at six on the sixteenth and the water is not going to go anywhere until the ground under this reach stops moving, and that is what I have, and I have said in a yard that the number is the only part of this I could do anything about and that was the truth and it is still the truth and the number has got me five pumps and a certificate and a hole in a path."

They stood at the bottom of the ramp.

"You did not shut that path because of the four Thursdays," said Milo Rook.

"No," said Orrin Sable. "I did not. I shut it because the ground under the last reach is going to stop moving in about three weeks and the only time to certify a level on it is before it stops. And I want you to understand that I read the minute of the twenty-first of this month when I read it, and I know that the named person stands at a door on the coal hatch side on the sixth and the thirteenth of this month, and I did not think about it, and I have thought about it since half past five this morning, and I have decided that I am not going to move the date, and I am telling you that I decided not to, and you may write that down, and it does me no good whatever."

---

The yard at half past one on the Sunday the ninth of next month had about forty people in it, which is the largest it has been since the eleventh, and they were in it at half past one in the afternoon instead of six in the evening, and almost none of them knew why they had come, and the word *November* was said in that yard eleven times by Milo Rook's count, which he did not make in the yard and made afterwards and admitted to Rue and was told off for.

Lena Marr heard the number before anybody else because the path was shut, so everybody came round the front, and the trestle table was out because the round was in at eight and there is nothing to do with a yard in the rain but stand at a table.

"Eleven days," she said. "The thirteenth is the fourth Thursday. That is the door. That is the only door in this that anybody has stood at four times, and on the ninth it shuts, and on the twentieth it opens, and the instrument is finished at noon on Friday the fourteenth."

Nobody said anything for a while, and the rain came off the portacabin roof in a rope.

"Right," said Lena Marr. "Well. That is what it is. I am not going to stand at a door that is not there. And I am not going to ask a foreman for a door, because the day I ask a foreman for a door is the day this is a work, and it is not a work." She put her hand on the table. "And I am not going to say it was a coincidence either, because Orrin Sable was in that yard on the sixteenth and put three pumps on a street he is not responsible for and told us the number was the only part of this he could do anything about, and I have been thinking for three days about a man who says out loud the one thing he can do."

"Then what," said Rue.

"I do not know," said Lena Marr. "I have said *I do not know* in this yard twice now and I am going to say it a third time and then I am going to stop saying it in this yard and start saying it in kitchens where it is a smaller thing."

---

The thing that came out of the Sunday, and it came out of the works, not the yard, and it was on the Thursday and it was worth more than anything anybody said in the rain, was this.

Danner came down the ramp at half past five on the Thursday the tenth with a form in his hand and put it on the trestle table in the porch, face up, and it was a variation of the second stage, and the box for the hours was filled in his own hand, and the box that said *alternative considered* was filled in his own hand because he will not leave a space on a district form, and the two lines he wrote in it were the whole of the eleventh of next month and they read:

*Reach brought to schedule level over the soft lens reach on or before the fifth of this month, and the level certified on ground recorded as settled on the first of this month. No alternative available in the period. Certifying officer: T. Danner, foreman, district flood stage. I record that the certification is made in the fourteenth day of a standard deviation in the readings at the trench, and that I will not be able to say in a room that I stood behind it, and I am certifying it because the level has to be certified by a man and the only man is me.*

"There," he said. "That is my version of what you lot call a document that cannot be corrected from inside itself, and I have written the untrue half of it in the box that only takes it, and if somebody comes at me in a room they can come at that line, and I have put it there so that they come at that line and not at the district."

"Do not do that," said Milo Rook.

"It is done, it is dated, it is initialled, and the office has it, and I could not leave a space and I could not leave the half that matters, and I have not done it for you and I have not done it because you are all standing there, and if any of you had been standing there on the sixteenth I would have written the same thing and stayed on half rate for the rest of it." He squared the form. "And I am going to tell you the only thing I have got, which is that on the twenty-eighth I put a three-foot spirit level across a reach with two feet of settling under it and the bubble in the middle, and the joint finding of the twentieth of this month says a surface was made level across a place which had been two surfaces for six years, and that finding went to a Bench on the twenty-first of October and sat in the file there, and the paragraph is true, and the paragraph is about me."
