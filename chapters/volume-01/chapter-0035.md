# Chapter 35: A Minor Seam

Cass Ardery came out of the gate at ten past two on Saturday the eleventh of the month with a folder he did not open, and he had the manner of a man who has been somewhere unpleasant on purpose and has got a good report out of it.

"Saturday," said Danner.

"It's a report, Mr Danner, it doesn't care what day it is."

"It's a report that puts a classification on a site I've got a machine on, and there's nine men on this compound who'll read it before the district does, so yes, it does care what day it is."

Milo Rook was in the trench at the low end with a rod and a boy holding a tape when the two of them came down the ramp, and he came up out of it with his knees going, and Cass Ardery waited for him, which was decent of him, and then handed him the folder, which was also decent, and which Milo Rook did not want.

**SURVEY ENFORCEMENT — FIELD REPORT**
*Site: withdrawn alignment, service court and works footprint, river core, district reference as ordered.*
*Date of inspection: 9th. Date of report: 11th.*

*Supports. Public name: withdrawn from the street index and the district name schedule, six years, no variation recorded. Ordinary routes: closed to all service access from the first working day following the timetable review of the 28th of Sazur, in force. Witness: none maintained.*

*Findings. The site supports at most one service and cannot maintain its own witness. No public route is offered. No person has a standing duty at the site. No rota, schedule, roster, or return arrangement is maintained at the site by the district, by the works, or by any party having authority in this matter. The residual alignment is being brought to a schedule level under an eleven-week regrade and is not a public route.*

*Persons affected: nil.*

*Determination: minor seam, unstable, not a public route, no service maintained. Classification threshold for further public interest is not met.*

*Recommendation: continued withdrawal. No further public interest requiring enquiry. No variation required to the order in force.*

*Signed: C. Ardery, Survey Enforcement.*

Milo Rook read it twice on the flat ground above the low end, standing up, in the wind, and then he read the fourth finding a third time because the fourth finding was the one that had taken the whole of the room in August.

*No rota, schedule, roster, or return arrangement is maintained at the site by the district, by the works, or by any party having authority in this matter.*

"They're right," he said.

"They are not," said Danner.

"They are right, and they are right in the way paragraph six is right, and I want you both to hear the shape of it because it is the same shape and I have been waiting since the twenty-eighth of Sazur for somebody to put it in a different document." He gave them the book out of his coat and found the page, which was the page with six words on it from the Friday, and then found the earlier page, which was the one from August.

"The works' own report, six years ago, first week. Paragraph six. The three calls are not a signal to persons and indicate no entry and no refusal." He shut it. "That is a true sentence. It is a true sentence about a bell, made in the first week by an engineer about a sequence, and it governed this case for six years, and nobody could correct it from inside the report, because there is no box on a safety report for *this is a bell and also a person*."

"Milo," said Danner.

"No, listen, because this one has got a box on it and the box is worse." He turned the folder round. "It says no rota is maintained by the district, by the works, or by any party having authority in this matter. Not one word of that is untrue. There is a rota. It is a sheet of accountancy paper folded in three in a satchel on a courier round and it has five lines and five names, four people behind four of those names, and a fifth line with a name on it and nobody behind it, and a promise at the foot that anyone can cross their own name off and nobody else crosses it for them. And there is no party in this district with authority over that sheet, and there could not be, because the whole design of it is that a landlord cannot ask about it and a clerk cannot inspect it and the day somebody with authority can see it, it stops working, and everybody who has ever used it knew that before the district did."

"So the report is a lie," said Cass Ardery.

"No. The report is a true statement about the supports, made in a form that cannot be corrected from inside the form, by an officer who has spent two days on a site and has asked the right three questions and cannot get an answer to the fourth because the fourth is not a question anybody with a badge is allowed to ask." He handed the folder back. "And I have been in this district six weeks learning that this is the only way this city loses a street, and it is not with a sword. It is with a true sentence in a box."

---

The yard at six o'clock that evening had about thirty-five people in it, and nobody had called it, and Rue had made no notice of any kind, and what happened was that the courier round goes out at eight and comes in at four and everybody in Cedar Court knows what time the works shift changes at the pump-house gantry, because it has been six o'clock every evening since before the closure and it is the only fixed thing in the district.

"It goes at ten to two," said Rue, to nobody in particular. "It has gone at ten to two for forty-five years and it is a shift change and the whole district knows it, and about thirty of us are stood in this yard at six o'clock on a Saturday for no reason at all."

Milo Rook had a watch and did not need one.

The call came up off the water out of the canyon the way it always did, because of the height of the frontage wall, and it was the sound of a rope and a hammer and a bracket, and thirty-five people in a yard in a converted pump works heard it arrive.

It was not a mystery and it was not dramatic. The drill on the far side of the wall stopped, and the winch stopped, and two men in the trench thirty feet away came up the ladder and stood on the flat ground, and a man in a cab with the window down put his hand down, and about nine seconds later everything started again.

Nine seconds. Jo Lask had said eleven and Rue had said nine and the yard did the arithmetic out loud and got it wrong twice, and a woman of about seventy on the second-floor landing said it was eight and had said it was eight when she was young, and the yard let her have it because she had been on that landing when the three calls went at ten to two and she had heard the interval from inside the building.

"Eight," said Milo Rook. "And it is between eight and eleven, and the difference is the plant, and the plant is closer now than it was, and that is the only reason the number has moved in thirty years and it will move again when they take the works out, and I am not going to write any of this down because a thing that changes when you measure it is a thing that is not a measurement."

He looked round the yard.

"That is the witness," he said. "That is what a witness is, and I have been trying to write one down for four months and it is nine seconds of a works crew putting their hands down because a bell went, and there is no box anywhere in this city that it will go in. Ardery has written *no rota, schedule, roster, or return arrangement is maintained* and he is right and he is also writing down that there is nobody there, and there are thirty-five people here who have just watched a machine stop for eight or nine seconds in a district where a man has put a steel plate on the low streets, and the man who wrote the report was in a trench for twenty minutes on Thursday and nobody told him about the bell because nobody has ever thought of telling anybody about the bell."

Cass Ardery, who had stayed, said: "They put their hands down because that is a shift change. I have worked on a works. You stop when a bell goes and you start when the bell has finished going. It is not a decision. It is a habit."

"Yes," said Milo Rook. "It is a habit. It has been a habit for six years in this district and the fourth finding of your report says there is none, and I am not being unkind to you, and I would like you to understand that the sentence you have written is the exact one that was written about those three calls six years ago and that the sentence survived the replacement page and that I have spent four months trying to find out who took out the other one."

The yard was quiet, and thirty-five people were in it, and the machine went on the other side of the wall, and the bell had gone at ten to two in the afternoon a hundred times in six years in a canyon with nobody at the far end of it, and the third call had been sounded into a district full of people every evening at six o'clock, and the third call was a check, and a check is a person saying a space is clear, and the machine had just answered it, and the machine is a thing and does not know it and nobody asked it to.

Rue did not write anything down either. Milo Rook noticed that and did not mention it.

At about a quarter past six, in the porch, a woman nobody was looking at said, "It's a shift change, and I'll tell you what else it is."

Mara Quill had been in the yard for an hour and eleven minutes without saying anything, which was the longest she had been in it.

"I am not going to argue with the plant," she said. "I am going to say one thing about a document and then I am going to go home, because I have a review on my own hand on the third of next month and I have not prepared for it and that is my own fault and nobody else's.

"Mr Ardery's fourth finding is the sentence that will decide this case in October and it will decide it without anybody choosing to decide it, because a Bench takes a report and a report says *no person has a standing duty at the site* and then a Bench has to decide what happens to a place where nobody has a standing duty, and the answer is always the same, and the answer is that the order stands because there is nobody to say otherwise. That is how a street stays closed. Not by a decision. By a sentence nobody can correct."

"And what is the correction," said Cass Ardery.

"I do not know," said Mara Quill, "and I have wanted to say those four words in this yard for four months, and it is a strange thing to want. The correction is not *the rota exists*. The rota existing is a sentence about a sheet. The correction is a document that says a person is there, in terms anybody in a Bench can act on, on the authority of a person who can be cross-examined on it. And there is not one in this district and I have looked, and the reason there is not one is a promise at the foot of a piece of accountancy paper that says nobody else crosses your name off, and that promise is doing more civic work than any document I have filed since I was twenty-six years old, and I would like somebody to write that in a report somewhere, and I am not going to, because the moment it is in a report the promise has an authority over it and stops working."

She picked up her bag.

"So in October there will be a report that says nobody is there," she said, "and there will be four accounts that say a great deal was, and one mechanic who will not put his name to his own because he has got four years of a pension, and the thing that decided the sixth of six years ago will decide it again on the twenty-first of next month, and it will decide it in about eleven seconds."

Milo Rook said, "The difference is that the sixth time there were forty-one people in a court and this time there is a plate going on tomorrow at ten past six in the morning."

"Yes," said Mara Quill. "And the plate is not what I am frightened of. I am frightened of the twenty-first of next month, and I have got ten days, and I do not know what to put in front of a Bench, and none of the four of us has any idea what to put in front of a Bench."

---

A card came through the round on the Monday morning the thirteenth, in Rue's hand on the back of a delivery docket, and it was from a boy of seventeen who was in a fenland village with no official address and had been there since the twenty-eighth of Sazur.

It said one thing and then it said it four times in four different ways, which was the most like him anybody had ever seen.

*Ostrow asked me who keeps a road that is not on a map.*
*I said the people who live on it.*
*He said that is what the list says, and he has had the list a long time, and that is the part I cannot get past either.*
*Do not tell me what to do with this. I have not written his name on anything and I am not going to. — P.*

Rue read it over Milo Rook's shoulder at the trestle table and did not comment on it, and Milo Rook put it in the pocket of the Field Book's cover, unfolded, which was where he kept the things that were not evidence and were not nothing, and which was the subject of a conversation he had promised to have with himself and had not yet had.
