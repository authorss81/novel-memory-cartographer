# Chapter 51: Twenty-One Days

Orrin Sable announced it on Sunday the sixteenth of the month at about eleven in the morning, in a yard, standing on a trestle table, with a sheet of paper in his hand and about sixty people in front of him, and he did it three weeks early, out loud, on purpose, and he said so before he said anything else.

"I am going to do this now," he said, "and I want the first thing out of my mouth to be why I am doing it now, and I want it written down that I said why. I am doing it now because I have learned something in this district that I did not know in October, and it is that in this district a thing that is said late is a thing that is suspected. So I am saying it early and I am saying it where you can all hear it, and if it goes wrong in three weeks you will not be able to say I did it quietly, and I will not be able to say it quietly, and that is worth more to me than being liked, and I have not been liked once in seven weeks."

The sheet was a works notice. It had the employer's head on it and the same typeface as the plate notice and the low-streets notice, and it went up on the same hoarding at about half past eleven, and Milo Rook read it twice, standing up, in the rain, and then read the whole thing a third time because there were three paragraphs and the middle one had a number in it.

**DISTRICT WORKS — NOTICE TO THE PUBLIC**
*Low streets reach, second stage, final test. The final test of the reach flood-control installation will be carried out on Saturday the sixth of next month. The reach will be brought to a schedule level over the soft-lens reach between the second and the fifth of that month inclusive, and the level will be certified on ground recorded as settled on the first of that month. Access to the works compound for the test will be by the north path only.*

*The following will be in force on Line 7 from the first of next month, under the authority's own amendment and not under any instruction of the district: the Alder Quay–Bridge Street loop is withdrawn and is not reinstated. The diversion is permanent. The running time at the Ferry Road turn, restored to the journey on the sheet of the first of the current month, is withdrawn again, and nine seconds are taken out at the Alder Quay interchange, which is to remain on a through running time while the reach is made level beneath it. The count at the Ferry Road turn and the count at the Alder Quay interchange are both automatic. There is no staff turn at either.*

*No alternative provision is made. Signed for the works: Mr R. Rennick, site manager. Entered under the standing instruction of the office of the Prefect, ref. 11/D/6.*

That last line is the one Milo Rook read four times. He is not supposed to know what a Prefect's reference looks like, and he does, because he has spent six weeks reading the front of documents, and because Orrin Sable had told him in the first week that there was a standing instruction behind all of it from a desk that had never sent anybody to the district and never would.

---

"Twenty-one days," said Lena Marr.

"Twenty-one days," said Orrin Sable, from the table. "And I am going to say the rest of it now so that nobody has to ask me for it, and then I am going to get off this table and go and be an engineer, because that is what I am paid for and I have been doing this for eight minutes now and it is the longest I have spoken to more than four people in six years."

He said the rest of it in about two hundred words and Milo Rook wrote none of it down while it was being said, which is the second time in eleven weeks.

"The ground under the last reach is eleven years of river silt with a lens in it about twenty foot across. It is booked to take about two further feet and then stop, and it stops on Monday the first of next month, and the book has been right twice. A level is no use to anybody on ground that is still going down. The final reach can be laid, levelled, proved and certified on the second, the third, the fourth and the fifth, and on no other four days in this calendar, and the trench for it is four foot deep and about eight foot wide and goes under the only made ground on that reach. The floodgate test is on the sixth because the test wants a made surface on its flood side, and the test is the only thing in this city that has ever tested whether the wall behind this district can take a river. And the gate at the western culvert comes out to full flow at about twenty past six that morning, because that is what the test does, and the gate has been wound down to eighteen inches of sill since the sixth of Sazur because a full seal would back water into my own works zone, and everybody in this yard has heard the wrong version of that for six weeks and I would like it correct before I start."

Nobody said anything for about four seconds.

"You are winding it out," said Rue.

"I am driving it out to full flow for six hours and winding it back to a working aperture afterwards, and there is a form for that and I will fill it in and you can have a copy of it," said Orrin Sable. "It is not sealed. It has never been sealed. A man in this district has been telling you it was sealed since the sixth of Sazur and he has been doing it to make my case easier and I would be obliged if he would stop."

"Nothing has been making your case easier," said Milo Rook.

"Then I withdraw the remark and I would like it noted that I withdrew it in front of sixty people and not in a corridor," said Orrin Sable, and got down off the table, and did not shake anybody's hand, and went.

---

Barrow came on foot on the Monday morning of the seventeenth, at about a quarter past ten, with the authority's sheet under his arm in the cardboard cover he makes out of a district envelope, and he came to the yard rather than to the works office, which he has never done, and Rue made him stand at the trestle table because it was raining and there was nowhere else.

"Two things," he said, "and then I am going back to the division, and I have asked for this to be minuted and I have written the minute myself and I have brought two copies, and Mr Quill can have one."

He put one sheet of paper on the table. It was not a timetable. It was a minute, headed with the authority's own division mark, in Barrow's hand, and it was four lines long.

> *Noted: district works notice of the sixteenth of the current month, final test Saturday the sixth of the month following.*
> *Noted: Line 7 amendment, first of the month following, diversion permanent, loop withdrawn and not reinstated, nine seconds withdrawn at the Alder Quay interchange, no staff turn at the Ferry Road turn or the interchange, count automatic at both.*
> *Noted: a temporary arrangement is not a reason to vary an order, in advance, in a file, and that having been said in advance it is very difficult to unwrite.*
> *Noted: the district states that the count is a person and that a signal is not a person. I record that the authority's position is that a count is a function of the timetable division. I have minuted the district's position as stated, and not adopted it, and there is a difference.*

"There," said Barrow. "That is the whole of what a timetable clerk can do and I have done all of it in four lines so that there is no room in it for a manager to write a paragraph later. You have your date. You have the nine seconds. You have the two boxes with no person in them. And you have my division saying, in writing, on a piece of paper in its own hand, that a temporary arrangement is not a reason to vary an order, and I have put that in because it is true and because I would rather it were in my file than in yours."

"You have minuted a sentence against yourself," said Mara Quill, who had come down for it and who had a raincoat on and a folder under her arm.

"I have minuted a sentence that is true," said Barrow. "It is not against me. It is against everyone, and it is against me most, because I am the man who can minute it and I am the man who wrote the plate variation, and if a person in Crown Heights ever wants to know whether this district's four Thursdays were a reason to vary an order, they will not have to ask the district. They will read it in my hand on a sheet with my division's mark on it, and it will say *in advance*, which is the two worst words in that sentence, and I put them in because they were true and I am not going to be a man who has only written the parts that were flattering."

He squared the two copies.

"And there is a third thing and I am telling you because you would find it out in a week and I would rather you had it in advance, which is the entire subject of today's minute." He put his hand flat on the table, not on the paper. "On the twenty-ninth of the month a division in Crown Heights refused an application on the Filewell precedent, in four paragraphs, and in the fourth paragraph it notes, among the matters considered, that a Bench minute of the twenty-first permits one named person to walk a threshold on four dates, and that the department is aware that no similar arrangement is proposed after the fourteenth of this month. It is in a file. It does not grant anything. It can be produced by anybody in that building with a form, and it is now a fact about this district that the district's own last legal arrangement has expired, in a department's own hand, and that the department wrote down that it knew."

Nobody spoke.

"So when you go into a room, which I assume you are going to do about eleven times between now and then," said Barrow, "the first thing you will be asked is not about the sixth of December. It will be about the fourteenth of this month, and the answer is going to have to be in the room with you before somebody else puts it there, and I have just spent nineteen years of my life learning that the answer you have not brought with you is the one that gets minuted."

He gave one copy to Advocate Quill and one to Lena Marr and put the cover back under his arm and walked out into the rain without an umbrella, and the yard was quiet for a while afterwards.

---

The decision was made that afternoon in about nine minutes, and Milo Rook was the worst man in the district about it, and he has written that down four times in four places since.

The four Thursdays had expired at noon on the fourteenth, in front of nine people, and there had been nothing on the fifteenth, and the district had spent the weekend discovering that a sheet of paper with a contractor's head on it had a man's name in the space at the foot of it, and the coal hatch path had come back open on the Thursday the twentieth at about ten in the morning without anybody having made a decision about it, and on the Sunday morning the seventeenth, with a date and a timetable in the same hour, the yard's first question was the only question anybody could have asked.

Lena Marr had a card out. She had had it out since about eleven.

"Three things can be done in a room and I am going to read them out and then I am going to say what I think of each one, and then nobody is to say anything clever for ten minutes."

"Go on," said Rue.

"One. Ask for a fifth Thursday." Lena Marr put the card down. "No. A fifth Thursday is a form. It is a form with a start and an end on it and the end is the thing that kills it, and I am not going to be the woman who spends the district's last good hour on a document that dies on a date somebody in Crown Heights picks out of a diary."

"Two."

"Two. Go to the Bench before the sixth and say the word." She put her thumb on the card. "No, and this is the hard one. If we go in now and say the word, then for the rest of this case the district is the people who said the word, and the department will find the classification in the file in about ninety seconds, because it says *persons affected: nil* and it cannot be excluded, and it was made by a competent officer about a matter he was competent to classify, and after that the only two sentences anybody hears are ours and his. And after the sixth it is not a claim, it is an event, with witnesses and a time on it. I am not saying do not go. I am saying go after, and I am not going to spend the district's one good afternoon on the alternative."

"And three."

"Three is the count. The two boxes with no staff turn in them. Jo Lask has told us twice he will not be the man in a box and he was right both times and I have stopped arguing. But that is not the same as not wanting the count, and a count is a person saying a number to a person, and if the count is a machine then something in this district is going to have to be a machine, and every one of us in this yard has spent six weeks building things so that no one of us has to be."

Nobody argued with her, and Milo Rook did not say anything at all, which is not his habit, and Rue said one sentence to him afterwards at the gate.

"You are not going to ask for a passage," she said.

"I am not going to ask for a passage."

"Nobody has asked you for one and you have been waiting eleven weeks for somebody to ask you for one, and it is not going to happen, and I want to say it to you now in the rain while there are two of us, because in about three weeks there are going to be forty people in this yard telling each other the surveyor got it done." She put the satchel over her shoulder. "And on the fourth of Sazur you got it done, and on the sixth of that month you got it done, and on the eighteenth and the twenty-first and the twenty-sixth you got it done, and on the tenth of last month you stood under an awning with your hands empty and the door was shut, and the reason you did not go in that day was not a rule and it was not a woman with a clipboard. It was that there was nobody at a door. And on the sixth of December there is going to be a works gang and a foreman and a district officer and a tram and about four hundred people on a tram platform, and there is not going to be one person at a door who has agreed to be there, and that is going to be the last time the thing is open and everybody in it will be busy, and I am telling you now so that you cannot say afterwards that nobody put it in writing."

He did not answer that and she did not wait, and it is on the page because Rue put it in words and Milo Rook wrote it down the same night and did not argue with any of it.

---

Mara Quill got about four hours out of the Sunday and used all of them, and what she produced on Monday the seventeenth at about nine in the evening, in a front room above a chandler's, with about eleven people in it and a table made of two boards, was not a document and was not a plan, and she said so in the first four seconds.

"This is not a minute. I want to be extremely clear about that, because I have made four things in this case that are minutes and every one of them has ended up in a file in Crown Heights described as an arrangement, and the word *arrangement* is how a district gets to be a matter considered. So this is a list, in my hand, of what will be asked of me in a room if I go in on the eighth of December, and there are six things on it and I cannot do any of them on the day."

She read it out.

"One. Speak for a household. I cannot. There are about forty people in that building and I know four of them by name and one of them by a floor, and if I stand up in a room and say *I know these people*, a good advocate on the other side of that room will ask me how, and the answer is that I know their arithmetic, and that is the end of me.

"Two. Produce the sheet. I will not. It is not a record, it has a contractor's head on it, it has a man's name in a space in one of five copies that was not his, and it is held by two women who do not read it. I will say in a room that I know of no residents' document, which is the sentence I said I would say in this district on the twenty-ninth and I am going to keep saying it until somebody makes me stop.

"Three. Call a witness." She put her hand flat on the table. "Three. Jo Lask on the count. Rue on the rota and the round, who will come and will not be thanked and will not be asked to hold anything overnight. And the accounts: three sworn and one unsworn, and the unsworn is a mechanic's sentence that is true and he will not put his name to it, and I am going to call it as a document and not as a man, because that is the only way it can be in a room without costing him four years of a pension.

"Four. The order's safety case. That I can do, and it is the easiest thing on the list, and it is going to be the hardest thing to do in three weeks because the argument against this order is the same argument I would make if the order were right.

"Five. The four Thursdays, and their expiry. And on that one, Mr Barrow has this morning saved me about four days by putting *in advance* in a minute in his own hand, and I want him thanked in a yard and not in a document, because if I thank him in a document then it is a document.

"Six. The dead man. Jonas Venn, one loss recorded, no cause, no next of kin, and a replacement page that was filed eleven days after the report it replaced, and a sentence about *no evidence was found of any hazard to persons at the service court* that is not in the file any more. That is the thing the order was built on and I cannot get at it, because a record nobody can produce is a record nobody has to answer for, and the department has now told us in writing on the twenty-ninth of last month that it is aware we have nothing, and the district has supplied that to them."

"And on the sixth of December," said Lena Marr, "what happens to six."

"On the sixth of December there is going to be an event, and an event makes a record that has to be written down by somebody, and if I am standing in front of the only record anybody has of a man who died in a pump house six years ago, then the record gets written, and it gets written in a room I am in. That is why we are going on the eighth of December and not on the sixteenth of November. I am not going to let a department take a closure notice off a page in a fortnight. I am going to wait until it is in front of a room on a day when a man died in it."

Nobody in the room argued with her, and two people afterwards said the thing she had not said, which is that a public safety appeal in this city is answered inside a fixed number of days, that the answer can be a hearing or it can be a refusal, and that a refusal is a document, and that she would then have both.

---

The Field Book was silent on the sixteenth and on the seventeenth and on the eighteenth, and he wrote the three dates down in the back and did not pair them, because a silence of three days with a second silence of three days beside it is four dates and not a pair, and he has been caught building a set to match.

The Lark token was cold on the seventeenth, in his coat pocket, with his hand on it for about a minute in a doorway, and he did not take it out.

And the thing he carried out of the seventeenth and wrote down at twenty past eleven that night, in about four lines, in his own hand, is this:

*Seventeenth of the month, 23.20. A man announced a date twenty-one days early, out loud, on purpose, and told sixty people why, and the reason was that in this district a thing said late is a thing suspected. I have written that down because it is the second time that man has been right about something that has nothing to do with engineering, and I am not going to be able to use that, and it is the first thing in eleven weeks that has made me feel that I have been in the wrong trade.*

*Twenty-one days. A four-day window on the second, the third, the fourth and the fifth, and a foreman who has to certify a level on ground that stops moving on the first, and nine seconds taken out of a timetable at a place where the ground under it has been made level, and two boxes with no person in either of them, and a minute in a clerk's hand saying in advance, and a file in Crown Heights with our own expiry in it in four paragraphs.*

*And the district has decided, in nine minutes, on my account and not hers, to go to a room on the eighth of December instead of today, and Rue has told me in the rain that nobody is going to ask me for a passage, and that on the sixth there is not going to be one person at a door who has agreed to be there, and I have written it down and I am not going to argue with her about it, and I am not going to be able to do anything about it on the sixth either, and those are two separate facts and I have kept them in two separate lines for the first time in six weeks.*
