# Chapter 27: The Second Reference

Rue announced it in the yard on the Monday morning of the twenty-ninth of Sazur at eight o'clock, with the list on the trestle table, and there were about fourteen people in it and eleven of them were about to go to work at the works gate.

"Seven," she said. "Not five. The round goes in on foot through the works gate on the shift change, from the gate to the old painted wall and back, and it picks up three and four on the way, and it takes twenty-two minutes each way, and it means I cannot do the ground and the second on a Wednesday, and Idar has the Wednesday, so that works.

"That is eleven weeks. I have costed it and it is eleven weeks on a Sunday and eleven weeks on a Thursday, which are the two days I was going to have, and it is eleven weeks in the rain on a made path with a machine on it, and it is unpaid, because the round is unpaid and always has been. And if the gate-man is on a break then the round does not go, and if the round does not go then a woman who is expecting a letter does not get it, and that is the same as the last eleven years and I have done it every week, and I am not doing it from the outside any more."

"You can't do that eleven weeks," said Lena Marr.

"I have done two of four for eleven years," said Rue. "That is what two of four is. You are all waiting for me to say it is a problem and it is not a problem, it is the arrangement, and the arrangement is thinner than it was and I am going to make it the same thickness by standing in a gate for twenty-two minutes at a time, and that is what a round is.

"And I am saying the price out loud because nobody else is going to." She put the pencil down. "Eleven weeks. Two hundred and thirty-one people in the low streets and a gully that comes up in a dry month and a dead storm main behind it, and a foreman who says the low streets flood in November, and a school place I cannot get anybody into, and eleven letters in a hole in a hoarding that came down yesterday morning and are not going anywhere. Eleven weeks and I am the one holding it. Say that back to me now so that I have said it in front of people and not only to a surveyor with a book."

Lena Marr said it back to her. Fourteen people heard it, and Milo Rook wrote none of it down, which was the correct thing to do and the hardest thing he did that month.

---

Mara Quill came back at half past four on the Monday with a folder and a face, and she came back into the corridor rather than the alcove because the alcove has a door and the corridor has not, and she put the folder on the counter and did not open it.

"I have the answer to the withdrawal," she said. "It has taken four days and it has cost me something and I am going to tell you the cost before I tell you the answer, because I have been thinking about the order of those two things for an hour and I have decided on the cost first, and I would like you to notice that I have decided that and to understand why."

"Tell me the cost."

"The reference on the index line is not a district reference." She turned the folder round without opening it. "It is a repository consultation reference. Crown Heights. A district continuity order is signed by a district officer and the district keeps its own book, and I have been in that book this afternoon, and the reference on page nine is not in it, and it never was.

"So I put the question. I put it in the only form in which the Registry will answer a question about an internal reference, and the form requires the applicant to state a personal interest in the file, in writing, in her own hand, and give a reason for the interest that is not the job."

"What did you write."

"I wrote that I have been working this file off my own hours for six years in a records alcove and have not told my review panel," said Mara Quill, "and that I consider that a conflict of interest and I am disclosing it, and I have signed it and dated it, and it went in at half past two this afternoon, and it is a document in my own name about my own conduct."

She looked up.

"Now. The answer. The reference is a repository consultation reference. Page nine of the Bellwether file was withdrawn on the instruction of a Crown Heights desk, eleven days after the date of the original safety report, and the desk is not a division of the Registry and is not in the Registry's own organisation, and the Registry cannot tell me whose it was, and has not refused, and will not."

"So there is a second reference in the same form as the consultation reference on the copy register. A second reference, on a different document. Not a second pair of marks — a pair is two marks in two hands on one page, and what I have got is a clerk's reference in one hand on a bound book and a clerk's reference in one hand on a file index, and I am being very careful this afternoon about which of those two things I am writing down."

"Yes," said Mara. "In the same form. Four letters and two numbers, and both of them entered by a clerk, and neither of them a name, and I have been sitting in a corridor since half past four turning that sentence over in my mouth and I cannot make it into anything that is not a coincidence, and I have promised myself for a month that I would not make a structure into a coincidence, and I have now got two of them and the second one is in a form that a clerk cannot decline to answer and cannot answer falsely, and the reason it is in that form is that somebody wrote the withdrawal line and the return line in the same week with the same pen in the same kind of hand."

"And the annotator is not—" said Milo, and stopped.

He had got as far as the first two letters of something on the twenty-ninth of Sazur in a corridor and had stopped, and the person he was talking to had heard him stop, and he had not been able to think about anything else for a day.

"Write it down or do not write it down," she said. "That is what the four weeks is for. But do not say half a name to me in a corridor at half past five and then stop, because I have been on the receiving end of half a thing in this case for six years and it is the only thing about the Registry that has ever frightened me."

"I did not say a name."

"You said a letter," said Mara, "and you stopped, and I am going to leave that exactly where it is, and I would like it recorded that I left it there, and I would like it recorded on a day when I have not just filed a disclosure about my own conduct, because on a day like this one everything anybody says gets a use."

---

They stood at the counter for a while. Down the corridor a clerk put a box down on a trolley and did not look at either of them.

"The thing about the cost," Milo said. "I want to say it once and then I am not going to say it again in a corridor for the rest of this case."

"I have not asked you for it."

"You have not." He put his hand flat on the folder and took it off it again. "I told you on the eighteenth of Sazur that this district is my mother's district, and you offered to file it, and I refused, and you wrote the refusal down with the date on it and made me sign it, and I have thought about that refusal every day since and I have not found a good reason for it once, and there is not going to be one.

"You have spent six days this month holding a disciplinary disclosure on arguments you have told me are bad, and the reason it got as far as a filing at half past two this afternoon instead of being handed over on the twentieth is me. That is the arithmetic and I have had a week to do it and it comes out where it comes out."

"Yes," said Mara Quill. "It does."

"So here is what I am going to do, and it is not an apology, and it is the whole of what I have." He took the pen out of his hand rather than the counter and looked at it as though it were the wrong instrument. "I am not going to ask you to hold anything else for me. Anything. From today, if I have a thing that bears on this file, it comes to you in writing, in my hand, with the date on it, whether you can file it or not and whether it helps me or not. You do not have to be able to use it. You have to be able to have it.

"And I want you to hear the shape of that, because I have had a month of learning it and I have only just got it. On the fifteenth of this month you could not work with a disclosure you were not allowed to file, and you were right, and I was wrong, and it took you a fortnight to work out what to do about it and the thing you did about it was to put a disclosure about yourself into a building that was reviewing you. I made that necessary. I would like you to be angry with me about it and not to let go of it for me, because if you let go of it I will have got away with it, and getting away with it is the whole of what I have been doing since the fourth of Sazur."

Mara Quill put both hands flat on the counter.

"No," she said.

"What?"

"You are not going to be angry with me about it, because I am not going to hold it, and I have thought about that too." She turned the folder a quarter turn and left her hand on it. "You refused to file it and you were not entitled to refuse it and you are not entitled now to feel badly about it in a corridor and hand it to me as a thing you have done for my benefit. I have made my filing. It is a bad filing in the sense that it is a filing I would rather not have made, and it is not bad, and I do not need you to be a man who is sorry about it.

"And I am not going to ask you for it," she said. "Not because I have decided not to. Because if I ask you for it you will give it to me inside a minute out of the thing you have just described, and it will be in a Registry file within an hour, because that is what a disclosure is. Lark Street would be in a building in Crown Heights for the rest of my working life, in a division I would have to keep walking past, and every clerk who ever indexed it would be doing a perfectly correct job, and that is precisely how a district goes to be lost. Not by a decision. By four hundred correct jobs.

"So I am going to keep not asking, and you are going to keep not offering it, and both of us are going to be perfectly correct about that for four weeks, and it is going to cost me something every single day of it, and I would like you to stop apologising and start being useful."

"Right," said Milo Rook.

"Also you have lost a route," said Mara. "You are holding that folder the way a man holds a thing when he does not know which end of the corridor he is standing in, and you have lost one since Friday because I have watched you walk into this corridor from the north end four days running and go round the wrong way, and if you do it once more this afternoon I am going to walk you out to the turnstiles myself and you are going to be humiliated."

---

On the Tuesday of the thirtieth of Sazur the plant came onto the old alignment at seven in the morning, and Milo Rook was at the works gate at ten past seven because he had asked in writing at ten past five and Danner had told him to come or not come and had said, honestly, that he did not care which.

He watched for about two hours and learned four things, and three of them were on the fourth column of the works' own site sheet in Danner's handwriting and the fourth was not on anything at all.

The fill moved. That was the first. A machine with a plate on the end took the silt off the top of the old alignment and the alignment went down about nine inches over the length of the court, and the ground under the fill went with it, and it went unevenly, so that by half past nine there was a new dip in the low end that had not been there on the twenty-second and that was deeper than the dip the handrail was standing over.

"Forty feet settled nine inches on the twenty-second," said Danner, with his hands behind his back. "And that was one river for four hours in the biggest of it. This is three passes with a plate and a machine's weight on it, and the fill is eleven years old and nobody put it in properly, and it is going to keep going for a month and then it will stop. And when it stops it will stop wherever it stops, and I will be the man who signed a level across it, and in ten years somebody will dig a trench for a water main and find out."

The second thing was that the court's own drainage had changed owner. With the diversion out of the alignment, the water that came down the old service court no longer went anywhere a person could see; it went into a new cut the works had made along the eastern side, into a pipe that was valved, into a storm main that had been dead since before Danner came to the district.

"That is the whole of the winter," he said. "We have taken a street that used to be a drain and made it a drain again, and the drain is ours, and ours is dead, and I have got a valve on it and I can shut the valve, and if I shut the valve then the court fills and the court is a hundred and forty metres from two hundred and thirty-one people, and if I don't shut the valve then it drains, and it is the only reason I did not sign a variation on a door on the twenty-second, and there it is again in a different coat."

The third thing was the plant noise, and it was not important, and Milo wrote it down because he had learned to write down the things that were not important.

The fourth thing was that the service court was empty, and that it was empty in a way it had not been empty on any previous day of the case, because on every previous day there had been something in it: a lamp, a marshal, a chalk line, a woman in a blue coat's door, a foreman's rod, a hundred and forty metres of people getting out of a building. On the thirtieth of Sazur there was a foot and a half of grey silt in ridges, three empty storage cages, a handrail four feet out of true over a dip, and a personnel door standing open eleven inches above the low point of an alignment that was being taken up by a machine, and there was nobody in it, and nobody had said they were going to be.

Milo got the book out at about eleven o'clock, standing on the flat ground above the court with the machine going, because he had a reason, which was that he wanted to know whether the seam was still answering at a time when the answer would be useful.

The page was clean.

He had been expecting a line, or a word, or something short and wrong and quotable, because it had come on the ninth minute of the drill on the twenty-first and it had come on the ninth minute of the crossing on the twenty-sixth, and it had come twice in twenty-three days and it was on a page, and the man on the page was on a made street a foot and a half down in silt with a machine on it and the entire structure coming out of his ear.

Nothing came.

He shut it and put it away and wrote it on the facing page, and it is four words long and it is the shortest line in the book for the whole of August.

*Nothing. Noted, not read.*

Then he wrote, underneath, the thing that had been standing in the back of his mind since the twenty-sixth and that he now understood he could not put off until October.

*What a lane does when the road is taken out from under it. Nobody in this district can answer that and I have asked two people this week and one of them is a physician and the other one is a foreman and neither of them pretended. Ada Fenn can answer it and will not, and has refused eleven times, and the twelfth would be the one that got her into a file, and I am not going to ask her the twelfth because a street that can only be answered by one person is a street with one person standing on it, and that is not a map. That is a rota with a face on it.*

*And the road is gone as of this morning and the face is sixty-eight.*
