# Chapter 59: Open the File

The complete file came out of the department's store on Saturday the thirteenth of next month at about eleven in the morning, on the second day after the order was opened for investigation, and the store does not open on Sundays so the Saturday was the first chance anybody had, and it is nine boxes, and it took two men and a barrow, and about forty people from the river core stood in a corridor in a building in Crown Heights and looked at it going past them on a trolley, which is not a procedure anybody sanctioned and which nobody stopped.

The death record was in the third box.

It is a card, and it is a card the size of a playing card, and it is on the district's own ledger for a pump-house second shift for the flood season of the sixth year back, and the entries on it are these, and every one of them is now on the page, and it has taken six years and about four people to get them into a room together.

> *VENN, J. — pump crew, 2nd shift — relocation due, pending.*
> *One loss recorded. (Reopened for investigation, the thirteenth of next month.)*
> *Cause of death: on reopening, **fatality, second-stage main, pump house, lower level.** Not entered in the original entry. The original entry carried no cause.*
> *Contributory condition entered on the original form, the first week, in the same hand as the report: *the frontage is at all times a service access and not a public route*.*
> *Next of kin: (original entry, blank) — (reopened, the thirteenth of next month) **WIFE, living. Address recorded.** The address is on the district's own index and has been on it for six years, and the woman has been at that address for six years, and no one has ever sent her a letter about any of it.*
> *Disposal: ground named on the authority's return, plot **numbered and not on the district's map**, in a ground whose register for that district **was not kept after the closure.** The return is in this box. It is the only thing there is and it is six words long and it has a plot on it.*

Nobody in the district wrote a name on a piece of paper for six years. The next-of-kin line was empty because the man who could have filled it in had sat on it, and everyone in this case has known that since the twenty-first of October, and it took a department's card index and a woman's own address to prove to a room that a man in this district had known where she was for six years and had said nothing, and it is on the page that the department's clerk established it in about four seconds by reading a card index, and that the clerk did not say a word about it, and that nobody in this district has ever thanked her and is not going to.

---

Lena Marr went on the Sunday morning the fourteenth of next month, at about ten o'clock, in a taxi, with about nine pounds of her own money, to four rooms behind a boatyard on the Ferry Road side, because Doreen Sankey was going with her and would not let her go alone and would not go in, and stood on the step with his hands in his pockets in the rain and said one sentence, which was: "I have been behind this gate for nine years and I have never been past the first room, and I am not going to today either, and you can tell her I said that."

The woman is fifty-four and mends other people's clothes and her name is not on the page and has not been on the page in six years and is not going to be. She came to the door with a jacket on over one arm and did not ask them in and Lena Marr read the six words off the return and did not make her ask a single question.

"It is a plot number," said Lena Marr, "and there is a ground, and the ground has got a name and it is on the authority's paper, and the register for that district was not kept after the closure so there is nothing else and there is probably never going to be anything else, and I have not been able to find out who is in the ground and I have not been able to find out whether anybody put a stone up, and I have been sitting with that since the twenty-first of October and I did not come because I did not know what to ask you, and I have not come back in twenty-four days because I did not know what to ask you, and that was cowardice and it was mine and it was not yours and I have said that out loud in a yard about nine times in a month."

She gave her the number and the name of the ground and did not make her write it down, and did not make her ask where to go, and did not make her thank anybody, and did not say the word *sorry*, because she has said in a yard that she does not intend to apologise to a woman for something a department did, and the woman has said she does not want her to.

"You have brought me the number," said the woman.

"I have brought you the number."

"Nobody has brought me anything in six years," she said. "Not even a bill. I have had no bill. I have had a fortnight's relief in the first year because I applied for it and I did not know what I was applying for and a woman in an office said it could not be done and gave me the money anyway and would not look at me, and I have thought about her about twice a week for six years and I do not even know her name."

"That was the Recovery officer," said Lena Marr, "and I can give you her name, and she has said out loud that if you come to that counter she will not put you on anything, and I am telling you that so that you do not go and ask her to."

And that is the whole of the fourteenth of December, and it took about forty minutes, and neither woman put a name to anything, and the count of people in that room was three and one on a step in the rain and the total money spent was about nine pounds and nobody has asked for it back.

---

Pell Harrow came down on the Sunday night of the fourteenth of next month at about ten past ten with a piece of chalk in his hand and put an arrow on the outside of Milo's own building at about five feet, at the foot of the stairs, pointing up, with a date under it, and did not say a word about it and went back up the yard at about four miles an hour.

It is on the inside of his own front door too, at about four feet, with the twenty-eighth of November under it, and that one is not the boy's — it is the surveyor's own, put there by him at about half past ten at night five weeks ago with a piece of chalk out of his own coat, and chalk is a gate-man's practice in this district and it is not his, and he has written that down. The third is on the wall of the landing on the second floor, and it has no date under it, and it is not the boy's either, and it was there on the tenth and it was still there on the fourteenth, and nobody has ever asked who put it there. Advocate Quill has asked him twice which of his two marks he considered the important one and he has refused twice.

---

The room was on the Monday morning the fifteenth of next month at about ten o'clock, in the Hall of Wards, and it is not a hearing and it was not called a hearing and about nine people in this district have written down that it should not have been called a hearing, because a hearing is a thing that is decided by a chair and a thing that is decided by a chair can be got out of by an appeal, and what happened in that room was a department answering a statutory appeal on the sixth working day, which is a different thing and is a worse thing for the district and a better thing for the order.

Mara Quill was there with eleven people and Milo Rook was one of them and was not going to speak and had been asked by her in advance whether he was going to speak and had said no and had been told that was the correct answer and had said, in a corridor, that he had wanted to speak and had been told no twice and had agreed twice.

What was granted is these five things, and they are on the page in the terms the department's answer used.

> *A temporary route* at the withdrawn alignment, in the form of a supervised passage on named dates, in daylight, with a named person outside the reach holding a count, and a named person walking in and out, and no schedule of persons.
> *A temporary stay* against demolition and against forced relocation, until the conclusion of the investigation, and not thereafter unless the investigation recommends it.
> *Access*, to every household, to its own housing file and its own compensation file, on application, in person, at a counter, and to no household's file by any other party.
> *A public hearing* on the order, in this building, on a date to be set, to which the district is entitled as of right and not as of favour.
> *The public name of the withdrawn alignment restored as a contested community and memorial name* — entered on the community and memorial register, and **not on the street name index**, and **not as a rateable place**, and the entry is to be marked *contested* in the terms of the register, and is capable of being produced against any person who uses it.

"Read the last one out again," said a woman of sixty from the second landing, from the second row, in a hall, to a department.

"Entered on the community and memorial register, and not on the street name index, and not as a rateable place, marked *contested*, and capable of being produced against any person who uses it," said the man from the department, and he read it because she asked him to, and nobody in that room has ever called that a courtesy and it was not one.

And the trap came at twenty past eleven in the morning, and it came because nobody in the room had solved it and it could not be solved, and it is the fifth thing and nobody had it on a list.

---

The intake opens on a tenancy, a temporary registration, or a Continuity referral, and every one of the three asks for a place, and seven children come back in the spring if it opens, and two of them are lost for good and were lost in October because a woman stood in a kitchen and named herself on a Bench instrument and has said in a yard that she would do it again and has been told by a woman at a school door that it will stay two.

A tenancy needs a street. There is no street.

A temporary registration needs a head of household, and the head of household for that block is the last registered one, and the register was read out by a clerk of the Recovery office at about twenty-five past eleven on the fifteenth of next month in about four seconds, correctly, in a hall, in front of about thirty people.

"The last registered head of household for the block is entered at the third floor and is cross-referenced to a file number."

Nobody said anything. The clerk looked at the room and found about thirty faces and about four hundred words of material in front of him and he did the thing that every clerk who has come to that counter in six years has done, entirely correctly, and he read out the file number, and he did not look it up, and he was not going to look it up, and **he noticed nothing and said nothing, and there is nothing in those four seconds that anybody in this room will be able to remember him by except a file number.**

"Then the form cannot be completed on that line," said Mara Quill, "and I am not going to ask anybody in this room to look up a file number, and nobody is going to wake anybody up, and I would like it minuted in the department's own answer that Advocate Quill did not ask and that nobody in this room asked."

Which left the seven, and the compensation, and a woman of sixty from the second landing who said, in about eleven words from the second row: "Then there is no way in and I would like somebody to say so out loud, because I have lived on a landing for nine years and I have been told there is no way in by five different people in five different coats and I have never once been told it by anybody who had to."

There is a third way, and it was found by the clerk in the hall, and it is not a solution to the trap and it is the trap, paid.

It is a **temporary accommodation household with a named head who is a rateable person**. It is entered on a per-head additional schedule, not on the street index and not on a rate book and not as a rateable place, so the compensation — which is assessed on displacement from a named place, and which is not assessed on a tenancy, and which a current address for the block would end — is untouched. The seven children can be enrolled on a Continuity referral that names a responsible person for the household, because a referral asks for a place and a place needs a person.

And the responsible person is the head of the household, and a head of household is a person, and a rate is built on a property, and there is no property, and **a person in a room is not a property, and a rate cannot be built on a door, and if the district cannot build a rate on a door then somebody has to stand at the door, and it cannot be the third floor, and no clerk in this room is going to be the one who notices.**

The consequence was stated aloud in the hall, on the fifteenth of December, at about half past eleven in the morning, by the man from the department, because he had to state it, in these terms.

"If the block is entered as a temporary accommodation household with a named head, then the per-head additional schedule for it is charged to that head and not to the district rate. The rate is two and four a head a week. It is charged for every person entered in the household, and it is charged week by week, and it is charged in advance. It is not a one-time sum and it does not have a term, and it can be ended only by the household ceasing to exist, or by the district producing a rateable basis for the block, which cannot be done because the block has no street."

The hall was very quiet.

"Somebody has to sign it," said Mara Quill, "and I am not going to pretend that the department can do it, and the department has correctly said it cannot, and a Continuity referral has to be signed by the person responsible for the household, and if that person is not a rateable person then the referral does not open the intake. That is the whole of it. That is what a rate is. It is a thing that has to be stood at by somebody, and everything in this city for six years has been waiting for somebody to be the one who stands at it, and the answer has been a man asleep on a third floor and about forty people in a building with no number."

Nobody in that hall said a name. It is on the page that in a room of about thirty people on the fifteenth of December, at about half past eleven in the morning, in the Hall of Wards, nobody said the name of the man on the third floor, and that the register had been read out correctly, and that nobody had woken him, and that no clerk had noticed anything, and that this was the correct conduct of about four officials and has been recorded as such by one of them.

Lena Marr stood up at about ten to twelve.

"I have got two questions and I am going to ask them both and then I am going to sign it and I am not going to be thanked, and I have written both questions down this morning and I have shown them to a woman of sixty-three on a step and she has read them and she is not here and she has said that if I get either of them wrong she will come on Wednesday."

---

Milo Rook wrote it down at about one o'clock that afternoon, in about a hundred words, because he was in the corridor and he had said in advance that he was not going to speak, and what he wrote down is what the four terms of a resolution are, and it is four lines and it is the last page of his own book that month that anybody has asked him for.

*Fifteenth of next month, 13.05. What it cost, in a room, in about ninety seconds, and the whole of the resolution in them.*

*One. A route, a stay, the files, a hearing, and a name on a register marked contested, and every one of the five of them is temporary and none of them is a street and the name is not on the street index and will be capable of being produced against her.*

*Two. The money. A rate is built on a property and there is no property, so the block was entered as a temporary accommodation household with a named head who is a rateable person, and the per-head additional schedule of two and four a head a week is charged to Lena Marr, in advance, week by week, for as long as that building is a building. She signed it at about ten to twelve. Nobody thanked her. The seven children are enrolled on a Continuity referral in the same hand in the same minute, and the two lost in October are still two and she said so herself before she signed, twice, in the room, unasked.*

*Three. The count. The department will accept a certified head count of two hundred and fifty-two in place of a schedule of rooms, on Advocate Quill's certificate in her own hand, dated the fifteenth, and the per-head schedule is adjusted from two hundred and thirty-one to two hundred and fifty-two and the twenty-one short is recovered, and the thirty-three rooms on no list stay on no list, and no room in this district is on a rate book today because of anything that happened today. The certificate is a declaration of a matter in which the certifying officer has an interest in the district and it is the third thing in a file about Advocate Quill's own hand, and it is the thing that has finished her review, and the review was on the third of December and went against her, and it took her preferred access and put a two-year note on her, and she appealed it on the fourth in one line and it is not stayed.*

*Four. And the whole of the resolution is that the district is now paying a woman of about thirty-four, who is not on the rate roll and never has been and has two children of her own, two and four a head a week for every person in a building with no number, and that a man of sixty-one in a knee is on a drainage record that nobody will strike out again, and a conductor of thirty in a printed rule book, and a foreman of thirty years in a file that a department can now produce, and a name of sixty-eight who is on nothing and has not been asked, and a surveyor's own front door with three chalk arrows on it and a book with a blank nine lines long in it.*

*Two hundred and fifty-two people got back into a per-head schedule today because one woman stood up. I have not been able to make that into a lesson and I am not going to try. I have been in this district eleven weeks and I have written down nine rules and four findings and one arithmetic, and the sum of all of it this afternoon is that a woman stood up at ten to twelve and put her name in a box, and I was in a corridor and I was not asked to do anything, and that is what the six weeks of giving things away were for, and I have not got one line in my own book that says what it is like to be the person in the corridor.*

Mara Quill filed her formal objection at about ten past three in the afternoon of Monday the fifteenth of next month, at the Registry, in a Crown Heights envelope with a division mark on it, and it is nineteen paragraphs, and it is the second instrument in a case that already has four, and it is her own hand, and it costs her.

It is a **formal objection and a public safety appeal** against the order of the sixth year back and against the classification of the eleventh of the current year and against the substitution of an authority's engineering classification for a safety finding in a matter in which persons were present, and against the removal from the original safety report of the sentence *no evidence was found of any hazard to persons at the service court*, and against the six-year delay in the death of a named maintenance worker, and it ends with one sentence about the *Bridge Reach* entry and one about the standing instruction of the office of the Prefect. It has three attachments, and the second of them is the carbon, and she filed the carbon in the *attachments* to a document about an order and not in a separate submission, and she has said in a corridor that she knows precisely what she has done and that she has done it on purpose, and that it will be produced against her in every room she ever enters again, and that it was going to be produced against her anyway because it was in the file and she had read it, and that **a document in a file is a thing somebody can produce, and a document in a file that somebody has read and understood is a thing somebody can produce, and the only question left is whether the person who read it is the person who wrote the objection, and she decided that it was going to be.**
