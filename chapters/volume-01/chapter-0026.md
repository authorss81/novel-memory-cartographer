# Chapter 26: The Timetable Review

The round went out at seven on the morning of Sunday the twenty-eighth of Sazur with five stops on it and came back at four with five, and it was the last time a courier went through the works footprint on a bicycle in the district's history, and nobody put that on a notice.

Rue did not say anything about it at the gate. She said, "Three and four," to the gate-man, and he let her through, and she was gone twenty-two minutes and came back the other way with a lamp she had lit and put out again on the way, which is a thing couriers do and is not a thing anybody else does, and she wrote 3 and 4 in the tick column and drew a line through both and put the list in the satchel and did not write a word.

That was the first thing. The second was at ten o'clock, in the works office, which held nine people and a heater and a large sheet of board with the amendment pinned to it and a man from the timetable division standing in front of it with a list on a clipboard, and his name was Barrow and he had been in the job nineteen years, and he had a list on a clipboard and a sheet of board with the amendment pinned to it, and he was going to work through nine items in front of whoever turned up.

"This is a meeting and not a hearing," Barrow said, before anybody sat down. "I have said that out loud so that nobody spends the morning preparing. I have a list of nine items and two of them are yours and the other seven are stops and intervals, and I intend to be through the seven before the coffee and then I will take the two, and I will tell you now that I have read the minute of the fourteenth of Sazur and I know exactly what the stay did and exactly what it did not do, and I do not need it explained to me by a man who has been in my building for three weeks."

Nobody had been in his building for three weeks.

"The stay of the tenth of Sazur, as minuted," said Barrow, "prevents the Line 7 service being re-cut around the old service court before the date of this meeting. It does not suspend the order. It does not restore a name. It does not lift anything. It was a filing made by an advocate and it was minuted clause by clause at my desk's request and I would like the advocate to know that I asked for that, because in fourteen years that has never once happened."

"Thank you," said Mara Quill.

"It is not a favour. It is so that in five years nobody can say you hid it." He turned a page. "The stay ends at the conclusion of this meeting. I am not able to extend it and I have not been asked to and I would not, because it does not do anything."

He wrote one line in a book and turned it round and it was the shortest document in the history of the Bellwether file.

Then he did the certification box, and he did it in about four minutes, and it went the way Milo Rook had written on the outside of the form on the twenty-fourth.

"There is no person familiar with the route withdrawn," said Barrow. "And before anybody in this room runs the two together, that is a box on a tram authority form and it is not the Registry's mark, which is a different instrument answering a different question. Mr Rook's note of the twenty-fourth is on the form in his own hand and I have read it twice and I have written *no person available* in the box, which is the correct entry, and which means the fallback applies."

"The fallback is the surveyor of record from survey," said Mara.

"The fallback is the surveyor of record from survey," said Barrow, "and the surveyor of record has written a schedule level and a date and a geometry and has left the use of the alignment blank, and has signed it, and I want to say in this room that the amendment goes through this afternoon on a description that is a description of a bend, and that I have never before in nineteen years laid an amendment on a description of a bend, and that I am laying it because the alternative is a description of a household and there is no household on this form and has not been for six years."

"What happens to the part that is blank?" Danner said.

"Nothing happens to it," said Barrow. "It stays blank in a public document, which is the whole point of a box, and in about four years somebody with a grievance will find it, and it will be the only thing on that sheet anybody can argue with."

---

The amendment was made at twenty past twelve on the morning of Sunday the twenty-eighth of Sazur and took effect on the first working day following, which was the Monday, and the whole of the river core ran on a new timetable from the first run on the Monday morning, and the extra stop was not on it, and the disused platform at Alder Quay was going to be recovered and the nameboards taken off it.

Milo Rook asked the only question he had asked in the room, and he asked it to Danner and not to Barrow, and he had asked it in writing on the twenty-first and again on the twenty-fourth.

"You said eleven weeks and you said the low streets go back to a drain the size of a man's wrist. Read it into the minute."

"It is not my minute," said Barrow.

"It is the same wall," said Milo, "and there are nine people in the room and four of them came here to find out what happens to the low streets, and you have got the number and I have not."

Danner looked at Barrow, and Barrow said, "That is out of order."

"It is out of order and it is true and it is in a public meeting and I am asking the foreman to say the number," said Milo Rook, "and if he says no then I shall say the number myself, out loud, in this room, and I have got it from a woman with a lamp and a boy with a rod and I would rather it was in a minute."

Danner put his clipboard down.

"District drainage along the old alignment is a lead lateral laid in 1904 and shown on the district record as two foot six and checked sound," he said. "The lateral is a lead lateral the size of a man's wrist. The real culvert under Bridge Street is the works' storm main, and the storm main is dead, and the storm main has a valve on it, and the valve has been shut since before I came here. So when the tram stops putting a river down that alignment on a flood-season detour, the surface water of the low streets goes back to a pipe from 1904 that nobody has looked inside since the year the district started writing *sound* on its own opinion." He picked the clipboard up. "The low streets will flood. Not on the twenty-eighth. In November, in a dry month, the gully opposite the wall with the old paint on it will come up, and it has come up for eleven years, and now there is nothing behind it at all."

"Is that in the amendment?"

"No," said Barrow. "The amendment is a service amendment and drainage is a district works matter and it is a different division and it is on a different form."

"Write it in the note at the bottom," said Mara, "under my name, and I will serve it, and it will go to the district works office and it will be answered in the ordinary course."

"I have written it in the note at the bottom," said Barrow. "It is not going to be answered in the ordinary course. I have been in this job nineteen years and there is no ordinary course for drainage; the ordinary course is that a gully comes up and a woman complains and eleven years later somebody writes *sound* on a piece of paper."

---

Jo Lask came to the yard at about half past eight on the Sunday evening with the new working timetable under his arm, which he had collected at the division at eight in the evening on the day before the day it came into force, because Jo Lask collected the new working timetable on the day before the day it came into force as a matter of personal habit that nobody had ever asked him to have and that he had never once told anybody about.

"Amendment's made," he said. "Then the sheet's printed. And I've read it, and there's a thing in it, and I'm going to read it out because there is nobody else alive who will read it out."

"Jo—"

"I'm suspended. I know what I am. I'm not driving, I'm on the yard and the shed, and a man who is suspended from driving has got nothing to lose and a conductor who is not has got a whole career, and that is the only reason I'm standing here and both of us know it." He put the sheet on the trestle table and turned it round. "Alder Quay to Bridge Street loop withdrawn. Through services by Ferry Road and Low Street. That's yours, that's the notice, everybody knows that. Here is the part that isn't the notice.

"The diversion was worked at a schedule that gave the box an extra minute and a half at the Ferry Road turn, because a diversion is a street that is not a street and you give a man room. The new sheet takes that minute and a half back. It gives it back out of the Ferry Road turn and it gives it to the running time, and the running time on the diversion is now eleven seconds shorter at the point where a car comes out of the turn and goes past the box.

"Eleven seconds," said Jo Lask.

Somebody at the back said, "That's not a lot."

"It's not a lot," said Jo. "It's a count. That's what a count is, at that end. You come out of a turn and you go past a box and there is a woman standing at a door and you have got until the next signal to say the number and hear it come back, and the number is not called into a hole. It is called to a person and the person repeats it, and if she does not repeat it you stop, and if you stop the diversion takes a half of one line behind you and the whole thing is late and everybody behind you is on a bridge in the dark.

"Eleven seconds is the difference between handing it over and not handing it over. And I have written to the division about it, and I shall write to them again, and I am going to keep writing to them, and I am under a suspension and a half-pay with a hearing on a Thursday and I have just spent the last of my good standing in a yard on a Sunday evening on a matter that concerns a street that is not going to be there on Monday.

"And I want one thing noticed," he said, "and then I'll take the sheet away. Nobody has asked me to read that out. There are about twenty people in this yard and eleven of them work for a contractor and four of them are going to be in a trench on Monday at seven. I have read it out because I am the only man in Alderquay who can tell you what a count costs in seconds, and I would like that on the record of somebody who is not me, and I am aware that is a thing I have wanted for six years and have not had the nerve for until a man with a bandage stood on an embankment on the fourth of Sazur and believed me."

Lena Marr wrote it on the back of a Recovery form and read it back and then she said, "You'll be at the inquiry on the Thursday and this will be in it."

"Yes," said Jo Lask. "And it will be the only thing in that hearing that is actually about a bell."

---

The seven other items took until a quarter to twelve and Barrow read them out and nobody argued with any of them, and at the end of it he took the board down off the wall and put the amendment up in its place, printed, with a date on it, under a drawing pin, which is what the district does, and which is how a thing becomes true in Alderquay.

And the room emptied, and the notice was read by about four hundred people in the district over the following nine days, and there is a line at the foot of the amendment that says drainage is a different division on a different form.

At about four o'clock on the Sunday afternoon, in the yard at Cedar Court, with the amendment up in Alder Quay and nothing at all on the porch wall, Lena Marr stood with her back against the coal hatch side and read the notice from a copy of the earlier notice because there was nothing newer, and she read it to about nineteen people, and when she came to paragraph two she stopped.

"Closed to all service access," she said. "From the Monday. Eleven weeks. That is the low end of the alignment and the footpath that goes round it, and there is no other footpath, and a woman with a pram goes round that and has gone round that for six years because the alternative is past the second hatch."

"Somebody go and tell her," said a man from the back.

"I will tell her," said Lena. "I will tell her this afternoon. And I am telling you now that the thing I cannot tell her is that nobody can fix it, because the works are doing the only thing anybody can do with a street that has settled nine inches over forty feet, and if they do not do it then the next floodgate test takes the alignment out and takes a wall with it, and there are two hundred and thirty-one people behind that wall who are not rateable and who are being charged two and four a head a week to be behind it."

"Then what is it for," somebody said, "if it can't be fixed?"

"It is for a drainage connection," said Lena Marr. "That is all it has ever been for. That is the whole reason this district is in a case at all, and it is in a paragraph on a board, and it has been there since the sixth of Sazur and nobody has read it since the ninth because everybody in this district has been reading the name instead of the drainage."

She folded the notice.

"And I want it noticed," she said, "that it is Sunday, and the round was the short one, and it went through that footprint at eight this morning for the last time, and Rue came back at four with five on a list of nine, and nobody put that on the board either."

Nobody put anything else on the board. Milo Rook went back to the office at half past four and wrote the day down in four lines, and the fourth line was the one he would read back in a week and in a year.

*Twenty-eighth of Sazur, 16.40. The stay ended at ten twenty-five this morning in one line and it never did anything else and it was the only thing in this case that never cost anybody anything.*

*The amendment is made and the alignment comes out and the court's own drainage goes back to a lead lateral from 1904 and the low streets flood in November in a dry month, and I have it in a note at the bottom of a form under Mara Quill's name, and it will be answered in the ordinary course, which means it will not be.*

*Nine places released. Two stops gone. A letterbox gone. A handrail four feet out of true over a dip that will be there in forty years.*

*And the count of what this cost is not a number about a street. It is five, in a yard, on the twenty-sixth, and Iona Sen has the tally, and the tally is the only thing in this district that is going to be exact.*
