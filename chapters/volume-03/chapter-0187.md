# Chapter 187: Half Past Two On An Ordinary Monday, And A Gate, And About A Hundred And Forty People Watching A Thing They Cannot Stop

**The round went out at about ten past eight on Monday the twenty-third of November and came in at about ten past six with the bag empty, and it is a Monday and a Thursday round and it has been since the second of January and it is one man short, and the bag has been empty since the twenty-eighth of August, and there is no gang on that road and there has not been since the thirty-first of August, and the barriers were still out and the road was still open and both of those are correct.**

The page home is the three hundred and sixty-fifth day. The token is cold on the three hundred and fifty-first. The plate in the grass at the low end of Bridge Street is two hundred and thirty-one days old and has been told to nobody. The condition column is two hundred and eighty-four days and is not there. **A foreman of fifty-four is thirty weeks and six days past four months and nobody is going to ask him.**

**It had not rained for nine days. It had not rained properly since the Sunday. There was no wind. It was about four degrees and grey and the light was the flat light you get in this city in the last three weeks of November and everybody saw it and nobody looked at the sky, because you do not.**

---

## About half past two, in the low end of Bridge Street, in a lane

He was about two hundred yards down the low end with the bag over his shoulder doing nothing, because the round has had nothing in it since the twenty-eighth of August and it is not a walk and nobody has called it one.

**The first thing was not a sound. The first thing was that the water in the drain stopped sounding like water in a drain.**

He has stood at the end of a lane in this district four times in November and he has heard that drain on every one of them. It is a stone drain and it runs under a grating about four feet in from the fence and it is about a hundred and forty years old and it is the council's and it is not maintained and it has a job. At about half past two on that Monday it made a noise that is not the noise it makes when there is nothing in it and not the noise it makes when there is a lot in it. **It made the noise it makes when the thing it is taking is not arriving from where it arrives from.**

Then the grating went under. Not fast. It went under in about eleven seconds, the way a thing goes under when the water has already got behind it, and the lane filled from the grating outward and not from the low end inward, and **he has written that down and it is the only technical thing in this entry: the water came out of the drain, and not up the lane from the fen, and everything that happened afterwards on this side of the city happened because it came out of a hole in the ground that the council's owns and does not know is there.**

---

## And then the frontages, and about a hundred and forty people, in about nine minutes

It was in the doorways of the first four houses on the low end inside nine minutes. It was over the sole of a door that has been over the sole of that door about twice in twenty years and both times were in November and both times were called something else by the people who lived there.

**And then everything in that street did what about a hundred and forty people do when a street floods, which is that they came out.**

A man of about seventy came out with a chest of drawers and did not go up the steps, he went across the road to a wall, because he is seventy and he has done it before. Two women of about thirty came out with a pram and a bag of laundry between them and neither of them asked anybody for anything. A man of about fifty came out of a yard in a high-visibility jacket with a shovel in one hand and stopped in the road, and put the shovel down, and stood there, **and did not begin shovelling, because you cannot shovel a street, and he knew it, and he had the shovel out of habit.**

**Nobody shouted except two people about a car, and both of them were about a car.**

A woman of about sixty came out of the row of rooms and went down the middle of the street on the crown of it where the camber is about four inches higher, and she walked up the crown all the way to the corner with her hand on the wall, and she did not look at the water once. **That is not heroism. That is a person who has been in this street for about sixty years and knows where the high line is, and there is nobody in this city who can put that on any form, and she has not told anybody about it and she is not going to.**

And the man of about sixty with the shed eleven minutes away came out of the end of the lane carrying a box of tools and a length of chain about four feet long, and put both of them on the door step of his own shed, which is four inches above the lane, and went back inside and shut the door.

**He knows that lane. He has known it for about forty years. He is sixty-one or sixty-two and he has never once been asked about it and nobody is going to, and the four inches above that lane is the whole of what he got out of it and he got it up four inches in about nine seconds without appearing to hurry.**

---

## About a quarter to three, the whole of the low end is under, and the thing everybody does next is nothing

It was about a foot in the road at the corner by about a quarter to three and it was not moving fast. **That is the whole of what an ordinary flood in this city is: it does not arrive, it fills, and the difference between those two is about eleven minutes and everything that matters happens in the eleven minutes.**

What about a hundred and forty people did next was stand on the crown of the road and on the front step of the shop and on the wall at the corner, and look at the water, and talk.

They talked about the drain. They said *the drain has gone* about forty times, which is true and is not a diagnosis, because **a drain is a hole the council put in a street about a hundred and forty years ago and it is not maintained and there is no form in this city in which a person can say that a drain is the reason a street is under, and everybody in that street has known that since before anybody in it was born.**

A man of about thirty said, to nobody, "**It'll be the gates.**"

And about six people said something to him, and it was not agreement, and it was not the sentence you get in a story. It was *how do you know*, and *I don't*, and *don't say that*, and *they're in the city*, and then nobody said anything for about a minute.

**And Milo Rook, standing on the crown of the road with the bag over his shoulder, said nothing at all, and wrote nothing, and did not say the word gates, and did not ask the man of about thirty where he had heard it, and did not write down afterwards where the gates are, and that last one is on the page and the reason is the oldest sentence he has got: a description of a place is how a person gets found.**

---

## What the gates are, and who built them, and why there is nobody in this street to be angry at

**The central gates are the city's. They are about a mile and a half from the end of this street and they are not in this district and they were not built by anybody who lives in it and nobody in this district can be angry with anybody who operates them, because not one of the men on them has ever heard of this street and none of them ever will.**

He has not been inside them. He has not seen them. He knows about them the way everybody in eleven streets knows about them, which is that they are a set of gates on the water at the top of the reach, and that they are worked by men in a building, and that they are correct, and that when they are doing what they are for the water stays where it is.

**A gate is built to hold a difference in level between one side of it and the other. That is what a gate is. It is a thing for a difference, and a difference has to be a difference, and what came on the afternoon of the twenty-third of November was not more water against the face of them. It was the level on both sides going up together until the difference was about nothing, and a gate with no difference in it is not a gate. It is a hole in a wall with a door in it and about nine men who did exactly what their instructions said doing the right thing at the right time on the afternoon of an ordinary Monday.**

**And that is why nobody in that street is angry, and it took him about four minutes to work out standing on the crown of the road and it is the finding of the whole of this month and he is not going to improve it: a thing that fails while it is being operated correctly is not a fault. It is a limit. And there is no form in this city in which a limit can be entered, and ground (d) on a key printed in the middle of May is a question about money and is not anybody's.**

**And the four hundred yards between that gate and the drain at the end of a lane is not a fault either, and it is a hundred and forty years old, and it is the council's, and about nine of them are on this side of the river and every one of them is somebody's idea of where a thing was.**

---

## About four o'clock, the far side of the bridge, and getting back across it

**He went out onto the far side of the bridge at about four o'clock because the bridge is the only way to see what is happening to the ground behind the low end, and he went out past the second post and up to about the fourth and stopped.**

The path on the far side was under. The path is about three hundred yards and it is a raised path and it is raised about nine inches, and it was under to about four inches at the near end and eleven inches at the far end, and the water on it was not brown, it was the colour of the water in the low end, which meant it was coming from the same place and not from the fen.

He came back at about twenty past five when there was about four inches of a fall in it on the near side, and it took him about two hours to be able to walk back from the far side to the crown of the road in Bridge Street and he did it in three goes with a man of about forty-five and a folding rule between them, and nobody fell over and nobody went in.

**Nobody drowned. Nobody was hurt. Nobody was taken out of a house. A shed roof at the north end came off at about ten past four and went down the crown of the road and stopped against the wall of a shop, and about nine people stood and looked at it, and a man of about forty got a bar and shifted the whole thing about four feet so that a car could get out, and nobody thanked him and he did not expect to be.**

The water was down to about two inches in the road at the corner by about seven. By about nine there was a smell of drains in the whole street and about four inches of grey silt on the sole of the door that is over the sole of that door about twice in twenty years.

**Over in hours. Not a catastrophe. In front of about a hundred and forty ordinary people who watched a thing they could not stop and then watched it go, and about four hundred people in this city had the same afternoon.**

---

## And the four, and this one, and the difference, and the reason there is no fifth

**There are four things that happened in this district in five months that cannot be shown to anybody. A rectangle of standing water on the twenty-ninth of January. A hundred and ten yards of frontage in the rain on the nineteenth of February. A plate in a field on the sixth of April. And about nine people in eleven streets standing in the weather for nothing at all on the fifteenth of April.**

**That is four and it is still four and this is not one of them, and the difference is on the page and it is about nine words long: one of the four had nobody in it, and this one had about a hundred and forty.**

The standing water in January was in a field and there was nobody watching it and nobody could be told about it and it did not happen to anybody. The hundred and ten yards of frontage in the rain in February was a man walking in weather that was not doing anything unusual. The plate is still in the grass and four people in this city know it is there and have said nothing. The nine people in the weather in April stood there and there was no reason.

**This one had a hundred and forty people in it and every one of them saw it and about nine of them have already said a true thing about it to somebody else today, and there will be about nine of them on Friday who say it to him, and there is a difference between a thing that happened to a place and a thing that happened in front of about a hundred and forty people, and the difference is not that it was worse. It is that it will be talked about.**

**And he is not counting them and he is not going to, and there is no total anywhere and there is not going to be one, and a fifth would be a series and a series needs somebody to be surprised, and about a hundred and forty people were not surprised this afternoon because they have all seen it twice.**
