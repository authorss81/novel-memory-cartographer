# Chapter 173: Ten Feet Wide, And A Post Four Inches Short, And Nobody Has Levelled It

**The man of about fifty with the mower had the van outside the gate at ten to eight. It is a white panel van with a rack on the back for mowers and a company name on the side in a colour that has gone the colour of the weather, and it belongs to a firm, and the firm is not him and the van is not a day off, and he has said so both times this year without being asked to.**

"Two stops," he said. "Then I am back at the yard."

"You are going to the bridge."

"**I am going to the bridge.**"

**It is not a day off for you and I am not going to pretend it is**, he said, and got in the front.

**Tuesday the sixth of October.** The page home is the three hundred and seventeenth day. The token is cold on the three hundred and third. The plate in the grass at the low end of Bridge Street is a hundred and eighty-three days old and has been told to nobody. The condition column is two hundred and thirty-six days and is not there. **A foreman of fifty-four is twenty-three weeks and six days past four months and nobody is going to ask him.**

---

## The city half, which is nine years of his life, done in an hour

He cuts the city half and he has cut it for nine years and he does it in about an hour with a bar and not a mower, and the mower stays on the rack, and the reason is a load calculation in a drawer in this city from about 1974 with the low end of it not re-checked.

Milo Rook stood at the parapet and watched him do it and did not ask about the calculation, because he asked about it in August and got ninety seconds and the whole of it, and the man has said he is not going to say it again to anybody.

What he did instead, at about eleven, was stop the bar and look across.

---

## Three things at that line and it is one boundary

"There are still three things at that line."

"There are."

"**I know there are. I said that in September and I have not said it since and I am not going to say it now.**" He went back to the bar. "**I said it to you because I had been holding it since the seventh of August and I had waited for somebody to say the wrong thing about it so that I could say the right one, and I have said it, and it is done, and I am not a party to it and there is nothing else I have got.**"

**Nobody said anything else about it. There are three things at a mowing line and it is one boundary, and the boundary is the two, and the three boundaries in this district are three and are not joined, and a fourth kind has not been added and will not be.**

Then the mower man did the thing that made this Tuesday different from every other Tuesday, and it took him about four seconds and it was a walk.

He walked out onto the deck, past the line, about nine feet onto the far half, and stopped, and looked down at the grass, and came back.

"That is as far as I go."

"You said you were not going over."

"**I said I was not going to put a machine over it. I did not say anything about walking.**" He shouldered the bar again. "**In four years one of these is going to want a figure for the far half and it is going to be a figure in yards, and there is going to be a man in a room who cannot get a figure, and then there is going to be somebody who works out a figure from a piece of paper from 1974, and that figure is going to be about two hundred and forty, and I am not going to be here when it is.**"

---

## And here is the wrong-shaped thing, and it is the schedule

**The bridge is on the schedule.** He had seen it on Thursday and he had not gone back and looked for it, and he had gone and looked for it in his own head on a bus and got it approximately right, which is the worst way to get a thing right.

Column four, ground (a), *no maintained seam of record*, and a figure.

**One thousand and ninety yards.**

He said it out loud on the bank, once, to nobody, and then he wrote it down that afternoon and did not improve it.

**One thousand and ninety yards is the length of surface and drainage works executed on a bridge and its approaches under a third-quarter contract that expired on the thirty-first of August.** It is not the length of the bridge. The bridge is about a hundred and forty feet and eleven inches, and the parapet to the first post is about a hundred and thirty yards, and the third post is about ninety yards past the second, and the path on the far side that eleven men and a foreman used to walk twice a week is about three hundred yards.

**A thousand and ninety yards is a figure about a set of works, and a length is a third kind of place and is not a remnant, and a schedule wants a quantity, and a quantity is a length, and nobody in this city has ever costed a bridge in anything else.**

He stood in the middle of the deck and worked out, in about four minutes, the whole of what had happened to a bridge in eight months, and it took him four minutes and it was this:

*A bridge was two ends and a middle. It is now a line with three things stopped on it and a figure on a board and a post that is four inches short and a file that says replaced like for like. **The line has three things on it and the board has a yardage on it and the yardage does not know about the line, and the line is not in the file, and the file is in a drawer in a building about a mile from here and is about 1974 and the low end of it has not been re-checked, and the only man in this city who knows all three of those things is a man of about fifty with a bar and he is not going to be here when the figure is wanted.** And a figure is what a schedule wants. And a schedule is a thing that is either maintained or not maintained, and this one says not, and the not is going to be true by Friday.*

**A boundary is a place where two people agreed to have stopped. A boundary has two ends and no middle and cannot be measured in yards, and a mowing line that is ten feet wide and cut with a sickle by a man on his way in is not a boundary either, and the whole of the difficulty is that the schedule does not have a column for a place where two things stopped and it has a column for a length, and a length is the only thing it can count, and it counted one.**

---

## And the rate, which nobody put on the board and which is the reason the board exists

He worked that out on the bank at about ten to twelve and it took him about four minutes and it is the part of it he has not been able to put down since.

**A figure on a schedule is not a measurement. A figure on a schedule is a priced measurement.** Somebody in a works office in a building about a mile from here took a length of frontage and put a rate on a yard of it, and the rate is what turns a length into a season and a season into a sum of money, and the sum of money is the only reason anybody in this city has ever wanted to know how many yards there are anywhere.

And here is the part that put the whole thing over.

**A district with no rateable unit has no rate.** It has had no rateable unit since about 1911, and a rate is levied on a rateable unit, and a rateable unit is a thing with a street on it, and there is no street in this thing to put the unit on. So the office could not get a rate for eleven streets.

**And it did not need to. It has a rate from a contract.** A third-quarter surface works contract carries a schedule of quantities, and a schedule of quantities carries a rate, and the rate is a figure about money and it was put there in about 1919 or whenever contracts of that kind started carrying them and it is about as old as the district list and it has never once been checked against anything, and it is the number that put this district on a board in a corridor in Crown Heights.

**Two thousand three hundred and forty yards at a rate from a contract for a set of works that ended on the thirty-first of August is a sum of money. That is what is in the schedule. Not a district. A sum of money, about a place, at a rate for a district, from a document about a road.**

He stood on the bank for a while after that and did not say it out loud, and it is the fifth thing this city has done in eight months that is about money and there has not been one before, and the other four were all about a name. **He wrote one sentence that evening and the sentence was not this one.**

---

## The van's two stops, and the second one was refused in about four seconds

"The second stop," said Milo Rook, on the way back.

"**No.**"

"You said two stops."

"**I said two stops on the sixth and I have said two stops on the sixth and every Tuesday for about two years, and one of them is a place I get my coffee and I am not going to be a second stop for this.**" He did not look over. "**You are not going to be a second stop for anything, and I am not going to be a man who drives out of his way and I would have to remember that I did it.**"

"That is fair."

"**It is fair and it is also not the point.**" The mower man put the bar back on the rack. "**The point is that if you had asked me at the gate this morning instead of at eleven o'clock on a bridge, I would have said no to the bridge, and you would have had a day instead of a morning, and you would have spent it in a different direction, and I would have been a stop instead of a driver. You did not ask me at the gate. That was the mistake and it was not mine.**"

Milo Rook did not answer that for about four minutes.

"**Do not write that down,**" said the mower man.

"I was not going to."

"**You were, and I could see you, and that is twice in about two hours and I have said that I am not going to say anything else so I am not saying it again.**"

---

## The post

**The handrail post on the city side is four inches short of the one it took out. The file says *replaced like for like*. Nobody has levelled it and nobody is going to, and about nine families navigate this path by that post, and there is nobody in Crown Heights who can say which post it was, and it is four inches and it has been four inches since before the works and it will be four inches after the works.**

He was standing about four feet from the only man who cuts both halves of that bridge.

**He did not ask him about the post. He did not ask him on the seventh of August and he did not ask him on the eighteenth of September and he did not ask him this morning, and he has been four feet from that man three times now, and he is not going to make a column of it, because a column is worse than a count and a count is a number somebody can be given.**

At about ten past twelve the mower man knocked the bar against the parapet twice to get the mud off it and said:

"You are going to want to know why I have never once put a mower on that half."

"No," said Milo Rook.

"**No,**" he agreed, and went back to the yard, and did not say anything else for about an hour.

---

## The rest of the day

The van was back at the yard at about twenty past two and did not stop again, and he was in eleven streets by about half past four with nothing in his hands and one sentence in the book, and the sentence was about a thousand and ninety yards.

**And a week in which the only thing he had done on the ground was walk out nine feet onto a bridge deck and come back, and the mowing line is still one boundary and there are still three things at it, and the man is still not a party, and he has said he is not going to say it again and Milo Rook has now been told that twice, on the same morning, by the same man, and the second time was in answer to a question that was not a question.**

Nobody thanked anybody. There is a man of about fifty who cut about nine feet of grass he was not going to cut and did not have to and came back in under a minute, and he is not thanked and is not going to be, and he has not asked to be thanked, because if you thank a man for a thing like that then you have made it a thing, and a thing can be written down, and he does not want it written down and said so in September and has said it again this morning in a shorter sentence.
