# Chapter 149: No Defect Observed, In Pencil, By A Man Of About Twenty-Six

**The gang was on the back length at half past seven on Monday the twentieth of July, eleven men and a foreman with a clipboard, and it was the thirty-first walk since the first of April.**

**The condition column had four words and a figure in it for the second time in a hundred and fifty-eight days.**

---

"**Sixty yards, nothing.**"

The foreman wrote it himself, at the top of the column, in pencil, in the hand he has used on that sheet for thirty-one years, and it is a figure and four words and it says what a length was like and nobody in this city has ever asked a gang what a length was like.

> **Mon 20/7. 60 yds. No defect observed.**

"There was nothing in that column from the twelfth of February to the thirteenth of July," said the man of about twenty-six in the high-visibility jacket, from about four feet away, not to anybody in particular.

"No."

"I have walked this length for about four years and I have never once had anything to write in it and I did not know that was unusual."

"It is not unusual. **It is what it is for.**"

---

**A column that wants to know what a length was like is a column that has been used for a hundred and fifty-eight days to record a hole, and a hole is an event, and an event is something that happened to a gang, and a gang can be asked whether an event happened. A state is not an event. A state is a thing that was true on a Monday morning and it will be true on Tuesday, and a man who has written *no defect observed* on a sheet has written something that is true at four o'clock on Monday and is not thereby a fault and not thereby a clearance and not thereby anything anybody can be asked about, and the whole of the difference between the two is the four words.**

Cyril Sale, forty-six, works clerk, said it at about ten past eight and did not say it twice.

"**That column is the only one in this building that is allowed to be empty. The other five are about what we did. That one is about what it was like.** And I told you in May that a thing that has never been asked about is not protected, it is just not yet asked about, **and it has now been asked about twice in eight months by two men who are not me, and it is answered twice, and the answer is on a sheet for the first time in five months and a half, and it is seventeen weeks late for a district that is not going to be maintained after the thirty-first of August.**"

Nobody said anything to that. He had said it at a gate and the gate was the wrong place and he had known that when he chose it.

---

**And at about twenty past eight the round went out, and it is one man short, and it has been one man short since the twenty-eighth of December, and the second side of the sheet has a line on the Monday because the round is on the Monday and the sheet is who does what and on which day and it is not a clock.**

**And at about half past eight the man of about twenty-six in the high-visibility jacket sat down on the low wall at the north end of the length with the duplicate carbon and a hard pencil.**

**The duplicate is a carbon. A foreman's pencil on the top sheet comes through onto the copy underneath as a grey smear, and it has been a grey smear on every sheet in this district since the second of January, and nobody has ever minded, because there has never been anything in the condition column to mind about.**

He copied the four words and the figure onto the carbon, in pencil, by hand, letter for letter, in about four minutes, so that the sentence would be legible on the copy.

Nobody asked him to.

Nobody thanked him.

**He was about four minutes. The duplicate goes into a tin, and the duplicate goes into a second tin on the bottom shelf of the plan room of the Civic Atlas Office under a repair schedule, and the second tin went to thirty-one on Friday the seventeenth. So a sentence a foreman of fifty-four wrote in pencil at half past seven on Monday the twentieth of July is legible in two buildings in two hands in about four years' time, and no clerk typed it and no machine took it anywhere, and the only reason it is legible in the second place is that a man of about twenty-six has been on this length for four years and nobody ever told him there was a reason to be careful over a smear.**

---

**And Milo Rook did not thank him either, and there is a rule about that in this book that nobody wrote down except the man who has been keeping to it for eight months, and the rule is that a person who has thanked somebody is a person who has asked, and a conductor of thirty-eight said that in April about a card, and it is the best sentence anybody in this city has said about paperwork, and it is a conductor's and it is not a surveyor's and it is not improved here.**

---

**And in the afternoon he did not go anywhere, and there is a printed side in a bag in a room in eleven streets that he has not looked at since Friday, and the round came in at about four, and the sheet for the week of Monday the twentieth of July is in a drawer in the quay section office, because a rack day is a Friday and the last rack day was Friday the seventeenth of July and the next one is Friday the twenty-fourth, and there is no rack day in this week yet and there will not be one until tomorrow.**

---

**And on the night of the twentieth, in a book in a coat, in a front room, at about eleven, the ninth thing of that shape appeared in eight months, and it is six words, and it is not going to be given to anybody.**

> **A printed ground holds nobody up.**

He wrote it and looked at it for about a minute and a half and did not act on it and does not know what it is about, and it is the ninth time anything like it has appeared in a book in eight months, and he is not going to hand it to a woman of sixty-one at a counter, and he is not going to say it in a yard, and there are about nine people in this district and none of them has it, and there is a woman of thirty-four in a village who would understand it and he is not going to tell her either, and there is a man of fifty-four on a gate who would argue with it.

*Monday the twentieth, 23.15. One day off the nineteenth.*

*One. **The condition column has two entries in it in five months and a half and the second one is four words and a figure written by a foreman of fifty-four on a Monday morning, and the first one is the same four words and the same figure on Monday the thirteenth of July, and neither of them is a fault, and a column that is used for a state instead of an event is the wrong shape for what it is for, and a works clerk of forty-six said so at a gate at ten past eight in about nine words and then did not say it again.***

*Two. **The carbon is a carbon, and a foreman's pencil comes through onto it as a grey smear, and it has been a grey smear on every sheet in this district since the second of January, and a man of about twenty-six sat down on a wall at half past eight on Monday the twentieth of July and went over the four words and the figure again in hard pencil so that the sentence would be legible on the copy, and he was about four minutes, and nobody asked him to and nobody thanked him and I did not thank him, and that sentence is a rule in this book that a conductor of thirty-eight gave me in April and it has not been improved and it is not going to be. A sentence that is illegible on a carbon is not a second copy of a sentence. It is a smudge, and a smudge is not a record and cannot be produced, and a man of twenty-six made one legible on his own account and not on ours, and in about four years the four words will be produced by a room and nobody will be able to tell that it took a man four minutes and a hard pencil and not a clerk, and that is the first time in eight months that I have wanted a thing to be legible in two places and I have not written down why and I am not going to.***

*Three. **The page home the two hundred and thirty-ninth day, blank, four marks in four hands and none of them mine, and the plate is a hundred and five days in the grass today and it is also the page home on the sixteenth of June and those are two different registers and a reader of this book is going to try to make them one.** The token cold, the two hundred and twenty-fifth day, in the inside pocket, and it has been cold for two hundred and twenty-five days. Eleven remembered routes, ten crossings, the tally is ten lines, Hettie Brann's paper, Dr Sen's pocket, not looked at since the thirteenth of January and not going to be looked at in August either.*

*Four. **The second side of the sheet is right about the eighth of July and the fifteenth of July and about every one of them since the eighth of April, and the only person who stood in the weather in eleven streets on either of those days was a man of about sixty at a shed at Cardew Docks, and a district entered as nil is the second reason and the second reason is a different kind of reason, and I am not counting them and I have not counted them since the first of July and I am not going to start again in the last week of a month that has done this to me.***

*And the standing position: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.*
