# Chapter 151: A Season, Counted Down In A Building Where Nobody Says The Number

The second tin on the bottom shelf of the plan room of the Civic Atlas Office went to thirty-two on Friday the twenty-fourth of July, and nobody telephoned about it, and the man of thirty did not ask the man of about fifty whether the number was right, because the number is not a thing anybody in this city has ever asked about.

Milo Rook heard about it on the Monday morning, from the man of about forty-one on no list at all, who was in the corridor with a handcart and who was not sent.

"**Thirty-two.**"

"That is a lot of weeks."

"**It is a lot of weeks. It has been twenty-eight since June and now it is thirty-two, and there is no room above it and it does not need a room above it.**"

---

**And the whole of the week of Tuesday the twenty-first of July to Monday the twenty-seventh is that eleven streets has a count in it and has nothing else, and the count is the only thing anybody in this district can do with a week.**

He did it in the corridor on the Tuesday morning, standing up, with a pencil and the back of a sheet of his own paper, in about four minutes, and he did not enjoy doing it and he did it anyway, because it is the kind of arithmetic he is good at and because nobody else in this district is going to do it.

**From the twenty-first of July to the thirty-first of August there are twelve Mondays and Thursdays.** The first of them is Thursday the twenty-third of July, which is tomorrow. The last of them is Monday the thirty-first of August, which is the last day of the contract and is itself a gang day. **A printed date does not know what day of the week it is, and the thirty-first of August is a Monday, and the last of the twelve is the same morning as the end of the contract, and nobody in the office that printed the form looked at a calendar, and this is the fifth time he has written that arithmetic down since Friday the seventeenth of July and he has improved it none of the five.**

**And between the twelve there are six Fridays**, and a rack day is a Friday, and there is a rack in the corridor four rooms above a fitter's shop that somebody empties of nothing, because a rack is a piece of furniture and nobody empties a rack, and on the last of the six a sheet goes into it that is the last sheet that will ever be printed for this gang, and on the seventh Friday after that there is a rack with nothing in it in it and there is no form in this city that says a rack is finished.

He wrote the two numbers down. **Twelve. Six.** He looked at them for about a minute and a half.

*There is no column anywhere in this district that wants either of those numbers. There is no column that wants how many mornings are left, and there is no column that wants how many Fridays are left, and the form in the bag about a mile away has got six columns and every one of them is about a district and not about a week. A district is entered as stable or not stable and a week is walked or not walked, and a district entered as stable can be printed on four sides and a week cannot be printed anywhere at all.*

---

**And on Wednesday the twenty-second of July the second side of the sheet of who does what and on which day was right about the day, and it was right about it for the reason it is right about every one of them, and there is a second reason now and the second reason is a different kind of reason.**

The round goes on a Monday and a Thursday. It went on Monday the twentieth and it was one man short, as it has been one man short since the twenty-eighth of December. It did not go on Wednesday the twenty-second, and it was not going to.

**The only person who stood in the weather in eleven streets on Wednesday the twenty-second of July was a man of about sixty at a shed at Cardew Docks, eleven minutes away, who has been there since before the war, and who is not on the Row on a Wednesday and has not been since before the war, and who does not know that a piece of paper exists and is not going to be told.** And the second reason, eleven miles away, is that a district entered as nil has not got a second side of anything and could not have one.

The plate in the grass at the low end of Bridge Street is a hundred and seven days old. The grass has closed over the place where it went down and it is being cut on the Sunday by somebody who is not the works and not the council and is not anybody this district has a name for. Nobody has reported it. Nobody has been asked about it. That is a decision and it was made on the first of June and it has been told to nobody, and there are four people in this city who are not told about it and one of them is Milo Rook.

---

**And on Thursday the twenty-third of July the gang was on the back length at half past seven, eleven men and a foreman with a clipboard, and it was the thirty-second walk since the first of April.**

The round went out at about ten past eight. It is one man short. The man of about twenty-six in a high-visibility jacket was on the length and did not say anything, and the condition column on that morning's sheet had nothing in it, and the reason column had nothing in it, and both of those were correct.

**The condition column has had two entries in it in a hundred and sixty-one days and it has had them both in eleven days, and they are the same four words and the same figure, and they are on the thirteenth and the twentieth of July, and a works clerk of forty-six said at a gate that the column is for a hole and a hole is an event and a state is not an event, and nobody has asked the column anything since and nobody is going to.**

The page home is the two hundred and forty-second day. The token is cold on the two hundred and twenty-eighth day, in the inside pocket of the coat, and it has done nothing since the seventh of December. The remembered-route list is eleven and the crossings are ten and the tally is ten lines, and he has not looked at the tally since the thirteenth of January.

---

**And at about eight o'clock on that Thursday morning, with eleven men on a length and a foreman with a clipboard and a man of about twenty-six on the wall, a bell went once.**

It came from past the end of the length. Not from the near end, where the works yard is. Past the end, and further than the wall, and it was one stroke and it was not far, and it did not go again.

Milo Rook stood in the middle of a length of road with a pencil in his hand and did not move for about four seconds.

There is a gate at a gate in eleven streets and there is a handcart through it four times a day. There is a school at the other end of this district and it has gone in about 1968 as a school and the building is something else. There is a landing four miles away with a bell frame on it, or there is not. **He does not know what it was. He wrote the time down, which was eight o'clock, and he wrote the word *bell* next to it, and a word is not a finding, and he has written the word down in a book in a coat and has not written down what it was.**

**He did not tell anybody.** That is the ninth thing of that shape in eight months and it is the first one that has cost nothing, and he has written down that it cost nothing and that he does not know whether that is because it is smaller or because it is going to be bigger.

---

**And at about half past four on the Thursday afternoon he sat on the low wall at the north end of the length with the Field Book out of the bag and the rule on the facing page in front of him, and he wanted a seventh column, and he did not draw it.**

The condition column wants to know what a length was like and has been used for a hundred and sixty-one days to record a hole. What he wanted, on that wall, at half past four, was a column that wanted to know what a sound was. **He had it in his hand in about eleven seconds. Six words and a head. It would have been the best one he has ever drawn and it would have been the same mistake for the tenth time, and the mistake is a column that wants a person, and there is no person in it — there is a length and a time and a word.**

The rule on the facing page is in his own handwriting and says **no seventh column before the first of September**, and it is eleven days off, and he wrote in the margin of the page, in pencil, and it is the first time in eight months he has written a date on a rule he made:

*I want one. That is the whole of it. I want a column that takes a sound, and there is no such column, and a sound at eight o'clock on Thursday the twenty-third is not a thing I can put in a box without putting a man in it, and there is no man. **I am not going to wait for the first of September either and I want that written down in the same hand as the rule, because the rule is about a month and what I am actually doing is about me.***

---

*Field Book, Thursday the twenty-third of July, 23.20, a front room in eleven streets, one day off the twenty-second.*

*One. **The list on the facing page reads twenty-nine and it has not moved since the sixteenth of July and it is not going to move in August, and the reason is on the page and it is the only reason I have got for anything this month.** I have written an entry on most nights since the sixteenth. An interval of one day is not a pair whether or not either of the two days has anything on it, and a night after a night is one day, and I have three weeks of consecutive nights in this book and not one pair in it, and **the list does not move and that is correct and it is the fifth time I have said that sentence and I have improved it none of the five.** A pair is a fact about where I was and not about what happened, and twenty-nine is the number of times the distance between two of my own nights has been a number instead of a day.*

*Two. **The count.** Twelve Mondays and Thursdays between today and the thirty-first of August, and six Fridays, and one of the twelve is the last day of the contract and the last of the six is the last sheet that will ever be printed for this gang, and there is a seventh Friday after that with nothing in the rack in it. **Those are the three numbers this district has for the month of August and none of them is on a form and there is no column that wants any of them, and a form about a district with six columns on it and four of them about money cannot be made to want a week.** I have been in this district eight months and that is the first time the thing I have been counting has been the thing that matters, and I have not put it anywhere, and I have written it in a book in a coat.*

*Three. **A bell, once, at about eight, from past the end of the length, on a Thursday, with eleven men on the road and nobody looking up.** One stroke. Not near. I wrote the time and the word and that is all. **I am not going to go and find out what it was this week. I have got a rule on a facing page about a column and I have written on the same page that I want a column, and the wanting is the tenth of the ten and the first one where the thing I want is not a person and is worse, because a column that wants a person is at least about somebody.***

*Four. **The page home the two hundred and forty-second day, blank, four marks in four hands and none of them mine. The token cold, the two hundred and twenty-eighth day. Eleven remembered routes, ten crossings, the tally is ten lines, Hettie Brann's paper and Dr Sen's pocket, not looked at since the thirteenth of January and not going to be looked at in August. The condition column is a hundred and sixty-one days empty and has two entries in it, both in July, both four words. The reason column is empty. The plate is a hundred and eight days and has been told to nobody. The second tin is at thirty-one,** and it does not go to thirty-two until tomorrow morning in a building eleven miles away, and the tin does not know that tomorrow is a rack day either. A foreman of fifty-four is thirteen weeks and two days past four months and is on a gate and not in a building and nobody is going to ask him, and that silence is on the facing page of this book in my own handwriting and I put it there and I cannot get it out of it.*

*And the standing position: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.**
