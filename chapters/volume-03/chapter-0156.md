# Chapter 156: A Bell At A Landing, And A Man Who Is Not Going To Be There Tomorrow

**The flatbed was in Mireward at about half past ten on Friday the fourteenth of August and he got off where the driver was going and walked, and the second tin went to thirty-five in a building eleven miles away this morning and the plate went to a hundred and thirty days in the grass and the page home went to the two hundred and sixty-fourth day and the token to the two hundred and fiftieth and the condition column to a hundred and eighty-three days empty, and none of those five numbers is on any paper he can reach and none of them is about anything that is happening today.**

He got to the landing at about twelve.

---

**Garn Ollerton is thirty-seven and has run the water on four landings for twenty-six years and there is no piece of paper on any of them.** Her method is on the page already and she gave it to him in June and it is the only route instrument in this series written by somebody who was not going to be on it: if you want to know whether somebody can get from the fourth landing to your office, you do not ask the fourth landing; you stand on the fourth landing and you watch them come in, and you go backwards from the boat, and the last thing you do is the first thing they will do, which is get off it.

**She was sitting on the low step at the top landing with her hands round a mug when he came up, and she had seen him from about two hundred yards, and she did not get up.**

"**You have come out on the Friday again.**"

"I have come out on the Friday again."

"**The Tuesday lorry goes to a yard at the north end and it does not go to a landing, and the Friday one comes in at half past ten and goes back at four, and you have been out on four of them since the end of June, and there is no bus and there has not been one since about 1961.**" She drank some of it. "**What have you come out for. And I want the true one and I have got about four of the true ones left.**"

---

"Three of them," said Milo Rook. "There is one at a top track above a holding and a man rings it twice before he starts and once when he stops. There is one on a bracket over a schoolroom door in Cobb Lock and a woman of thirty-four rings it once and a child pulls the rope and nobody knows how many pulls is right. And I have not got the other one and I have got a bell on a post at the old ford that I heard nine times in June from about four hundred yards off and could not put a ring on any of them."

He had not meant to say it. He has written down that he had it ready before he got to the step and that he said it anyway, and that a man who has a sentence ready before he arrives somewhere has already decided that somewhere is where the sentence goes.

Garn Ollerton put the mug down on the step beside her.

"**Then that is four.**"

---

**And that is the whole of the recovery in this month, and it took about two seconds, and she had not been asked, and it cost her something, and it cost him nothing, and he did not know for about four days that it was the best hour he had had since the twenty-sixth of June.**

"**I am not going to tell you where the other two are and I am not going to tell you which of the three you have got wrong, and I have said four and that is one more than I have said to anybody in twenty-six years and I said it because you said the nine and I have never once in my life wanted somebody else to know how many times something was rung.**"

"You did not have to say four."

"**No. I did not have to say it and I have been not saying it since 1979 and I have just said it to a man in a wet coat on my own step, and I would like it noticed that I have not been thanked and I am not going to be, and I have said that in about nine words before and I am not going to say it again.**"

---

**And then she told him the other thing, and she told it because she had decided to and not because he had got there, and that is a distinction she has been making since the twenty-fifth of June and has never explained to him and never will.**

"There is a frame on the wall at the top of this step and there is a bell on it. It is about four inches across the mouth and it came off a boat."

"What is it for."

"**It is for the first one of the day and it is for the last one off, and it is for a boat that is coming in the dark, and it has been for all three of those since before I was doing this and I have done all three of them and I have never once written any of it down, and there is no form for it and there is not going to be one.**"

---

"And you ring it every day," said Milo Rook.

"**Every day.**"

"Even when nothing comes in and nothing goes out."

"**Every day. At ten past seven and at four in the afternoon, and there are days when I am not on this water at either of those and I do it anyway, and on Sundays, and I have done it every day since about 1979 and I have not missed one.**"

She said it flatly, the way a person says a figure they have never had cause to check.

Then she said the rest of it, and it is about four sentences and she gave them once.

"**If I do not do it, it does not get done. And if it gets done, it is because somebody is here. And I would rather it was because a bell went off on a wall.**" She picked the mug up. "**You are going to tell me that is a mistake. You are going to tell me a thing that happens every day at the same hour is a timetable, and I have heard that before, off a man, on a Saturday in June, and you are right about it and you can have it, and I have got twenty-six years and about nine people who use this landing and a route that cannot be produced cannot be asked after, and I have decided which of those three things I would rather have, and it is not the one you would pick.**"

"I have not said anything."

"**No. You have not. You have been standing on my step for about ten minutes with your hands in your pockets and you have not said anything, and that is twice now, and the first time was the twenty-fifth of June and I remember it.**"

---

**And she was right about the first half of it and he knew she was right about the first half of it before she had finished, and he did not say the second half, and the second half is this: a thing that happens every day at the same hour is a timetable, and a timetable in this city is a document with a column on it, and the moment a bell at a landing is rung every day at ten past seven it stops being a thing four people do and becomes a service, and a service is a thing somebody is responsible for, and the moment anybody in this district is responsible for it there is a form with a signature on it, and the four people stop.**

**That is the sixth wrong-shaped thing he has found in eight months and it is the only one he has found in somebody else's work and did not put there, and he has written it down and he has not improved it, and he has not said it to her and he is not going to.**

*A bell on a wall at ten past seven and four o'clock, every day, for twenty-six years, by a woman of thirty-seven who would rather it was the bell than a person. **A practice is not a place and it is not a day. She has made hers a day, on purpose, because a day is a thing that keeps going without you, and a day is also a thing that a department can be told about, and those two are the same fact and she has decided to live on it. She is right about her thirty-one years and wrong about the other thing and she will not be told and there is nobody in this district who is going to tell her and there is a man in a wet coat who could and is not going to.***

---

**And he got on the flatbed at ten to four and it was in the yard at the city end at about half past four, and he had asked her nothing, had offered her nothing, and had said the word thank you to one man on a bridge and to nobody else in fourteen days.**

**There is a plate in the grass at the low end of Bridge Street that is a hundred and thirty days old. There is a written line home in a book that is the two hundred and sixty-fourth day and has four marks in four hands in it and none of them are his. There is a brass token in the inside pocket of a coat that is cold on the two hundred and fiftieth day and has done nothing since the seventh of December. There is a list in the same book with twenty-nine pairs on it that has not moved since the sixteenth of July and is not going to move in August, because he has an entry on most nights and an interval of one day is not a pair.**

**And there are four bells, and he is the only person in this city who knows there are four, and he has not written the four down, and he has not told anybody, and there are four people who have not been asked whether it is four.**
