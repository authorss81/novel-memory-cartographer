# Chapter 145: Five Men And A Bar And Not One Of Them On Paper

**The sluice is on the top track and it belongs to a body that was abolished in about 1968.** Coll Sarrowe holds a bar on it on the Tuesdays and the Fridays when the ground is soft, and he has done that for thirty-one years, and he is paid nothing for it out of a farm wage by the hour for ditching, and the farm pays him for the Tuesday and the Tuesday is not a ditch.

**The page home is the two hundred and thirty-third day and the token is cold on the two hundred and nineteenth day, and on the Monday the thirteenth of July the gang walked the back length for the twenty-ninth time since the first of April and the sheet was right about his not being on it, and nobody telephoned, and on the Thursday the sixteenth they will walk for the thirtieth time and the sheet will be right about that as well.**

He was on the top track at about ten on Tuesday the fourteenth with nothing in his hands, because it is not his ground and he had not asked to be on it.

---

"There are five of you."

"There are five of us."

"That is not what I asked."

"**Then you asked it wrong.**"

---

He was working the bar and the four of them were working the other three, in a line, in about ninety feet of bank, and Milo Rook stood at the top of it for about half an hour and did not understand any of it and worked out all of it.

The man of forty-four did not introduce the other four. **He did not say their names and he did not say where they lived and he did not say how long they had been doing it, and when Milo Rook asked how long they had been doing it he said *longer than you have been asking*,** and that was the end of it.

They are not in eleven streets and they are not in Mireward. One of them lives at the north end. **One of them is about nineteen and cannot read the water at all and can lift.**

He watched the whole thing twice.

---

"**You have four men on a bank in a district with no rateable unit and you have had them for some years.**"

"Nine years."

"Nine years and you have never said so."

"**I have never said so to a man in a good coat and I am not going to start on a Tuesday.**"

---

**And then, at about half past eleven, Coll Sarrowe did the changed thing, and he did it without stopping work, and it was not a sentence.**

The nineteen-year-old was on the wrong end of the run.

**Coll Sarrowe walked him up and down the ninety feet four times, by the arm, without a word, and then put him on the other end and went back to the bar.** That is the whole of the changed method and it took about nine minutes and it was not about the water and it was not about a bar, and it was about what a man does on a Tuesday in a wet week when the man who put him on that end is on a farm an hour away.

"He is not the water," said Milo Rook.

"**He is the Tuesday.**"

---

**And then he stopped, and wiped his hands, and looked at Milo Rook for about four seconds, and said the only sentence he said that was not about water and about a bar.**

"I have been getting them ready for nine years and I have never once told them I was getting them ready, **and I have never once written it down, and there is going to be a day when I am not able to come and there is going to be five of us here and not one of us will be on a piece of paper about it, and that is not a fault and it is the only thing I have got.**"

He went back to the bar.

"**You are not going to thank me for that.**"

"No."

"**Good. Do not thank me for that. If you thank me for that then in about four years somebody is going to ask you what I said, and then I am a man who said something, and then I am on a piece of paper, and there have been four of us named in this district in thirty-one years and three of them left inside two years and the one who is still here is the one who is not.**"

---

**And Milo Rook stood on the top of a bank for another half an hour after that and was not helped by a single sentence that was said on it, and worked it out by himself, on the walk back, in about eleven minutes, and it is the third best hour he has had since the first of April and it is worse than Garn's and it cost nothing.**

*A place is not stable because nothing has happened in it. A place is stable because there is nobody writing down what happens in it.*

**And this district has got five men and a bar on it and not one of them is on any paper in this city, and the water authority cannot bill Coll Sarrowe for the water he is holding and cannot employ him to hold it and cannot write a letter to him that would arrive, and the sluice belongs to a body that was abolished in about 1968, and there is no form in this city for a man with a tractor going into a dyke, and there was one rescue in nine years and it took two hours and the chain came off the second time.**

**And there is a printed instrument about a mile away with four grounds on it and one of them is *no resident witness*, and this is what a no resident witness looks like from the inside: it looks like five men who cannot be named, doing a thing on a Tuesday, for nothing, in a district that is not a place, and if it stops there is no column for the stopping and no column for the doing and no form for a chain coming off the second time.**

**That is the recovery. It is not mine, it is nine years old, it is his, and it is not a word, and I have been helped by a man of forty-four on a Tuesday morning and I have not got one sentence to put in a book about it and I am not going to get one, and I am not going to do the thing I do.**

---

**And the thing I do is this: I have got a sentence in a bag in a room above a kitchen that says *why this district's works are not of record*, and it is about a hundred and ten words and it is true and it has no name in it, and it is the ninth one, and every one of the nine of them has been better than the last one, and I have put a hundred and ten words on a piece of paper about a place where five men are on a bank and not one of them is on paper.**

**The instrument wants a sentence about this district. The district is made of things that are not sentences.**

---

*Tuesday the fourteenth, 22.50. One day off the thirteenth.*

*One. **Five men and a bar, and four of them are not from this village, and the nineteen-year-old cannot read the water and can lift, and the man of forty-four has been getting them ready for nine years and has never said so to anybody who writes anything down. And he said the one sentence about it and then he said do not thank me for that, and I said no, and he said good, and that is the whole of the transaction and it is the ninth thing in this book that has been a man telling me what not to do and it is the only one I have wanted written down.***

*Two. **The sluice belongs to a body that was abolished in about 1968, and the water authority cannot bill a man with no address and cannot employ him and cannot write a letter to him that would arrive, and a district with no address cannot have a second man on anything, and that is not an injustice and nobody has done anything wrong and it is a design.** I have now got that sentence about this district twice — once about a postman and once about a bar — and the two of them are the same sentence about two different jobs and I am not going to join them, and a reader of this book is going to, and the joining is a pattern and a pattern is me deciding two things are the same.*

*Three. **The page home the two hundred and thirty-third day and I am not writing that it is there. The token cold, the two hundred and nineteenth day. Eleven remembered routes, ten crossings, the ninth still unexplained and I am not going to explain it before the end of July. The condition column is a hundred and fifty-two days empty. The reason column is empty. The plate is ninety-nine days in the grass. The second tin is at thirty and the last rack day was Friday the tenth.***

*Four. **I did not thank him and I am not going to and I have written down here, in a book in a bag, in a village, at eleven at night, that a man of forty-four was thanked by nobody on a Tuesday and did not want to be and that I have got no sentence about it, and that this is the second time in eight months that the best thing that has happened to me has happened because somebody refused me, and the first one was a woman with a folding rule who put my own figure right in four seconds and cost nothing.***

*And the standing position: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.**
