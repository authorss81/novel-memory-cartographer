# Chapter 148: Two Inches Of Ruled Space At The Bottom Of A Card

**Sunday the nineteenth of July was an ordinary Sunday and the round did not go, and there was no gang on a Sunday, and about four hundred yards of grass on the far side of a fence at the low end was being cut on a Sunday by somebody who is not the works and not the council and is not anybody this district has a name for, and the second side of the sheet of who does what and on which day is right about today for the same reason it is right about every other day since the eighth of April, and a shed at Cardew Docks, and a district entered as nil eleven miles away.**

**He went out in the morning to look at a hundred and forty feet and eleven inches at the low end of Bridge Street, from the corner end, alone, for the fourth time since the twenty-eighth of April. The gutter peg has not moved a tenth of an inch in fourteen weeks and could not have, because the low end floods about twice in four years and the water has not been here since February. The hundred and forty feet and eleven inches is a hundred and forty feet and eleven inches and there is no box in this city for it and there is going to be no box in this city for it and a kettle moved it in the direction of a fence in May, correctly, and nobody is going to be told.**

**The plate is a hundred and four days in the grass. The grass has closed over the place where it went down. Nobody has reported it, nobody has been asked about it, and a woman of sixty-three with a hand barrow asked him in a doorway on the twelfth of June and he did not tell the works, and that is a decision and not a refusal and it was made on the first of June and there is a woman of sixty-one at a counter, a man of fifty-eight with a pad, a woman of sixty-three with a barrow and a man of about fifty with a machine who are all not told about it.**

---

**And at about twenty past four on the Sunday afternoon he was in a front room with a district returns card on the table in front of him.**

> **MIREWARD. Rateable units: nil. Returns received: nil. Reason: —**

and two inches of ruled space at the bottom that has been there for about forty years and that almost nobody fills in.

**He did not have the schedule with him. He had been carrying it for four days and he had left it in a bag in a room in eleven streets at about one o'clock, on purpose, and he has written down that it was on purpose and that on Saturday morning in a room in a village he had not done it on purpose and that the difference between those two things is about a day and a half and he does not know what happened in the day and a half.**

---

**And here is the whole of it, and it took about four seconds to do and about forty minutes to be able to sit here and write it down.**

A district with no rateable unit has no correspondence address, and therefore has no envelope, and therefore has nobody to complete a form for it, and therefore has no row completed on the sixteenth, and therefore has a sentence in column five that says a thing about a place with no answer in it.

**The form says: to be completed by the person responsible for maintenance in the district. And there is no such person in Mireward, because a person responsible for maintenance is a person at an address, and there is no address, and that is not a refusal and it is not a mystery, and a woman of thirty-four said it in June in the plainest words anybody has said anything in this book: *a village that has no address is not a place that has been forgotten; a place that has been forgotten was on something and came off it; this was never on anything.***

**And the district's own use of the bottom of that card is two inches of ruled space, and there is nobody in it, and a man of thirty is a person, and he is not a ratepayer and is not a resident and is not responsible for maintenance in any district in this city, and none of the three facts stops him writing his own name in a space that is there for the district to use.**

He wrote it at about twenty-five past four.

> *Received 17 July. The district has no rateable unit and no correspondence address and no person responsible for maintenance. The works of record in this district are a number cast into the collar of a culvert and about nine feet of drain that a man of fifty-eight writes four lines about once a year into a pad in a drawer in Crown Heights. — M. Rook, surveyor.*

**And then he stopped, and read it, and it is the shortest thing he has written in eight months and it is the only one of the nine or the ten that he wrote on a form about a place, and it is the only one he signed.**

---

**And four things about it, said in the order he worked them out, and the fourth one is the reason he left it there.**

**One. It is not the six words.** He does not know whose hand *asked, nothing came* is in, he has not found out, he is not going to find out by asking a machine, and there is no connection between six words in a reason column on the seventeenth of June and a paragraph in the district's own use of a card on the nineteenth of July, and he has written that down in this book for the first time because a reader of this book is going to put the two together inside a week, **and putting them together would be him deciding they are the same, and he has done that three times in eight months about three other things and each of the three was wrong.**

**Two. It is not an instrument and it is not a form and it is not a return and it is not a record.** It is a paragraph in two inches of ruled space that has been there for about forty years, and the district is not a place and the space is not a repository and nobody has asked for it and nobody is going to.

**Three. It cost him four seconds and he is the fifth of his own five and it is the first one he has paid for.**

The plate was about a person and it cost him not saying a thing to a woman of sixty-three with a hand barrow. The seventh column on a table in his own office was about a person and it cost a boy of seventeen nothing and a man of forty-one four seconds of not-asking. The not-telling was about a person and it has cost Mara Quill eleven weeks and whatever it is going to cost after that. **The seventh column before the first of September was about a habit, and stopping it cost Roy Pryce a silence that is now partly in my handwriting.** And this one is about a person, and the person is me, and I have put my name on a piece of paper in a building about a mile away for the first time in eight months, on purpose, in a space that was left blank on purpose, and nobody asked me to.

**Four. He wrote it face down and did not put it in the machine and there is a reason, and the reason is the whole of why it works, and the reason is worth more than what he wrote.**

**A card put down face up goes back through the machine once a week on a line at the foot of a run sheet headed *re-run — face up only*, and it comes back as a second piece of paper with a reference number in the top left corner in a series running from something like 41,000 upward, and about four hundred of those go out of that room a week, and a sentence with a reference on it is a thing a person can be asked for.**

So he did not put it face up. **The hand stays on the paper. The machine does not take the hand over.**

**And that is the first time in eight months that I have wanted the machine not to take a hand over, and I have not written down what that is, and I am not going to, and I have written down that I am not going to, which is the same shape of thing and about nine of them and every one correct.**

---

**And on Sunday the nineteenth of July, at about five to six in the evening, a district of a hundred and forty people with no rateable unit and no street number since 1911 and no envelope and no correspondence address had a man's name on a piece of paper in it for the first time in about sixty years.**

**And the nil is still a nil. The stability column is still hand-transferred and it is still not typed and there is still a row for it and the row is as wide as the others. The three nils about Mireward are three and this is the same zero. Nobody has been frightened. Nobody has been asked for anything. A district that is not a place cannot be frightened, and I have given a place a name, and the name is mine, and it is not a rateable unit and it is not an occupier and it is not a witness, and it is the only one of the five things I have given up that I cannot get back.**

**Because a card with a thing struck through it is a card with a mark on it, and the re-run is on a Friday at about four, and about four hundred of them go out of that room a week.**

---

*Sunday the nineteenth, 23.20. One day off the eighteenth.*

*One. **Two inches of ruled space and a paragraph and a signature and about four seconds, and it is the fifth of five and the first of the five I have paid for and the first of the five that is about a person, and the person is me.** The four before it: the plate, the table, the not-telling, and the rule on the facing page, and the four of them cost somebody else something and this one cost me a name on a piece of paper, and the name is retrievable, and about four years from now a room can produce this paragraph and there will be one name in it and it will be mine.*

*Two. **It is not the six words and I have written that down here for the first time because somebody is going to join them. *Asked. Nothing came* is in a hand on a card on the seventeenth of June and I have said it out loud twice and it is not in this book. A paragraph in the district's own use of a card on the nineteenth of July is not a sentence anybody is going to be sent to look at, and if it is, it is a paragraph with a number cast into a culvert collar in it and that number was on a printed side in May and it was on a printed side before I put a hand on it on Friday, and I did not write the number down and I am not going to.** Joining the two is a pattern and a pattern is me deciding two things are the same, and I have done that three times in eight months and I was wrong three times.*

*Three. **A resident witness is a person on a list, and I have written a sentence about that in this book in June and in July, and a man of thirty is a person, and there is a piece of paper in a building a mile away on which he now appears, and a district that is not a place cannot be frightened, and I have not reduced the nil and I have not made the column typed and I have not added a row, and I have put my own name in a space that was left blank so that a form would not have a blank in it, and that is the whole of the motive and it is the eighth time in eight months I have done something to stop a column being empty.***

*Four. **The page home the two hundred and thirty-eighth day, blank, four marks in four hands and none of them mine. The token cold, the two hundred and twenty-fourth day. Eleven remembered routes, ten crossings, the ninth unexplained, and I have said I am not going to explain it before the end of July and it is the nineteenth and I am holding to it. The tally is ten lines. The condition column is a hundred and fifty-seven days empty. The reason column is empty. The plate is a hundred and four days. The second tin is at thirty-one and the last rack day was Friday the seventeenth. The walk is on Monday the twentieth and it will be the thirty-first since the first of April, and the last walk of the season will be on **Monday the thirty-first of August, which is the last day of the contract and is itself a gang day**, and a rack day is a Friday, and the last one will be Friday the twenty-eighth, **and there will be no sheet for the week of the thirty-first, so that walk will not be on anything, and the second tin will stop at thirty-seven, and the rack will still be in the corridor on Friday the fourth of September with nothing in it in it.***

*And the standing position: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.*
