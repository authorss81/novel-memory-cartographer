# Chapter 183: The Machinery Is Still Printing, And There Is Still No Chair, And A Counter That Is Not A Post

**The round went out at about ten past eight on Monday the ninth of November and came in at about four with the bag empty, and it is a Monday and a Thursday round and it has been since the second of January and it is one man short, and the bag has been empty since the twenty-eighth of August, and it is not a walk and nobody has called it one.**

The page home is the three hundred and fifty-first day. The token is cold on the three hundred and thirty-seventh. The plate in the grass at the low end of Bridge Street is two hundred and seventeen days old and has been told to nobody. The condition column is two hundred and seventy days and is not there. **A foreman of fifty-four is twenty-eight weeks and six days past four months and nobody is going to ask him.**

---

## The Row, and the brush, and the trays, and a hundred yards of nothing that is correct

The man of about forty was on the Row at about eight with a brush and did not stop and has still not been asked for his twenty-eight undated words and is not going to be.

The fishmonger of about forty-five had her trays out by nine and was not thanked and was not asked and said nothing, and **nobody keeps this Row, and that answer has been said and is not going to be said again, and this morning it did not need saying because nobody asked.**

**The barriers were still out on the back length and the road was open.** That is the ninth morning since the thirty-first of October and it is the ninth morning since Monday the thirty-first of August that there has been nobody on that road at half past seven, and both of those facts are correct, and a site is not cleared by a date.

**And a rack, four rooms above a fitter's shop, open at the front, about four feet high, with nothing in it in it.** It has had nothing in it in it since the fourth of September. There is a mark on the shelf about nine inches from the left edge and about two inches wide and it has not changed, and **nobody empties a rack, and nothing in this city says a rack is finished, and there is no instrument anywhere for a mark that has not changed, and he did not go and look at it on the Friday and he is not going to look at it on this one.**

The second tin is on a bottom shelf in a plan room in another building, under a repair schedule, at **thirty-seven, and there is no thirty-eight**, and the tin is not going anywhere, and the repair schedule is a repair schedule and nobody has asked when it ends and he is not going to ask.

---

## The machinery is still printing, and what it prints about this district is nothing, and that is on the schedule

A district returns card comes off a machine in a room about a mile away on a Wednesday morning, about nine inches by five, four columns across the top and about two inches of ruled space at the bottom. It goes into a bundle on a Thursday morning. It is not printed for this district because **this district has no rateable unit and a district with no rateable unit is not printed**, and the season's work went out on the first of October with a box in it that wants a length of frontage in yards, and the office completed that row on a Thursday in about a minute and a half out of a contract that expired on the thirty-first of August, and it is done, and nobody has to do anything again this season.

**And that is the whole of the printing and it took him about four minutes on the Monday morning to accept that it is a comfort rather than a defeat, and he has not decided which and is not going to.**

**A district that returns nothing is a district with nothing to report. A district with nothing to report is a district where there is nothing to insure against. That is ground (d) on a key printed on the second side of a district schedule in the middle of May, and the key is correct, and in October that ground was used to write down a village of a hundred and forty people eleven miles the other way, and the key is still correct, and he has got nothing against it.**

## And there is still no chair

**There is no chair. There has been no chair since the fourteenth of April and there is no date and the matter stands over and is not adjourned and is on no list, and it was not on the business of anything this month and is not going to be on the business of anything next month, and nobody has asked for one and he is not going to ask, and a thing with no date on it cannot be brought forward by anything that happens in it.**

He wrote that down in about eleven seconds on the Monday morning and it is the same sentence he has written six times since the fourteenth of April and he has improved it none of them, and **a sentence written seven times is not a rule and is not a resolution and is a third thing, and he said that about a different sentence in October and he is not going to improve this one either.**

## At about half past ten, at the end of the lane behind Bridge Street, four feet off, for about ten minutes

There is a lane about two hundred yards long that comes off the low end of Bridge Street and goes back towards the drain, and at the end of it there is a shed and a man of about sixty with it.

**He does not know that a piece of paper exists. Nothing that has happened since the sixth of April has touched him and there is no form anywhere for a man who is not on anything.**

Milo Rook stood about four feet off him for about ten minutes on the Monday morning and watched him do something to a length of chain that had nothing to do with a length of chain, and the man did not stop and did not look up, and **Milo Rook did not ask him a single question, and he wrote in his own book that night that he had stood four feet from a man for ten minutes on the ninth of November and had wanted to ask him about the far side of the bridge, and had not, and that this was the first of the two occasions and he had not known at the time that it was one of two.**

He wrote it in about four minutes and he did not put a place in it, and there is a reason for that which is on the page in Chapter 189 and not here.

## The water clinic at the water steps, the third consulting room, and a counter that is not a post

Hettie Brann is sixty-one and has been on that counter nineteen years and she was there at about ten past eleven and there was nobody in the room.

**He asked her one question and it was a fair one.** *Has anything come back for the district.*

"No."

"**And it is not going to.**"

"No."

"**And if it did.**"

"**Then I would not take it in.**" She did not look up, because she was counting, because it was a Monday and it is a counter. "**You have come to ask me whether this counter is a post for a district. It is not, and it is not going to be, and I have known that since about 1975 and I have never tested it, and you are the first person who has made me say it out loud and I would rather I had not.**"

"Why not."

"**Because the moment a piece of paper for a district comes through this door, this counter is a place a piece of paper is kept, and a place a piece of paper is kept is a repository, and a repository is a place, and there are three pieces of paper face down under this counter that have been counted out loud in a yard in April, and there is a woman of sixty on a second landing in a building with no number who is not on anything, and neither of them is going to be found through me.**" She finished the row she was counting. "**The clinic is a clinic. If it is also where the papers go, then in about four years somebody is going to send a card here for a district that has no address, and I am going to have to write something in a box about a place I have never been to, and I am not going to do that.**"

"That is right," he said.

"**I know it is right. It is not a favour to you and it is not a favour to anybody and I have not said it to be helpful, and do not thank me, because a person who has thanked somebody is a person who has asked.**"

He did not thank her.

**And nothing came back, and nothing is going to come back, and the row in the schedule is done, and the figure in it is correct, and the counter is a counter. A correct refusal that changes nothing and costs the woman who makes it about four seconds of her working morning, and she is sixty-one, and she has been on that counter nineteen years, and nobody asked her to say any of it and she said all of it.**

## And two other things that happened on an ordinary Monday, and neither of them is a thing

**A man of fifty-eight went out into Mireward on the first dry week of November with a rod and a chain and wrote four lines about about nine feet of drain into a pad in a drawer in Crown Heights.** He has done that once a year since about 1974. **Nobody has ever asked him for one, nobody asked him for one this month, he is not an office, and he did not become one, and there is no form in this city for asking a man of fifty-eight for a pad.**

**A man of about fifty in a room with four machines sent a district's card back in a bin on a Friday in September and has not been asked about it since, and he is not in eleven streets and he is not on anything, and there is a description of the office he is in on a printed side that is three weeks old and has been acted on by nobody.**

*Field Book, Monday the ninth of November, 23.20, a front room in eleven streets, one day off the eighth.*

*One. **I wrote no entry about Tuesday the third and I am going to say why here so that I do not make it a rule.** A day I spent in a schoolroom is not a day I had. There are four benches and about twenty children in that room and a lath with a line burnt on it and a bell on a bracket over a door with a new rope on it, and a roll in a woman's head is a roll in a woman's head and not a sheet, and a day in a room with a person in it is a room that belongs to the person. **I have got a field book and she has got a lath. I asked her a question I had the answer to and I knew I had the answer to while I was asking it, and she told me it was two questions wearing one coat, and I could not ask the question I had come with because I had not come with one.**

*Two. **The machinery is still printing and it has printed nothing about this district, and I have decided that this is not a defeat and I have not decided what it is.** A row that is done is a row that is done and there is no second printing and no season in this district's name. Ground (d) is the reason and the key is correct and in October it wrote down a village eleven miles away and there is still nothing to be angry at.

*Three. **There is still no chair.** No date, not adjourned, on no list, not on the business of anything. Seventh time I have written that sentence. **A sentence written seven times is not a rule and is not a resolution and is a third thing, and there is a woman of thirty-one in a records room about a mile away who has a line in a return she reconciles on a Thursday and has never described to anybody in about two years, and I am not going to ask her, and a promise about not asking is the only kind of promise this book has ever held.**

*Four. **Four feet off a man of about sixty at the end of a lane for ten minutes, and I wanted to ask him about the far side of the bridge, and I did not, and I have written down that I did not, and I did not write down the place, and I have not written down the day either, and a man who writes down that he did not do a thing has still done the thing.** He does not know that a piece of paper exists and he is not going to be told and I am not going to tell him. **I stood four feet off him and I did not ask. He does not know that a piece of paper exists, and there is no answer I could give him that is not a sentence about a person, and that is going to be the reason every single time, and a reason is not a rule and I am not going to call it a habit.**

*Five. **A counter that is not a post.** A woman of sixty-one who has been on it nineteen years will not take in a district's paper, and gave the reason in about eleven seconds without being asked for a document, and it is right, and it changes nothing, and it is the only good thing that happened to me this week, and I did not thank her and she told me not to and she was right twice. **A correct refusal is a room that has not become a repository. That is all it has ever been and it is not a gift and it is not a rule and I am not going to put it in a column.**

*Six. **Nothing has moved.** Eleven remembered routes, ten crossings, the tally ten lines, not looked at since the thirteenth of January. The page home the three hundred and fifty-first day, blank, four marks in four hands, none of them mine. The token cold, the three hundred and thirty-seventh day, and it has done nothing since the seventh of December. **The list on the facing page reads twenty-nine and it has not moved in one hundred and sixteen days, and an interval of one day is not a pair, and a pair is a fact about where I was and not about what happened, and if the count of pairs in this month is small and not large that is correct.** The condition column is two hundred and seventy days and is not there and the reason column is not there and no new figure is to be printed for either. The second tin is at thirty-seven and there is no thirty-eight. The plate is two hundred and seventeen days in the grass and has been told to nobody. A foreman of fifty-four is twenty-eight weeks and six days past four months and nobody is going to ask him. **The four unshowable things in five months are four and there has been no fifth and I am not counting them and I am not going to start.**

*And the standing position: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.**
