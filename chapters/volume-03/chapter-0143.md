# Chapter 143: As Far As The Third Post

**The flatbed comes in.** It leaves the city end at about a quarter to seven on a Friday morning and it is here in Mireward at about half past ten, with the week's post and the sacks on the bed, and it goes back to the city on the Friday afternoon and is in the yard there at about half past four, and it does not wait either way. There are two motor vehicles a week in Mireward and neither of them waits, and the other one is a lorry to a yard at the north end that comes in on a Thursday morning, and there is one tractor, and there has not been a bus since about 1961.

He was standing at the gate at ten to seven on Friday the tenth of July with a bag and a coat over his arm, and the driver did not ask him anything, and he got off here at about half past ten where the driver was going, and the driver went back at about four in the afternoon.

**He did not go out on the tenth in order to be in Mireward. He went out on the tenth because the last day for the district's answer was the tenth and the only vehicle that would have carried an envelope back to a building about eleven miles away went back at about four, and he was standing at a gate at ten to seven with the form in his bag, and he has written down in his own book that the second reason is the one that was true and the first one is the one he would have written in.**

**The weekly sheet went into the rack on the Friday the tenth of July in a building about eleven miles away, and the second tin on the bottom shelf of the plan room went to thirty, and neither of those needed him and nobody telephoned and the sheet is right about that as well, and neither of those is anywhere he went that day.**

---

**And at about half past ten on the Friday morning he was on the far side of the bridge, which is the side the file says is EXT.**

He had crossed it in June in about nine seconds and nobody stopped him and there was nobody on either end. That was a fortnight ago and he had not been back, and he had told nobody he was going and nobody asked, and there is no bus and there has not been one since about 1961, and the eleven yards of grass between the kerb and the raised path is not grass you can walk across in July.

**He went along the raised path.** The post was where it has been since about 1979, and it is still four inches shorter than the one it took out, and the file says *replaced like for like*, and it was not, and about nine families use it, and there is nobody in Crown Heights who can say which post that was.

**Nobody has levelled it and nobody is going to.** The woman who runs the landing said in June that she does not want it fixed, and that she wants it written down that it is low, because the day a man comes out here with a level and puts it right, about nine families lose the only thing they have got, and there is no form in this city for a thing that nine families use and that is not a building. He has not asked her about it again and he has not written it down anywhere, and it is written down here and this is a book in a coat.

---

**And the boundary on this side of the bridge is not a thing anybody walked, and that is the third kind in eight months and it is a column.**

He had the file with him because it is a quarter of an inch and it came in the same envelope as a schedule of works in 1974 and he had been in the same room as it in June. It is nine inspections in a hundred and thirty-one years, a load calculation revised twice, and a schedule of works with a column for *side*.

**Every entry in that column says CITY. Two of them say EXT. The key on the inside cover says *external — see the drainage board*, and the drainage board was abolished in about 1968 and its functions went to a body called the fen commission, and there is no fen commission on any letterhead in this city and has not been one since 1968.**

He stood in the eleven yards and read the key on the inside cover with the file open in his hand and the wind going through it.

**Half of this bridge has been maintained by this city for a hundred and thirty-one years. The other half has been maintained by nobody since 1968. And the half that is maintained is the half a lorry can stand on, and the half that is not maintained is the half that people walk on.** That was in June and it has not changed and it is not going to, and the man of about fifty with a mower has cut the city side for nine years and has never been over.

And the boundary itself — where the city's work stops, and where it has stopped since a body was abolished, and where nobody agreed to anything and no person has ever stopped anywhere.

**A boundary is a place where two people agreed to have stopped. The one at the low end of Bridge Street is a corner and a fence. The one in June was a mowing line and two things that stopped for the same reason and neither of them a person. This one is a column in a file, and the column is right, and the file is a quarter of an inch, and the two ends of the line it describes are a kerb and a bank about a hundred and thirty yards apart, and the paper that says where they are is in Crown Heights, and there is nobody standing at either of them.** All three are boundaries. He has written all three down and he has not joined them and a reader of this book is going to try, and joining them would be him deciding they are the same.

---

**And about two hundred yards along the path, on the same side, there are three posts.**

They are not in the file. They are not in any file he has ever been in. They are iron, about four feet out of the ground, set in a line that runs away from the bridge toward the water, and the first of them is about a hundred and thirty yards from the parapet and the second is about ninety yards from the first and the third is about ninety yards from the second, and the spacing is the spacing of something that was set out with a chain and not with a wheelbarrow.

**Two sets of marks are about ninety feet apart near the second one.** Two hands. The same water on the same days. Neither of them is in a file and the file is on the other side of the water. That is the whole of the June finding and it has not been added to, and he did not go and look at them again, and there is nothing in the two sets of marks and the three posts and the nineteen cuts in a post at a ford four miles away that is one thing, and the three sets are three sets.

---

**And beside the third post, in about four feet of ground, there is the head of a culvert.**

It is a pipe coming out of a bank at a low angle with a cast collar round the top of it, and the collar has a number cast into it, and Milo Rook took the schedule out of his bag and held it against the collar at about a quarter past eleven.

**The number on the collar is on that schedule, in column three, under *works of record*, in a typeface about half the size of the numbers on the other columns.** It is there in a run of about nine numbers, and it has a district reference against it that says MIREWARD, and the district reference says nil in the stability column, hand-transferred, because the column is not typed and has never been typed.

**A cast number on a drain is a work of record. That is exactly what it is and exactly what it is for, and it is the only piece of the eleven miles between the gate and this bank that anybody has ever put on paper, and it is a piece of paper about a pipe.**

He stood there for a while and did not write it down.

**Because a number on a collar is a thing a person can be asked for, and it has just gone from a thing in the ground to a thing on a form in a bag in a coat that goes into rooms, and nobody in this district has asked him for it and he has had it in his hand for about four minutes and he is not going to be the reason it is asked for.**

And the post is not on the schedule. The post is four feet away from the collar and it is not on the schedule and it is not in the file and it is on nobody's paper, and nobody put it there, or four men did, in a week, with a chain.

**The two of them are about four feet apart and one of them is a work of record in a district with no rateable unit and the other one is the reason a man of fifty-eight and a woman of thirty-seven and a man of forty-four can all say where the water is going, and I am not going to stand here and decide which of them the boundary is, and I have written that down, and the decision would be mine and it would be in about nine seconds and there is nobody to stop me making it.**

---

**And he went as far as the third post, and he did not go past it, and the reason is that there is no reason.**

Past the third post the path goes over the top of a bank, and the bank is the top of a dyke, and in a dry week in July a dyke top is a bank like any other bank and there is nothing on it and nobody on it and nothing to stop a man.

He stood at the third post for about eleven minutes.

**There is nobody on the far side to stop me, and there has not been for six years, and that is the whole of the reason, and I have got it written on the facing page of this book from the sixth of April about a fence at the low end of a different street, and I have never once walked over a fence since and I am not going to be the man who is the first thing to happen on a dyke top in Mireward in about nine years on a Friday in July because nobody thought of it.**

**And the true reason, which I am writing down because I have written the other one down twice and it is a smaller reason than the one I am actually using: a bar is about nine hundred yards that way and a bank is what a bar is for, and the man who holds it is forty-four and he is not here and he has not been asked and he is not going to be told that I stood at a post for eleven minutes because of him.**

He turned round and went back along the path, past the marks, past the second post, and did not touch anything.

---

**And at about four o'clock that afternoon the flatbed went back, and the form was still in his bag, and the last day for the district's answer was that day, and it went back without it, and there is no second vehicle until Thursday and the Thursday one is a lorry to a yard at the north end and it does not go to Crown Heights.**

*Friday the tenth, 23.30. Cobb Lock, a room above a kitchen, one day off the ninth.*

*One. **I was on a gate at ten to seven with the form in my bag and the only vehicle that would have carried it back went back at about four without it, and the last day was the day I was in a fenland, and I have written that down as a fact and not as a reason, and the reason is on the facing page of this book and the reason is about me.*

*Two. **A cast number on a culvert collar is a work of record and it is the only thing about this district that is on anybody's paper except a card with a hand on it, and I held a schedule against it for about four minutes and did not write it down, and I have not written it here, and I am not going to, and a number in a ground is a thing a person can be sent to look at and nobody has sent and I am not going to start in a fenland on a Friday.***

*Three. **The boundary is a column in a file that says CITY or EXT, and the column has said EXT for two entries since 1974, and the key sends you to a body that was abolished in about 1968. And the two ends of it are in Crown Heights and there is nobody at either of them. I now have three boundaries in eight months and I have written all three down and I have not joined them and a reader of this book is going to try.***

*Four. **The page home the two hundred and twenty-ninth day and I am not writing that it is there. The token cold, the two hundred and fifteenth day. Eleven routes, ten crossings, and the ninth still unexplained and I am not going to explain it before the end of July. The condition column is a hundred and forty-eight days empty. The plate is ninety-five days in the grass and nobody has been told and nobody is going to be. The second tin is at thirty. The walk is on Monday the thirteenth and I will not be on it and nobody will telephone and the sheet is right about that as well.***

*And the standing position: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.**
