# Chapter 178: Two Places, One Tuesday, And Nine Words From A Man With A Handcart

**The round went out at about ten past eight on Monday the twenty-sixth of October and came in at about four with the bag empty, and it is a Monday and a Thursday round and it has been since the second of January and it is one man short, and the bag has been empty since the twenty-eighth of August, and it is not a walk and nobody has called it one.**

The page home is the three hundred and thirty-seventh day. The token is cold on the three hundred and twenty-third. The plate in the grass at the low end of Bridge Street is two hundred and three days old and has been told to nobody. The condition column is two hundred and fifty-six days and is not there. **A foreman of fifty-four is twenty-six weeks and five days past four months and nobody is going to ask him.**

---

## The Row at eight, and the barriers, and a rack

The man of about forty who sweeps it was on it at about eight with a brush and did not stop. **Nobody keeps this Row.** It has never been kept and it is not going to be, and the man with the brush is not a service and there is no schedule with his name on it, and if he stopped tomorrow the Row would be the same on Wednesday as it is on Monday except for the grass.

The fishmonger of about forty-five had her trays out by nine and was not thanked and was not asked and said nothing.

**The barriers were still out on the back length and the road was open, and both of those are correct, and a site is not cleared by a date, and there has been no gang on that road since the thirty-first of August and there is not going to be one, and on Friday the thirty-first of October there is going to be a schedule in a building in Crown Heights that says the works are not going to be maintained, and the two of those facts are about four hundred yards apart and they are not the same fact and nothing in this city is going to put them together.**

**And a rack, four rooms above a fitter's shop, open at the front, against a wall, with nothing in it in it.** Nobody empties a rack. There is a mark on the shelf about nine inches from the left edge and about two inches wide and it has not changed, and there is no instrument in this city for a mark that has not changed, and he did not go and look at it on any of the Fridays since and he is not going to go and look at it tomorrow.

---

## The arithmetic, and it took eleven minutes, and it is not the answer

He did it at the kitchen table with a pencil and the back of a sheet of his own paper, standing up, on the Monday morning, because sitting down to do arithmetic is a thing he does about four times a year and gets the same four answers.

*Monday and Thursday: the round. Out at ten past eight, in at about four. **Mine. It is not a walk and nobody has called it one, and it goes out because it goes out, and it is one man short, and there is nothing in it and there has been nothing in it since the twenty-eighth of August.***

*Friday: the flatbed. In from the city at about a quarter to seven, in this fenland at about half past ten, back to the city on the afternoon and in the yard there at about half past four, four sacks on the bed, and it does not wait either way. **So a Friday is four miles the other way and back and I sleep in my own bed.***

*Tuesday: the van, which belongs to a firm, which is the mower man's, which has got a rack on the back for mowers and a company name on the side in a colour that has gone the colour of the weather, and it has two stops and then the yard, and it is not a day off, and he has said so both times this year without being asked to. **So a Tuesday is four miles the other way and back and belongs to a man of about fifty.***

*Wednesday, Saturday, Sunday: **he does not leave the district. A bus goes past the end of the street every twenty minutes on a weekday and I have not been on one since the second of January, and on a Saturday and a Sunday there is not one, and there is not one in Mireward either and there has not been one there since about 1961 because a bus wants a place to stop and a place to stop is a rateable thing.***

He stood and looked at it.

**A Tuesday is the only day of the week he has. And on a Tuesday the only vehicle in this district that will take him four miles belongs to somebody else.**

Then he wrote the two places underneath, because he had to, and he wrote them without dates, which took him about four seconds and was the hardest thing on the page:

*ONE. A village eleven miles the other way. About a hundred and forty people. A schoolroom. A bell on a bracket over the door that has been there since about 1911. A bus goes past the end of the street every twenty minutes on a weekday and two of them a day go as far as that village and I have been on one of them twice.*

*TWO. A building in Crown Heights, about a mile from where that schedule was drafted, and a drawer in it, and a sheet of street numbers in the drawer, and about nine hundred of those drawers in this city, and a bus past the end of the street every twenty minutes on a weekday, and I have not used one since the second of January.*

**And then he stood there and worked out, in about nine seconds, that the arithmetic is not the answer, and he wrote that down too, because he wanted it in his own hand before anybody could give it to him:**

*It is not that I cannot get to both. One is a day on a bus. The other is an hour there and an hour back on the same bus. I could do both on a Tuesday and be in this front room at eleven at night and I know that because I have done harder days than that since the sixth of April and been no use to anybody at the end of any of them. **THE ANSWER IS NOT THAT I HAVE NOT TRIED HARD ENOUGH. I HAVE GOT A TUESDAY.***

---

## What is actually in the way, and it took him the rest of the morning

**They are opposite, and going to one of them undoes the other one, and that is the whole of it, and it is not a matter of time.**

*An objection is the only thing in this city that can stop a schedule, and an objection has to be filed against something, and something has to be addressable, and a district with a number is a district with a correspondence address, and a correspondence address comes out of a drawer on a floor of street numbers from 1911. **So going to the drawer is how the village gets protected. And the drawer does not stop being how the village gets a number, and the number does not stop being in every column of every schedule in this city for ever, and there is a woman of thirty-four in that schoolroom who has kept a place off paper for nine years and has said no to four people this year and has said, without being asked, that she is not going to be the woman who hands a district its first piece of stationery, and I have not asked her one word about it.***

*And if I do not go, then Cobb Lock cannot be objected to, and the schedule stands, and a schedule is a thing that is either maintained or not maintained, and that sentence is printed at the top of it in the office's own words, and nobody has to do anything for it to be true.*

*And the eleven streets is not in this. The eleven streets came in off a contract, and a contract is a thing an office gives, and that is a different errand in a different building and I have got no reason to think it is the same errand and I am not going to put them in the same column.*

**And then, at about half past eleven, he got to the part that has actually stopped him, and it is the worst of the three, and it is that he cannot find out which one to do without doing one of them.**

*The thing that would tell me is in the drawer. If that floor of numbers has a sheet that covers the fen country, then going there is a way of saving that village and I will be able to say so out loud to somebody. **If it does not, then going there is a way of losing her, permanently, and having spent the only day I have on it.** I cannot know which without going. And going is the choice.*

---

## The third door, and it is shut, and he is not going to knock on it again

**There is one person in this city who could tell him what is in a drawer in that building without opening it.**

**Bettina Sallow, fifty-four, four years at the ninth desk on the left in the Survey School's repository beneath Crown Heights, thirty-four steps up, a plan chest, a window that does not open, a chair under it.** She will not give a shelf number, and she gave him the reason in about eleven seconds on the twenty-second of September, and the reason was that a number on a shelf in a room she is responsible for is a reference, and a reference is a thing a person can be asked for in about four years, and if she says it then in about four years a man can say it, and then he can ask her whose hand wrote it down, and then there is a person.

**And she said, on the twenty-ninth, that if he asked her a second time she would give him the same answer, and he would not ask her a second time.**

He did not ask her a second time.

He stood in a front room on the twenty-sixth of October at about half past eleven and did the thing he does — he built the sentence, all of it, in about eleven seconds, in the order it would be said in, and then he did not say it to anybody, and it took him about four seconds to let go of it, and the letting go is not a resolution and is not a rule and is not a third thing, it is just a man standing in a room.

**And he wrote down that he is not going to write to her, and that a letter would make her a correspondent of this district, and that a correspondent of this district is a party, and that a party is a person, and that on the twenty-seventh of August a woman of thirty-one went through a register of correspondents standing up in about nine minutes and entered nothing, and that she was right, and that he is not going to do it again either.**

---

## And at about ten past four, in a corridor, nine words

The man of about forty-one came through the corridor with a handcart. He is not on any list at all. He has been in this office four times a day since the twenty-eighth of December and he has never once been sent anywhere and he is not going to be. He is not the man of about forty-one who holds the key to the drawer in Crown Heights and the second one is in no book.

Milo Rook had the question ready and it was a fair one and it was the only question he asked anybody that day.

"Where does a district get its number."

"**No. I carry them. I have never made one.**"

He got the handcart through the gap and did not add anything, and did not wait, and did not say thank you, and **a person who has thanked somebody is a person who has asked.**

**Nine words. The number of words he says is nine and the number of occasions on which he has said anything to me since the second of December is a different number, and I counted neither today and there is no total anywhere and there is not going to be one.**

---

## The end of the Monday, and what he did not decide

He did the round's business, which is going about a district that has nothing to collect, and got back in at about four, and washed his hands, and did not go and get a bus.

**He did not decide. Not on the twenty-sixth, and not on the twenty-seventh, and he wrote down on the Monday night that he has not decided and that a thing that is going to come to a head is a column and a column is worse than a count, and that he has been writing sentences like that since the seventh of September and has improved none of them, and that the sentence about not going to the drawer has now been written down four times and it is still a sentence and not a rule and not a resolution and he has not got a word for the third thing and has not had one since the fourteenth of April.**

---

*Field Book, Monday the twenty-sixth of October, 23.30, a front room in eleven streets, one day off the twenty-fifth.*

*One. **The choice, in the shape of two things he cannot both be in, and the arithmetic is not the answer.** A Monday and a Thursday are the round and a Friday is the flatbed four miles the other way and back and a Tuesday is a van that belongs to a firm and has got two stops and then the yard. **A Tuesday is the only day of the week I have and I am not going to pretend the answer is that I have not tried hard enough. I have got a Tuesday.** The two places are a bus ride of a day and a bus ride of an hour, and I could do both and be in this room at eleven at night, and I have done harder days since the sixth of April and been no use to anybody at the end of every one of them.*

*Two. **And they are opposite, and going to one of them undoes the other one.** An objection is the only thing that can stop a schedule. An objection has to be filed against something. Something has to be addressable. A number comes out of a drawer on a floor of street numbers from 1911, and there are about nine hundred of those drawers, and I have not used a bus since the second of January. **So going to the drawer is how the village gets protected, and it is also how the village gets a number, and the number is in every column of every schedule in this city for ever, and a woman of thirty-four in that schoolroom has kept a place off paper for nine years and said she is not going to be the woman who hands a district its first piece of stationery, and I have not asked her.** And if I do not go then Cobb Lock cannot be objected to, and the schedule stands, and a schedule is a thing that is either maintained or not maintained, and that sentence is printed at the top of it in the office's own words and nobody has to do anything for it to be true.*

*Three. **And the worst of it, which is that the thing that would tell me which one to do is inside one of the two.** If there is a sheet on that floor that covers the fen country then going there saves that village and I can say it out loud to somebody. If there is not, then going there loses her, permanently, having spent the only day I have. **I cannot know which without going and going is the choice.** And the one person who could tell me what is in a drawer without opening it has told me she will give me the same answer a second time, and I am not going to ask her a second time, and I have written to her about nothing and I am not going to start, and a correspondent of this district is a party and a party is a person, and a woman of thirty-one went through a register standing up in nine minutes in August and entered nothing and she was right and I am not going to do it again either.*

*Four. **Nine words in a corridor at about ten past four, from a man of about forty-one who is on no list at all and has never been sent anywhere and is not the man of about forty-one who holds the key to the drawer in Crown Heights. No. I carry them. I have never made one.** He got the handcart through the gap and did not add anything and I did not thank him, because a person who has thanked somebody is a person who has asked. **The number of words he says is nine and the number of occasions on which he has said anything to me since the second of December is a different number, and I counted neither today and there is no total anywhere and there is not going to be one.**

*Five. **Nothing has moved.** Eleven remembered routes, ten crossings, the tally ten lines, not looked at since the thirteenth of January. The page home the three hundred and thirty-seventh day, blank, four marks in four hands, none of them mine. The token cold, the three hundred and twenty-third day, and it has done nothing since the seventh of December. **The list on the facing page reads twenty-nine and it has not moved in a hundred and two days, and an interval of one day is not a pair, and a pair is a fact about where I was and not about what happened, and if the count of pairs in this block is small and not large that is correct.** The condition column is two hundred and fifty-six days and is not there and the reason column is not there and no new figure is to be printed for either. The second tin is at thirty-seven and there is no thirty-eight. The plate is two hundred and three days in the grass and has been told to nobody. A foreman of fifty-four is twenty-six weeks and five days past four months and nobody is going to ask him. **I have not decided and I am not going to decide on a Tuesday, and a thing that is going to come to a head is a column, and a column is worse than a count, and that sentence is now the fifth time I have written it and I have improved it none of them.**

*And the standing position: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.**
