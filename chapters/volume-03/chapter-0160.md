# Chapter 160: A Rack With Nothing In It In It, And A Question With A Practice In The First Of Its Parts

**On Tuesday the first of September there was a half past seven on the back length and there was nobody on it.**

The page home is the two hundred and eighty-second day. The token is cold on the two hundred and sixty-eighth day. The plate in the grass at the low end of Bridge Street is a hundred and forty-eight days old. The condition column is two hundred and one days. The second tin is at thirty-seven, in a building eleven miles away, on a bottom shelf, under a repair schedule.

**The barriers were still out. The road was open. Both of those are correct and a site is not cleared by a date, and there is a difference between a barrier that is up because men are coming back at half past seven and a barrier that is up because nobody has come, and the two look the same from a bus and there is no vehicle to see it from.**

He walked it at about twenty past seven because he was thirty and he had a room two streets away and there was no reason to be in eleven streets at all on a Tuesday morning except that he slept in it.

---

## The ordinary day, which was ordinary

The man of about forty who sweeps the Row was on the Row at about eight with a brush and did not stop. The fishmonger of about forty-five had her trays out by nine. **There is a man of about sixty with a shed eleven minutes away and he is not on the Row on a Wednesday and has not been since before the war, and it is not a Wednesday, and he does not know that a piece of paper exists and is not going to be told and nobody in this district is going to tell him.**

**Nothing keeps this row. It has never been kept and it is not going to be kept, and the man who sweeps it is not a service and there is no schedule with his name on it, and if he stopped tomorrow the Row would be the same on Wednesday as it is on Tuesday except for the grass.**

**And nothing romantic happened, and nothing is going to, and there are about nine people in this district and four of them have refused something in the last six weeks and not one of them has been thanked, and he is not going to thank any of them in a book in September.**

---

## The thing that stopped, and it is not the works

**There has not been a satchel out of eleven streets since Friday the twenty-eighth of August, and there is not going to be another one, and that is the whole of what has stopped in the post, and the works are a third of what has stopped, and the other two thirds are a rack with nothing in it in it and a tin that has stopped, and you would not know from either of those that anything had happened at all.**

A district with no correspondence address is on no post. The post that has come to eleven streets since the sixth of January has come on a Friday morning in a satchel that went out with the works' papers and came back on the Friday afternoon, and the reason it went out with the works' papers is that the works' papers were the only thing in this district that ever weighed anything.

**So from Monday the thirty-first of August there is a district that nobody in this city has written to, and the district returns card that has come in that Friday post since the middle of January has not come, and it is a printed side, and it is one, and it is the only one that arrives, and it arrives weekly, and it has not arrived.**

**Nobody was told. There was no notice. The notice is a printed side that says the works are not to be maintained after the thirty-first of August, and the notice is about the works, and the works are the post, and the post was never mentioned, and nobody in an office about a mile away looked at a calendar, because a printed date does not know what day of the week it is, and that sentence is the truest thing anybody has said in this district for eight months and it explains nothing at all.**

He has not made a form about it. He has not made a form about a printed side, or a cell with a dash in it, or a typed name in a box, or a sheet that was never printed, or a week that was never printed, or this, and the reason is not modesty and it is not a rule on a facing page. **A form is the only instrument anybody in this story has ever had, and every single thing that has gone wrong in this district in eight months went through one, and the things that are still true went round them.**

---

## Friday the fourth of September, and a rack

**The round did not go, because the round is a Monday and a Thursday round and has been since the second of January, and it goes out at about ten past eight and comes in at about four, and it is one man short and has been since the twenty-eighth of December.** He walked up at about ten in the morning on his own, which is four hundred yards and a fitter's shop, and there was nothing in the bag, because there has been no sheet to bring back since Friday the twenty-eighth of August and there is not going to be another one.

**The rack is a piece of furniture. It is about four feet high and it is open at the front and it is against a wall and it is where the weekly sheets have gone on a Friday for nine months, and on Friday the fourth of September there was a rack with nothing in it in it.**

He put his hand in it. It was cold. There was a mark on the shelf where a bundle of sheets had been lying, about nine inches from the left edge, and the mark is about two inches wide. **It is the only trace of nine months of weekly sheets in this city and nobody put it there on purpose.**

**Nobody empties a rack. That is not a decision anybody made. It is a thing that has never had to be done, and a thing that has never had to be done does not have a form, and the absence of a form is not a fault, and there is nothing in this city that says a rack is finished, and there is going to be a rack with nothing in it in it on Friday the eleventh and on Friday the eighteenth and on the Friday after that.**

**And the second tin is on a bottom shelf in a plan room in another building under a repair schedule, and a repair schedule is a schedule, and a schedule is on a printed side somewhere, and nobody has ever asked when it ends, and he is not going to ask, and he has written that down and is not going to improve it.**

---

**And at about ten past four the man of forty-one on no list at all came through the corridor with a handcart, and he was not sent, and he is not going to be, and he asked him one question, and it was the only question he asked that week, and it was a fair one.**

"Does anybody empty that."

"**It has been got from and never got from.**"

That is nine words and there is no column anywhere for the number of times he has said them. **He has been in this office four times a day since the twenty-eighth of December and he has watched that rack, and he is not going to be the man who says which of those it is, and neither of those is in the nine words and both of those are why the nine words are the nine words.** And nobody thanked him, and Milo Rook did not thank him, **and there is nothing on any paper in this city that says a man of forty-one on no list at all was asked a fair question in a corridor on Friday the fourth of September and gave a correct answer to it.**

---

## And the men

**There were eleven men and a foreman on this road twice a week for nine months and more, and as of the first of September the district does not know where they are, and there is no way of finding out, and there is not going to be one.**

**The contract is between the city and a firm. A firm is rateable. A firm has an address and a file and a name and can be written to, and the district cannot, and there are no eleven men and no foreman in the file at all, and the only trace that any of them was ever on that road is a figure in a tin in a plan room in another building, and a figure in a tin is not a record of a man, and two entries in pencil, four words and a figure, on the thirteenth and the twentieth of July, and the line on the front of a boy's blank card under a brick has not been taken off either and there have been three things under that brick since the twenty-first of January.**

**That is not a lament. It is an inventory, and he has gone through it twice, and there is no ninth item on it, and about fourteen people are on it and not one of them has been asked to be on it.**

---

## The standing question, in three parts, with a practice in the first of them

*Field Book, Friday the fourth of September, 23.20, a front room in eleven streets, one day off the third. Nothing has moved and the day-counts have gone on without me. The page home the two hundred and eighty-fifth day, blank, four marks in four hands, none of them mine. The token cold, the two hundred and seventy-first day. The pairs are twenty-nine and they have not moved since the sixteenth of July and the reason is the same reason and it is the right reason. Eleven remembered routes, ten crossings, the tally is ten lines, not looked at since the thirteenth of January. The condition column is two hundred and four days and is not there. The reason column is not there. The plate is a hundred and fifty-one days in the grass and has been told to nobody. The second tin is at thirty-seven and there is no thirty-eight. A foreman of fifty-four is nineteen weeks and two days past four months and he is not going to be asked and I am not going to ask and the date of that silence is now written down, and the block in which the silence got a date on it is the block in which the works stopped, and I did not put the date there and I could not take it out and I have not tried.*

*Why does weather memory threaten a region the Registry claims is stable.*

**(a) It is not a form any more. It is four bells and a refusal to write them down, and the refusal is the oldest thing I have.**

**A place is not stable because nothing has happened in it. A place is stable because there is nobody writing down what happens in it. Eleven streets has had a gang, a foreman, a weekly sheet, a rack, two tins, a condition column and a reason column, and as of this morning it has none of them except the foreman, and it is still on exactly one printed side in the whole of this city, and the side names it as a thing that is not going to be maintained, and that is the one line it has ever had and it is a line about stopping.**

And a practice is in the first part of it now, and this is the whole of the new thing and I am going to write it as plainly as I know how.

**A handbell on a nail in a house with no number, rung twice before a man starts work and once when he stops, for about thirty-one years, by a man who will not say for whom, because if he says a name then in about four years somebody can ask him which date goes with it.** A bell on a bracket over a schoolroom door, rung once, by a woman of thirty-four who keeps a roll in her head on purpose, and four children take turns on the rope, and they give it nine and seven and eleven in a term, and she has never corrected one of them because she does not know that she is entitled to. A bell about four inches across the mouth off a boat, on a wall at the top of a landing, rung at ten past seven and at four every day for twenty-six years including days when nothing comes in and nothing goes out. And a bell on a post at a ford that has been dry since about 1919, which I heard nine times in June and could not put a ring on any of them.

**Four bells, four places, four people, no row, no form, no date, no name, and not one of them has ever been written down, and there are four people who have not been asked whether it is four, and one of them told me on a Friday in July that whatever I write down will be the first time anybody has written down any of it, and that it is not a service, that it is a door.**

**And the finding that goes with it, unimproved: a practice is the only thing in this district that has a shape no form was ever built to fit, and the reason there is no form for it is not that a form is missing. It is that the woman on the schoolroom step would have to decide how many pulls of the rope is the right number before she could put a figure in a box, and four old women who are dead taught four children that number, and she does not know that she has the right to flatten it out, and I do not know that she has, and neither of us has asked her, and there is no form for *I do not know that I have the right*, and that is not a gap in a form, it is the whole subject.**

(b) **There is no second outlook.** The seasonal outlook is the last one. A man of about fifty has been in a room nineteen years and has never known anybody who has seen a second one, and nobody has asked for one in writing since May, and the absence is not explained and is not going to be explained by me in September. **Unmoved. Second part. Not moved in forty-six days, and it is the second part of a three-part question, and it is the only part of anything in this book that has not moved at all, and I have stopped finding that satisfying and I have not stopped noticing it.**

(c) **An instrument a person cannot produce.** A punch roll about nine inches long that knows how many and not who. A yard man's flag and a book of times. A field book. A pad with four lines in it in a drawer in Crown Heights that nobody has rung up about since 1969. A roll of nineteen children in a woman's head. The smell of a fen in a dry week, which is the strongest of them and the one thing a person produces and cannot produce on demand. **And a bell that knows it is the first one of a day and the last one off and, once a year, that it is a year ago today and there is nowhere in this city to put that, and which throws the date away when it has finished, and a bell cannot tell you a date, and a bell cannot tell you a number that matters, and the number at the end of a practice turns it into a rule, and a rule can be broken by a person.** Seven things that are not one. **Half an answer is not an answer, and there is a fourth part under the third that I have never once been down to, and I wrote in July that I was not going to go down it in the last week of a month, and it is the first of September, and I have not gone down it, and I am not going to in the first week of one either, and that is the sixth time I have written that sentence and I have improved it none of the six.**

**And the category is named in this book since the thirty-first of July and it has got me a question and not an answer, and there is a street in this city that would settle it in about a day, and I am not going to look at it, and I have written that down twice and the reason is the same both times and the reason is me and not the street.**

---

*And the four. Four bells, four people, a practice, no row, and a man of forty-one who has watched a rack for two hundred days and will not say whether it is ever emptied, and a district on one printed side, and a sheet that was never printed, and a week that was never printed, and a rack that is never finished, and nobody has been thanked.*

*And the standing position: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.**
