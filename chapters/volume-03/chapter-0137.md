# Chapter 137: Nine Tides Is Not Nine Years

He came back on the flatbed at about four in the afternoon on Friday the twenty-sixth of June, and the flatbed does not wait and had not waited since about six, and he was not on it early because he was on the far side of a bridge at eleven in the morning with a folding rule.

**The weekly sheet for the week of the twenty-second went into the rack in the quay section office at about ten past ten that morning, the duplicate went into a tin, and the duplicate went into a second tin on the bottom shelf of the plan room of the Civic Atlas Office under a repair schedule, and that tin now holds twenty-eight weeks of gang carbon and one page that is not a gang sheet. He was not there when it went in and the office does not keep a list of who was there.**

---

**And the crossing was read out in a yard at about half past five on the Friday, to about nine people, with the tally there, and it is the eighth time it has been read out and the fourth time in a place that is not this district's yard.**

The paper is Hettie Brann's. The pocket is Dr Sen's. **The tally has ten lines on it and it is the record and he has not looked at it since the thirteenth of January and he is not going to look at it in July and he has now said that in this book eight times and improved it none of the eight.**

"Say the number."

"Ten."

"Say the date."

"Thursday the twenty-fifth of June. In at twenty past two, out at five past three. Forty-two minutes, and I was two minutes over and I came out at the time I said."

"Say who walked you."

"**Garn Ollerton, ferry pilot, thirty-seven, twenty-six years, and she was doing her run and I did not ask her and she did not offer, and she came out two minutes over and told me I was two minutes over and said that was not the point.**"

"Say who held the count."

"**Sabra Trewin, thirty-four, nine years teaching in a schoolroom at Cobb Lock, and she held it in her head, and there are nineteen children at that school who are on no roll anywhere in this city, and she is not writing this one down either, and I did not ask her what the count was afterwards and I am not going to.**"

"Say the four conditions."

"A person walks me in and out and they are not me. A count held by a person and not by a box. The time stated before I go. The clinic in the morning, and the clinic in the morning was a nurse of twenty-three on a bank from half past ten, unasked, on her day off, and she has a bag and she did not look at my hand."

"Nobody asked her."

"Nobody asked her. A man of fifty-eight told her on Saturday in about nine words and she came and she is paid by a clinic that has no record of where she went and that is the price and she named it herself."

Dr Sen wrote on the paper with Hettie Brann's paper and said, "**You are across ten times. You were across nine times in December and there is one of those ten that did not cost you a route and I have never been able to make the arithmetic work and I have stopped trying, and I am not going to stop asking you the number.**"

---

**And on the Friday morning at about eleven, which is four hours before any of the above, he was back on the bank of the drain at the old ford with a woman of thirty-seven and a folding rule, and she made him put his own number down twice.**

"You said nine years."

"I said the top mark was about nine years newer than the bottom one."

"That is not nine years." She got the rule out and put it against the parapet without hurrying. "**That is nine tides. A man does not cut a mark because a year has gone by. A man stands here when the water is at a place and he cuts a mark, and the water comes about four times a year, and if he has cut three in a row and the top one is nine down the list then it is two years and a bit, and a bit is a spring tide or an autumn one.**"

"Then it is not nine years."

"It is not nine years and it is not a date, and you wrote a number of years on a mark that has never had a year on it, and you did it because you have got a book in a coat with four marks in four hands in it and you are so used to dates that a number without one looks wrong to you." She put the rule away. "**The file for this bridge says 1911 and the file is right about 1911 and the file says nothing whatever about these marks, because the file is on the other side of the water, and the marks are on this side, and the marks have been cut by about four people since about 1919 and not one of them has ever had a reason to be recorded.**"

---

**And that is the recovery, and it took about ninety seconds, and it was her work and not his, and it did not cost him anything, and it is the best hour he has had since the first of April.**

He had got one thing wrong on a parapet. A woman of thirty-seven read it back to him in four seconds with a rule, and he did not write it in a form, and he did not write it in a form on the Friday and he has not written it in a form since, **and the reason he has not is that there is nowhere to put it. There is no returns card for a mark on a wall. There is no column on a gang sheet. There is no box on a bridge file, and there is not going to be one, because the marks are on the half of the bridge that has been nobody's since 1968, and the file has a column for side and the side is not CITY.**

**Two sets of marks about ninety feet apart, cut by two different hands, recording the same water on the same days, in a place with no address, and neither set of them is in a file, and the pair of them is a return route, and there is no paper of any kind that could be produced about it, and a route that cannot be produced cannot be asked after, and that is the only protection any route in this city has ever had.**

"I would like to say that out loud in a yard," he said.

"No."

"That is a perfectly good sentence."

"**It is a perfectly good sentence and it is about a mark on a wall that I could not tell a stranger about, and there are about four people alive who know what I mean by the low one on the path, and if the two of us start saying that a bell and a wall and a post are a route, then in about four years somebody writes it down, and a thing that is written down is a thing somebody maintains, and the day after that it is a thing somebody can be asked about, and I have watched that happen to about nine routes on that water and I am not doing it to two marks and a bell.**"

---

**And they walked back to the fourth landing and she put the boat in and said one more thing at about ten past twelve, unasked, the way a person says a thing when they have decided to and not when they have been asked to.**

"You will want to go back in."

"I will want to go back in."

"**Then do it from here. Not from your end. You do not know where you are until you know where you are going to be, and everybody who has ever crossed water has known that, and it is the only thing about you that is not a document.**" She did not look at him. "And I am not going to take you. Not this week. And that is not a rule and I have not got a reason you would print."

---

**The Field Book, Friday the twenty-sixth of June, 23.10, a front room in eleven streets, one day off the twenty-fifth, and there is an entry on every night from tonight and I have written that down as a fact and not as a resolution, because a run is a run and a run is not a method.**

*Twenty-sixth, 23.10. One day off the twenty-fifth.*

*One. **Nine tides is not nine years, and I had it wrong on a parapet, and a woman of thirty-seven with a folding rule put me right in about four seconds, and that is the whole of the recovery this fortnight has.** I wrote a number of years onto a mark that has never had a year on it because I have got four marks in four hands in a line home and I have got so used to a number with a date behind it that a number without one looked wrong. **The file says 1911 and the file is right about 1911 and the file says nothing at all about the marks, because the marks are on the half of the bridge that has been nobody's since 1968, and the file has a column for side and the side is not CITY.** And I have got a seventh column in a bag with a hundred and ten words in it and I have had the same disease since January and she fixed one of mine in ninety seconds with a folding rule and did not make a thing of it, and she will not let me say it in a yard, and she is right, and the reason she is right is that two marks and a bell are a route and about four living people know it and that is the whole of the custody there is.

*Two. **And the two sets of marks, which I am not going to join to anything.** Two sets, ninety feet apart, two hands, the same water on the same days, and neither of them in a file, and the pair of them is a return route, and a route that cannot be produced cannot be asked after. **That is the first time in seven months that the finding has gone the other way. Every conclusion I have come to in this district has been that something was going to be found out about a person. This one is that a thing is safe because there is nothing to find, and I have no instrument for that and I am not going to build one, and a smell, a mark, a bell and nineteen cuts in a post are four instruments in that class and there is no form for any of them.**

*Three. **And the tenth, at a gate in a high wall at about half past four on the Friday, and it is nine words.** *I was not there. I am not going there.* **I have got nine of them in about six months and I have never once written down what proportion of them were refusals of a job, and I noticed on the Friday afternoon that I could not have told you, and that is the difference between a number and a column and I have gone six months without noticing it. The count is ten. I am not going to put it in a yard, and the man said the other half of it in the gate before I got to the second sentence of mine, which is that he has not been south of that bridge since he was a boy and he is not going to start at thirty-eight, and he said it the way you say a thing you have said to yourself.**

*Four. **And what has not moved, in order, because the list is the only shape this has.** Ten remembered routes and ten crossings, and the ninth's failure to cost one is still unexplained. The tally is ten lines, Hettie Brann's paper, Dr Sen's pocket, and I have not looked at it since the thirteenth of January. **The page home the two hundred and fifteenth day, blank, four marks in four hands and none of them mine, and I am not writing that it is there.** The token cold, the two hundred and first day, and it was on a bank at a ford at half past two on Thursday and it did nothing, and a street that opens because of something in a man's coat is a place kept by that man, and I wrote that on the sixth of December in my own hand and I have not improved on it in six months. Two things lost that have no name on them, a sound and a taste, and the sound went on the Wednesday at a clinic counter for about four seconds and came back and did not stay, and no face and no place and no person and no name has ever come with either, and I decided in the first week of January not to decide and I have not. The plate is eighty-one days in the grass. A round is one man short. The condition column is empty and has been for a hundred and thirty-four days and the reason column is empty for the fourteenth week and nobody has ever asked about the reason column, and **a thing that has never been asked about is not protected, it is just not yet asked about, and the second tin has been on a bottom shelf in that plan room under a repair schedule since before I came into this building and the man who owns that shelf has been there eleven years and has never asked about it, and I have worked out this fortnight that Percy and he are the same kind of person, and I am not writing that down anywhere but here.**

*Five. **And the standing question, which has three parts and has gained a person and a pad and a bar and a post.** *Why does weather memory threaten a region the Registry claims is stable.* A place is not stable because nothing has happened in it; a place is stable because there is nobody writing down what happens in it. **And a resident witness is a person on a list, and the forecast for a season of weather-driven seam events was issued for a district entered as nil on the ground that there is no resident witness, and there has not been a person on a list in Mireward since about 1911, and a hundred and forty people have been living in it the whole time, and the entry has never once been wrong about a fact.** The other two parts have not moved and I am not going to move them. There is no second outlook and I am not going to ask for one in July. **And an instrument a person cannot produce: I have got a punch roll and a flag and a field book and a pad and now a smell, and five things that are not one, and half of an answer is not an answer, and there is a fourth part under the third that I have never been down to and I am not going to go down it in the first month of a volume.**

*And the standing position, which is the last line and does not improve: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.*
