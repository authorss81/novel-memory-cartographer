# Chapter 142: The Best Answer Anybody Is Going To Hear

The sheet went round the gang on the back length on Monday the sixth of July at about ten past eight, because that is what a works clerk of forty-six has done every Monday and every Thursday since the second of January, and because on Monday the sixth it was the twenty-seventh walk since the first of April and the condition column was empty on it and the reason column was empty on it and both of those were correct.

Cyril Sale read the district schedule out on the length. He read all of it, including the key, including the four grounds, and including the line at the foot about a district that has not returned, and he read it in the voice he reads a sheet in, which is not a voice anybody argues with.

The round went out at about ten past eight and came in at about four. It is one man short and it has been one man short since the twenty-eighth of December.

---

**And on Tuesday the seventh of July at about ten in the morning the foreman came into the quay section office, which is four rooms above a fitter's shop, and put his hat on the counter, which nobody does, and said the thing he had come to say in about eleven seconds.**

"I am the person responsible for maintenance in the district."

"You are."

"Then that is me, and I have read it twice and I have not understood one word of the second column and I have understood all four of the last ones, and I would like somebody to tell me which of the four is the one that is true, because I have been on that length thirty-one years and there is nothing wrong with it."

---

**And Milo Rook did not answer him, and the reason he did not answer him is that the four grounds are all true of about nine hundred districts and only one of them is true of the eleven streets, and the one that is true of the eleven streets is the one that ends the works.**

"There are four," he said. "Read them out."

"I have read them out. Cyril read them out on the length yesterday in front of eleven men and I have not got anything to add to Cyril."

"Read them again anyway."

Roy Pryce, fifty-four, thirty-one years, a man who has not said nine words in this district for five months and is not going to be asked about it:

> **(a) no maintained seam of record.**
> **(b) no resident witness.**
> **(c) nil returns.**
> **(d) works not reimbursable at the present rating.**

"Right," he said. "Now. **(a) is not true and I will tell you why it is not true and then you can stop me. There is a level of record. It is in your own building, in a file, with a date on it, under my signature, and it is level with the rest of the district and it has not moved since the fourth of December, and if the department does not know that is my business and not mine to say, and it is not the same as there being no seam of record.**"

**That is the first half of the best answer anybody is going to hear in this fortnight, and it is completely correct, and it is about eight months old, and it is a level in a file and not a sentence, and a level is not what the form wants, and the form does not want it, and there is no box on that sheet for a level.**

"**(b) is a lie.**"

"**It is not a lie and you know it is not a lie, and I am not going to stand in this room and have you pretend it is a lie on a Tuesday morning. There are two hundred and fifty-two people in a building with no number and I know the figure and you know the figure. There is not one of them on a list. That is what (b) means. It does not mean nobody is here.**"

"**(c) is not true either.** The sheet comes Monday to Sunday and it goes into the rack on the Friday and the duplicate goes into a tin, and it has gone in every Friday since the second of January, and there is not one Monday in nine months that has not had a sheet in it.**"

"**(d) is a question about money and I do not know the answer and it is not mine.**"

---

**And then he did the thing that made him the best answer anybody is going to hear, and he did it without being asked, and it took about four seconds, and Milo Rook has written in his own book that it is the only time in eight months that a person handed him the right object for a question he had not managed to ask.**

Roy Pryce went out into the corridor and came back with the weekly sheet for the week ending Sunday the fifth of July, out of the rack, and put it on the counter squared with the top edge facing him.

"**There. That is the state of the works. It is the fifth of July. It is Monday to Sunday and it has five days on it and two blank, and the two blank are the Saturday and the Sunday and they are blank every week and have been since the second of January and nobody has ever asked me for them either.**"

He put his finger on the columns, left to right, and named them the way a man names things in his own office.

"**Length. Day. Men. Hours. Condition. And then the reason, and the reason has been empty since about the fourth of April and the condition has been empty since the twelfth of February.**"

"And what is the state of the length."

"**There is nothing wrong with it.**"

---

**And there is no box on that form, or on the sheet, or in this building, or in this city, for *there is nothing wrong with it*.**

That is the shape of it and it took him and Milo Rook about eleven minutes to get all the way round it, standing at a counter, and neither of them was in a hurry.

The form's column three is *works of record*. The works of record for eleven streets, in July, is a piece of paper about nine inches by fourteen that lives in a rack on a Friday and a duplicate that lives in a tin and a second copy of the duplicate in a plan room in another building on a bottom shelf under a repair schedule. **That is not a schedule of works. It is a piece of paper and two copies of it, and there is no column anywhere that takes a piece of paper and two copies, and the reason there is no column is that nobody has ever needed one, because the piece of paper was never supposed to be the works of record. It was supposed to be a note that a foreman had walked.**

"You have printed four ways for a district to lose its works," Milo Rook said, at about ten past eleven, "and you have not printed one way for a district to keep them."

"**I have.**"

"You have not."

"**I have. Look at the foot of it. *A district that has not returned within the period stated will be completed by this office.* There is one way to keep them and it is on the Friday, and it is not printed as a way to keep them, it is printed as a way to complete them, and both of those are the same sentence with a different Friday in it.**"

---

"And the condition column," said Milo Rook.

"**What about it.**"

"You have not put a word in it since the twelfth of February."

"**No.**"

"Nobody in this city has ever asked a gang what a length was like."

"**No.**" He did not stop, and he did not raise his voice, and he had put the sheet down flat on the counter with both hands either side of it. "**And I am not going to, and you are not going to ask me to, and if you put a question in that column in the time it takes you to walk back to that building then the sheet stops being a record of a length and starts being a record of a man, and a man can be asked what he wrote, and I have got eleven men and I have got a contract to the thirty-first and I am not spending any of it on that.**"

"Then what is the column for."

"**The column is for a hole.**"

---

**And the last day for the district's answer is Friday the tenth of July, and Roy Pryce said it, and it was not news to him and it was news to Milo Rook, and he had been carrying the thing in his bag for four days.**

"That is Friday."

"That is Friday. Seven days off issue."

"And if we do not send it."

"**Then they complete it on the sixteenth, and I have seen them complete a row, and it takes a person about a minute and a half with a pen, and they complete it out of what they have got, and what they have got about us is a district with a name and nothing else.**" He took his hat off the counter. "**I will have something for Friday.**"

"I do not need you to have something for Friday."

"**I know you do not need me to have something for Friday. I am going to have something for Friday.**"

---

**And at about half past four on the same Tuesday, in a front room, a man of thirty wrote down the only two things he had got.**

*Tuesday the seventh. **The form wants a number in column four and a word in column five and the man who is responsible has told me in about four seconds that the works are sound, and there is no box for the true answer, and the form is correct, and a form that asks a correct question and offers four wrong answers is a thing I have now met twice in eight months and the first one was a form about a length of lead pipe and a rent book, and I did not write it down then either.**

*Second. **He read me the four grounds and he took three of them apart himself, in his own office, standing up, in about forty seconds, and he was right about all three, and I have written down that he was right about all three, and he has still got to fill in a box. And I have still got a fortnight in me in which I could fill in column two myself, in about ninety seconds, with a true sentence about a morning in December, and there is a reason on the facing page of this book in my own handwriting and the reason is not about him, and a stop that a man wrote about himself is not a stop, and that sentence is the ninth thing I have written in eight months in the shape of a thing I should have worked out in the first week.***

---

**The condition column is a hundred and forty-five days empty and the reason column is empty and both of them were correct on the Monday and are correct now. The page home is the two hundred and twenty-sixth day. The token is cold, in the inside pocket, on the two hundred and twelfth day. Eleven remembered routes and ten crossings. The plate is ninety-two days in the grass.**

**And on Saturday the eleventh of July a man of fifty-four will be eleven weeks and three days past four months, and a Saturday is the day a foreman of thirty-one years is on a gate and not in a building, and nobody is going to ask him, and I am not going to ask him, and the reason I am not going to ask him is written on the facing page of this book in my own handwriting about four weeks ago and it is dressed up as a discipline and it is not one.**

*Tuesday the seventh, 23.30. One day off the sixth.*

*One. **Four grounds and a form that asks a correct question and offers four wrong answers, and a person who handed me the right object for it without being asked, and I have written that down and I have not improved it.** The return is due Friday the tenth and a foreman of fifty-four is going to have something for it and I have not got anything to say about that.*

*Two. **The page home the two hundred and twenty-sixth day, blank, four marks in four hands and none of them mine. The token cold, the two hundred and twelfth day. Eleven remembered routes, ten crossings, the ninth unexplained and not to be explained before the end of July. The condition column is a hundred and forty-five days empty and the reason column is empty and both are correct. The plate is ninety-two days. The second tin is at twenty-nine and the last rack day was Friday the third.***

*And the standing position: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.***
