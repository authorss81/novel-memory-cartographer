# Chapter 184: A Line On A Deck, And A Man Who Says Nothing The Third Time, And Water In A Lane

**The man of about fifty with the mower had the van outside the gate at ten to eight on Tuesday the tenth of November. It is a white panel van with a rack on the back for mowers and a company name on the side in a colour that has gone the colour of the weather, and it belongs to a firm, and the firm is not him and the van is not a day off, and it has two stops and then a yard. He has said the last of that twice without being asked to.**

The page home is the three hundred and fifty-second day. The token is cold on the three hundred and thirty-eighth. The plate in the grass at the low end of Bridge Street is two hundred and eighteen days old and has been told to nobody. The condition column is two hundred and seventy-one days and is not there. **A foreman of fifty-four is twenty-nine weeks exactly past four months and nobody is going to ask him.**

---

## He told the driver something at the gate and the driver did not argue

"I am not going to be at this gate on a Tuesday."

"**Then I shall not stop.**"

He said it without any particular weight at all, and he put the rack in, and **it took about four seconds, and Milo Rook has written down that this is now the fourth time in eight months that he has given something up in about four seconds standing up and that he is not going to make a column of the four seconds, and that a Tuesday is the only day of the week he has and he gave it away on a Monday night in his own handwriting and said it out loud at a gate, and neither of those is a decision and both of them are the same decision.**

**The van is not a day off. It comes at ten to eight because the grass on this half gets long in November and it goes back to the yard at about twenty past two and it does not wait either way.**

---

## Nine years on this half, ten feet of grass on the other, and a third time that nothing is said

He cuts the city half with a bar and not a mower, and the mower stays on the rack, and he has cut this half for nine years and does it in about an hour.

**There is a line in the middle of the deck and it is not painted. It is just where the mowing stops, and the grass on the two sides is different because a man used to cut the far side and he stopped, and nobody has ever put a line anywhere, and the man has never once gone round.**

"There are still three things at that line."

The bar stopped.

"**There are,**" said Milo Rook, and then he did not finish it.

**He had it ready. He had it ready from about a quarter past nine and he had got as far as the first nine words and the man put the bar down against the parapet and looked at him, and the man did not say it, and there was about four seconds in which neither of them said anything, and then the man picked the bar up again.**

"**You are not going to say it,**" he said.

"No."

"**Good. I said it in September and I said it on the sixth of October and I am not saying it a third time to anybody, and you have had two of them and you have had a third one this morning and you have stopped about nine words into it, and I want you to understand that I noticed you stopping.**"

"That is fair."

"**It is not fair. It is Tuesday and I have nine years on this side and about ten minutes a morning on that one and I do not get paid for the ten minutes and I have never told anybody about the ten minutes except a man in a good coat who was standing on the far half in September.**" He shouldered the bar. "**And you did not ask me at the gate, and that is the same mistake you made in July and I told you about it then, and you are doing it again this morning, and I am not going to say anything about it again.**"

He was on the far side before Milo Rook had decided whether to follow.

**And this time there was no walk out past the line and no stop and no coming back in under a minute. There were about ten minutes of a sickle on the far half at about eleven o'clock, and the mower stayed on the rack where it has been since 1974 or whenever a load calculation with the low end of it not re-checked was put in a drawer in this city.**

**Three things at that line. One boundary. Three boundaries in this district and they are not joined and there has not been a fourth kind and there is not going to be one. The man is not a party to it and he is not a party to anything and he has now been asked for that sentence twice and has given it twice and this morning he gave Milo Rook nothing at all, and the nothing was the whole of the answer, and a man who gives a man nothing for a third time has not been asked a third question.**

---

## The handrail post, and the fourth time he did not ask, and the number of families

**The handrail post on the city side is four inches short of the one it took out, and a file in a drawer in a building about a mile away says *replaced like for like*, and about nine families navigate this path by that post, and nobody has levelled it and nobody is going to.**

He was standing about four feet from the only man who cuts both halves of that bridge. He had been four feet off that man on the seventh of August, on the eighteenth of September, on the sixth of October, on the thirteenth of October and this morning, and he had not asked about the post on any of them and he is not going to now, **and the reason he wrote down on the Friday before last is that a column is worse than a count and a count is a number somebody can be given, and this one is about four inches and there is nobody in Crown Heights who can say which post it was.**

Then the mower man crossed to the city half and looked down at the parapet and said, without any preamble at all:

"**There is water in your lane.**"

---

## And there was, and it was about nine inches deep at the low end, and he did not write it down

There is a lane about two hundred yards long that comes off the low end of Bridge Street and goes back towards the drain, and on the Tuesday morning at about half past eleven there was about nine inches of standing water in the bottom forty yards of it, clear, and still, and with a line of dead grass along both sides of it where it had been four inches a week ago.

**He looked at it for about four minutes and he did not write it down and he did not write the day down either, and he has said in his own book that the reason is that a depth of water in a lane in the eleventh of November is a fact about a lane and not about a district, and a man of forty-four told him in September that a depth of water is true on a day and a man of sixty-one told him in October that a yardage is about now and a date is a thing that happened, and both of those men were right and neither of them was talking about this lane.**

He did not go and find the man of about sixty with the shed at the end of it, and the reason is four words long and they are the same four words every time: **he does not know a piece of paper exists.**

"You are going to want to ask me whether the far side holds water in a wet week," said the mower man, from about nine feet off, on the deck.

"No," said Milo Rook.

"**No,**" he agreed, and went and cut the grass, and did not say anything else for about an hour, and the van was at the yard at about twenty past two and did not stop again.

---

## The rest of it, and a Tuesday that has now got nothing in it at all

He was in eleven streets by about half past four with nothing in his hands and one sentence in the book, and the sentence is four words long and it is this: *a route that cannot be produced cannot be asked after.*

**He has not drawn a map of the far half. He has not drawn a map of the lane. He has not drawn a map of the drain, or the ditch, or the place where the path comes off the far side, and he has been asked by nothing to, and there is no instrument in this city that would take one and hold it, and he is not going to build one.**

**And the nine is the wrong number to be holding and he knows it and he has not written the nine down: about nine people know how to get from the low end of Bridge Street to the ford and back without going round, and each of them knows it a little differently, and he has never asked one of them, and the reason he has not asked is that asking is the only way he would find out and finding out is the only way he could produce it and producing it is the thing he has spent eight months learning not to do.**

Nobody thanked anybody. There is a man of about fifty who cut about ten minutes of grass he is not paid for and did not have to and said nothing at all when he was asked nothing, and he is not thanked and is not going to be, and he has not asked to be thanked, and on the seventh of October he said *you did not ask me at the gate* and this morning he said it again and then said he was not going to say anything about it again, and Milo Rook has now been told twice and has stopped mid-sentence once and is not going to make a third.

---

*Field Book, Tuesday the tenth of November, 23.10, a front room in eleven streets, one day off the ninth.*

*One. **I gave up a Tuesday at a gate in about four seconds, standing up, and the man said he would not stop and did not argue and put the rack in, and that is now four times in eight months and I am not making a column of the four seconds.** A Tuesday is the only day of the week I have. I gave it to a village eleven miles the other way and the cost of that is not the day.

*Two. **A man of about fifty said nothing.** Three things at that line, one boundary, three boundaries and they are not joined, and I got nine words into a sentence I have had ready since a quarter past nine and he put the bar down and looked at me and I stopped, and he noticed me stopping, and he said he is not saying it a third time to anybody, and then he cut about ten minutes on the far half with a sickle and did not walk out past the line at all this time. **A man who gives a man nothing for a third time has not been asked a third question, and I have been asking the wrong question since September and the wrongness was mine and it is not going to be put right by asking it a third time.**

*Three. **About nine inches of standing water in the bottom forty yards of a lane, clear, still, with dead grass along both sides of a line that was four inches a week ago.** I looked at it for about four minutes and I did not write it down and I did not write the day down, and the reason is that a depth of water in a lane in November is a fact about a lane and not about a district, and a man of forty-four told me in September that a depth of water is true on a day and a man of sixty-one told me in October that a yardage is about now. **And there is a man of about sixty at the end of that lane who does not know that a piece of paper exists and I did not go and find him, and that is three times in two days and I am not going to make a habit out of not asking.**

*Four. **I have not drawn the far half and I am not going to and there is no instrument in this city that would take a drawing of it and hold it and I am not going to build one.** And the thing underneath it, which I wrote on the bus and which is the only thing in this entry I am going to keep: **about nine people know how to get from the low end of Bridge Street to the ford and back without going round, and each of them knows it a little differently, and the only reason those nine are in agreement about it at all is that they have all walked the same ground. Ground is a document. There is no box for it on any form in this city and I am not going to draw one.**

*Five. **Nothing has moved.** Eleven remembered routes, ten crossings, the tally ten lines, not looked at since the thirteenth of January. The page home the three hundred and fifty-second day, blank, four marks in four hands, none of them mine. The token cold, the three hundred and thirty-eighth day, and it has done nothing since the seventh of December. **The list on the facing page reads twenty-nine and it has not moved in one hundred and seventeen days, and an interval of one day is not a pair, and a pair is a fact about where I was and not about what happened, and if the count of pairs in this month is small and not large that is correct.** The condition column is two hundred and seventy-one days and is not there and the reason column is not there and no new figure is to be printed for either. The second tin is at thirty-seven and there is no thirty-eight. The plate is two hundred and eighteen days in the grass and has been told to nobody. A foreman of fifty-four is twenty-nine weeks exactly past four months and nobody is going to ask him. **The three boundaries are three and are not joined and the three sets of marks are three and are not joined and nobody has gone past the third post and nobody has levelled the handrail post.**

*And the standing position: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.**
