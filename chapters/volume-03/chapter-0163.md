# Chapter 163: A Mowing Line With Three Things Stopped On It And Still One Boundary

**The flatbed came in from the city on Friday the eighteenth of September at about a quarter to seven and was in Mireward at about half past ten, and he got off where the driver was going, and the driver had four sacks on the bed and did not ask him anything, and the flatbed goes back to the city on the Friday afternoon and is in the yard there at about half past four and it does not wait either way, and there is no bus and there has not been one since about 1961.**

The page home is the two hundred and ninety-ninth day. The token is cold on the two hundred and eighty-fifth. The plate in the grass at the low end of Bridge Street is a hundred and sixty-five days old. The condition column is two hundred and eighteen days and is not there. A foreman of fifty-four is twenty-one weeks and two days past four months and nobody is going to ask him.

---

## The far half, and the ten minutes that are not on a time sheet

He was on the city half at about eleven and the far half had not been cut, and the far half has not been cut since a man stopped, and the man stopped before the war, and the grass on it is about four feet high and it is not a lawn, it is a fen.

The man of about fifty with the mower stopped the machine about four feet short of the line, shut it off, and stood there.

"There is a strip along the top of the bank."

"There is."

"**Ten feet wide. Cut with a sickle. Ten minutes a morning, on the way in, before I start on this side, nine years, not on my time sheet, and I have told nobody until you.**" He did not look up. "**I have told you so that in about four years nobody can find out I have been over here and think I did it for a reason.**"

"I have not asked you about it."

"**No. And I have not asked you why you have been out here on four Fridays since the end of June, and it is not a walk and it is not a job, and I am not going to be the one who writes down the reason.**"

---

## The boundary, and the man who is not a party to it

He crossed the deck in about four seconds and stood on the far half and the grass came up to about his knee, and he walked out along the path to the first post, which is about a hundred and thirty yards from the parapet.

**And then he said the thing, standing at the top of the bank with the sickle in his hand, and he said it because he had thought about it since the seventh of August and he had been waiting for somebody to say the wrong thing about it so that he could say this.**

"There are three things at that line now."

The man of about fifty with the mower had come over the deck, which he has not done in nine years, and stood about four feet away.

"**There are two things at that line and you are standing on the third one, and I have thought about it since the seventh of August and I have got it worked out.**" He put the sickle down. "**A boundary is a place where two people agreed to have stopped. Two things stopped at that line and neither of them was a person and neither of them agreed to anything, and that is not my fault and it is not anybody's, and the mowing stopped on that half because a man could not get a machine over a bridge with a rail on one side and a slope on the other, and the grass stopped because the man who cut it stopped, and neither of them is a person and neither of them signed anything.**"

"And you."

"**I stopped there as well. And I did not agree with anybody. I stopped because there is a load calculation in a drawer in this city from about 1974 and the low end of it has not been re-checked, and a machine of mine weighs about two hundred and forty, and there are about three hundred yards of path on the far side that eleven men and a foreman used to walk twice a week, and there is going to be no gang on that path after tonight because the works stopped on the thirty-first of August, and I have not put two hundred and forty over it and I am not going to.**"

He picked the sickle up again.

"So there are three things at that line."

"**There are three things at that line and it is one boundary, and the boundary is the two, and I am not one of the two, because a party is somebody who agreed and I did not agree with anybody. I am a man who has stopped. There is a difference and it took me six weeks to find it and it is the only thing I have got to say about it and I would like it noticed that I have not said it to anybody else and I am not going to.**"

Nobody thanked him. **Milo Rook has three boundaries on a page in his own book and he wrote them down in the order he met them, and the second one has a man on it now, and the man says he is not a party to it, and the book still says there are three boundaries, and they are three, and they are not joined, and this is another time in eight months that a man has corrected my arithmetic about a place and been right and I have not improved the page.**

---

## The post that is four inches short, and the two sets of marks he did not go to

The handrail post on the city side is four inches short of the one it took out. **The file says *replaced like for like*. Nobody has levelled it and nobody is going to, and about nine families navigate this path by that post, and there is nobody in Crown Heights who can say which post it was.**

He did not ask the man about it. He was standing four feet from a man who cuts both halves of this bridge and he did not ask him, and he wrote that down, and it is the second time he has been four feet from that man and asked him nothing.

The second post is about ninety yards out. **Two sets of marks are about ninety feet apart near it. Two hands. The same water on the same days. Neither of them is in a file, and the file is on the other side of the water.**

He was about eleven feet from them on the seventh of August and could have walked ten yards and did not. He was about eleven feet from them on the eighteenth of September and did not, and he wrote one line about it, and the line was that he had stopped writing down what it was worth.

**A boundary is a place where two people agreed to have stopped, and a set of marks is not a boundary and a post is not a set of marks and the three sets of marks in this district are three and none of them is a fourth clue and none of them is a second instance of anything.**

---

## The third post, and four seconds

The third post is about ninety yards past the second. Beside it, in about four feet of ground, is the head of a culvert with a number cast into the collar, and **it is in column three of a district schedule printed in the middle of May, and in a land desk about a mile from where that schedule was drafted there is a second copy in a run of nine, and nobody in eleven streets and nobody in Mireward has been told there are two of them.**

He did not read it out loud this time. In August he had read it out loud to nobody in a fenland and had written down that a thing said out loud where there is nobody to hear it is not a record, and he had said at the time that the only reason that is true is that fenlands are large and there is nobody in them, and that in about four years this city will be less large and that will not be true and it is not a thing anybody in this district gets to decide.

**He stood at the third post for about four minutes.**

At about the second of them the sound went.

It went the way it has gone twice in eight months. There was no sound at all — the wind in the grass, the water going along a drain somewhere off to the left, a crow about two hundred yards off, the van's engine cooling four hundred yards behind him under a bridge — and none of it. And under it, in the same four seconds, there was the taste he has never put a name to and has never put a place to, and it came and it went in the same four seconds, and it was not the same as the silence and it was not in it.

Then everything came back and it was all exactly where it had been.

**He did not write the time down.**

He wrote, in the book, on the Wednesday, that it had come and gone in about four seconds and that it had no face on it and no place and no person and no name, and that it acquired nothing, and that a lost sound and a bell that rings are two different things and he is not going to write them in the same column — which is the only rule on that page which is not about a month, and which he wrote on the twenty-fourth of July and has not improved and is not going to.

**And he wrote down, on the same line, that it did not become the mowing line and it did not become the post and it did not become the marks, and that if it ever becomes a thing he will write the name of that thing in this book at the time, and that until then it is four seconds and a taste and nothing, and that he is not going to be the man who gives a four-second thing a name because a name is the only thing in this story that anybody can be asked for.**

---

## Nobody has ever gone past the third post

Past the third post the path goes over the top of a bank, and the bank is the top of a dyke, and there is no fence, and in a dry week a dyke top is a bank like any other bank, and there is nothing on it and nobody on it and nothing to stop a man.

**Nobody has ever gone past the third post. Nobody is going to.** That is not a rule and it is not a resolution and it is not on a facing page. It is the line because two people agreed in about 1974 to stop maintaining one half of a bridge, and there have not been two people out here since about 1968 and there are not going to be.

He was back over the deck at about twenty past twelve and he went out along the path again and up to the gate at the north end, and the flatbed was on its way back to the city at ten to four and did not wait, and the four sacks were still on the bed where they had been that morning.

*Field Book, Friday the eighteenth of September, 23.50, a front room in eleven streets, one day off the seventeenth.*

*One. **Three things at a mowing line and one boundary.** Two things stopped there and neither of them was a person and neither of them agreed to anything, and now a man of about fifty has stopped there as well and he has told me, in about ninety seconds, that a party is somebody who agreed and he did not agree with anybody, and that he is a man who has stopped, and that there is a difference, and that it took him six weeks to find it. **I have three boundaries on this page and the second of them now has a man on it who says he is not a party to it, and the three are three, and I am not going to have a fourth and I am not going to join any of them to any other.** A man with a sickle ten feet wide for nine years, ten minutes a morning, not on a time sheet, and he told me so that nobody could decide he crossed for a reason, and he is not thanked and he does not expect it and he has said he is not going to say it again to anybody.*

*Two. **A handrail post four inches short of the one it took out, replaced like for like, and about nine families navigate by it, and I have been four feet from the only man on that bridge twice and I have not asked him about it and I have written that down twice.** A post is a post. The file is wrong in a way that will stay wrong and nobody is going to level it and about nine people use it every day. **A post that has been replaced not like for like is the wrong shape for a boundary and the right shape for nothing else, and the load calculation that says so is in a drawer in a building about a mile from here and it is about 1974 and the low end of it has not been re-checked, and that is the sentence a man of about fifty gave me standing on a bank with a sickle and it is the best reason anybody has given me for anything in two months.**

*Three. **Four seconds, at the second of four minutes at the third post.** A sound gone and a taste come and no face and no place and no person and no name, and it acquired nothing, and it did not become the mowing line and it did not become the post and it did not become the marks, and **a lost sound and a bell that rings are two different things and I am not going to write them in the same column.** I did not write the time down, which I have not done before, and I have written down that I did not do it, and a man who writes down that he did not do a thing has still done the thing, and there is no form for that and I am not going to build one. **And I have written down in the same breath that if it ever becomes a thing I will write the name of that thing in this book at the time, and that until then it is four seconds and a taste and nothing. I am not going to be the man who gives a four-second thing a name, because a name is the only thing in this story that anybody can be asked for.**

*Four. **Two sets of marks about eleven feet off, twice, and a cast number in a collar that is in column three of a printed side in two buildings, and I did not go and look at the marks and I did not read the number out loud.** The three sets of marks are three and none of them is a fourth clue and none of them is a second instance of anything and a post is not a set of marks. **Nobody has ever gone past the third post. Nobody is going to. I stood at it for four minutes and two of them were not mine and I did not go on.**

*Five. **The pairs are twenty-nine and have not moved in sixty-four days and the reason is the same reason.** Eleven remembered routes, ten crossings, the tally ten lines, Hettie Brann's paper in Dr Sen's pocket, not looked at since the thirteenth of January. The page home the two hundred and ninety-ninth day, blank, four marks in four hands, none of them mine. The token cold, the two hundred and eighty-fifth day. The condition column is two hundred and eighteen days and is not there and no new figure is to be printed for the reason column, and there never was going to be one. The second tin is at thirty-seven and there is no thirty-eight. **A foreman of fifty-four is twenty-one weeks and two days past four months, and the contract ended on the thirty-first of August, and the man is on a firm's books and not on a district's, and nobody is going to ask him, and the date of that silence is in my own handwriting on a page I cannot get it out of.**

*And the standing position: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.**
