# Chapter 174: A Top Track, A Bell On A Nail, And A Man Who Said Not To Come Back

**The man of about fifty with the mower had the van outside the gate at ten to eight on Tuesday the thirteenth of October. Two stops and then the yard, and it belongs to a firm, and it is not a day off, and he said all of that in the same order he said it on the sixth.**

"You are going to the top track."

"I am going to the top track."

"**Then say the rest of it.**"

"**I am going up a track to ask a man of sixty-one a question he told me in July not to come back and ask, and I have had the sentence ready since Friday, and I know what that means.**"

The mower man looked at him for about three seconds and then said: "**It is not a day off for you.**" and put the rack in.

**Tuesday the thirteenth of October.** The page home is the three hundred and twenty-fourth day. The token is cold on the three hundred and tenth. The plate in the grass at the low end of Bridge Street is a hundred and ninety days old and has been told to nobody. The condition column is two hundred and forty-three days and is not there. **A foreman of fifty-four is twenty-four weeks and six days past four months and nobody is going to ask him.**

---

## The house that is not made of brick and has no number

The top track is about four miles the other way and it is a cart track for about a mile and then it is a path, and the van got as far as it could and stopped, and he walked the rest.

**There are four plots up here and a house on the first of them that is not made of brick and has no number and has never had either.** The walls are a poured shell and the roof is a roof. There is a woodstove, a table, a chair, a bed, a shelf with about nine books on it, and a jar on the table with coin in it.

**Barnaby Pike is sixty-one. He has been digging in this district for about thirty-one years. He is on no rate roll and has no employer and is paid in coin out of the jar and has no wage, no record, no holiday, and no name on anything in this city.**

He was in the top plot when Milo Rook came up the track, about half past ten, digging, and he did not stop and did not look up, and he worked out who was there from about the fourth stride, which is a thing Milo Rook has never been able to explain and has stopped trying to.

**He rang a handbell on a nail inside the door before he started, twice, and he rang it once when he stopped, and he does it with his left hand while he digs with his right and he does not stop digging to do it.** That is the whole of what the bell is on this page. Nobody arranged it. There is no meeting. Nobody is watching.

Milo Rook stood about four feet away for about two hours and did not ask him about the bell and has written down that he is not going to.

---

## The sentence, which was ready before he got there

He had it by the time the van stopped. He had it ready on Friday afternoon and he had been carrying it about for three days, and he wrote that evening that this is the second time in eight months he has arrived somewhere with a sentence in his mouth, and **a man who has a sentence ready before he arrives somewhere has already decided that somewhere is where the sentence goes, and the first time it cost a boy of seventeen four seconds in a yard in front of about nine people, and this time he did not even give anybody the chance to refuse it quickly.**

"Mr Pike."

"**Pike.**"

"I know."

"**Then you know why I have not written to you and am not going to, and you know about a letter, so do not stand on my track and be formal.**" He straightened up. "**You are not going to tell me you are not coming back again.**"

"No."

"**No.** Good."

---

## The ask, and the refusal, and they are both of them right

"I need a length."

Pike waited.

"**A length of what.**"

"The frontage in eleven streets. The real length, walked, not on a contract." Milo Rook did not look at the jar. "**The schedule went up in a corridor on Thursday. It has two thousand three hundred and forty yards in it for this district, and that is the length of surface works somebody did in a quarter, and if a season gets costed on that figure then a season of work gets done against a set of works instead of against a place, and I cannot stand in a corridor on a Monday morning and say that to anybody, so I have come up a track in a van to ask a man of sixty-one.**"

Pike put the spade down against the leg of the plot and did not sit on anything, because there was nothing to sit on.

"**Why do you want it.**"

"Because it is wrong."

"**Say the other reason.**"

"Because I want to be the man who put it right."

"**There it is.**" Pike did not raise his voice and did not move for about four seconds. "**Now I am going to say a thing and you are going to hear it, and then you are going to go down that track in that van and you are not going to come back, and if you come back I am not going to say it again.**"

---

**"I have got nineteen years of dates in my head. That is the only ledger in this district and it is in here."** He knocked the side of his head with two knuckles and did not smile. "**A yardage is a different kind of thing from a date. A yardage is about now. You can say a yardage out loud to anybody and it is a true thing about this afternoon, and if a man writes it down it does not follow me about for the rest of my life. A date does follow you. A date is a thing that happened. And there is no column anywhere in this city for a thing that happened.**"

"I am not going to write down a date."

"**You are going to write down a number and then you are going to want to know when you got it.**" He picked the spade up. "**And then in about four years there is a man in a room who comes and asks me for the day, and I will have to say a day, and there is nowhere to put a day, and he will go away and write down that it was completed by the office, and it will not be wrong, and that is the part of it I cannot get past.**"

---

"And the other thing," Milo Rook said. "The thing underneath it."

"**There is a jar on this table with thirty-one years of wages in it in coin and there is no wage and no employer and no name of mine on anything, and there is not one of us in this district that can be produced.**" He started digging again. "**You have spent eight months working out that a thing which cannot be produced cannot be asked after. You have written it in that book four or five times, I have not read it and I do not need to. That is me. That is the jar and the bell and the plot with no number on it. If I say a yardage to a man in a good coat in a front room, then in a year somebody wants a figure about a drain, and in four years somebody wants a figure about a date, and in about nine years there is a man in a room who can produce me, and he gets there by asking one man about a length of frontage in eleven streets.**"

"And you are the first."

"**I would be the first. There is nobody else to ask.**"

---

## The cost, and it is not curable, and he is not going to be asked about it either

He worked on the top plot until about half past twelve and then went and did the grass on the first plot, which is not his and which he has done for about nineteen years, and Milo Rook stood at the gate and watched him do it and did not help, because helping would have been a way of staying.

**He got in the van at ten past one and the van was full of mowers and the driver did not ask him anything on the way back and the van was at the yard at about twenty past two.**

**And the cost of Tuesday the thirteenth of October is not the length. The cost is that he went.**

*He was told on Friday the twenty-fourth of July, standing in a doorway on a top track, not to come back. He has not been up that track since the twenty-eighth of August. He wrote it down twice — once on the twenty-fourth and once in the front room — and both times he wrote the same thing, that he was not going to, and he went on the thirteenth of October at about half past ten in the morning in a van that belongs to a firm, with a sentence he had been carrying since Friday.*

**A man who says not to come back has said a thing about the future and Milo Rook has broken it, and there is no form anywhere for the difference between a man who was asked to come back and did not come and a man who was not asked and came, and there is not going to be one.**

**And the part of it that is not curable is this: he cannot ask Pike whether it was all right. The only way to ask a man whether you should have come is to come again, and coming again is the thing that was asked not to be done, and so the question cannot be put, and so it stays on the page, and so it will be on the page in four years, in a book, in a coat, in a man's own hand, with his name at the top of it and nobody else's.**

Nobody thanked anybody. There is a man of sixty-one who dug a plot and did a grass he was not paid for and would not say a number out loud, and he is not thanked, and he is not going to be thanked, **because a man who says a number to the right person does not need to be thanked and a man who has been asked does not need anything from the person who asked.**

---

*Field Book, Tuesday the thirteenth of October, 23.15, a front room in eleven streets, one day off the twelfth. Nothing has moved and the day-counts have gone on without me.*

*One. **I went up a track I was told not to go up, and I asked a man of sixty-one for a number, and he said no, and he was right, and the reason he gave me is the best reason anybody has given me for anything since a man of forty-four told me a depth of water is true on a day.** A yardage is about now and a date is a thing that happened, and there is no column anywhere in this city for a thing that happened. **And underneath that: I would have been the first. There is nobody else in this district they can ask, and the jar on the table and the plot with no number and the bell on a nail are the only reason I am not in a room in Crown Heights this evening, and he knows it, and I have known it since the second of January and have written it down in this book four times and I have never once had to use it on anybody.** I used it on him and he knew before I got there.*

*Two. **The cost is not the length and I am not going to write it as a column.** I went. I was told not to and I went and I had the sentence ready on Friday, and **a man who has a sentence ready before he arrives somewhere has already decided that somewhere is where the sentence goes, and that is now twice in eight months, and the first time it cost a boy of seventeen four seconds in a yard in front of nine people, and the second time it cost a man of sixty-one the only thing he has got, which is that nobody can produce him.** And I cannot ask him whether it was all right and I am not going to try, and there is no form for that and there is not going to be one. **I am not going to put this on either of the two lists. A list is a column and a column is a column and the two lists on the facing pages are two lists and the sum of them is not a number, and this is neither of them, and I have just written three sentences on the same of those two subjects and that is enough.***

*Three. **The bell.** A handbell on a nail inside the door of a house that is not made of brick and has no number, rung twice before he starts and once when he stops, with the left hand while the right hand digs, not stopping to do it. **I stood four feet off for two hours and did not ask him about it and I am not going to and there is no column on this page for it and there is not going to be one.** Four bells in four places is four bells in four places and the fifth thing is the one that would turn it into a row and the fifth thing is somebody writing down the four.*

*Four. **Nothing has moved.** Eleven remembered routes, ten crossings, the tally ten lines, not looked at since the thirteenth of January. The page home the three hundred and twenty-fourth day, blank, four marks in four hands, none of them mine. The token cold, the three hundred and tenth day, and it has done nothing since the seventh of December. **The list on the facing page reads twenty-nine and it has not moved in eighty-nine days, and an interval of one day is not a pair, and a pair is a fact about where I was and not about what happened, and if the count of pairs in this block is small and not large that is correct.** The condition column is two hundred and forty-three days and is not there and the reason column is not there and no new figure is to be printed for either. The second tin is at thirty-seven and there is no thirty-eight. The plate is a hundred and ninety days in the grass and has been told to nobody. **A foreman of fifty-four is twenty-four weeks and six days past four months and nobody is going to ask him.** A woman of thirty-four in a village eleven miles the other way has not been asked by anybody this month and a name of hers went onto nothing today because there is nothing to put it on and there is not going to be.*

*And the standing position: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.**
