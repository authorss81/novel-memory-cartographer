# Chapter 179: The Ordinary Day, And A Thing He Gave Up On Purpose About A Person

**The round went out at about ten past eight on Thursday the twenty-ninth of October and came in at about four with the bag empty, and it is a Monday and a Thursday round and it has been since the second of January and it is one man short, and the bag has been empty since the twenty-eighth of August.**

The page home is the three hundred and fortieth day. The token is cold on the three hundred and twenty-sixth. The plate in the grass at the low end of Bridge Street is two hundred and six days old and has been told to nobody. The condition column is two hundred and fifty-nine days and is not there. **A foreman of fifty-four is twenty-seven weeks and one day past four months and nobody is going to ask him.**

**And this is the last Thursday of a month, and the return goes on a last Thursday of a month, and he was not in that room and did not go and is not going to write down what is in the line she has never described to anybody in about two years, and a promise about not asking is the only kind of promise this book has ever held.**

---

## The morning, and the lorry, and a man with a handcart

There is a lorry to a yard at the north end on a Thursday morning. It is the second motor vehicle in this district in a week and neither of the two is his, and he stood at the corner at about ten and watched it come in and go out again and did not get a lift and did not ask for one, because asking for a lift is asking, and he has asked enough people in eight months to last him until the end of the volume.

The Row was there. The man of about forty swept it with a brush and did not stop and has still not been asked for his twenty-eight undated words and is not going to be. The fishmonger of about forty-five had her trays out by nine and was not thanked and was not asked.

**And a man of about sixty with a shed eleven minutes away was on the Row at about half past ten on a Thursday and said good morning, and he does not know that a piece of paper exists and is not going to be told, and nothing that has happened since the sixth of April has touched him, and there is no form anywhere for a man who is not on anything.**

The barriers were still out on the back length. The road was open. A site is not cleared by a date.

At about ten past four the man of about forty-one came through the corridor with a handcart, and Milo Rook said good afternoon, and that was all, and the man said nothing, and he has not been sent anywhere and is not going to be, and he is on no list at all.

---

## The forms went out and nothing came, which is what a post is

**The season's work went out on the first of October with a form about a length of frontage in yards, and it went out of a building in Crown Heights on the Thursday, and there is no satchel out of this district any more and there has not been since Friday the twenty-eighth of August, and the works' papers were the only thing in eleven streets that ever weighed anything, and the post stopped when the contract stopped, and nobody was told, and the notice was a printed side about the works.**

So: the return went out. The amendment went out. Nine hundred and something rows went out with a column in them and a number of yards in them. **And none of it came, and none of it was going to come, and it was not going to come on Friday either, and the office will complete the rows in about a minute and a half on a Thursday, and every one of them will be correct.**

He wrote that down on the Thursday night in about four sentences and then did not write anything else about it for about an hour.

---

## And then the thing he gave up, at about nine, in a front room, on purpose

**He had built it on the Monday. All of it, in the order it would be said in, in about eleven seconds, standing in a corridor with a pencil: *the bus goes past the end of the street every twenty minutes on a weekday and two of them a day go as far as that village, and I will be on one at eight in the morning, and I will be there by eleven, and I will ask a woman of thirty-four one question, and the question is what does the village want.***

He got as far as *what does the village want* in about four seconds and then he worked out, on the twenty-ninth of October, at about nine in the evening, in a front room in eleven streets, in about two minutes, why he was not going to do it.

**Not because she would refuse. He has known since the seventh of September that she would refuse, and being refused by her would be about four seconds long and would cost her nothing and would be the fourth time this year and she has said she is not going to be the woman who hands a district its first piece of stationery and she said it without being asked and he has written it down twice.**

**The real reason, and he wrote it in his own hand and it is the truest thing in this entry and he is not going to improve it:**

*If I go there on a Tuesday then I will be told what I have already decided to do. **I have been in this position since the seventh of September and I have not decided anything, and that is the position I am proud of, and it is not a position, it is a way of not having to find out what I would do if I were told.** And if I am in that schoolroom for four hours I am going to agree with her, because she has been keeping it off paper for nine years and I have been keeping a field book for eight months, and she is better at it than I am, and agreeing with her is not asking her, and then I will come away from that village with the thing I went there for, and I will not have asked her a single question, and I will have done the thing to her that the office did to eleven streets with a contract, which is decide about a place using a piece of paper about a place.*

**So he gave it up. Standing up, in about four seconds, on an ordinary Thursday, with nobody watching him decide.**

---

## The cost, which is not curable, and which is about a person

**The schedule takes effect tomorrow. Friday the thirtieth of October. And a schedule is a thing that is either maintained or not maintained, and that sentence is printed at the top of it in the office's own words, and the eleven streets and a village eleven miles the other way are both on it, and neither of them has been asked, and one of them cannot be and the other of them has not been.**

And here is the part that does not come off:

**He cannot ask her afterwards. That is not a feeling and it is not a mood and it is not a mood he can be talked out of on a bus in about three weeks' time. It is that a question asked before a decision is a question, and a question asked after a decision is a request to be told that the decision was right, and everybody in that schoolroom would hear the second one, and there are seventeen of them on a Friday and nineteen on a Tuesday and twenty-one on a Monday, and it is a roll in a woman's head and not a sheet, and nobody has ever rung it up and nobody is going to.**

**And by the middle of November there will be a printed side somewhere with a number on it, or a bus will have gone past the end of that street and stopped, and one of those two things will have happened, and then it will not be a question any more. There is no instrument in this city for converting a decision back into a question. I have looked for one for about nine years of other people's experience and there is not one and I am not going to build one.**

**And the roll is a roll. Seventeen of them on a Friday, nineteen on a Tuesday, twenty-one on a Monday, and it is in her head and it is not a sheet, and there is a gap on Mondays of two children and she has never once written down why, and nobody has ever rung up for it, and I am not going to be the man who goes up a track or a village or anywhere else and asks a person for a number of that kind. A number of children on a day is the most producible thing in this district and it is the one thing I am not going to say out loud.**

**Nobody in that village is going to be told anything by anybody, and a woman of thirty-four is not going to be asked, and the cost of that is hers and it is permanent and it is mine, and neither of us has been asked whether we can carry it.**

---

## And the two other things that happened on an ordinary Thursday, and neither of them is about a village

**One.** A man of fifty-four with thirty-one years in charge of a gang is on a firm's books and not on a district's. He has a name in three boxes on two printed sides and a remark in a darker typeface on a board in a corridor, and he signed none of them, and he has not been asked about any of it, and nobody is going to ask him, and he is twenty-seven weeks and one day past four months. **Milo Rook did not say the seventy-eight words on Thursday the twenty-ninth of October and there is no reason on earth why he would, and they are not any other number, and nobody is going to improve them.**

**Two.** There is a page in a drawer with a coat in it, and it is hers, and it is the eleventh block, and neither of them mentioned it in one word today and they were not in a corridor today, and he has not told her about the season, and she has not asked since July, and she found out he had stopped counting by noticing that the number stopped going up and has not told him she noticed and there is no column for that and there is not going to be one.

---

*Field Book, Thursday the twenty-ninth of October, 23.55, a front room in eleven streets, one day off the twenty-eighth. The last Thursday of a month and she reconciles the return today and I was not in the room and did not go and I am not going to write down what is in a line she has never described, and a promise about not asking is the only kind of promise this book has ever held.*

*One. **A thing I gave up on purpose, in about four seconds, standing up, on an ordinary Thursday, with nobody watching me decide, and it is about a person and it is not a habit, and I am not putting it on either of the two lists.** The two lists are two lists and the sum of them is not a number and a reader in about four years is going to add them together and get something that does not exist, and I wrote that down in September in my own hand and I am not going to improve it. **A third list is a column and a column is a column, and I gave this one up so that I would not have to make one.**

*Two. **And the reason, in my own hand, and it is the truest thing in this entry.** If I go there I will be told what I have already decided to do. **I have been in that position since the seventh of September and I have not decided anything and I am proud of it and it is not a position, it is a way of not having to find out what I would do if I were told.** And she has been keeping that schoolroom off every piece of paper in this city for nine years and I have been keeping a field book for eight months and she is better at it than I am, and agreeing with her is not asking her, and then I will come away with the thing I went there for and I will not have asked her one question, and that is what the office did to eleven streets with a contract. **Decide about a place using a piece of paper about a place. That is the whole of what I have been writing down about for eight months and it is what I was about to do to a village on a Tuesday with a bus ticket.**

*Three. **The cost and it is not curable.** The schedule takes effect tomorrow and a schedule is either maintained or not maintained and that sentence is printed at the top of it, and neither of them has been asked, and one of them cannot be. And I cannot ask her afterwards, because a question before a decision is a question and a question after a decision is a request to be told that the decision was right, and about twenty children in that room would hear the second one, and a roll in a woman's head is not a sheet and nobody has ever rung it up. **By the middle of November there will be a number on something, or a bus will have gone past the end of that street and stopped, and then it will not be a question. There is no instrument in this city for converting a decision back into a question and I have not gone looking for one and I am not going to build one.** The cost is hers and it is mine and neither of us has been asked whether we can carry it and nobody is going to ask us.

*Four. **Nothing has moved.** Eleven remembered routes, ten crossings, the tally ten lines, not looked at since the thirteenth of January. The page home the three hundred and fortieth day, blank, four marks in four hands, none of them mine. The token cold, the three hundred and twenty-sixth day, and it has done nothing since the seventh of December. **The list on the facing page reads twenty-nine and it has not moved in a hundred and five days, and an interval of one day is not a pair, and a pair is a fact about where I was and not about what happened, and if the count of pairs in this block is small and not large that is correct.** The condition column is two hundred and fifty-nine days and is not there and the reason column is not there and no new figure is to be printed for either. The second tin is at thirty-seven and there is no thirty-eight. The plate is two hundred and six days in the grass and has been told to nobody. A foreman of fifty-four is twenty-seven weeks and one day past four months and nobody is going to ask him. **The four unshowable things in five months are four and there has been no fifth, and I am not counting them, I have not counted them since the first of July, and there is no total anywhere and there is not going to be one, and a fifth needs somebody to be surprised and nobody in this district has been surprised about anything since the third of July.**

*And the standing position: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.**
