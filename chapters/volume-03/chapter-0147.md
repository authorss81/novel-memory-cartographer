# Chapter 147: The Same Print Run, About A Mile Away, On A Saturday Morning

The records counter is open to the public on a Saturday morning from about ten and there were two people in front of it and Milo Rook was one of them, and he had come into eleven streets on the flatbed at about half past four on the Friday afternoon and had walked from there, because there are two motor vehicles a week in Mireward and neither of them waits and the one that had come in at a quarter to seven had not waited for him either.

**Mara Quill was not in front of the counter. Mara Quill was behind it, in a coat, on her own Saturday, doing her own work, which is not a thing that happens, and she had told him on the Friday evening in about eleven words that it was happening and had not explained it and he had not asked.**

"You have got the schedule."

"I have got side four."

"Give me that."

---

**And she had it in about nine seconds, and she had it on a Saturday morning with two people waiting behind him, and she did not go into a room.**

"Column two," she said. "**Stability of district. That is the column. That is the whole of the second use and I found it in about nine seconds on Tuesday and I have been sitting on it since Tuesday waiting to see whether you would find it before I told you.**"

"That is a column about whether a district is in a condition."

"That is a column about whether a district is in a condition, and it is a column of about nine hundred rows, and about eight hundred of them have a word or a zero in it, **and it is a hand-transferred column and it is not typed and it has never been typed, and there is a row in it for a district with a hand-transferred nil in it on the ground that there is no maintained seam of record, no recorded loss, no resident witness and no schedule.**" She squared the sheet on the counter. "**A land schedule does not want a condition. A land schedule wants to know who is on the ground. And the second column of this form says, in the handwriting of a man with a machine, in about eight hundred rows, that there is nobody there. And the third column of this form is a list of works of record, and a work of record is a thing on the ground, and a thing on the ground in a district that column two says has nobody in it is a thing on the ground that nobody is on.**"

"Show me the number."

"**The cast number on the collar beside the third post is in column three, in a typeface half the size of the others, in a run of nine, against MIREWARD.** This schedule was printed in the middle of May and issued in the first week of July, and it went out of the same press run as a schedule that has been sitting in a land desk about a mile from where the form was drafted since about the twentieth of May. **A land desk is a room with four desks and a counter and it does not do weather and it does not do works. It wants a schedule that says a place is unoccupied, and it has had one for about eight weeks, and the copy in your bag arrived nine days ago, and nobody in eleven streets and nobody in Mireward has been told that there are two of these.**"

---

"And the line in your return," said Milo Rook.

"**No.**"

"That is the second—"

"That is the second time and I said once, and that was on the twenty-seventh of June, and it is the eighteenth of July and I have not been asked a third time and you did not ask me a third time and I have noticed that you did not." She put the sheet down. "**I am going to give you one more fact and it is a fact and not the line, and then I am not going to do it again. The last Thursday of every month I reconcile a return in that room. The last Thursday was the sixteenth. I have reconciled it on the last Thursday of every month for about two years before that and it has never come up in a conversation with anybody.**"

"That is four facts and none of them is the line."

"That is correct and it is deliberate and it is the last of it."

---

**And then they had the disagreement, and it is the one this volume is going to have, and it is correct on both sides, and it is not repaired here and it is not going to be repaired in this volume.**

"You should put the district's answer on the record."

"On which record."

"On that one. **In about four years a man in a room is going to ask why the eleventh street district's works were discontinued on the ground that there is no maintained seam of record, and there is going to be a file, and the file is going to have side four in it and nothing else, and the whole of what this district did in eight months is going to be a sentence somebody typed in a minute and a half.**"

"That is a column with a sentence in it about a district with two hundred and fifty-two people in a building with no number and about a boy of seventeen and about a woman of sixty on a second landing who is the ninth of twenty-six names and is not going to be told anything by anybody."

"**I know that. That is why it has to go in. Not because it is safe. Because it is the only place in this city where an objection is a thing that exists.**"

"It is the only place in this city where an objection is a thing that can be asked for by a man with a box in about four years."

"**Yes. That is the same sentence and you have heard it and I have heard it and neither of us has a better one.**"

---

"You do not think it matters."

"I think it is the only weapon in this city and I think a weapon you have to fire from inside the room it is aimed at is not a weapon, **and I also think that if there is one sentence in that file with this district's name on it then in about four years there is a man who can ask a room for that sentence, and the room will produce it, and the sentence will be about the sixteenth of December, and it will be produced against a woman of thirty-four who knows twenty-six names and has not written them down for four years on purpose, and against a foreman of fifty-four who is not going to be asked about December and will be asked about a file.**" He had not raised his voice at any point and he was aware of that. "**And I have said that to a works clerk of forty-six on a Friday afternoon and he said the same sentence back to me in four seconds and I have not improved his.**

"You have told me about the season eleven weeks running," said Mara Quill.

"**Twelve.**"

"**You have stopped counting.**

"I stopped counting in May and I started dating it and I wrote down that dating it is a column, and a column is worse than a count, and you said in June that you were not going to ask, and you have not asked, and that was correct of you and I have not thanked you for it and I am not going to."

"I have a page of my own in a drawer with a coat in it and I am not going to say anything about it, this week or in July, **and that is not me being cold, and you have known that since May.**"

"Yes."

"So we are two people who have both said that and neither of us has moved and neither of us is going to."

"**That is where we are. And I am not going to improve it in a records room on a Saturday with two people waiting.**"

---

**And at about eleven o'clock she read him the rest of it out loud, standing up, in the order the print run went, because that is what she does and because on the third of June a woman of sixty-one read four returns out loud to a man who had not asked and it is the only method in this story that has ever worked on anybody.**

And the ninth row from the bottom, in a typeface half the size:

> **MIREWARD — 41-2, 4 to 12 inclusive — works of record — unoccupied, no rateable unit.**

"Nine," she said. "**Nine plots, held on shares, and there is a book of shares, and the book of shares is in a different building and it is the only book about this district that anybody in this city has ever opened.**"

"Nobody has asked for a number off a collar before Friday and I did not write it down."

"You did not write it down," she said, "**and it is on a printed side since the fourteenth of May, and it was on a printed side before you put a hand on it on Friday, and the fact that you put a hand on it on Friday has changed nothing at all and I want that written down somewhere, because I have watched a man put a hand on something and I have watched what it did last time.**"

---

**Nothing romantic happened on the eighteenth of July and nothing is going to.**

There is no kiss in this chapter and there is no declaration and there is no jealousy scene and there is no rescue, and she did not soften the drawer, and he did not ask about it for the eighth consecutive block, and it is hers and only hers and it is in a drawer with a coat in it and it was not produced, not named, not confessed to and not mentioned in the words either of them used.

**And on the way out he did not ask her a second thing, and she did not offer a second thing, and he wrote it down on the Sunday night in about nine words and the nine words are that his not-asking about the drawer and his not-asking about the line are the same act and he has known that since the fifth of May and has done nothing about it, and a date is a column, and he has now put a date on the not-asking and that is two columns where there used to be one.**

*Saturday the eighteenth, 22.40. One day off the seventeenth. **The page home the two hundred and thirty-seventh day. The token cold, the two hundred and twenty-third day. Eleven routes, ten crossings, the ninth unexplained and not to be explained before the end of July. The plate is a hundred and three days. The condition column is a hundred and fifty-six days empty and on the thirty-first of August it will not be empty, it will not be there. Eleven remembered routes and ten crossings and the tally is ten lines and I have not looked at it and I am not going to look at it in August.***

*And the standing position: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.***
