# Chapter 161: A Week In Which The Only Thing This District Has Is An Absence

**The round went out at about ten past eight on Monday the seventh of September and came in at about four with the bag empty. It is a Monday and a Thursday round and it has been since the second of January and it is one man short, and the bag has been empty since the twenty-eighth of August, and it is not a walk any more and nobody has called it one.**

The page home is the two hundred and eighty-eighth day. The token is cold on the two hundred and seventy-fourth. The plate in the grass at the low end of Bridge Street is a hundred and fifty-four days old and has been told to nobody. The condition column is two hundred and seven days and is not there.

**And the second tin is at thirty-seven, in a plan room in another building, on a bottom shelf, under a repair schedule, and it is not going anywhere, and the tin does not know that and neither does the repair schedule.**

---

## The count, and there is nowhere to put it

He did the arithmetic on the Monday morning, standing up in a front room, with a pencil and the back of a sheet of his own paper, and it took him about four minutes, and he did not enjoy it and he did it anyway, because it is the kind of arithmetic he is good at and because nobody else in this district is going to do it.

From the thirty-first of August to the seventh of September: **seven days.**

From the last rack day to today: **two Fridays, the fourth and the eleventh, and the eleventh is tomorrow.** A rack day is a Friday because a sheet covers Monday to Sunday, and there has not been a sheet since the twenty-eighth of August, and so there have been two rack days in this district that were not rack days, and the second of them is tomorrow, and nobody in this city can tell the difference and nobody is going to.

From the last walk of the season to today: **six Mondays and Thursdays, and there is not a gang on any of them.**

He wrote the three numbers down. Seven. Two. Six.

*There is no column in this district that wants any of those three numbers. A district is entered as stable or not stable. A week is walked or not walked. A day is gone or not gone. **Nothing in this city can be told that a week went by and there was nothing in it.** A form wants a figure for a period, and a period with nothing in it is not a zero, it is a dash, and a dash in a cell is a statement about the post and not about the district, and this district has no cell because it has no return because it has no post, and that is not a fault and it is not a gap in a form. **It is the shape of the thing.***

**An absence is not a form.**

He looked at the three numbers for about a minute and a half and then turned the sheet over.

---

## The form he did not draw

He had the four columns in his hand in about eleven seconds and he wrote the four headings out in pencil on the back of the sheet, in order, because he is good at this and gets to be good at it about four times a year and every time somebody else has to hold the paper.

*Week ending. What was done. By whom. What was found. Why nothing was found.*

Four columns and about two inches at the bottom for the district's own use.

**It is the best one he has ever drawn and it is the same mistake for the eleventh time in eight months, and the mistake is a column that wants a person, and there is no person in it: there is a week and a nothing.**

He put the pencil down and did not draw it, and he wrote the reason out underneath in the same hand, which is a thing he has done four times in eight months and the page is getting short:

*Four columns about a week and about what was done on a road by men who are not on any paper, and a fifth thing, which is why nothing was found, and the fifth thing is a column that wants a person and there is no person, because a foreman of fifty-four wrote two sentences in pencil in July and both of them said a length was all right, and the works stopped on the thirty-first of August, and the district does not know where eleven men are. **A column that wants a person and has nobody to be given one is the same thing as a column that wants a person and is refused one.** I have written that sentence down in June and in July and in August and I am not going to write it in September.*

And then, because it is in the book and the book is the only place in this city where he is allowed to be wrong in public:

*And I want a fifth column. **I am not going to draw one and I have wanted one since the twenty-third of July and the wanting has not gone away and the rule on the facing page ran out on the thirty-first of August and nobody released me from it, because a rule that runs out is not a release and it never was a stop.** A seventh column is the shape of this one. I do not know what the difference is between the two and I am not going to find out on the back of a sheet of my own paper in a front room on a Monday morning.*

---

## Tuesday the eighth, and the things that are still there

He walked up at about half past nine on the Tuesday, four hundred yards and a fitter's shop, on his own, because the round does not go on a Tuesday.

The barriers were still out. The road was open. Both of those are correct, and a site is not cleared by a date, and a barrier that is up because men are coming back at half past seven and a barrier that is up because nobody has come look the same from a bus, and there is no vehicle to see it from and there has not been one since about 1961.

**The rack was against the wall where it is always against the wall.** He put his hand in it. It was cold. There was a mark on the shelf about nine inches from the left edge and about two inches wide, and it was the width it was on the fourth, and **he wrote one line about it and no more, and the line was that it has not changed, and a mark that has not changed is not a measurement and there is no instrument in this city for a mark that has not changed.**

And then he stood in the corridor for about four minutes doing nothing, which is not a habit and is not a discipline and is a man of thirty standing in a corridor because there is nothing in it.

---

## The drawer, and the bus, and the second time he has written it down

**There is a drawer in Crown Heights with a sheet of street numbers in it from 1911, and about nine hundred of those drawers in this city, and a bus goes past the end of the street every twenty minutes on a weekday, and he has not used one since the second of January.**

He has not gone, and he wrote on Tuesday the eighth, in the same pencil, in the same book, that he is not going, and that this is the second time he has written that sentence down in two months, and that a sentence written twice is not a rule and is not a resolution and is a third thing, and that he has not got a word for the third thing and has not had one since the fourteenth of April.

**A woman of thirty-four told him on a Saturday in July in a room above a kitchen in a village eleven miles from here, without being asked, that if somebody went and got that sheet out and put it back then her village would be a place with a number inside a fortnight, and that she has spent nine years keeping a schoolroom with seventeen children in it on a Friday off every piece of paper in this city and she is not going to be the woman who hands a district its first piece of stationery.**

He wrote that down too. He wrote down that he had not gone, and that the not-going is about a drawer and about a village and about a boy of seventeen who is not in this block, and that in about three weeks something is going to come to a head about it, and that he has written down that something is going to come to a head, which is a column, and that a column is worse than a count.

---

## Friday the eleventh, and the second rack day that was not a rack day

The flatbed comes in from the city on a Friday morning at about a quarter to seven and is in Mireward at about half past ten and it goes back to the city on the Friday afternoon and is in the yard there at about half past four, and it does not wait either way, and a lorry to a yard at the north end comes in on a Thursday morning, and there are two motor vehicles a week in a district of a hundred and forty people and one tractor.

**He was in eleven streets all day on the eleventh and he did not go out, and being in a place is not a day off, and a person with an address can be in a place on a Tuesday because being in a place is not a day off, and a person without an address is the one who cannot afford the day, and the whole of that sentence is about a building with no number and none of the two hundred and fifty-two people in it is on any post.**

There was no rack day on the Friday the eleventh. There was a rack, and the rack had nothing in it in it for the second time, and he did not write that down in a book, and he has written that he did not write it down, which is one more of the things of that shape and he is not counting them and is not going to start in September.

At about ten past four the man of about forty-one on no list at all came through the corridor with a handcart.

He was not sent. He is not going to be. He has been in this office four times a day since the twenty-eighth of December and he has never once been sent anywhere, and he is not the man of about forty-one who holds the key to the drawer in Crown Heights, and the second one is in no book.

Milo Rook asked him one question, and it was a fair one, and it was the only question he asked anybody that week.

"Is there a form anywhere that says a rack is finished."

"**No. And there is not going to be one.**"

Nine words. He got the handcart through the gap and did not add anything, because the nine words were the whole of the answer, and the things he does not say are in his own head and are not on any paper and are not going to be, and there is no column anywhere for the number of times he has said them and nobody is going to make one.

Nobody thanked him. Milo Rook did not thank him, and a person who has thanked somebody is a person who has asked, and that is a conductor's sentence from April and it is not improved in September.

---

*Field Book, Saturday the twelfth of September, 23.30, a front room in eleven streets, one day off the eleventh.*

*One. **A week, and the only thing in it was an absence, and I have got three numbers for it and there is nowhere to put any of them: seven days, two rack days that were not rack days, six Mondays and Thursdays with nobody on them. An absence is not a form, and the reason is not that a form is missing. It is that the only form this city has for a period with nothing in it is a dash, and a dash is a statement about the post, and there is no post.** I have spent eight months learning that a place is stable because there is nobody writing down what happens in it and I have spent the last five days finding out what that costs a person who has to stand in a street, and the two sentences are the same sentence and the second half of it is the half nobody puts on paper.*

*Two. **The rack, the tin, the barriers, the road, the post.** A rack with nothing in it in it for the second time and a mark on the shelf that has not changed and there is no instrument in this city for a mark that has not changed. The second tin is at thirty-seven and there is no thirty-eight and the tin is under a repair schedule and nobody has asked when the repair schedule ends and I am not going to ask. The barriers are up and the road is open and a site is not cleared by a date. **And there has not been a satchel out of this district since Friday the twenty-eighth of August, and the district returns card that has come in that post since the middle of January is a printed side, and it is one, and it is the only one that arrives, and it has not arrived for three weeks.** Nobody was told. The notice was a printed side about the works, and the works were the post, and the post was never mentioned.*

*Three. **I have wanted a column since the twenty-third of July and I have drawn none, and the rule on the facing page ran out on the thirty-first of August and nobody released me from it and nobody is going to.** A rule that runs out is not a release and it never was a stop, and the reason I was not going to wait for the first of September was that I was hoping a month would make it correct, and it did not, and a month cannot make anything correct, and I have known that since the sixth of January and it is the plainest thing in this book. **There are five of the things I have given up, and four of the five are about a person and one of them is about a habit, and there are six of the other shape, which are the things I got by writing something in this book and not doing it, and the two lists are two lists and must not be merged, and the sum of them is not a number, and I have written that down and I am not going to improve it.**

*Four. **The drawer, and the bus, and the second time.** A sheet of 1911 numbers in a drawer in Crown Heights and a bus every twenty minutes and I did not go on the Tuesday, and it is the second time I have written that sentence down, and a sentence written twice is not a rule and is not a resolution and is a third thing and I have not got a word for the third thing. A village four miles the other way would be a place with a number inside a fortnight and a woman of thirty-four who has said no to four people this year and would say no to me, and has said, unasked, that she is not going to be the woman who hands a district its first piece of stationery. **In about three weeks something is going to come to a head about it, and I have written that down, and a thing that is going to come to a head is a column, and a column is worse than a count.**

*Five. **Nothing has moved.** The remembered-route list is eleven, the crossings are ten, the tally is ten lines, Hettie Brann's paper in Dr Sen's pocket, not looked at since the thirteenth of January. The page home the two hundred and ninety-third day, blank, four marks in four hands, none of them mine. The token cold, the two hundred and seventy-ninth day, and it has done nothing since the seventh of December. **The list on the facing page reads twenty-nine and it has not moved since the night of the sixteenth of July, and that is fifty-eight days now, and I have an entry on most nights of them, and an interval of one day is not a pair whether or not either of the two days has anything on it, and a pair is a fact about where I was and not about what happened.** A foreman of fifty-four is twenty weeks and three days past four months and nobody is going to ask him and the date of that silence is on this page in my own handwriting and I put it there and I cannot get it out of it.

*And the standing position: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.**
