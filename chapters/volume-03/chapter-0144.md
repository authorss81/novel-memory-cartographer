# Chapter 144: A Seventh Column That Is The Same Mistake For The Ninth Time

It was a room above a kitchen in Cobb Lock on Saturday the eleventh of July, and it was about four in the afternoon, and there were two pieces of paper on a table and one of them was a form and one of them was a card.

**The first piece of paper was a district returns card.** Single side, about nine inches by five, printed on the top half and ruled on the bottom.

> **MIREWARD. Rateable units: nil. Returns received: nil. Reason: —**

and two inches of ruled space at the bottom that has been for about forty years and that almost nobody fills in.

**The second piece of paper was four sides with six columns on the first side and a key of four grounds on the second and a line at the foot about a district that has not returned within the period stated, and the last day for the district's answer was yesterday.**

---

He had been carrying both of them since Wednesday. He had been in this room since ten in the morning, and he had a pencil, and at about ten past four he wrote at the top of a sheet of his own paper a heading and then about a hundred and ten words under it, and every word of them was true.

> **why this district's works are not of record**

He read it back. It was the best thing he had written since June and it was the same mistake for the ninth time in eight months, and he named it himself in about four seconds, standing up, in a room above a kitchen, to nobody, and he has not improved it in the eight months either.

**A column that wants a name and has nobody to be given one is the same thing as a column that wants a name and is refused one, and both of them are a form that wants a person.**

And then he looked at the facing page of his own book, which he had not brought into Mireward and did not need to bring into Mireward, because he can see it.

> **No seventh column before the first of September.**

Written on Wednesday the first of July at about half past four in his own hand, four lines long, and the cost written out under it nine minutes later, and the last line of the cost says that it is the most self-serving sentence he has written in seven months and that it is dressed up as a discipline and is not one.

**And a stop that a man wrote about himself is not a stop.** He said that out loud in a room in a village with a hundred and forty people in it, on a Saturday, in July, to nobody, and then he did not write the column out and he folded the sheet once and put it in the bottom of the bag face down.

**There are two pieces of paper in that bag now and both of them are face down and both of them are seventh columns and both of them are about a district with no rateable unit, and a bag is not a custody, and I wrote that in this book on the first of July, and I have not got a better one.**

---

**And then he did the thing he is good at, and it took about ninety seconds, and he wrote it in pencil on the back of the schedule, in a room eleven miles from the district it is about, on the day after the day it was due, and it is the truest sentence he has put on paper in eight months, and it went in the bag too.**

Column two. *Stability of district.* The row for the eleven streets, which is the only row on that form anybody in this room has ever been interested in.

> *A seam opened in this district at a length that has a level under a foreman's signature and a line in a file with a date on it, on the morning of Tuesday the sixteenth of December. It was closed on the evening of the twenty-second of December. The joint finding of the fourth of December is in the file. The classification of the eleventh is withdrawn and is not to be relied on by any party. The works on the back length have continued twice a week since the second of January and the condition column has been empty since the twelfth of February and both of those are correct.*

Ninety seconds. Every word of it true. **And if that sentence had gone into column two of that row on Friday the tenth of July, the check would not have found nil, the office would not have had a key to write down, the ground of discontinuance would have had nothing in it, and there would not have been a sentence in a file about a morning in December that a person in a room a mile away could ask for.**

He put the pencil down.

**The reason he did not send it is on the facing page of this book in his own handwriting and the reason is not about the district and it is not about a man of fifty-four and it is not about the thirty-one and it is about him, and it says *no seventh column before the first of September*, and a seventh column is a sentence you want somebody to have to answer, and he wrote that rule for himself in a front room on a Wednesday afternoon because he is good at this and gets to be good at it about four times a year and every time somebody else has to hold the paper.**

**And the sentence I have just written on the back of a schedule is not a seventh column. It is a fifth column. It is column two, filled in by a man who is not the person responsible for maintenance in any district in this city, in a room in a village, on a Saturday, on the back of a form, in pencil, and if I send it then the only row on nine hundred that has a man from another district's building in it has a man from another district's building in it, and the man is me, and about four years from now a room can produce it.**

**So there is no version of this that is not him deciding.**

---

**And at about ten past five Sabra Trewin came in, thirty-four, nine years teaching, and she had come in for something ordinary and it was still ordinary when she got there.**

She put a bag down and asked him whether he had seen the tractor on the top track on Thursday, and he had, and they agreed about the tractor, and then she said:

"Have you got a piece of paper with the First Surveyor's office on it."

"I have."

"Can I keep it for a week."

"No."

"**You have not asked me what for.**"

"You were going to tell me and I am not going to make you ask."

"I am going to tell you anyway because I have had four people ask me this month and I have said no to all four and I would rather say it once to somebody who has come from four miles away." She sat down on the end of the table. "**If the district has an address then the first thing that arrives at the address is a demand. And if a schoolroom has a form in the drawer then the schoolroom is a place, and I have got nineteen children in it on a Tuesday and twenty-one on a Monday and not one line of them is on any paper in this city, and the pencils are in that drawer and if I put a piece of paper in there with a district on it then in about a fortnight a man will come and ask me for the roll.**"

"You could hand it back."

"**I could hand it back to a man in a coat, and in about four years the piece of paper would be in a file and the file would say that a schoolroom in a district with no rateable unit had held it, and that schoolroom is mine, and there are nineteen children in it, and none of them is on the roll and all of them would be on it inside a month of the first demand.**" She did not soften it. "**I have watched four years of this from a chair with nineteen children in it and I am not going to be the woman who hands a district its first piece of stationery.**"

"You do not know what is on it."

"**I do not know what is on it and I have not asked and I am not going to, and I would like it noticed that nobody has had to ask me twice.**"

---

**And she said one more thing on her way out, and it was not about the form, and it was the whole of what the Saturday cost.**

"**Your foreman sent something on Friday.**"

"Sent something where."

"**I do not know where and he did not tell me and I did not ask, and I am telling you that he sent something, and that is a thing I know, and that is all the things I know and I have been thinking about it since the milk.**"

"To send it where."

"He said *it has gone in*." She was at the door. "**And I said where does it go to, and he said it has gone in, and he is a man who has said nine words to me three times in eight months and he said that one twice.**"

---

**And Milo Rook stood in a room above a kitchen and worked it out in about nine seconds, standing up, to nobody, and it is the ninth time he has named something himself before he has finished it, and it is the only one of the nine that was not a mistake in a column.**

A district with no rateable unit has no stationery. **A district that was never on anything has nothing to put anything in.** A foreman of fifty-four, thirty-one years, who had said he would have something for Friday, made something for Friday, and it did not go in a bag and it did not go in a post and it did not go into a building, because **the district does not have an envelope, and it has not had one since about 1911, and the numbers that would have let a clerk address one are in a drawer in Crown Heights and nobody has ever gone and got them out, and a woman of thirty-four said in June that if somebody did, they would be a place with a number inside a fortnight, and she is not going to be a woman who asks a city to do them a favour.**

**And I could have gone and got them out. That is the whole of it. There is a drawer in Crown Heights with a sheet in it from 1911 and about nine hundred of those drawers in this city and I have a key to this one in my own pocket because a man of about forty-one opened the door of the room it is in for me in February and did not ask why, and I have been carrying that fact around for five months like a stone in a shoe, and on Friday the tenth of July I was on a gate at ten to seven with a form in my bag and the drawer is eleven miles away and a bus exists.**

**A bus exists and I have not used one since the second of January and the reason I have not used one is that the round is a Monday and a Thursday round and I have never been on a Monday or a Thursday with nothing to carry.**

---

**And he did not go on the Saturday and he did not go on the Sunday, and on Sunday the twelfth of July at about eleven o'clock at night he wrote it down, and the page home is the two hundred and thirtieth day and the token is cold on the two hundred and sixteenth day and the plate is ninety-six days in the grass and a foreman of fifty-four was on a gate in eleven streets this morning, eleven weeks and three days past four months, and nobody asked him, and he is not going to be asked.**

**And what is in the bag is a card, a schedule, a folded sheet with a heading and a hundred and ten words in it, and a sentence in pencil on the back of a schedule, and none of them is a record and all four of them are about a place that cannot be sent a letter.**

*Saturday the eleventh, 23.55. One day off the tenth.*

*One. **The ninth seventh column, and it is the same mistake, and the reason there is no name to put in it is the same reason as the eighth one, and a person refused one and a person does not exist are the same shape of hole, and I have written that down in June and I have written it down again here and I have improved it none of the two times.***

*Two. **I wrote the answer to column two in ninety seconds in pencil on the back of their own schedule and I did not send it and I have worked out in about nine seconds tonight that there are only three reasons and two of them are good.** The first is that the classification of the eleventh is withdrawn and is not to be relied on by any party, and an entry that rests on a withdrawn classification cannot be made, and that reason is correct and it is the department's reason and not mine. The second is that about four years from now a room can produce it and I would be in it. **The third is that the rule I am hiding behind is four lines long and is in my own handwriting and is about me, and I put it there on the first of July because I am good at this, and a stop that a man wrote about himself is not a stop, and I said that out loud in a room in Cobb Lock at four o'clock this afternoon to nobody and it is the truest thing I have said in eight months and it is not a resolution.***

*Three. **A foreman of fifty-four made something on Friday for a district that has no envelope, and it has gone in nowhere, and I have found that out from a woman of thirty-four who was asked nothing and told me one sentence on her way to the door, and he has not been asked about it and he is not going to be asked about it, and I am not going to ask him, and I am going to have to be in a yard with him on Friday and I am not going to be able to say one word about it and he is going to think I do not know.***

*Four. **The drawer.** There is a sheet of 1911 numbers in a drawer in Crown Heights and a bus exists and I did not go, and I have known about the drawer since the fifteenth of June and I did not go then either, and I am not going to write down why, because I have got one reason and it is the same reason as the rule on the facing page and I have used it up.***

*And the standing position: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.**
