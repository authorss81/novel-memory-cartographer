# Chapter 152: Four Copies Of A Bell

**The flatbed comes in from the city on a Friday morning at about a quarter to seven and is in Mireward at about half past ten and it goes back to the city on the Friday afternoon and is in the yard there at about half past four, and it does not wait either way.** He was standing at the gate at ten to seven on Friday the twenty-fourth of July with a bag and no coat over his arm, because it had been raining in the city on Thursday and had stopped, and the driver did not ask him anything.

He got off here at about half past ten where the driver was going.

**There is no bus and there has not been one since about 1961, and there are two motor vehicles a week in a district of a hundred and forty people and one of them is this one and it goes back at four, and the other is a lorry to a yard at the north end on a Thursday morning. The plate in the grass at the low end of Bridge Street is a hundred and nine days old. The page home is the two hundred and forty-third day. The token is cold on the two hundred and twenty-ninth day.**

---

**He was on a top track above a holding at about eleven, and he was there because he had been told there was a man who would speak to him, and he had been told that by a woman of thirty-four who had said it in one sentence on her way to a door and had then said, without stopping, *he is there on a Friday and he is there in the morning and he does not stop.***

**Barnaby Pike, sixty-one.** He has dug in this district for about thirty-one years. He is not on a rate roll, because a ratepayer is a person at an address and there is no address. He has no employer, because the families pay him and they pay him in coin out of a jar on a table, and a coin is not a rate. He has no wage, no record, no holiday, and no name on anything in this city.

He was in a hole with a spade and a bar.

---

**And he rang a bell twice before he started and once when he stopped, and Milo Rook stood forty feet away for about two hours and a quarter and did not say one word, because there was nothing to say to a man who is working.**

The bell was a handbell. It hung on a nail inside the door of a house that is not made of brick and has no number and is about nine feet from where the hole was. **He rang it with his left hand and he dug with his right and he did not stop digging to ring it, and Milo Rook has written that down and has not decided what it means.**

There was nobody in the house. He went in and came out twice. The door was not locked. There was a jug on the table and a chair and nothing else that Milo Rook could see from where he was standing, and he did not go in, because he was not asked.

At about half past one, after the last stroke, something happened to the man's hearing.

It went, for about four seconds, the way it has gone twice in eight months. There was no sound at all. **The bell was still there and the spade and the man and the wind in a willow and the water going along a ditch eleven feet away, and none of it.** And under it, in the four seconds, there was the other thing, which is a taste he has never put a name to and has never put a place to, and it came and it went in the same four seconds, and it was not the same as the silence and it was not in it.

Then everything came back and it was all exactly where it had been.

Milo Rook wrote the time down. Then he wrote down the sentence, and the sentence is the one he will not improve:

***A lost sound and a bell that rings are two different things and I am not going to write them in the same column.***

---

**And at about half past two, when the bar was up and the mound was half back, Barnaby Pike came up the side of the hole and stood on the edge and wiped his face with the back of his wrist and said the first part.**

"**You have not asked me how many.**"

"No."

"**Everybody asks me how many.**" He put the spade in the ground and left it there. "**Everybody who comes out here. They want the number and then they go off up the path and I can hear them working it out. I have had that since about 1975 and I have never given it to anybody and I am not going to give it to you, and you can write down that I said that, because you have got a book.**"

"You do not know what is in my book."

"**I have got a spade and you have got a book and we are both stood on a top track in a district with no address and neither of us is going to be asked about it. Write down whatever you like.**"

---

**And then he told him, and he told it while he worked, and Milo Rook did not stop him and did not ask him anything for about forty minutes, and it is the longest single account of anything he has been given in eight months and it is four hundred words long and none of it was a document.**

"**Somebody dies. And there is nowhere here to put it.**"

"You mean the register."

"**I mean there is nowhere here to put it and I have not got a word for the other one. There is a place in this city with a counter in it and you can take a death there on a Tuesday and a man will write it down and give you a bit of paper, and that bit of paper comes back to a house and a house pins it on a wall. And we have not got a counter and we have not got a wall to pin anything on and we have not got a street, and the one bit of paper anybody in this district could ever be given would have to be posted, and there is no envelope comes here.**"

"So it is not registered."

"**It is registered. That is the thing I want you to hear because everybody gets it wrong. It is registered. Somebody signs it. There is a man in a town eleven miles away who has a book with a date in it and a name in it and he will give it to whoever asks and he is not wrong and I have never once said he was wrong.**" Pike moved a spadeful out of the way of his boot. "**What there is not, anywhere, in any book in this city, is a row. There is no row in this district for a death because there is no district to have a row in. So the paper comes back to the house and it goes on the wall and about two years after that the house changes hands or the damp gets it or somebody clears a shelf, and then it is gone, and what is left of that person is that about four people were in the room.**"

"And that is the bell."

"**That is the bell.**"

---

"**Here is the whole of it and I am going to say it once and then I am going to dig. When somebody dies here, the bell goes that day. That is the first one. And the bell goes again the following year, on the same date, and it goes the year after that, and it has gone every year since, and nobody arranged it and there is no meeting and there was no first one that anybody can point at.**"

"You go back on the date."

"**I go back on the date. I open the hole up and I put it right again and I do the grass and I take the weed off it and I go, and it takes me an hour and a bit and nobody is watching and nobody thanks me and I have never asked to be thanked, because if you thank a man for this then you have to give him something, and there is nothing to give me.**" He wiped his hands on his trousers. "**The bell is rung before I start and once when I stop. That is twice and that is once and that is how it has always been and if you ask me why twice before I will tell you that the man who started it is dead and I do not know what he was doing.**"

"Who is it."

"**I have told you I am not going to say. Not because it is a mystery. Because if I say a name then the name is a name I know, and if a name is a name I know then in about four years somebody can come and ask me which date goes with it, and the date is the whole of it, and the date is in here.**" He tapped the side of his head with two knuckles, once, and did not say anything else about it. "**I have got about nineteen years of dates in there and I have never written one of them down and I have never been asked and those two things are not a coincidence and the second one is the reason the first one is easy.**"

---

"**And I know there are more than one of us and I am not going to tell you how many and I am not going to tell you where the others are, and you can have that in about four words: I do not know the number either. I know the people I have seen. I have got an idea. An idea is not a number and I am not going to give you an idea dressed up as one, because you will write the idea down and in about four years somebody will ask you for the number.**"

"You think I am going to write it down."

"**I think you have got a book and you have not opened it while you were standing there, and that is the first time in two hours that you have done anything at all.**" Pike got the spade. "**And I will tell you the one thing about your book that I would tell anybody, and then that is all, and I am not going to be here on the next Friday so do not come next Friday.**"

"Go on."

"**Whatever you write down will be the first time anybody has written down any of it. And that is not a service. That is a door. There has not been a door in this for thirty-one years and there has not needed to be one, and I have never in my life seen a thing get written down and then go back to being how it was.**"

---

**And at about four o'clock the flatbed went back to the city and he got on it at ten to four in a yard at Cobb Lock and it was in the yard at the city end at about half past four and he walked from there, and the bell had gone three times and the hole was filled and there was nothing on the page of any book in this city about any of it, and he had not written it down.**

**The plate is a hundred and nine days in the grass. The page home is the two hundred and forty-third day and it is blank and it has four marks in four hands and none of them are mine. The token is cold on the two hundred and twenty-ninth day. The condition column is a hundred and sixty-two days empty and has two entries in it and both of them are in July. The second tin is at thirty-two and Friday the twenty-fourth was a rack day.**

---

*Field Book, Friday the twenty-fourth of July, 23.40, a front room in eleven streets, one day off the twenty-third.*

*One. **Four copies of a bell. That is the phrase and I made it, in this book, on Friday afternoon, in about four seconds, and it is the worst four words I have written in eight months, and it is mine, and nobody has got it but me. *Copies* says there is one thing here four times over and there is not one thing here four times over. He would not give me the number and he said in about four words that he does not know the number either, and that is the only sentence anybody has said to me about any of the four, and it is not about a bell.** Four bells in four houses and one of them is on a post on a bank four miles away and I have stood under it. Nine were counted on a Thursday in June and I could not put a ring on any of them. A handbell on a nail in a house with no number. A bell rung twice before a man starts work and once when he stops. **A bell for a death that is registered in a town eleven miles away and has no row in the district it happened in, and then again the following year on the same date, and again, and nobody arranged any of it, and there is no meeting and no first one anybody can point at.***

*Two. **A practice is not a place and it is not a day. I have four bells in four places and that is four things and not one thing, and there is no word in this city for what the four of them have in common, and the word is not *district* and it is not *route* and I am not going to look for one. If somebody in this district starts writing down the four then the four stop being four people doing a thing and become a row, and a row is a document, and a document is the door he has just told me about in about four seconds without being asked.***

*Three. **The instrument, and it is the seventh thing in part (c) of the question and it is the strangest of the seven and I have had it in my hand for two hours and I cannot produce it. A date in a man's head. **A bell cannot tell you a date. It can tell you that today is the day and it throws the date away when it has finished, and that is the only instrument anybody has ever found in this district that can hold a date without writing it down, and it is a bell, and a bell is a bell, and I am not going to build a form for it and there is no form for it and there is not going to be one.***

*Four. **The sound went for about four seconds at half past one and the taste came with it and neither of them had a face on it and neither of them had a place or a person or a name and they did not acquire one. It has not come since June and I have written down for the third time that I do not know whether that is a thing that has stopped or a thing that has not come, and I have improved it none of the three. **And I have written down beside it that a lost sound and a bell that rings are two different things and I am not going to write them in the same column, and that is a rule I have given myself and it is the only rule on that page that is not about a month.***

*Five. **The pairs are twenty-nine and they have not moved and I have written an entry on most nights for a week and an interval of one day is not a pair and that is the reason and it is the correct reason. Eleven remembered routes, ten crossings, the ninth unexplained and I have said in this book four times that I am not going to explain it before the end of July and the end of July is in a week and I have not done it and I am not going to do it in a week, and a promise about the end of a month is the only date in this book I have written down twice and I am about to break it for a third time and a date is a column and a column is worse than a count.** The tally is ten lines. A foreman of fifty-four is thirteen weeks and two days past four months this morning and nobody is going to ask him.*

*Six. **There are nineteen years in a man's head on a top track and there are nineteen cuts in a post at a ford four miles away and they are two different numbers and I have written them down next to each other on purpose so that a reader of this book can see me not joining them. **Cuts are not years. A man does not cut a mark because a year has gone by. That is a woman with a folding rule telling me something in June and it is the same shape of mistake and I made it once and I am not making it twice. I have not been back to that post and I am not going this week.***

*And the standing position: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.**
