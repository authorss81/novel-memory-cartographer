# Chapter 139: A Man Who Is Not Paid For Holding The Water Down

**On Monday the twenty-ninth of June the gang was on the back length at half past seven, eleven men and a foreman with a clipboard, and it was the twenty-fifth walk since the first of April, and the reason column was empty and the condition column was empty, and both of those are correct. The round went out at about ten past eight and came in at about four and it is one man short and has been one man short since the twenty-eighth of December, and the second side of the sheet has a line on the Monday because the round was on the Monday.**

**And on the same Monday morning, about eleven miles away, at a sluice that belongs to a body that was abolished in about 1968, a man of forty-four held a bar in both hands for about an hour and was paid nothing for it.**

---

He goes there on the Tuesdays and the Fridays now, when the ground is soft, and the rest of the week he is on a farm at the north end and he is paid by the farm by the hour for ditching, which is a job with a word for it.

"Two days a month," he said. "**That is what it is. Two days a month of it is a sluice. And the farm does not know I am doing it and the water authority does not know I am doing it and the sluice does not know I am doing it, and if I do not turn up on a Tuesday in a wet week then the water goes over the dyke at Cobb Lock and it is not a decision anybody took, it is a man who was somewhere else.**"

"You are not on the rate roll."

"I am not on anything." He said it without heat, the way he said water. "**A man with no address cannot be a ratepayer, because a ratepayer is a person at an address, and a man with no address is not a person to a form. So the water authority cannot bill me for the water I am holding and cannot employ me to hold it and cannot write a letter to me that would arrive, and I am doing it out of a farm wage by the hour, and the farm pays me for the Tuesday and the Tuesday is not a ditch and the farm does not know that either.**"

"So the cost is yours."

"**The cost is not mine. The cost is that it is a Tuesday. That is all. If I am somewhere else on a Tuesday in a wet week, then about eleven houses have a bad morning, and nobody will ever write that down, and in about four years somebody will say the dyke failed, and the dyke did not fail, and there is a bar in it.**"

---

**And there has been exactly one time in nine years that anybody has been pulled out of that water, and he told Milo Rook about it on the Friday, and it took about four seconds, and it is the only thing he has ever said that was not about water.**

"About nine years ago. A woman of about thirty and a boy of about nine. **The woman went in off the top bank because the top bank is not a bank any more, it is a place where the water has been four times in nine years and everybody knows it and everybody goes on it in July.**" He did not stop working. "Two hours with a chain and the tractor. The tractor is not insured to be in that water. I knew that. The chain came off the second time and I went round it three times and the boy came out on the third and the woman came out on the fourth attempt and the tractor is a tractor."

"Nobody reported it."

"**There is nothing to report it on.**" He put the bar down and stood up. "There is no form in this city for a man with a tractor going into a dyke. There is a form for an accident at work and I am not at work, and a form for a drowning and nobody drowned, and a form for a rescue and a rescue is somebody saving somebody, and that asks who they were and where they lived and what their number was, and I have got none of those, and the tractor has a serial number and the tractor is not a person. **So it happened, and there is one tractor, and there is a chain, and the next one will be the same, and the day the chain breaks and the tractor goes in is the day it stops, and I have thought about that more than I have thought about anything.**"

---

**And the refusal, and it was correct, and it was the man of fifty-eight's, and it was nine words, and it was said on the Tuesday morning at about eleven at the top of the raised path with a rod over his shoulder.**

"I leave at six. You have had the conversation."

"It is nine words."

"It is nine words," he said, "**and it is the fifth time I have said nine words to a man in your position in about two months and I am not going to explain it, because the explanation is that I have got to be able to leave at six, and I cannot leave at six if there is a conversation, and a conversation is the whole of what took the last four years off me, and I have one day a year and I do not want it to be a day I was talked at.**"

"I will come out on the flatbed."

"You will come out on the flatbed, and you will stand there at a quarter to seven, and I will not know that you are standing there, and that is the arrangement and I am not changing it and you are not going to be able to change it by being better at this than I am."

---

**And the price of that, said out loud in his own book on the Tuesday night, and it is the first price in seven months that is about a wage and not about a name.**

**There are two motor vehicles a week in a district with a hundred and forty people in it, and neither of them waits, and one of them is a van that belongs to a man who goes out once a year in June and will not know whether I am standing at a gate or not. The flatbed is on a Friday and the lorry is on a Thursday. Both of them are on a working day. And there is a desk in Crown Heights with a chair in front of it and about nine people in a floor who cover for a man who is out, and every one of those nine people is somebody's colleague, and the covering is done out of a morning that they were going to have.**

**That is the cost that is not curable, and it is not curable from inside this district and it is not curable from inside that one either, and nobody has done anything wrong, and it is a design. A person with an address can be in a place on a Tuesday because being in a place is not a day off. A person without an address cannot, because the being there is a day off and the day off is somebody else's morning. It is not a hardship and it is not an injustice and it is not going to be written about, and it is the reason there are nine hundred district returns cards a month with a nil in the reason column, and it is the reason a forecast can say there is no resident witness in a place where a man has been holding a bar for thirty-one years.**

---

**And it is not paid by the woman of about thirty-four who drafted the paragraph, and it is not paid by a woman of sixty on a second landing, and nobody in this district is going to be asked about it, and the round is one man short and will be one man short on Thursday.**

Field Book, Monday the twenty-ninth and Tuesday the thirtieth of June, one day off each other, and a Sunday between the twenty-seventh and this one with an entry on it, and the list has not moved since Thursday.

*Twenty-ninth, 23.15. One day off the twenty-eighth.*

*One. **A man of forty-four is paid nothing for two days a month of his life, and the reason is a name, and the name is the reason for nine hundred cards a month and for a nil in a column about weather.** A ratepayer is a person at an address. A man with no address is not a person to a form. So the water authority cannot bill him and cannot employ him and cannot write him a letter that would arrive, and the sluice he holds on Tuesdays and Fridays belongs to a body that was abolished in about 1968, and he is doing it out of a farm wage by the hour for ditching, and the farm pays him for the Tuesday and the Tuesday is not a ditch. **And that is not the cost. The cost is that it is a Tuesday. If he is somewhere else on a Tuesday in a wet week then about eleven houses have a bad morning, and nobody will ever write that down, and in about four years somebody will say the dyke failed, and the dyke did not fail, and there is a bar in it.** I have written that down in his words and I have not improved it and I have not put it in a yard and I have not said his name in this book.

*Two. **Nine years, one rescue, two hours, a chain, a tractor that is not insured to be in that water, a woman of about thirty and a boy of about nine off a top bank in July.** And there is no form for it, because a rescue asks who they were and where they lived and what their number was and he has none of those and the tractor has a serial number and the tractor is not a person. **So it happened and there is one tractor and there is a chain, and the day the chain breaks is the day it stops, and he has thought about that more than he has thought about anything, and he told me that in about four seconds while he did not stop working, and I have had nothing to add to it and I did not try.**

*Three. **The refusal, which is the fifth nine words in about two months and the third one that was not about a job.** *I leave at six. You have had the conversation.* **And the whole of it: a man who goes out on the first dry week has one day a year and he does not want it to be a day he was talked at, and a conversation is the whole of what took the last four years off him.** I am not going to be able to argue that and I am not going to try, and I am not going to be at a gate at a quarter to seven in June next year watching a man not know I am there, and that is the price of the fifth refusal and it is the first one I have paid in money and in a day of somebody else's morning. **Two motor vehicles a week in a district of a hundred and forty people and neither of them waits, and one of them belongs to a man who goes once a year and will not know whether I am standing at a gate or not, and the flatbed is Friday and the lorry is Thursday and both are working days, and a person with an address can be in a place on a Tuesday because being in a place is not a day off, and a person without an address cannot, because the being there is a day off and the day off is somebody else's morning.** That is not an injustice and nobody has done anything wrong and it is a design, and it is the design, and I have been in this district for seven months and it is the first one I have been able to name whole.

*Four. **And what has not moved.** The page home the two hundred and eighteenth day, blank, four marks in four hands, and I am not writing that it is there. The token cold, the two hundred and fourth day. Ten remembered routes, ten crossings, and the ninth's failure to cost one is still unexplained and I have not touched it. The tally is ten lines, Hettie Brann's paper, Dr Sen's pocket, not looked at since the thirteenth of January and I am not going to in July. The condition column is empty for a hundred and thirty-seven days and the reason column for the fifteenth week and nobody has ever asked about the reason column, and the second tin is on a bottom shelf under a repair schedule, and there is a pad in a drawer in the same building that the man who owns that shelf has never once asked about, and I have worked out this week that he and Percy are the same kind of person and I am not writing that anywhere but here. The plate is eighty-five days in the grass. **The order is suspended and not discharged and not varied and not withdrawn, the public name is not on the street name index, the removed sentence cannot be restored and has been asked about twice and not answered twice, the matter stands over and is not adjourned and has no date and is on nothing, the levy is unchanged and is not curable, and a round is one man short and will be one man short in July and in the fourth year.** I have not told Mara Quill about the season for twelve weeks and she told me in a corridor that there is a line in a return she reconciles that has nothing to do with me and that she has looked at it, and I asked what kind of line and she said that was the second time, and I did not ask again, and a date is a column and I have not asked her about the seventh of January since the fifth of May.

*Thirtieth, 23.20. One day off the twenty-ninth.*

*Five. **And the standing question, in three parts, and the first one has been standing in a field for two days and I have not decided to let it.** *Why does weather memory threaten a region the Registry claims is stable.* A place is not stable because nothing has happened in it. **A resident witness is a person on a list. A forecast of weather-driven seam events was issued for a season in which a district entered as nil on the ground that there is no resident witness, and there has not been a person on a list there since about 1911, and there are a hundred and forty people in it, and thirty-one of them are not even on a roll in a teacher's head, and a man of forty-four is not on a rate roll, and a woman of thirty-four is on a roll in a head because there is nowhere else to put it.** The entry has never once been wrong about a fact and it has been completely wrong about a place for sixty years, and a district that is not a place cannot be frightened, and the thing that has been standing at the old ford since Thursday is nine rings of a school bell and a tidemark and a smell, and I have no demand and no service and no failure out of it yet, and I have got nine more tides before the year turns and I am not going to write a seventh column about any of them.

*And the standing position, which is the last line of this book and of this month and does not improve: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.*
