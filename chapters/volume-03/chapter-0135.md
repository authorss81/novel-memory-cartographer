# Chapter 135: A Schoolroom With No Address And Nineteen Children In It

Coll Sarrowe was outside a sluice on the Tuesday morning of the twenty-third of June at about seven with a bar he had been holding in both hands for about an hour, and he is forty-four and he has held it for thirty-one years, and when Milo Rook asked him what the bar was for he said *water*, and when he was asked again he said *water*, and that was the entire interview for about four minutes.

**He is the other half of the man of fifty-eight and neither of them knows the other exists.** Percy goes out once a year and writes four lines about about nine feet of drain into a pad in a drawer in Crown Heights. Coll Sarrowe holds the water down every day of his life and there is no paper anywhere in this city with his name on it, and the two of them are the same missing record seen from opposite ends: **one of them writes down what he finds and nobody reads it, and the other one holds the thing down and nothing is written at all.**

"You go out with Percy," said Coll Sarrowe, at about ten past eight, when he had got the bar up and propped and could stop.

"How do you know that."

"**Because he tells me when he is going, once a year, in about March, in a car park, and he has done since about 1971, and I have never once been asked to go with him and I have never once asked.**" He wiped his hands. "He does a drain. I do the water. That is the whole of the arrangement and there has not been a word about it in thirty-one years."

"Then who wrote this up."

"Nobody. **The day I stop, it fills in about nine days and then it goes over the road at Cobb Lock and into about eleven houses, and after that it is not a question about a bar, it is a question about eleven houses, and there is a man from the water authority in a car in about four hours and he is a nice man and he does not know anything about drains.**" He said it without heat. "I am not going to be named on anything. **A named ditch keeper is a ditch keeper who can be got rid of. There have been four of us named in this district in thirty-one years and three of them left inside two years, and the two that left were the ones who were named, and the one who is still here is the one who is not, and that is not a mystery and it is not a grievance, it is just what happens.**"

---

**And the village is called Cobb Lock, and it has about a hundred and forty people in it in high water, and it has no address, and the address is not missing. It was never there.**

"A school is a rateable thing," said Sabra Trewin, who is thirty-four and has taught in the schoolroom at Cobb Lock for nine years. "A rateable thing needs a street. Cobb Lock had a street number in about 1911 and it was renumbered for a drain and the new numbers went on a sheet, and the sheet is in a drawer in this city, and nobody has ever asked for the sheet, and there are about four houses in this village that are on the new numbers and about a hundred and forty people who are not on anything."

"Nineteen children," said Milo Rook.

"**Nineteen on a Tuesday. Twenty-one on a Monday, and I do not have a piece of paper with twenty-one on it, and I have never had a piece of paper with any of them on it, and that is not a system of mine, it is the absence of a system, and I have got a roll in my head and it is the only roll of these children that exists anywhere.**" She put the register down on the desk. It was a school exercise book. "They are not on the roll at the education office. They are not on the relief list because the relief list goes to a street and there is no street. They have not been vaccinated in a clinic that has a record of them. **If there was a flood tomorrow they would be on a bus to somewhere, and the somewhere would be counted on the back of the bus by a man with a pen, and that is the first piece of paper that has ever been made about these nineteen children, and it would be made about them on the day, by somebody who does not know any of their names.**"

"Nineteen is not a number a bus can be counted out of."

"**Nineteen is exactly a number a bus can be counted out of, and it is the only number anybody would have, and it is in my head, and if I am on that bus then it is gone.**"

---

**And that is the refused address, and it was said out loud at about eleven in the morning at a door, and it is the sentence this district has been standing in for seven months with a different name on it.**

"You have got a village with a name," said Milo Rook. "Cobb Lock is on a map."

"**Cobb Lock is on a map the way a puddle is on a map.**" She did not raise her voice. "**A village that has no address is not a place that has been forgotten. A place that has been forgotten was on something and came off it. This was never on anything. Nobody did this to us. There is no order, there is no sheet, there is no meeting at which anybody decided that Cobb Lock should not have a street number, and there is a drawer in Crown Heights with the numbers in it from 1911 and it has been in that drawer the whole time, and if somebody went and got it out and put it back we would be a place with a number inside a fortnight, and nobody has ever gone.**"

"Would that be a good thing."

"**It would be a thing that could be taken away again, and we have been un-takeable for about sixty years by accident, and I am not going to be a woman who asks a city to do us a favour.**"

---

**And at about half past two on the same afternoon he walked the last half mile of the path with Coll Sarrowe, who had finished, and the water was down and the ditch was doing what it does.**

The smell came back about four hundred yards from the schoolroom. It is not a bad smell and it is not a bad day and there is nothing wrong with it, and it arrived the way it arrived on the bridge on the Friday, which is that it was not there and then it was, and there was no moment in between, and he wrote the time in the working book, and the time was twenty to three, and he has now got three times written in a working book and no explanation and he is not going to look for one.

"They will tell you it is the fen," said Coll Sarrowe.

"What is it."

"**It is the fen.**" He said it as though the question had been a stupid one. "It is there in a dry week and it is not there in a wet one and everybody under about forty has never smelt it and everybody over about sixty cannot remember a year without it. It is a smell of a place being a place. **I have never known anybody to put it in writing and I have never known anybody to be asked.**"

---

**And it came to him at about four o'clock, on the raised path, standing still, in about nine seconds, with nothing to do with the fen.**

*The district is entered as nil because there is no maintained seam, no recorded loss, no resident witness and no schedule.* He has had that sentence in his own book since February and he has never once been able to turn it over.

**No resident witness.** Not *no witness*. A **resident** witness. And the two halves of that are about as far apart as a card and a gate, and nobody in this city has ever been a resident witness to anything, because residency is a thing you have if you are on a list, and this whole place has been off every list since about 1911, and about a hundred and forty people have been living in a place that this city has classified as containing no persons, at the same time, for sixty years, and the classification has never once been wrong about a fact and has been completely wrong about a place.

**And a forecast of weather-driven seam events was issued for a season in which a district that has no address and no roll and no recorded loss was entered as nil, on the ground that there is nothing there to be frightened, and a person cannot be frightened in a place that does not contain a person, and that is not a design and there is nobody in it to have designed anything.**

**He wrote none of that down on the path. He wrote the time down and he wrote the word *resident* down and underlined it once, and he did not put a seventh column under it, and he did not go anywhere, because he was not going anywhere: he had been on that path four nights and he was going to be on it for two more, and about eleven miles away a man of forty-four was standing at a sluice holding a bar in both hands and being paid nothing for it.**

---

**And it was Wednesday the twenty-fourth of June, and he was about eleven miles away with his coat on, and the round did not go, and nobody in eleven streets stood in the weather, and the second side of the sheet of who does what and on which day had a blank on it, and that blank is the twelfth one since the eighth of April and the sheet is right about it.**

**And it was the only date this district has had since the fourteenth of April, and it was a date about a car, and it went at midnight on Tuesday, in an office in another borough, and nothing came of it.**

A tram authority's district office asked for a number — the number of persons carried on a working of 07:52 off Crown Heights on the morning of the ninth of June — by the twenty-fourth of June, and the district was not going to answer it, and the district did not answer it, and there is no form in this city for a date that arrives, and there is no form for a date that passes, and **the two are the same form and it is a piece of paper about a quarter that is already over.**

**A thing that has no form cannot be late. That is not a comfort and it is not a defence and it is the only thing this district has got: the return will be closed with the item standing open, and in about four years somebody will pull that file and find two item sixes, one of them with a June figure and a date, and one of them with a December figure and a date, and no column anywhere in that authority for the difference between the two, and both of them are correct, and neither of them is late, because late is a thing that happens to a person.**

He found out about it on the Saturday the twenty-seventh, in a yard, from a woman of sixty-three with a hand barrow, in about eleven words, and then went and did an ordinary day like the four hundred thousand other ordinary days in this city.

**And the page home is the two hundred and thirteenth day and the token the hundred and ninety-ninth, carried and not printed, and there is no entry in this book for six nights and the sixth of them is tonight, and I am not going to work out what the interval is going to be, and the plate is seventy-nine days in the grass and it is a decision and not a refusal and I made it on the first of June and nobody has been told, and a date came and went today and there is no column for it, and that is the whole of what I have got for a fortnight in which a village of a hundred and forty people told me twice that nobody has ever come.**
