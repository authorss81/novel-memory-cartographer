# Chapter 132: Four Lines About Nine Feet Of Drain

The gang was on the back length at half past seven on Thursday the eighteenth of June, eleven men and a foreman with a clipboard, and it was the twenty-second walk since the first of April with an empty condition column, and the reason column was empty too, and both of those are correct, and nobody has ever been asked about the second one in five months and it is now thirteen weeks.

A man of about fifty-eight was standing at the mouth of the yard at ten past eight with a rod and a chain in a bag and a pad under his arm.

He had been coming here at about this time since the middle of March. Everybody in this district knows that about him in the way they know the tide. **He goes out into Mireward on the first dry week of every summer and has done since 1974, and he writes four lines about about nine feet of drain, and the pad goes into a drawer in Crown Heights, and the pad in that drawer runs from this year back to about 1969.**

"Morning," he said.

"You have got a van."

"I have got a van." He put the bag down. "**And before you ask me anything, I have been going out on the first dry week since 1974, and I have never once written down that I have, and you are the first person in this city who has ever noticed, and I noticed you noticing, and I would rather say it now than have you ask me on the way back in a field with a chain in your hand.**"

---

**They went at about nine. It is about four hours each way by the road and there is no bus that goes there, and the last three miles of it is a raised path with a gate at the end of it, and the gate is not locked and there is nothing to lock it against.**

He drove. He talked for about twenty minutes and then stopped, and what he had said in the twenty minutes was that the district of Mireward has never had an office and does not have one now, and that he is not an office, and that a man who goes out in a summer and writes four lines about a drain is not an office, and he said it three times in three different ways without once sounding as though he were being asked to say it.

**And then he said the thing, on the raised path, at about eleven, with the rod over his shoulder, and it is the truest sentence anybody has said to me in seven months, and he did not know that and he is not going to be told.**

"They put that district down as nil," he said. "Every week. Four years I have had that card. **And a place is not stable because nothing has happened in it. A place is stable because there is nobody writing down what happens in it. Those are not the same and only one of them is a thing you can do something about, and I have been doing the second one for forty-one years and nobody has ever asked me a single question about it.**"

"You have just told me the sentence."

"I have just told you the sentence," he said, "**and I have told it to about four people in forty-one years and two of them were on my way and one of them was in a pub, and I have never had a reason to say it to anybody who could put it in a book, and now I have, and that is your fault, and it is not a favour either.**"

---

**He writes four lines. He has written the same four lines since 1969 and they are the same four lines this year.**

Line one is the date, in his own hand, in the ordinary way.

Line two is a number of feet, and it is about nine, and it has been about nine for as long as the pad goes back, and he said that a drain that is nine feet long has not changed length in forty-one years, and that is not a finding, and it is what the line says.

Line three is a water mark, and this is the one that is not the same every year, and it has been higher in six of the last eleven years and it was higher this year than it was last year, and he wrote it as a figure against a date and he did not say what the figure meant.

Line four is a name. **It is the name of the man who was with him, and for forty-one years the name in the fourth line has been the same name, and it is not his own, and Percy has never once been asked who it is and has never once said.**

"You have got a name in the fourth line."

"**I have got a name in the fourth line and I have never been asked who it is and I am not going to tell you and I am not going to be asked, and if you write it down I will take the pad home and that will be the end of forty-one years, and you would not do it, and I would not either, and that is the arrangement and it took about four seconds and I made it in about four seconds.**"

---

**And the refusal, and it was correct, and it cost him nothing, and it was the first refusal of this kind anybody had heard him make because nobody had heard him make any.**

They were on the path by the second gate at about half past eleven and Milo Rook had a notebook out, not the Field Book, a working one, the kind that is not his own property, and he had written four names on a page, and the man of fifty-eight stopped walking.

"You are not writing me down."

"No."

"**Then what are those four.**"

"People I have spoken to since February."

"**If you put me in a column I have to have a deputy.**" He said it without any weight on it at all. "**That is the whole of it and it has been the whole of it since about 1971. A man who goes out on the first dry week is a man who goes out on the first dry week. The day there is an office out there, or a name against a line on a sheet, or anybody at all who has to go because it is Tuesday and it is on a sheet — then there has to be a second one, because a thing that happens once a year cannot be left to a man who is sixty-eight, and the second one will not have been out here in a February, and he will not know which mark on that post is which year, and he will write a figure and put a name in the fourth line and it will be a different name, and then in about eleven years there will be a pad in a drawer with two names in it and no way of telling which line belongs to which man.**"

"And nobody has ever put you in a column."

"Nobody has ever put me in a column," said Percy, "**and that is not an oversight and it is not modesty, it is the only thing that has made this work, and there is no form in this city on which to thank a man for not being on a form.**"

---

**The thing he gave, he gave in a car park at about four in the afternoon with the engine off, and he gave it in nine words, and it was not a favour and he was clear about that.**

"I will take you out there. Tuesdays. Same as me."

"That is nine words."

"That is nine words," he said. "**And it is not Tuesdays. It is the first dry week and it is once a year and I am telling you the day. You are not getting a bus. There is no bus. There has not been a bus since about 1961 and there is not going to be one, because a bus wants a place to stop and a place to stop is a rateable thing and there is not one, and that is not anybody's fault and there is not anybody to write to about it.**"

---

**And the cost, which is the whole of the cost and which is not curable, and which he said in a car park with the engine off, and which is a fact about a drawer.**

"You asked me once, in a yard in March, why I have not asked for a copy," he said.

"You did not say you had not been asked."

"I did not say I had not been asked." He put his hands on the wheel and did not turn them. "**I have got forty-one years of the only record that has ever existed of a district that is entered as nil, and it is in a drawer in a building in Crown Heights that is not in Mireward and is not on the way to Mireward and has nothing to do with Mireward. There is one of it. It is not copied and I have never had it copied. Nobody has a schedule for it, nobody has a shelf number, nobody has ever rung me up and asked whether it is still in the drawer, and if this building were pulled down tomorrow there would be no copy of the only written account of forty-one years of a fen and the last thing anybody would know about it is that a man used to go out in the summer.**"

"Who has the key to the room it is in?"

"**A man of about forty-one who has been there eleven years and who is not a registrar and does not think of himself as one, and he is a decent man, and he has never asked, and in about four years he will retire and somebody will clear the drawer out because the drawer is in a room and the room is being made into something else.**"

"Then make a copy."

"**No.**" He said it fast and he was not angry. "**Because the moment there are two of it, somebody has to say which one is the record, and I am fifty-eight and I will not be here for the argument, and a copy of a pad is a copy of a man's handwriting and there is no form in this city that can tell anybody in about four years which pad was written by the man who was there.**"

---

**And the four lines are in the pad in the drawer and they were in the drawer in 1969 and they are the same four lines, and the pad is going to Crown Heights on Sunday night in the back of a van, and Milo Rook did not ask to see it and was not offered it and did not ask to be told what is in the third line.**

*He is not an office. He is not a district. He is not a representative and he would have said no to all three in about four seconds each, in the same voice, in the same order, and the order is the order of the years.*

---

**The Field Book, Thursday the eighteenth of June, 23.05, a front room, one day off the seventeenth, and there is a card in the inside pocket of the coat with six words on it that came off a machine this morning, and I have read the six words about nine times and I have not written them in here, because the day I write them in here they are in a book in a coat and a book in a coat is a thing.**

*Eighteenth, 23.05. One day off the seventeenth.*

*One. **Four lines, forty-one years, and the fourth one has a name in it that is not his.** The pad is in a drawer in Crown Heights from this year back to about 1969, one of it, not copied, no shelf number, no schedule, and the man who has the key to the room has been there eleven years and has never asked and does not think of himself as a registrar, and in about four years he is going to retire and somebody is going to clear the drawer out because a drawer is in a room. **And he will not have it copied, and he was right, and the reason he gave is that a copy of a pad is a copy of a man's handwriting and there is nothing in this city that can tell anybody which pad was written by the man who was there, and I have got about nine of those reasons in this book already and I have never once had it in that shape.** I have written that sentence on the blackboard in the drainage section myself, in February, in about eleven words, and a man of fifty-eight said the same sentence to me on a raised path at eleven o'clock this morning and neither of us knows the other said it and I am not going to tell him.

*Two. **The refusal, and it is the first one he has made and it took four seconds and it was against me and it was correct.** *If you put me in a column I have to have a deputy.* And then the whole of it, and I have not got an answer to any part of it: a thing that happens once a year cannot be handed to a man who is sixty-eight, and the second one will not have been out here in a February, and he will not know which mark on that post is which year, and he will write a figure and put a name in the fourth line, and in about eleven years there will be two names in a pad and no way of telling which line belongs to which man. **That is seven failures of a form in this district in about five months and the seventh of them is the first one where putting a name on a thing was the wrong answer, and the wrong answer was correct, and I did not think of it, and a man who goes out once a year worked it out standing up on a path with a rod over his shoulder in about four seconds.** I did not write him on the page with the four names on it. I am not going to. I have written it here instead, in my own hand, tonight, and a book in a coat is a thing, and I know that, and I have written it anyway, and the ninth resolution is going to have to be about this and I do not know yet what it is.

*Three. **What he gave, and it was nine words, and it was not a favour.** *I will take you out there. Tuesdays. Same as me.* And then he corrected himself before I had got the pen down: it is not Tuesdays, it is the first dry week, it is once a year, and he is telling me the day. **So I am going to Mireward on the day a man of fifty-eight goes, once a year, and I did not ask him to and I did not send him anywhere and he offered it in a car park with the engine off and he told me not to call it a favour, and I am not going to call it anything.** The road is four hours and there is no bus and there has not been a bus since about 1961 because a bus wants a place to stop and a place to stop is a rateable thing. **That is a district with no address, and the eleven streets have no address either, and the eleven streets have a bus that comes to the end of the street, and I have not once in seven months put those two facts next to each other, and they have been next to each other the whole time.**

*Four. **And the things that have not moved.** The page home the two hundred and seventh day, blank, four marks in four hands, and I am not writing that it is there. The token cold, the hundred and ninety-third day, in the inside pocket of this coat with a card in it. Ten remembered routes, ten. Nine lines in the tally, Hettie Brann's paper, Dr Sen's pocket, not looked at since the thirteenth of January. The condition column is empty for the twenty-second walk since the first of April and it was empty on the eighteenth of February and the reason column is empty for the thirteenth week and nobody has asked about the reason column, and **a thing that has never been asked about is not protected, it is just not yet asked about, and the only reason it has not been asked about is that a man in another building has been asking about a box on a different form.** The plate is seventy-three days in the grass. The round goes tomorrow at ten past eight and comes in at about four and it is one man short. The pairs are twenty-seven and there is an entry on every night of this month and every night of last month and the run of nights is fifty-four.

*Five. **And the standing question, which has a man in it now and did not have one in January.** Why a region the Registry enters as stable is on a forecast of weather-driven seam events at all, on the ground that there is no maintained seam, no recorded loss, no resident witness and no schedule. **There is no resident witness, and there has not been one since 1969, and a man has been going out once a year for forty-one years and writing four lines into a drawer, and the sentence he said to me this morning is the answer to the first third of it and it is not mine and it is not the drainage section's and I am not going to improve it in a yard or in a book or anywhere.** The other two parts have not moved. There is no second outlook and there is not going to be one in July and I am not going to ask for one. And there is an instrument a person cannot produce and I have got three of them and they are not one, and half of an answer is not an answer, and there is a fourth part under the third that I have never been down to and I have now got a fourth thing in the same shape, which is a pad in a drawer that nobody has ever rung up about, **and I am not going to write the pad down as a fourth part of the standing question, because that would be a pattern, and a pattern is me deciding that two things are the same, and I have refused to do that four times in a hundred and thirty chapters and I am not starting in June.**

*And the standing position, which is the last thing in this book and does not improve: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.**
