# Chapter 141: Four Grounds And None Of Them Is The Work

It came into the quay section office in a bundle with the week's papers on Friday the third of July, at about half past eleven, and it was four sides, and nobody had to ask anybody for it, and that is the whole of what happened on the Friday and it is worth two thousand words.

**CONTINUANCE OF SURFACE AND DRAINAGE WORKS — DISTRICT SCHEDULE, SEASON 3.** Printed both sides. Issued under a section number and not a name. At the top, in the same size as the title, the season's reference, so that anybody reading it in a hurry would know at the top that it was about a season of weather-driven seam events and could go on thinking that.

Under the title, in smaller type, and it is the sentence the whole of the form turns on:

> *To be completed by the person responsible for maintenance in the district and returned within seven days of issue.*

---

There were six columns across the width of the sheet, and Milo Rook read them in about forty seconds standing up, the way he has read four hundred things in that room.

1. **District.**
2. **Stability of district.**
3. **Works of record.**
4. **Units benefited.**
5. **Ground of discontinuance.**
6. *Signature of the person responsible.*

**Column five is the reason this is not a weather report.** The reason column on a returns card is about a district and a week. This one is a column about whether a district's work is to go on. There is a key printed on the second side under a rule, and the key has four grounds in it and no more, and every word of the key is a word this city can stand behind:

> **(a) no maintained seam of record**
> **(b) no resident witness**
> **(c) nil returns**
> **(d) works not reimbursable at the present rating**

And at the foot of the last side, in the same small type as the rest, the sentence that ends the argument before anybody starts it:

> *A district that has not returned within the period stated will be completed by this office.*

---

**And at the foot of the last side, under a short rule, there is a printed block, and the block is a box for a signature, and there is nothing on the rule.**

> **OFFICE OF THE FIRST SURVEYOR**

It is not an issuing line. The issuing line is the works division, and the works division has a name on it that Milo Rook has read ten thousand times. This is underneath it, at the end, after the terms, and it is a desk, and there is nobody standing behind it.

He looked at it for longer than the other five columns together. **A signature block is not a man and it is not a department, it is the place on a form where a man would put his name if the form were the kind that needed one, and this form does not need one, and there is no line anywhere for the office to ask for anything, or to receive anything, or to answer anything, and there is no person in this city who can be produced behind it.** He has looked for a person behind it for about four minutes and he has not asked anybody, and asking is the one move that would turn a desk into a man, and he is not going to make it, and he has written down that he is not going to make it and that writing it down is the ninth thing of that shape he has done in eight months.

---

**There are about nine hundred rows on that schedule and he read the names instead of counting the rows, because a number on that form is a thing somebody can be asked for and a name is only a thing somebody can be sent to look at.**

The names are the districts of this city in the order of a list that is a hundred and forty years old and is printed from a list of offices, and the fen is on it about two thirds of the way down, where it has always been, and every district on that sheet is a word.

Three rows are not words.

**MIREWARD. — Stability of district: nil. — and columns three, four, five and six empty.** The nil is hand-transferred, in ink, in the same pen the man in the room with the machine has used on an allocation sheet every Wednesday since the twenty-sixth of January, and **the column is not typed, and the column has never been typed, and there is a row for it and the row is as wide as the others.** The three nils about Mireward are three. This is the same zero off the same sheet and **it is not a fourth nil, and a zero that is on a card and a zero that is on a schedule and a zero that is on a sheet is one zero in three places and not three zeros, and I have written that down in three chapters now and improved it none of them.**

**The eleven streets. — and every other column empty.**

Not a dash. Not a nil. **Empty.** Five empty boxes on a row with a name in the first one, and the name printed, because the name is on a list that is a hundred and forty years old and the name is a good name and the eleven streets have been in it since before anybody in this building was born.

---

"And that is the row that has been empty since the day this form was designed," said the man of about forty-one on no list at all, from the doorway, about nine seconds later. He had a handcart and he had not been sent. "**And it is empty because the eleven streets has never sent one of these, because the eleven streets sends a works sheet and not this, and I have been in this office four times a day since the twenty-eighth of December and I have never once seen one of these go out and I never once saw one come in.**"

"That is not the same as the row being wrong."

"**It is the same as the row being empty, and the row is empty, and empty is the only thing this form can read.**"

---

Milo Rook carried it to the rack, in his own bag, and did not put it in the rack, because the rack is a rack of works sheets and this is not a works sheet.

**On Friday the third of July the second tin on the bottom shelf of the plan room of the Civic Atlas Office went to twenty-nine.** It is under a repair schedule and it is a different building from this one and it holds twenty-eight weeks of carbon and one page that is not a gang sheet, and it went in on the Friday the way it goes in on every Friday, and nobody telephoned about it, and the man of thirty-one did not ask the man of about fifty whether the number was right because the number is not a thing anybody has ever asked about.

**The condition column has been empty for a hundred and forty-one days.** That is the twelfth of February to the third of July, and a works clerk of forty-six worked out in about four seconds on the eighth of May that a column that wants to know why is the amendment, and said it once, and it stands and is not improved. **The reason column is empty and has been since about the fourth of April.** There is no column on that sheet, or on any paper in this city, that wants to know why a column is empty.

And the plate in the grass at the low end of Bridge Street is **eighty-eight days** old and has never been reported and is never going to be, and that is a decision and not a refusal and it was made on the first of June and told to nobody.

---

**And here is the thing the form is, in one sentence, and he wrote it out that night and did not act on it and did not know what it was about.**

**It is not a weather report. It is a land instrument wearing a weather coat, and the whole of what the weather does on it is to be the occasion.**

The occasion is a season of weather-driven seam events. Every district on that sheet is being asked what state it is in, so that the works division can decide in August which work goes on into the autumn. That is ordinary. That is what a works division is for in any city on earth, and eleven streets has a gang on a back length twice a week and a foreman with a clipboard and that is the arrangement, and the arrangement has been the arrangement since before the war.

**What is not ordinary is column five.** A column headed *the ground on which a district's work may be discontinued* is a column about whether a place deserves to be kept. And it is printed on the same sheet as a column headed *stability of district*, and the two of them are about the same place, and the second one is the reason the first one is legal.

**A district entered as stable is a district that cannot be frightened. And a district that cannot be frightened is a district where there is nothing to insure against. And a district where there is nothing to insure against is a district whose works are not reimbursable at the present rating, and (d) is one of the four grounds on the key, and the key is printed on the second side, and the key is correct.**

There are about nine hundred districts on that sheet and eight hundred and some of them have a zero or a word in column two, and the word is a district's account of itself, and a district's account of itself is what a ratepayer is.

**And the eleven streets has an empty box in column two, and an empty box in column five, and there is no box on that form for the reason.**

---

Field Book, Friday the third of July, 23.10, a front room, one day off the second.

*Third, 23.10. One day off the second.*

*One. **The instrument, in the words of the chapter that owns it, because a form is a thing somebody can be sent to look at and it is going to be sent to look at in about four years and it is going to be produced by somebody who was not in the room.** Title: CONTINUANCE OF SURFACE AND DRAINAGE WORKS — DISTRICT SCHEDULE, SEASON 3. Issued under a section number and not a name. Six columns: district; stability of district; works of record; units benefited; ground of discontinuance; signature of the person responsible. The season's reference is printed at the top in the size of the title. A key of four grounds: (a) no maintained seam of record, (b) no resident witness, (c) nil returns, (d) works not reimbursable at the present rating. At the foot: a district that has not returned within the period stated will be completed by this office. Under a rule, printed, empty, and not a signature: **OFFICE OF THE FIRST SURVEYOR.***

*Two. **It is not a weather report.** It is a land instrument wearing a weather coat and the whole of what the weather does on it is to be the occasion, and I have written that down at about eleven at night on the Friday it arrived and I do not know what it is about and I have not acted on it. Column two and column five are about the same place and the second one is the reason the first one is legal, and a district that cannot be frightened is a district where there is nothing to insure against, and nothing to insure against is ground (d) and ground (d) is on the key and the key is correct, and I have got nothing against the key and I want to be careful about that, because a man who wants to be careful about a key is a man who has already decided what is on the other side of it.*

*Three. **The row.** Eleven streets: five empty boxes and a printed name. Mireward: a hand-transferred nil in column two and four empty boxes, and the column is not typed and has never been typed and the row is as wide as the others, and this is the same zero off the same sheet and not a fourth nil. **And the eleven streets has never sent one of these because the eleven streets sends a works sheet and not this, and that is not the same as the row being wrong, and the row is empty, and empty is the only thing this form can read.**

*Four. **The last day for the district's answer is Friday the tenth of July**, seven days from issue, and the office that completes the row is the office that drafted the form, and the office of the First Surveyor is not that office and does not complete anything and does not ask for anything and cannot be asked for anything, and I have looked at the block for about four minutes and there is nobody behind it and I am not going to ask, and asking is the one move that would turn a desk into a man.*

*Five. **What has not moved.** The page home the two hundred and twenty-second day, blank, four marks in four hands, and I have not written that it is there. The token cold, the two hundred and eighth day, in the inside pocket of this coat. Eleven remembered routes, ten crossings, and the ninth's failure to cost one is still unexplained, and I wrote on the twenty-fifth that I was not going to explain it before the end of July and I am holding to that and a promise about the end of July is the only date in this book I have written down twice. The tally is ten lines, Hettie Brann's paper, Dr Sen's pocket, and I have not looked at it since the thirteenth of January. The condition column is empty for a hundred and forty-one days and the reason column is empty and both of them are correct. The second tin is at twenty-nine. The plate is eighty-eight days in the grass. **A foreman is eleven weeks past four months on Saturday the eleventh and he is on a gate on a Saturday and nobody is going to ask him, and that silence is on the facing page of this book in my own handwriting and I cannot get it out of my handwriting.** The walk is on Monday the sixth and it is the twenty-seventh since the first of April, and I have not written twenty-seven anywhere except here.

*And the standing position: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.**
