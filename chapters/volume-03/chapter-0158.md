# Chapter 158: The Last Thursday Of A Month, And A Cell With Nothing To Go In It

**Mara Quill reconciles the return on the last Thursday of every month and has done for about two years, and the last Thursday of this month is the twenty-seventh of August, and the return runs to the end of the month, and there is a week in it that has not happened yet.**

**A return is a grid. It has a row for every district that reports to it and a cell for every week of the month, and a cell is completed with a figure where the district returned a figure, and a reconciled return is one in which every cell carries one or the other, and there is no third thing and no blank, because a blank in a cell is not an answer, it is a query, and a query goes down.** She has never once had to raise a query on this return in about two years and she is not going to start on a Thursday in the last week of a month.

She was thirty-one, and she had a counter to open at ten and two people at it before she had the cover off, and she did their business first, standing up, in about four minutes, because a man of about sixty wanted a certificate on a permit from 1974 and it was not lost, and a woman wanted to know whether a thing she had brought in could be copied and it could, for a fee, and there is a box for that on the form and she filled the box.

Then she shut the counter and went in.

---

**The eleventh street district is on the fourth page from the bottom, and it has been on the fourth page from the bottom for as long as she has been doing this, because that is where it was entered and entries are not moved, and a moved entry is a thing somebody has to authorise.**

Its row is a district and a set of cells and nothing else. No address, because there is no correspondence address. No person, because there is no person. **The row has a cell for every week of the month, and the last of them is the week that begins on Monday the thirty-first of August, and it has not happened yet and is four days away, and every cell before that one is a figure, and she reconciles about nine hundred rows of this return twice a month and has never had to think about a cell in any of them.**

**And on Tuesday of last week a printed side came back from an office about a mile away that says, in the sixth column of the fourth side of a district schedule, that the works in that district are not to be maintained after the thirty-first of August.** She did not see the schedule. She saw the schedule's effect, the way everybody in this building sees the effect of things: a row in a hand-transferred column of about nine hundred rows that has read the same since before she was thirty, and a question that came in through the counter on Wednesday from a man who wanted to know what a discontinuance meant, and she told him, and it took her ninety seconds.

**It has taken her nine days to get to the fifth cell and she has not told anybody that and she is not going to.**

---

## The thing about a cell with nothing to go in it

The rule is printed at the foot of the second side and she could recite it and does not recite it, and it says that where a district has not returned within the period stated the cell is completed with a dash.

**She put a dash in it at about eleven in the morning, and she wrote it with a pen and not a pencil, and the pen was the wrong instrument and she knew it was the wrong instrument when she picked it up, and she has used a pen on this return once in about two years and the once was today.**

That is the whole of what she did on the last Thursday of August.

Then she sat looking at it for about four minutes.

**A dash in a cell is a departmental statement that nothing was received. It is not a statement about the district. It is a statement about the post.** And the post that comes to the eleventh street district comes on a Friday morning in a satchel that goes out with the works' papers and comes back on the Friday afternoon, because a district with no correspondence address is on no other post, and the works on the back length are not to be maintained after the thirty-first of August, and therefore there is no works' paper, and therefore there is no satchel out, and therefore from Monday the thirty-first there is a district that nobody in this city has written to, and there are two hundred and fifty-two people in a building with no number in it, one of whom is a boy of seventeen and one of whom is a woman of sixty on a second landing who is the ninth of twenty-six names, and not one of the two hundred and fifty-two is on the post.

**And the fifth cell will say, in the hand of a woman of thirty-one, in a return that goes to a board on the first Thursday of September, that nothing was received from that district for the week beginning the thirty-first of August. And nothing was not received. Nothing was not sent. And the form has one mark and the mark does not have a way of saying which of the two it is, and she is the one who has to put the mark in, and she has known the difference for nine days and she has told nobody.**

---

**And then she did the other thing, and it took about two minutes, and it was not a decision so much as a hand going to a book on a shelf and then not taking anything out of it.**

The register of correspondents is on the shelf behind her and it is about four inches thick and it is the oldest book in this room that anybody uses weekly. It is a list of addresses. **It is a list of addresses, and it was made to tell a department where a letter goes, and that is the only thing it has ever been for, and it is the wrong shape for a district, because a district is not somewhere a letter goes. A district is somewhere letters come from.** A district on that register does not receive demands. A district on that register is a place a department knows how to write to, and a place a department knows how to write to is a place that generates requests, and a request needs a reply, and a reply needs a name.

**She has opened that book in this room about four times in two years and she has never once had a reason to. She opened it at about ten to twelve and she found the page, and the page is a page of ruled columns and a heading, and the heading is a heading for an address, and she looked at it for about ninety seconds with a pen in her hand, and then she shut the book and put the pen down and did not enter anything.**

The reason, and she wrote it out afterwards on the back of a sheet and then tore the sheet up, which is a thing she has done about four times in eleven years:

**A district with a correspondence address is a district that can be written to. A district that can be written to is a district whose returns are held somewhere. A district whose returns are held somewhere is a district that somebody can ask for a card from. And the only card in this city with a hand on it for the fen country is a card with a paragraph in the two inches of ruled space at the bottom and a line struck through the paragraph and a signature under the line, and that card has been on a re-run every Friday at about four since the nineteenth of July, and it cannot be taken off, because a card with a thing struck through it is a card with a mark on it, and the mark is on a printed side in a run of about forty thousand.**

**And the name on it is his, and he wrote it in four seconds face down so that a machine would not take the hand over, and he told her so, in a corridor, on the Monday, and neither of them thanked anybody and neither of them has mentioned it since, and it was written on Sunday the nineteenth of July and it is thirty-nine days old and it is the only name on any paper in that district and he is the only person in this city who knows that.**

---

**And there is no version of the register that is safe, and she went through them, standing up, in about nine minutes, and this is the whole of it and she is not going to go through it again.**

If the district is entered and nothing is ever asked of it, the entry is an address with nothing behind it and in about four years somebody audits the register, because registers get audited, and the audit finds a district with an address and no replies, and the audit asks the district for a reply.

If the district is entered and the fen country is not the same district, then the entry is for a district that has no such thing, and there is no form for that either.

**And if nobody enters anything, then the eleventh street district stays on a printed side of a district schedule as a row that is not going to be maintained, and that is one printed side in the whole of this city, and it is the only one, and it is the only reason the district exists as a thing anybody in an office can see, and it says the works are stopping.**

She shut the book.

**A dash in a cell is a thing that exists and can be asked about. A name in a register is a thing that exists and can be written to. She has spent two years finding the places where a thing that exists can be asked about, and she has one of them, and she used it, on a Thursday, in about four seconds, with a pen, and the whole of the price was that it is true and that it is the wrong reason.**

---

**And she did not describe the line, and nobody asked her to, and that is the whole of the arrangement and it is two years old.**

There is a line in this return that she reconciles on the last Thursday of every month. She has looked at it. She told him once that she had looked at it and she is not going to tell him what it says, and he asked once in June and did not ask again, and on Saturday the eighteenth of July she gave him four facts and no sentence and he did not ask a third time, and she noticed that he did not and he noticed her noticing, and it has come up in a conversation with nobody in about two years including this one.

**A cell and a line are two different things in a return. A cell is a week and a figure. A line is whatever it is and she has not said and she is not going to say, and the two are not joined, and nobody in this building joins them, and the fact that they are on the same piece of paper in the same hand is the only thing they have in common and paper is not a reason.**

---

**And nothing about the season came up, and nothing about the drawer came up, and both of those are the same silence and it is the ninth time and she has stopped noticing which of the two she is doing.**

He has not told her since the middle of April. She has not asked since July. **He has stopped counting and started dating, which she found out about by noticing that the number had stopped going up, and a number that stops going up is worse than a number that is running, and she has not told him that she noticed, and that is a thing she has noticed and not said, and there is no column for it.**

And there is a page in a drawer in a room she is not in, with a coat in the drawer with it, and it is hers, and it has been hers a long time, and she has not produced it and has not named it and has not said one word about it to anybody since May, and it is not going to come up on the last Thursday of a month any more than it comes up on any other day, and that is not a decision she made this month.

---

**And at about ten past four on the Thursday he came through the corridor with the round's bag over his shoulder, because the round goes out at about ten past eight and comes in at about four, and it is a Monday and a Thursday round and it is one man short, and it comes in with nowhere to put the bag, and that is a round with no depot and has been one since the second of January.**

He was not in the room. He has not been in that room on a last Thursday of a month in about two years and she is not going to have him in it, and the reason is not the line.

---

"**I have put a dash in the last cell.**"

"You have put a dash in the last cell."

"**And I have known since Wednesday that you would want a sentence in it, and I have not said no to you today, because you have not been in that room, and I am not going to say it out here either.**

She had the sheet under her hand and she did not turn it round.

"**You did not have to have it out here.**"

"Then have it in here on Thursday."

"**No. And here is the reason, and it is one sentence and then I am going to a bus.**" She said it at the same speed she read forms out loud. "**If that district ever gets an address then this office holds its returns, and the day this office holds a district's returns is the day somebody can ask this office for a card, and there is one card in this city with a hand on it for that fen country and it has a line through it and a man's name under the line and the re-run is on a Friday at about four.**"

He did not say anything for about six seconds.

"That is my name."

"**I know whose name it is. I have not asked you and I did not have to and I am not going to make you tell me what you wrote on the nineteenth of July, and you told me on the Monday that you wrote it face down so that a machine would not take the hand over, and that was a sentence about a machine and it was not a sentence about your name, and I have not asked which you were telling me about.**"

"Say the other thing."

"**The other thing is that you cannot take it off. You have told me that. A card with a thing struck through it is a card with a mark on it and it comes round every Friday and it is a printed side in a run of forty thousand and your name is the only one on it.**" She took her hand off the sheet. "**And the whole reason it is the only one is that you wrote it, and if the district gets an address then a room can ask for it, and the room will produce it, and the sentence under your name in the two inches says the district has no correspondence address and no person responsible for maintenance and that the works of record in that district are a number cast into a collar and about nine feet of drain that a man of fifty-eight writes four lines about once a year into a pad in a drawer in Crown Heights.**"

"I know what is written on it."

"**Then you know that in about four years the answer to a question about that district is a surveyor's name and his paragraph, and the paragraph is the only sentence anybody has ever put on paper about that place, and it is in his hand, and you put it there, and I am not going to be the one who gives the room the address that lets them ask for it.**"

---

"**And I could have written one sentence in that cell instead of a dash,**" she said, "**and you have not asked me why I did not, and I am going to tell you, and it is the last thing I am going to say about it in August.**"

"Tell me."

"**Because a sentence in that cell becomes a thing this room can be asked for, and about four years from now a man is going to ask this room whether anything was received from the eleventh street district in the week beginning the thirty-first of August, and this room is going to say yes, and hand him the sentence, and the sentence is going to be true, and it is going to be mine.**" She put the sheet down on the desk. "**And a true sentence in my hand is the second time this year that a true sentence in somebody's hand is the whole mechanism, and the first one did not need a hand at all. It was a Thursday and a man with a pen and about a minute and a half.**"

He did not ask her which Thursday. She had not said which Thursday and was not going to, and the not-saying was the correct procedure and it was also the reason she could say the rest of it.

"**So it is a dash,**" she said, "**and the dash is true, and it is the wrong reason, and I have put a pen to it, and I am not going to take it out, and I am not going to tell this room why.**"

---

**And that is the disagreement, and it is the same one, and it is the same sentence, and it has now been said three times and there is still no better one and neither of them is going to.**

She said: "**It is the only place in this city where an objection is a thing that exists.**"

He said: "**It is the only place in this city where an objection is a thing a man with a box can ask for in about four years.**"

"**Yes. And I have proved both of those in one morning, at the same desk, with the same pen, and you were not in the room, and I am not going to pretend that either of us is going to win it, and I am not going to be warm with you about it in a corridor.**"

"Nor am I."

"**Good. Go and do your round, you are one man short and you have been one man short since December and the bag is not the district.**"

---

**And she stood in the corridor after he had gone and did not go back in, and she thought about the register, once, the way a person touches a doorframe on the way past.**

She has put a pen to a cell and shut a book and told a man the true reason for the first and the true reason for the second, and neither of them is a resolution and neither of them is going to be one, and on Friday morning the works' papers will go out in a satchel for the last time and on Monday the thirty-first of August eleven men will walk the back length at half past seven and there will be no sheet for it, and none of those three things has her name on it, and the fifth cell has.

**And the fifth cell is the only thing she has written this month and it is a dash and it is wrong, and she has left it, and she is not going to be thanked, and she has not been, and she is not going to be.**
