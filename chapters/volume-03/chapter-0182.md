# Chapter 182: Nineteen Is A Roll In A Head, And A Question Asked Badly, And An Answer Given Anyway

**Tuesday the third of November, and he is thirty and he is in a village eleven miles the other way with a district number and four hundred and ten yards of frontage on an amendment completed on Thursday the twenty-second of October by an office in about a minute and a half, and he did not get on this bus at eight this morning to tell anybody that, and he is not going to, and he came anyway.**

The page home is the three hundred and forty-fifth day. The token is cold on the three hundred and thirty-first. The plate in the grass at the low end of Bridge Street is two hundred and eleven days old and has been told to nobody. The condition column is two hundred and sixty-four days and is not there. **A foreman of fifty-four is twenty-eight weeks exactly past four months and nobody is going to ask him.**

---

## The road, and a hundred and forty people, and an ordinary Tuesday

The bus put him down where the road bends and he walked the last half mile, and **the village is on a raised road about two feet above the ground it stands on and the ground it stands on is not dry, and it has not been dry since about the middle of September, and the road is the only part of it that is.**

About a hundred and forty people live here and there is no post and there is no number on anything and there has not been since a list of streets was compiled in about 1911 by people who are all dead. The houses are along the road and about forty of them are behind it. There is a hall with a corrugated roof, a shop that shuts at four, a yard with a tractor in it and one other motor vehicle on the whole of this ground and it belongs to a farm and it is a lorry, and **two motor vehicles a week is a district of a hundred and forty people, and every one of those two vehicles is somebody else's job, and that is true here of the whole population and not of a round.**

He did not count anybody. **He walked up the road with about thirty people going the other way and he did not count them, and the not counting was on purpose, and it was the first thing he did in this village that he had planned, and he has written down that the reason is that a number of people in a place on a day is the most producible thing he has ever come across and he was not going to be the man who produced it.**

The schoolroom is a room at the end of the road with a bell on a bracket over the door that has been there since about 1911, and the bell is cast and it has a rope and the rope is new. **He looked at it for about four seconds and did not write it down and did not ask about it and did not count it. He has four bells he has never written down either, and the fifth thing is somebody writing down the four, and that is not going to be him, and a bell is a bell.**

---

## Sabra Trewin is thirty-four and has been here nine years and does not need anybody

She was in the room with the door open and about half the children in it and a jug and a stack of paper on the edge of a bench, and she did not stop and she did not look up, and she finished what she was doing, and then she said:

"**You are going to stand about in here for four hours. You can stand at the back. Do not stand in front of the window, because that is where the light comes from and it is the only thing in this room that anybody paid for.**"

He stood at the back. There are benches and a table and a stove and about four feet of floor between the front bench and the wall and a piece of linoleum that has been lifted and put back so many times it has come up in ridges. **The window has no bars and there is no door on it and the door is on the other side and there are two hooks and one is on and one is not.**

She taught for about fifty minutes and then she did eleven minutes of something with a chart on the wall that had four columns on it and a fifth one she drew herself in the morning with a ruler, and the ruler was a piece of lath with a line burnt on it.

**She has been here nine years. She teaches nineteen of them on a Tuesday. The roll is a roll in a woman's head and it is a roll in a woman's head on purpose, and it has never been on a sheet, and it is not going to be on a sheet, and the piece of lath with a line on it is for a straight edge and not for a number.**

He did not ask her how many. He stood at the back of that room for about four hours and he did not ask her how many, and **that is the first decision he made in this village and it took him about four seconds and he did not improve it afterwards.**

---

## And then, at about half past twelve, he asked her a question, and he knew while he was asking it

They went out onto the step because the room wanted air and the children wanted the yard, and he stood with his back to the wall of the schoolroom and she stood in the doorway and she had her hands in the sleeves of a cardigan with the cuffs turned up.

**Here is the question, and it is in his own hand in his own book that night, in the order it was said in, which is the way he builds every one of them:**

"Somebody has put your village on a list. I have not come to tell you what is on it, because I did not come to tell you what is on it, and I have not come to ask you to do anything about it either. **What would you like me to do.**"

**It is a bad question and it is bad in four ways and he knew all four of them before he had finished the sentence, and he has written them down, and he is not going to improve any of them.**

One. **It assumes he is a man who can do something.** He had been in the village for two and a half hours and he had not established that there was a thing in this city he could do about a printed side, and there may not be.

Two. **It is two questions wearing one coat.** *What would you like me to do* is a question about what she wants, and *what is on it* is a question about a piece of paper, and he had put them together so that he would only have to ask one thing, and that is a thing men do.

Three. **It gives her the whole of the decision in one move and takes the whole of the time out of it.** She had nine years of not being asked. He had about four hours and a bus.

Four. And this is the one he will not improve: **he had the answer already.** He had known since the seventh of September that she would say no, and he had known since the twenty-ninth of October that he was not going to ask her, and he had got on a bus at five past eight this morning on purpose, and the question was not a way of finding out. It was a way of being the man who asked.

**A man who has an answer and asks a question anyway is doing the thing the office did to eleven streets with a contract, and he wrote that down on the twenty-ninth of October and he did it again at half past twelve this afternoon, on a step, in a village of a hundred and forty people, with a cardigan with the cuffs turned up.**

She did not answer for about nine seconds.

"You have not asked me a question," she said. "**You have handed me a job.**"

"Yes," he said.

"**Then I am going to tell you what I would like you to do, and you are going to dislike it, and I am going to do it anyway, and I want you to stand there while I say it, because it is going to take about four minutes and I have said it in my own head four times this year and not once out loud.**"

---

## What she said, and it took about four minutes, and it is the whole of what happened in this village

"**Somebody has put us on a list. I have not seen the paper. Nobody has shown me the paper. I am not going to be shown the paper, and I want that said in whatever way you say things, because in about four years there is going to be a man in a room who asks me where the paper came from, and I am not going to be able to tell him, and the reason I am not going to be able to tell him is that I was not told, and I was not told because there is nowhere to tell me. That is not a thing anybody has done to us. That is how it has always been here, and there has not been an order and there has not been a sheet and there has not been a meeting.**"

"**I am not frightened.** I want to say that plainly, because I know what a person in a good coat is expecting and it is not that, and if you go away with the other thing you will be the fourth person this year who came to see whether I was all right, and there have been three and I have said no to all three and one of them was not going to be told either.**"

"**Here is what I have got, and it took me nine years to get and it is the only thing in this village that is worth anything.** There is no roll of paper in this room. There is no register. There is no number on this building and there is not going to be one. If a school has a roll it is a list, and a list is a thing a man can ask for, and the day somebody asks me for that piece of paper is the day this room is a place that can be asked about — not a room with a woman in it, a *place* — and about four years after that there will be a return, and after the return there will be a file, and I have watched that happen to a village about sixty miles from here and I was not sorry for them. **A roll of paper in a woman's head is the only thing about this room that cannot be produced against anybody, and I have kept it in my head for nine years on purpose and I am not going to hand it to a man in a coat on a Tuesday.**"

"**And I have not told anybody in this village, and I am not going to, and that is mine and I am not going to put it on you and I am not going to put it on a bus.**"

"**Now. Ask me the question you came with.**"

---

## And he could not, and she knew he could not, and neither of them pretended otherwise

**He had not come with a question. That is the true thing about the whole morning and it took him until about two o'clock to be able to write it down.**

She waited for about eleven seconds and then she said: "**Right. Then you have come to be in a room with me for four hours, and that is not nothing, and it is not what you said it was, and I am not going to help you pretend it is, because you came on a bus and you did not have to.**"

And then she said the sentence he wrote down word for word, and he has not been able to put it down twice, and it is the reason he came back on a bus:

"**You have not asked me a single question. I want that written down by somebody, and I do not care who, because in about four years you are going to have said that you asked, and I am going to have said that you did not, and one of us is going to be believed, and I would rather it was written down in a hand that is not mine.**"

**He did not write it down.**

He stood on that step for about a minute and a half and he did not write it down, and the reason is on the page and it is not a rule and it is not a resolution: *a thing she said to one person is a thing two people know, and I am the second, and if it is in a book in a coat then three people know, and one of the three is a woman of thirty-four who is alone with about twenty children and a stove and four feet of floor and a lath with a line burnt on it, and the other is me. **I am not going to be the second copy of the only piece of evidence she has got.***

---

## The rest of the day, and the evening bus, and no thanks anywhere in it

The afternoon was reading and the stove and a boy of about nine who was sent to fetch a woman from the second house and came back with her and told the room, in a voice that carried the way children's voices do, that the man from the city was here and had a bag, and about eleven people looked up, and the woman said, "**Well, you'll want the front step's dry**," and that was the whole of the notice this village has given him.

**He had given his name at the door at about a quarter to eleven, because a woman of about sixty had opened it and asked what to call him, and he said it. A name given at a door in a place with no address is the smallest thing he has done this year and it is not nothing, and it is not a party, and it is not a letter, and it is the only thing in eleven miles that he put into this village and he did not even decide to.**

At about ten past four he went and stood by the bus stop and did not write anything down, and the bus came at about twenty past four and he got on it.

**A bus goes past the end of this street every twenty minutes on a weekday and two of them a day go as far as this village, and one of them goes back at about twenty past four and he was on it, and the fare was the fare in both directions and the fare is not about carrying anything.**

He was in eleven streets at about ten past six. **Nobody thanked him. Nobody was thanked. A woman of thirty-four gave him four minutes of the truth about a village she has kept off paper for nine years and he asked her a bad question on purpose and she answered it anyway, and a person who has thanked somebody is a person who has asked, and he did not thank her and he is not going to, and she did not expect it and said so.**

**And he did not get a number. He did not hand her a piece of stationery. He did not give her a form, or a figure, or a figure she could use, or a thing with a box on it. And he did not go to the drawer, and he did not find out, and he did not ask anybody on the bus, and the not-finding-out is the price of the day and it is permanent and it is not going to be looked up in four years by anybody including him.**

**And the whole of what he takes away is this: a woman of thirty-four has decided, alone, on an ordinary Tuesday in November, not to tell a hundred and forty people that their village is on a list, and she is not going to be helped with it, and she said that out loud to a stranger on a step, and she should not have had to, and the reason she had to is a man who has a sentence ready before he arrives somewhere.**

---

## And he did not write an entry about this day, and the reason is in the next one

He did not write anything at eleven o'clock that night. **He did not write a Field Book entry about the third of November and the reason is not a rule and he is not going to make it one:** a day I spent in a schoolroom is not a day I had, and I was not going to convert it, and there are four benches and about twenty children in it and a lath with a line burnt on it and a bell on a bracket with a new rope on it, and **a roll in a woman's head is a roll in a woman's head and not a sheet, and a day in a room with a person in it is a day that belongs to the person, and I have got a field book and she has got a lath.**

He wrote one line on the back of a sheet of his own paper and put it in the coat and did not put a date on it:

*She was right about the question. I was not, and I asked it anyway.*

**And that is all he took out of eleven miles and a whole day, and it is four words, and it is a sentence about himself on a sheet that goes into a coat, and there is no form anywhere in this city for a thing a man has learned about himself at a quarter to one on a step, and he is not going to build one.**
