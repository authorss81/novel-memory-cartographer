# Chapter 168: A Yard, On The Last Morning Of A Gang, And The Sixth Of A Kind Of Thing That Is Not Mine

**Monday the twenty-eighth of September was an ordinary day. The Row was there. The man of about forty who sweeps it was on it at about eight with a brush and did not stop and has never been asked for his twenty-eight undated words and is not going to be. The fishmonger of about forty-five had her trays out by nine. There is a man of about sixty with a shed eleven minutes away who is not on the Row on a Wednesday and has not been since before the war, and it was not a Wednesday, and he does not know that a piece of paper exists and is not going to be told.**

The page home is the three hundred and ninth day. The token is cold on the two hundred and ninety-fifth day. The plate in the grass at the low end of Bridge Street is a hundred and seventy-five days old. The condition column is two hundred and twenty-eight days and is not there. **A foreman of fifty-four is twenty-two weeks and five days past four months.**

The barriers were still up. The road was open. Both of those are correct and a site is not cleared by a date, and **on the twenty-eighth of September eleven men and a foreman were on the back length at half past seven, which is how this district found out where they had been, and it is the only thing the twenty-eighth of September added to the inventory.**

**And about nine people were in the yard at about half past nine, because it is a yard, and a yard in this district is a place where people stand, and there is no form anywhere in this city that says a yard is for standing in.**

---

## The boy, first, and he said it in four words

Pell Harrow came into the yard at about ten past nine with an empty handcart and stopped for about nine seconds and then said:

"**It went back on Friday.**"

"What went back."

"**The card. It was in a yard at the north end on Thursday morning with a foreman's name on it and the foreman read it standing up and put it in a bin and posted it back in somebody else's envelope on the Friday.**" He did not unload the handcart. "**I was not sent and I am not going to be and I did not ask him anything and he did not say anything to me and he did not know anybody had sent it.**"

"How do you know he did not know."

"**Because he looked at the envelope first and then at the card and then at the yard, and that is what a man does when a thing has come to him that he did not order.**"

Then he went off up the length the way he goes, and that was the whole of it, and nobody thanked him, and **nobody in this district is going to tell Roy Pryce that a surveyor gave a man in a room with four machines a district to hold, and nobody is going to tell the man in the room with four machines either, and the two men are each going to go on not knowing about the other for as long as either of them is in this city.**

---

## The seventy-eight words, said for the second time by not saying them

Roy Pryce had the barrow loaded by about ten — a barrow, a shovel, two brooms, a coil of wire and a folding barrier off a road that is going to stay like that — and he was in the yard and he put the folding barrier against the wall and squared it with his boot, which is a thing a man of fifty-four who has been in charge of a gang for thirty-one years does.

**There were about nine people. There were four hours. Milo Rook had said the seventy-eight words to nobody on the last morning of a contract and had written them out in his own book, and they are seventy-eight words and they are not eleven words and they are not any other number, and nobody is going to improve them.**

He did not say them.

He stood in a yard for about two hours and twenty minutes and he did not say them and he did not say anything else either, and about nine people heard two hours and twenty minutes of a man in a good coat saying nothing at all, and that is not a file and there is no column for it.

**And at about half past twelve Roy Pryce said one thing, and he said it to nobody in particular, and it was about a barrow.**

"**There is a place about two hundred yards up the length where the verge has gone and it is not on a schedule and it is not in a condition column and it is not going to be in anything, and in about four years it will be a hole.**"

"Do you want it written down."

"**No.**" He picked the barrow up. "**If it is written down then it is a fault, and a fault is a thing somebody gets a form for, and the form goes to a firm, and the firm is me. And if it is not written down then in about four years it is a hole and I am a man who was told about a hole and said nothing, and I would rather be that.**"

Nobody said anything to that either. He had said it at about half past twelve in a yard and it was correct and it changed nothing.

---

## And then the thing he gave up, and it is not a habit, and nobody asked him to

**A yard is the only instrument in this district that has never once turned into a document.**

He had known that in June. He had written it in his own book at the end of August, in the entry about the last walk of the season, in about eleven words: *a yard is the only instrument in this district that has never once turned into a document, and I knew that in June and I have not used it and I am not going to be able to use it again this year.*

**And at about twenty past one on the twenty-eighth of September, in a yard, with about nine people in it and a foreman of fifty-four loading a barrow, he decided that he had been wrong about the second half of that sentence, and that the sentence was right and the year was wrong, and that a year was the wrong length of time to put on an instrument.**

A thing said in a yard by about nine people is true, and it cannot be produced, and it cannot be dated, and it cannot be attributed, and it cannot be given to a room in about four years, and it cannot be asked about by anybody including him. **A route that cannot be produced cannot be asked after, and that is the only protection any route in this city has ever had, and every conclusion this district has produced in eight months has been that something was going to be found out about a person, and this one is that a thing is safe because there is nothing to find.**

And he was the only person in this city who could put a sentence into a yard, because he was the only person in this city who had anything he wanted to say in one, and he had had it ready since the seventeenth of July, and it is about a hundred and ten words and it is true and it has no name in it.

**So he gave it up. On purpose. On an ordinary Monday. In about four seconds, standing up, with nobody watching him decide.**

---

**It cost Roy Pryce, and Roy Pryce does not know it was a decision, and it will not be told to him.**

Because Roy Pryce said the whole of his argument out loud, in a yard, on the twenty-fourth of August, and it was correct, and it is the best thing said in this district in eight months, and he said at the time that he was not going to say it again, and he was right to say that, and he has not said it again. **And the only place that sentence will ever exist is about nine people and a man of forty with a brush, and the one man in this city who could have put a second thing beside it — so that there were two — is standing in the yard saying nothing, on purpose, and nobody asked him to, and he is not going to be thanked for it by anybody, because a person who has thanked somebody is a person who has asked.**

And that is the sixth of a kind of thing, and it is not the six of the other kind.

*The plate. The seventh column. The not-telling. The rule on the facing page. The paragraph and the signature in two inches of ruled space, and those last two are one thing and not two, and **that is five, and four of the five are about a person and one of them is about a habit.** And the six of the other shape are the six things I got by writing something in this book and not doing it. **And this is a sixth of the first shape and the two numbers are now the same, and the two lists are two lists, and the sum of them is not a number, and there is no sum of them, and a reader in about four years is going to add six and six together and get something that does not exist, and I have written that down in the same hand as the four words and the signature and I am not going to improve it.** It is not about a habit. The rule on the facing page is the habit. **This one is about a man of fifty-four with a barrow and about nine people and about a sentence that was correct, and it is the only instrument in this district I have ever given anything away on, and I gave it away standing up, and it took about four seconds, and I have not written down what the four seconds felt like and I am not going to.*

---

*Field Book, Monday the twenty-eighth of September, 23.10, a front room in eleven streets, one day off the twenty-seventh.*

*One. **The card went back on Friday and a foreman of fifty-four put it in a bin and posted it back in somebody else's envelope, having read it standing up and looked at the yard afterwards, and he did not know who had sent it and nobody is going to tell him and nobody is going to tell the man in the room with four machines either, and those two men are each going to go on not knowing about the other for as long as either of them is in this city.** A boy of seventeen saw it and said four words about it and went off up the length and was not thanked and is not going to be. **So the Thursday bundle is a road that goes nowhere and I built it in nine seconds and it has already been walked back by a man I have never spoken to, and neither of them knows about the other, and that is the whole of what the last eight days produced and it is worth more than anything I have written down since the sixth of April.**

*Two. **THE YARD, AND IT IS THE SIXTH OF MY OWN AND NOT THE SIX OF THE OTHER SHAPE, AND IT IS NOT A HABIT.** A yard is the only instrument in this district that has never once turned into a document and I knew that in June and wrote in August that I was not going to be able to use it again this year, and the sentence was right and the year was wrong, and a year was the wrong length of time to put on an instrument. **I gave up a hundred and ten words about a place where about nine people were standing, and it cost a foreman of fifty-four who said the whole of his argument out loud once, in a yard, on the twenty-fourth of August, and correctly, and has said he is not going to say it again, and he was right.** A route that cannot be produced cannot be asked after, and that is the only protection any route in this city has ever had. Nobody asked me to give that up.*

*Three. **Two lists and the same number in both of them.** Five of my own, six of the other shape, and now six of my own and six of the other shape, and the two lists are two lists, and the sum of them is not a number, and a reader in about four years is going to add them together and get twelve, and there is no twelve, and the sum of them is not a number, and I have written that down in this book at about eleven at night on an ordinary Monday and I am not going to improve it.*

*Four. **Nothing has moved and nothing is cured.** Eleven remembered routes, ten crossings, the tally ten lines, Hettie Brann's paper in Dr Sen's pocket, not looked at since the thirteenth of January. **The page home the three hundred and ninth day, blank, four marks in four hands, none of them mine. The token is cold on the two hundred and ninety-fifth day and has done nothing since the seventh of December.** The list on the facing page reads twenty-nine and it has not moved in seventy-four days, and an interval of one day is not a pair, and a pair is a fact about where I was and not about what happened, and if the count of pairs in this fortnight is a small number and not a large one that is correct. The condition column is two hundred and twenty-eight days and is not there and the reason column is not there and there is no new figure to be printed for either. The second tin is at thirty-seven and there is no thirty-eight. The plate is a hundred and seventy-five days in the grass and has been told to nobody. **A foreman of fifty-four is twenty-two weeks and five days past four months, he walked a road this morning with a barrow, and nobody is going to ask him, and the date of that silence is in my own handwriting on a page I cannot get it out of.** There is a page in a drawer with a coat in it and it is the tenth week in a row that I have not asked about it and I am not allowed to, and she has not mentioned it, and it is hers.

*And the standing position: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.**
