# Chapter 164: The One Good Question He Asked Anybody In Six Weeks, And What It Cost A Boy Of Seventeen

**The round went out at about ten past eight on Monday the twenty-first of September and came in at about four with the bag empty, and it is a Monday and a Thursday round and it is one man short, and the bag has been empty since the twenty-eighth of August, and the flatbed is the only thing that comes to Mireward and it does not wait either way.**

The page home is the three hundred and second day. The token is cold on the two hundred and eighty-eighth. The plate in the grass at the low end of Bridge Street is a hundred and sixty-eight days old and has been told to nobody. The condition column is two hundred and twenty-one days and is not there. The second tin is at thirty-seven and there is no thirty-eight.

---

## The thing he worked out on Sunday, standing up, in about nine minutes

A district returns card comes off a machine in a room about a mile away on a Wednesday morning. It is a printed side, about nine inches by five, and it has four columns across the top and about two inches of ruled space at the bottom. One has been arriving in this district every Friday since the middle of January, because the post that comes to eleven streets comes on a Friday morning in a satchel that went out with the works' papers.

**There has not been a satchel since the twenty-eighth of August. Three Fridays have gone by and the card has not come.**

And at the foot of the other side, the sentence he has read about nine hundred times and has never once had to think about:

> *A district that has not returned within the period stated will be completed by this office.*

He stood up on Sunday the twentieth, in a front room, and worked out in about nine minutes that the office completed the row in August, in about a minute and a half, on a Thursday, and typed a name into the sixth column, and that the row is now done, and that **done is the only state a row can be in, and that the only thing that stops an office completing a row is a return, and that a return is a card that goes out and comes back, and that there is nothing in this district that can carry anything.**

Then he worked out the second half of it, and it took about four seconds, and he sat down.

**If nothing goes out, then nothing comes back, and the next thing that happens to that row is that the office does to it again.**

---

## The room with four machines, and the one good question

He was in there from about a quarter to ten until about twenty past eleven on the Monday morning.

The allocation sheet comes off the machine on a Wednesday at about nine. Five rows. Four columns. One line under the words *officer responsible* that has never been filled in. A man of about fifty transferred five figures out of it with a pen, the way he has done every Wednesday since the twenty-sixth of January. **The fourth column was empty and had been filled in three times in nineteen years. The zero in the row for the fen was a zero and it has been a zero every Wednesday and nobody has reduced it and nobody is going to.**

There are four machines in that room and none of them has been told to stop, because being told to stop is a document, and this is the district that cannot produce documents.

Milo Rook had the question ready and it was the only question he asked anybody in six weeks and it was a good one and he has written in his own book that it was a good one and that it cost more than anything he has said in eight months.

"Is there anything that goes out on a Friday."

The man of about fifty did not turn round.

"**No.**"

"Anything at all."

"**The Thursday bundle.**" He kept the pen down. "**It goes to the works office at about eleven and it has gone every Thursday since before you started coming in here. It is a firm. A firm is rateable. A firm has an address.**"

---

## And then he asked for it, and there was no question in the asking

He had it in about four seconds. He did not build it. He has written that down and it is the worst line in this chapter.

**"Could the card go in that bundle."**

The man of about fifty put the pen down and looked at him for about three seconds.

"**You are asking me to put a district's returns card into a bundle going to a contractor.**"

"Yes."

"**Say what you are doing.**"

"I am going to give the eleventh street district a correspondence address," said Milo Rook, "**and it is going to be a firm's, and you are going to do it because nobody has ever asked you for anything in this room and I told you in August there was nothing in it anybody could want, and I was half right, and this is the other half.**"

The man of about fifty turned back to his sheet.

"**Then that is all it is, and you have said it, and I am not going to be the man who stops a conversation that has been had.**" He wrote a figure. "**The card comes off on Wednesday. I will put it in the bundle at about eleven. It will be in the yard on Thursday.**"

---

## The three things that happened after that, and none of them in the machine room

**He walked out at about twenty past eleven and stood in a corridor for about four minutes and wrote nothing down, and the three things are these, and he had all three of them before he got to the bottom of the stairs.**

One. **The man of about fifty has now been given a use.** He has been in that room nineteen years and there has been a machine that prints a card for nine hundred districts and nothing anybody could want, and he was told that by his own face in August because of a lie Milo Rook put past him in half a second, and he has not forgotten it and is not going to. **And now a district is in his book, and a district that a machine holds is a district that a machine can be asked for, and in about four years a room can ask that room for every card it has put in that bundle since the twenty-third of September, and it will produce them, and the man who put them in is the man who pressed the button, and his name is not on any of them and he is the one who can find them.** He did not know that when he said yes. Milo Rook did not tell him. That is the price and it has been taken.

Two. **The eleventh street district has a correspondence address for the first time since about 1911, and it is a contractor's yard.** A woman of thirty-one shut a book on a shelf on the twenty-seventh of August and entered nothing in it, in about two minutes, and gave the reason out loud in about nine seconds, and the reason was that a district that can be written to is a district whose returns are held somewhere, and a district whose returns are held somewhere is a district somebody can ask a card from. **He has not asked her. He has not been in her room on a last Thursday in about two years and was not going to be and was not, and he has done the thing she refused to do, by a different road, on a Monday morning, in nine seconds, and he did not tell her, and telling her is not the repair and he knows that it is not the repair.**

Three. **A card that goes out to a firm's yard and comes back has to be carried from that yard to eleven streets and back, and it has to be carried by a person, and a person without an address cannot afford the day off, and the round is one man short.**

---

## The yard, and the asking, and the cost

He found him at about half past four in a yard four hundred yards from the north end of the back length, where about nine people were doing the ordinary Monday thing of standing about.

**Pell Harrow is seventeen. He is a courier and a Streetkeeper. He has no power of any kind. His mark holds for a working shift and it does not hold overnight. He has never been asked for anything by anybody in this district, and everybody knows that, and it is one of about fourteen people on a list in a book in a coat and not one of them has been asked to be on it.**

He had a handcart and he was not going anywhere with it. Milo Rook had it ready, in about eleven seconds, in the yard, in front of about nine people, and he has written that he built it in about eleven seconds and that a man who has a sentence ready before he arrives somewhere has already decided that somewhere is where the sentence goes.

"The card comes on Thursday."

"Then it comes on Thursday."

"It has to come back. **And the round is one man short.**"

---

And the boy did not have to be asked twice, and nobody in that yard was going to be asked twice, and it took him about four seconds.

"No."

"Pell—"

"**No.**" He did not stop with the handcart and he did not raise his voice and about nine people heard it, and nine people is not a file and there is no column for nine. "**I know what you are asking me. You want the round to have a thing on it. You have had no thing on it since the twenty-eighth of August and you have walked up there on your own twice, and now there is a card and you have not got a hand, and I am the hand.**"

"That is not—"

"**You said that to my face in August and it is a load calculation about a post. There is nothing in the round. You have written on the facing page of your own book in your own handwriting that the round is one man short and that is not curable from inside here, and you wrote it in front of nobody and you have not taken it out.**" He got the handcart through the gap in the fence. "**And I am not being the reason a piece of paper exists every Friday. I have got three things under a brick in this district and one of them is mine and it has been blank since the nineteenth of January and nobody has taken it and nobody is going to, and I am not putting a fourth thing on a list because a man in a good coat has run out of hands.**"

He went off up the length the way he goes.

---

**Nobody said anything. There were about nine people in a yard and none of them said anything, and the man of about forty who sweeps the Row went on sweeping at about ten to five, and Milo Rook stood in a yard and could not say one word, and the seventy-eight words he wrote out in his own book on the Sunday night are still seventy-eight words and he did not say them, and they are not eleven words and they are not any other number, and nobody is going to improve them.**

He had not asked the boy to be short-handed. He had asked him to carry a card, and the boy had heard the rest of it, and the boy was right about the rest of it.

*Field Book, Monday the twenty-first of September, 23.40, a front room in eleven streets, one day off the twentieth.*

*One. **I asked.** A good question, in a room with four machines, about a Friday, and the man of about fifty gave me the answer before I had finished asking and I asked for it in four seconds and I did not build it. **A machine that prints for nine hundred districts can be given one district, and a bundle that goes to a firm every Thursday can be given one printed side, and I gave that room a use after I had told a man in it in August that there was nothing in it anybody could want. The cost of that is not that anybody was deceived. The cost of that is that a man who has been in a room for nineteen years has now pressed a button on a district, and in about four years a room can ask that room for every card in that bundle, and it will produce them, and the hand is not on them and the man is.** I did not tell him and I am not going to and he has not asked and there is no column for it.*

*Two. **The eleventh street district has a correspondence address for the first time since about 1911 and it is a contractor's yard.** A woman of thirty-one worked that out in about nine seconds in a records room in July and I worked the same thing out in four seconds on a Monday morning and did it, and she shut a book and entered nothing and I asked a man with a machine. **A district that can be written to is a district whose returns are held somewhere, and a district whose returns are held somewhere is a district somebody can ask a card from.** I have not told her and she is not going to be told by me on a day she has not chosen and I know that telling her is not the repair.*

*Three. **A boy of seventeen, in a yard, in front of about nine people, and he said no in four seconds and he was right.** The cost of this failure is not the card. **The cost is that I asked him. Nobody in this district has ever asked that boy for anything and everybody knows it and it is one of about fourteen people on a list in this book, and I have put myself on the list of people who have asked him, and it took eleven seconds to do and I cannot get it off, and a no that a man in a good coat has heard in a yard in front of nine people is not the same as a no that nobody heard, and there is no form anywhere for the difference and there is not going to be one.** He has not been thanked and I am not going to thank him and thanking is asking.*

*Four. **The standing temptation is not a column any more.** It was a fifth column in June and a seventh column in July and a fifth column in August and every one of them wanted a person. **What it is now is the use. It is the thing a man in a room with four machines would be glad to be given, and I have given it to him, and the reason I gave it to him is that he has nothing and I wanted to hand something to a man who had been standing in a room for nineteen years with nothing to do, and that is not a reason, and it is the reason, and I have written it down in the same hand as the sentence about the column and it is worse than the column was.** I did not draw a column. I did not want one on Monday. I wanted a district to be on a card and a district is not a person and a card is not a person and a bundle is not a person and none of the three of them can be asked what happened in a week.*

*Five. **Nothing has moved and the day-counts have gone on without me.** The page home the three hundred and second day, blank, four marks in four hands, none of them mine. The token cold, the two hundred and eighty-eighth day. **The list on the facing page reads twenty-nine and it has not moved in sixty-seven days, and an interval of one day is not a pair, and a pair is a fact about where I was and not about what happened, and if the count of pairs in this month is a small number and not a large one that is correct.** Eleven remembered routes, ten crossings, the tally ten lines, Hettie Brann's paper in Dr Sen's pocket, not looked at since the thirteenth of January. The condition column is two hundred and twenty-one days and is not there. The plate is a hundred and sixty-eight days and has been told to nobody. **A foreman of fifty-four is twenty-one weeks and five days past four months and the contract ended on the thirty-first of August and he is on a firm's books and not on a district's and nobody is going to ask him.**

*And the standing position: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.**
