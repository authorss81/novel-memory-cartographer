# Chapter 172: A Landing Is Not A Rateable Thing, And She Writes The Day Backwards

**The flatbed came in from the city on Friday the second of October at about a quarter to seven and was in Mireward at about half past ten, and he got off where the driver was going, and the driver had four sacks on the bed and did not ask him anything, and the flatbed goes back to the city on the Friday afternoon and is in the yard there at about half past four and it does not wait either way, and there is no bus and there has not been one since about 1961.**

The page home is the three hundred and thirteenth day. The token is cold on the two hundred and ninety-ninth. The plate in the grass at the low end of Bridge Street is a hundred and seventy-nine days old. The condition column is two hundred and thirty-two days and is not there. **A foreman of fifty-four is twenty-three weeks and two days past four months and nobody is going to ask him.**

---

## Four landings, and a woman of thirty-seven who does not need anybody

She was on the third of them when he got there and she did not stop and did not look up, and then she finished it and came back up the cut and said:

"**You are going to stand there for two hours and I am going to be doing the second one, and then I am going to do the third, and then I am going to do the fourth, and if you want to be useful you can hold the painter for about ten minutes and I will tell you when, and if you cannot hold a painter then stand where you are.**"

He could hold a painter. He held it for about fourteen minutes.

**Garn Ollerton is thirty-seven and has been doing this for twenty-six years and there are four landings in this reach and she knows the water by what it did last time it was here, which is not a thing anybody can be taught and is not a thing anybody can be asked about, because the answer is a story about a Tuesday in a year nobody has written down.**

She walked him in and out of the tenth crossing. He did not ask her to. He has not said her name to her in a sentence of thanks and is not going to, and **a person who has thanked somebody is a person who has asked, and that is a conductor's sentence from April and it is not improved in October.**

The second landing took an hour and ten minutes. The third took fifty. He stood on the bank for the whole of both and understood about a fifth of it, and the fifth was the bit that mattered, which is that she does not hurry the last eight feet, and he asked her about that on the first Friday in September and she told him in one sentence and he wrote it in his own book and did not improve it.

---

## The refusal, which is off the page and is on a form, and is hers, and is ordinary

**The season's work went out on Thursday and this district is in it, and Mireward is in it, and a form came round about it on Monday, and it went to a firm, because a man with no address is not a person a form can be sent to and a boat with a number on it is.**

It is a single side and it is a different instrument from the one that went to a farm in September about a figure in feet, which came back in somebody else's envelope with a man of forty-four's answer on it. He has seen both. **He had this one in his hand by eleven o'clock because she had not thrown it away, which is the only reason he ever sees anything at all.**

> **WORKS AT LANDINGS AND STAGES — CONFIRMATION OF WORKS**
> *To be completed by the person in charge. State the length of frontage, in yards, proposed for maintenance in the season. A return not received within the period stated will be completed by this office.*

**A length of frontage, in yards, for a landing.**

She had written one word on it in pencil, in the remarks box, and drawn a line under the word, and the word was:

> **NO**

And in the date box, in the same pencil, a date. He did not read it. It is hers, it is about when she decided, and he is not going to be the man who asks a woman of thirty-seven when she decided anything.

---

He put the form down on the gunwale while she took the painter off.

"You wrote no."

"**I wrote no.**"

"On a form about a length."

"**A landing is not a rateable thing.**" She did not stop unpacking the painter. "**That is not mine. That is a department's. A landing is not in a street and it is not in a rate roll and it has got no frontage, because frontage is a thing you measure along an edge that faces something, and a landing is a place a boat stops, and a place a boat stops is not a length and cannot be put in yards by anybody. In about four years a man in a room is going to send me a form asking me what a landing is and I am going to write the same word on it, and that is the whole of my career as far as that office is concerned.**"

"Would a mooring number do it."

She stopped, and looked at him, and it was the first time she had looked at him since about ten past eleven.

"**Where did you get that.**"

"Nothing. I made it up about four seconds ago."

"**Then don't.**" She put the painter under the seat. "**A mooring number is a place. A place is a thing you can write to. And the day you can write to a landing is the day there is somebody to write to about, and there is nobody to write to about, and that is not a hardship, that is how a landing works. I have been doing this for twenty-six years and I have never once been at an address, and I could not tell you the name of this reach, and I could take you to the fourth landing in the dark from here.**"

"And the figure."

"**There is no figure.**" She took the painter out again. "**Put four in that box and it will be wrong, and wrong is worse than a blank, because a blank gets asked about and wrong gets believed. That is not a rule I have. That is a Tuesday.**"

---

**And here is the part that is the finding and it took him about eleven seconds standing on a bank in the middle of a reach, and he has it in his own book that evening.**

*A form asks a place for a length. A place that is not a length cannot produce one. And a place that cannot produce a figure gets asked again, and a form that gets asked again is a form with a person on it, and a person is a party, and a party is a name in a box. **The eleven streets could not produce a figure, so an office went to a contract. A man on a bank could not produce a figure, so he wrote no and posted it back in somebody else's envelope. And a landing cannot produce a figure at all, and it wrote no, and it did not even have to be asked twice.** And the difference between those three is not a matter of character. It is that one of them has a piece of paper and two of them do not, and the piece of paper is what makes the office go looking.*

He did not say any of that out loud. She was doing the third landing and he was not needed for it and he stood on the bank for another hour and ten minutes.

---

## A word he has got and did not use, and the reason, in one line

**The water is about two miles above the ford that has been dry since about 1919, and it has a name, and the name is in a book in a coat, and it is about a mile and a half from where he was standing.**

He stood on a bank at about half past one in the afternoon with a boat that knows where the water goes and a woman who has been doing it for twenty-six years, and he had a word that would have been worth something to somebody once, and he did not say it.

*There is no form for a word in a coat and I am not going to build one. **A word out of a book is not a description of a thing, and this word is the name of a water and it does not say what the water does, and the water does not say what the word means, and I have written that down four times now and I am not going to write it a fifth, and I did not say it to a woman of thirty-seven who could have taken me to the fourth landing in the dark.** A route that cannot be produced cannot be asked after, and that is the only protection any route in this city has ever had, and a name in a coat is the safest kind of name there is, and I have had one since about eleven o'clock on Tuesday the twenty-ninth of September and I am not going to spend it on a Tuesday in October.*

**And he did not join it to the schedule. He had the schedule in his head since yesterday and he had the word in his head since Tuesday and the two of them were in the same place for about an hour and ten minutes, and he did not put them next to each other, and he wrote down that he did not, and a name on a wrapper and a district on a printed side are two things and the reason they were in the same place is that he was in the same place.**

---

## The rest of the day, and the fourth landing

**The fourth landing is four miles up and it is the one nobody uses, and she did it last, and he noticed that and did not ask, and it is on the page in his own book that evening and it is the only sentence he has about it.**

The water was the same as it had been at eleven. The grass on the bank above the third landing had gone over in one place and had not in another, and he did not know what that meant and she did not look at it.

**The flatbed was on its way back at ten to four and did not wait, and the four sacks were still on the bed where they had been that morning, and he was in the city at about half past four and did not go up to eleven streets until about six, because there is no vehicle after that and eleven streets is four hundred yards from the end of the street the bus stops at.**

He had the form in his coat with **NO** on it in somebody's pencil and he did not take it out again.

---

*Field Book, Friday the second of October, 23.20, a front room in eleven streets, one day off the first of October.*

*One. **A working day, done well, by somebody who is not me, and I held a painter for fourteen minutes and understood a fifth of it.** Garn Ollerton, thirty-seven, twenty-six years, four landings, and she does not hurry the last eight feet. **She walked me in and out of the tenth crossing and I have not thanked her and I am not going to, and a person who has thanked somebody is a person who has asked, and there is no column for the number of times I have wanted to say a word out loud to her and have not.**

*Two. **A form about a length of frontage, sent to a landing, and the word NO in the remarks box in her own pencil, and nobody asked her to write it and nobody has thanked her for writing it.** A landing is not a rateable thing and frontage is a thing you measure along an edge that faces something, and a place a boat stops is not a length. **Put four in that box and it will be wrong, and wrong is worse than a blank, because a blank gets asked about and wrong gets believed.** I have that from a man of forty-four on a bank in September about a figure in feet and it is the same shape and it is not the same sentence and I have improved it none of them.

*Three. **The finding, and it is the fourth time this year that I have arrived at the same place by three different roads and every time it has been somebody else who got there first.** A form asks a place for a figure. A place that has no figure gets asked again. A form that gets asked again is a form with a person on it. **The eleven streets had no figure so an office went to a contract. A man had no figure so he wrote no and posted it back. A landing has no figure at all and wrote no and did not have to be asked twice. The difference is not character. The difference is that one of them has a piece of paper and two of them do not, and the piece of paper is what makes the office go looking.**

*Four. **A word I did not use, and did not join, and wrote down that I did not join.** The water is a mile and a half from a bank where I was standing and it has a name and the name is in this book and it is not in anything else, and a woman of thirty-seven who could have taken me to the fourth landing in the dark was forty feet away for an hour and ten minutes and I did not say it. **A word in a book does not make it rain and does not stop it raining, and there is no form for a word in a coat and I am not going to build one, and a name on a wrapper and a district on a printed side are two things and the reason they were in the same place is that I was in the same place.**

*Five. **Nothing has moved.** Eleven remembered routes, ten crossings, the tally ten lines, not looked at since the thirteenth of January. The page home the three hundred and thirteenth day, blank, four marks in four hands, none of them mine. The token cold, the two hundred and ninety-ninth day. **The list on the facing page reads twenty-nine and it has not moved in seventy-eight days, and an interval of one day is not a pair, and a pair is a fact about where I was and not about what happened, and if the count of pairs in this block is small and not large that is correct.** The condition column is two hundred and thirty-two days and is not there. The second tin is at thirty-seven and there is no thirty-eight. The plate is a hundred and seventy-nine days in the grass and has been told to nobody. A foreman of fifty-four is twenty-three weeks and two days past four months and nobody is going to ask him.*

*And the standing position: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.**
