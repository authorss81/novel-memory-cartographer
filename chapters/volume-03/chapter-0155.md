# Chapter 155: The Fifth Column, And A Machine In A Room About A Mile Away

**The allocation sheet comes off the machine on a Wednesday at about nine. Five rows. Four columns. One line under the words *officer responsible*, and that line has never been filled in.**

A man of about fifty transferred five figures out of it with a pen, the way he has done every Wednesday since the twenty-sixth of January. **The fourth column was empty and had been filled in three times in nineteen years.** The line under *officer responsible* was empty. The zero in the row for the fen was a zero and it has been a zero every Wednesday since the twenty-sixth of January and nobody has reduced it and nobody is going to.

There are four machines in that room and none of them has been told to stop, because being told to stop is a document, and this is the district that cannot produce documents.

**The page home is the two hundred and sixty-second day and the token is cold on the two hundred and forty-eighth day and the plate is a hundred and twenty-eight days in the grass and the condition column is a hundred and eighty-one days empty and the second tin is at thirty-four. A foreman of fifty-four is sixteen weeks past four months today, exactly, and he is on a gate and nobody is going to ask him.**

---

**And on Wednesday the twelfth of August a man of thirty stood in that room for about two hours and a quarter and did not ask one question, and it was the hardest hour of his month, and it cost a man of sixty-one about four hundred miles away something he will never be told about.**

He had it ready. He had had it ready since the Friday, in a book in a coat, and it was a **fifth column.**

**A district returns card is one printed side, about nine inches by five, and it has four columns across the top — *district. rateable units. returns received. reason* — and about two inches of ruled space at the bottom for the district's own use.** The four columns are all about a district as a thing a department can count. The two inches are about a place.

**A fifth column across the top would be a column that took a name.** Not a name of a ratepayer. A name of a person. And the hand would go in it the way a hand goes in the reason column, because the reason column takes a hand and a hand in that column is the only sentence anybody has ever seen in it in nineteen years.

He had the heading. He had it in about eleven seconds standing at the machine, and it is in a book in a coat and it is not in this room and it is not going to be in this room.

**They do not set a machine for one district.** He did not know that until the man of about fifty said it, and he did not ask, and the man of about fifty said it anyway, in about nine words, while transferring the fifth figure, and then went back to the sheet.

---

"What is it about," said the man of about fifty, at about ten past ten, not turning round.

"Nothing."

"**You have been standing at my machine for two hours and you have got a piece of paper in your coat and you have not looked at it, and the last person who stood at this machine for two hours without looking at it was a young man from the drainage section in about 1979, and he wanted the fourth column.**"

"I have not come for the fourth column."

"**No. Nobody has ever come for the fourth column. You have come for a column that is not on it.**" He put the pen down. "**And I am not going to say no, because you have not asked, and I have got about nine hundred of these a month and I am not going to be the man who stops a conversation nobody started. I am going to tell you the two things and then I am going to pick the pen up.**"

"Go on."

"**One. A column is set once and it stays. It is set for nine hundred and not for one, and it is not set again in August, because in about four years somebody is going to want to know the day a card was issued and the day is a column and you cannot take a column out without taking the day out.**"

"And two."

"**Two. I do not know what you would want to put in it, and I have not asked, and I have had a machine that prints a card in this room for nineteen years and I have never once seen a hand in the reason column before the seventeenth of June and I have not asked about that either and I am not going to ask about yours.**" He picked the pen up. "**So. No answer, because there was no question. Put your paper away.**"

---

**Milo Rook put his hand on the inside pocket where the book was and left it there for about four more seconds and then took it out and did not open it and put it back.**

"That is right," he said.

"**I have not said anything.**"

"That is right, and thank you."

"**Do not do that.**" The man went back to the sheet. "**You have been in this building since about January and you have said that to about four people and every one of them is a person who had told you something for nothing, and I am the fifth, and I have not told you anything, and there is nothing on this sheet you could use even if you wanted to and I would rather you did not find out by asking me.**"

---

**And then, at about ten past eleven, he asked the only question of the morning, and it was not a good one, and Milo Rook has written in his own book that it was the only question he asked in two hours and a quarter and that it was a lie and that he made it in about half a second without stopping to build it.**

"Are you the one that wants a column."

"No."

"**I did not think you were.**" The man of about fifty turned a page. "**I have been in this room nineteen years and I have never been the one anybody wanted, and I have never been able to work out whether that is because nobody wants anything from me or because there is nothing here anybody could want.**"

He did not say anything else for about an hour. The sheet went on the pile. The machine went on running. **Milo Rook stood in a room about a mile from a corridor where a rack is a piece of furniture and a rack is not emptied, and he had said the word *no* to a man who has been in a room for nineteen years and who put a card down face up for the first time in nineteen years on the seventeenth of June, and the man has not connected the two, and Milo Rook is not going to connect them for him, and he has written down that he said no to a man who told him nothing and that the cost of the lie is not that anybody was deceived.**

**The cost of the lie is that the man of about fifty has now been told by his own face, in a room with four machines in it, that there is nothing here anybody could want, and he had not worked that out before, and he will not forget it, and Milo Rook did that in half a second on a Wednesday morning for a reason that has nothing to do with the man of about fifty.**

---

**And that reason, and it took him the rest of the morning, and he wrote it out in a book in a coat at about eleven o'clock at night on the same day, and it is about a man of sixty-one on a top track four miles this side of nothing, and the man of sixty-one is not going to be told any of it.**

*There is a fifth column in this city that would take a name for a district with no rateable unit, and there is a machine in a room that would print it, and the machine does not care which district it is for, and I did not ask. **And I did not ask because a man with a spade said four words to me on a Friday: whatever you write down will be the first time anybody has written down any of it, and that is not a service, that is a door.***

*And here is the whole of the price of that door, and I have had four days to find it and it took me about an hour, and it is this. **A man of sixty-one has been paid in coin for thirty-one years and has no employer and no wage and no name on anything, and there are nineteen years of dates in his head and no piece of paper, and if a family wanted this week to give him something for thirty-one years there is no form in this city on which to enter it and no employer to enter it against and the money has been in jars on tables. He cannot be asked, so he cannot be claimed. He cannot be claimed, so he cannot be paid, and he cannot be paid, so nothing has ever been wrong, and the reason nothing has ever been wrong is that nothing has ever been about him.**

*And I did not ask, and by not asking I have kept him unaskable, and that is the sixth thing in eight months that I have got by writing something down in this book and not doing it, and this time I know exactly what it cost and to whom, and knowing is not a reason and is not a stop and is not on the facing page.*

---

**And at about eleven o'clock at night, in a book in a coat, the tenth thing of that shape in eight months came, and it is six words, and he wrote it down and looked at it for about a minute and a half and did not act on it and did not know what it was about, and it is the opposite of the truth and he has not been able to prove that it is the opposite of the truth, and it is going to stay.**

> **Nothing written down can be got wrong.**

*There are nine people in this district and none of them has it. I am not going to say it in a yard. I am not going to say it to a man of about fifty who has been in a room for nineteen years and who put a pen down for me. I am not going to say it to a woman of thirty-four in a village who would tell me the reason it is a lie in about four seconds and who would be right, and I am not going to say it on a top track, and I am not going to say it to a man of sixty-one with a spade, because he is the fourth person in this book who has told me what not to do and he has not been thanked for one of them and he has never once asked me to be there.*

*And the rule on the facing page is **no seventh column before the first of September** and it is twenty days off and I wrote on Thursday that I was not going to wait for the first of September, and I meant it, and the reason I meant it is that the rule is about a month and the thing is about me, and a rule that is about a month can be waited out and a thing that is about me cannot.*

---

*Field Book, Wednesday the twelfth of August, 23.30, a front room in eleven streets, one day off the eleventh.*

*One. **The fifth column, not asked for. **A card has four columns and about two inches, and the two inches are the only part of that form that is about a place, and I have used them once, on the nineteenth of July, face down, with my own name in them, and it is the fifth of five things I have given up and the only one I cannot get off. A fifth column across the top would take a name and the hand would go in it the way the hand went in the reason column on the seventeenth of June, and there is a machine in a room that would print it, and it would be set once and it would stay, and in about four years somebody could ask a room for every card in that column and the room would produce them all, and a row of names is a list, and a list is a place somebody can be sent to look at.***

*Two. **What it cost, and it is the first time in eight months that the thing I have not done has had a name and a place and a price on it. It is a man of sixty-one who cannot be asked and therefore cannot be claimed and therefore has never been paid. **I have got a rule in this book about a column and a stop and a facing page and a plate and a table, and they are all about me, and the sixth of them is the only one that is about somebody else, and I found it by not doing something, and I have written that down because the day I stop writing these things down is the day they stop being true and I stop noticing them, and that is the whole of what a field book is for and I am not going to be nicer about it than that.***

*Three. **Nothing has moved. The pairs are twenty-nine. Eleven remembered routes, ten crossings, the tally is ten lines, Hettie Brann's paper and Dr Sen's pocket, not looked at since the thirteenth of January and not going to be looked at in August. The page home the two hundred and sixty-second day, blank, four marks in four hands, none of them mine. The token cold on the two hundred and forty-eighth day and it has done nothing since the seventh of December and it has been in a coat in a room and in a coat in a fenland and in a pocket in a machine room. The condition column is a hundred and eighty-one days empty. The reason column is empty. The plate is a hundred and twenty-eight days. The second tin is at thirty-four and the last rack day was Friday the seventh. The fourth column on the allocation sheet is empty and has been filled in three times in nineteen years and the line under *officer responsible* has never been filled in and neither of those is a fault and I did not ask about either of them and I am not going to.***

*Four. **The four unshowable things in five months are four and this block has not made a fifth. Wednesday the twelfth of August is right about the second side of the sheet for the same reason it is right about every one since the eighth of April, and the man of about sixty at a shed at Cardew Docks is not on the Row and has not been since before the war and does not know that a piece of paper exists and is not going to be told, and a district entered as nil is the second reason, and I am not counting them.***

*And the standing position: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.**
