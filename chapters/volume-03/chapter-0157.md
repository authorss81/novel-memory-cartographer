# Chapter 157: A Name Off A Contract, In A Box, Typed, On A Thursday

**On Thursday the twentieth of August an office about a mile away returned side four of a district schedule with something in column six, and it was a name, and it was typed, and nobody in eleven streets was told until the Monday.**

Milo Rook saw it on the Friday morning in a corridor four rooms above a fitter's shop and he read it in about four seconds standing up and then went and did the round, which goes out at about ten past eight and comes in at about four, because the round goes on a Monday and a Thursday and it is one man short and has been one man short since the twenty-eighth of December.

> **Ground of discontinuance: (a) no maintained seam of record**
> **NOT TO BE MAINTAINED AFTER 31 AUGUST**
> **Signature of the person responsible: R. PRYCE**

**The date under it is the twentieth of August, which was a Thursday, and they complete them on the Thursday, and it has been a Thursday thing for about eleven years and it is not a punishment and it is not a meeting.**

---

**And here is why, and it took him about nine minutes at a counter on the Friday morning, and nobody lied, and there is a second time in eight months that there is no one to be angry at and it is worse than the first.**

Column six is the signature of the person responsible. A signature is a thing a person does and the office cannot do it. **And a district with no rateable unit has no correspondence address, so it cannot be sent anything and cannot be asked anything, and this district has been exactly that since 1911.** So the office did the only thing available to it: it went to the document it could reach.

**A contract for surface and drainage works in the eleventh street district is a thing between the city and a firm, and a firm is rateable, and a firm has an address, and a contract names the people who are working on it.** And the contract for the back length names the supervisor, because a contract has to be able to say who is in charge of a gang on a Tuesday, and the man who is in charge of that gang on a Tuesday is a man of fifty-four called Roy Pryce, and he has been in charge of it for thirty-one years, and his name is on that contract in his own handwriting in about 1974 and again in about 1986 and again in about 1998.

**So the office took the name off the contract and typed it into a box on a printed side, and in about four years a room can produce that sheet, and the room will produce a page with a foreman's name in the column that says he signed it, and he did not sign it, and there is nothing in this city that can make a room give a typed name back to the man it belongs to.**

---

**And on Monday the twenty-fourth of August the gang was on the back length at half past seven, eleven men and a foreman with a clipboard, and it was the forty-first walk since the first of April, and the condition column had nothing in it and the reason column had nothing in it and both of those were correct.**

The plate in the grass is a hundred and forty days old. The page home is the two hundred and seventy-fourth day. The token is cold on the two hundred and sixtieth day. The condition column is a hundred and ninety-three days empty and has two entries in it, both in July, both four words and a figure. The second tin is at thirty-six and the last rack day was Friday the twenty-first, which was a week ago and was the one he was not here for. A foreman of fifty-four is seventeen weeks and five days past four months and is on a gate and is not going to be asked.

---

**And at about half past six that evening there were about nine people in a yard and there was no coffee, and a man of fifty-four put a folded piece of paper on the low wall and squared it with his thumb, which is a thing a man of thirty-one years does with a weekly sheet, and did not say anything for about a minute and a half.**

"It came to the office," he said. "**Thursday. The clerk read it out to me and I asked for it back and she gave it back and it is not mine, it is a copy, and I have got it because I asked for it, and I am aware of what that is.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**It is a notice that the works on the back length are discontinued after the thirty-first of August and it has got my name in a box on the last line and I did not sign it and I have not seen it and nobody asked me.**" He put his hand flat on the wall. "**And I am not going to stand here and be the man who complains about it, and I am going to tell you why, and then I am going to go and wash.**"

"Go on."

"**Because if I say I did not agree to it then it is a disagreement. And a disagreement wants two. And there is one.**" He did not raise his voice. "**And there is no second one, and there is not going to be a second one, because the district has got no address and the office could not write to us and it went to the contract instead, and I am on the contract, and that is the whole of how I got into a box on a Thursday. And if I stand in this yard and say it is wrong, then in about four years there is a piece of paper in a file with a man in a yard on it saying the eleventh street district disputes the discontinuance, and there is no district, and I will have built the first piece of paper there has ever been about this place, and it will be about me, and it will be wrong, because the works are going to stop on the thirty-first whatever I say.**"

"So what do you want said."

"**Nothing.**" He picked the paper up. "**And I would like it noticed that I have written two sentences in a condition column since February and one of them is four words and one of them is four words and a figure, and I have done that because a length is a length and a hole is a hole, and I have not put one word in that column about a thing that is not a length, and I am not going to start on Monday.**"

---

**And then he asked Milo Rook one question, and it was the only question anybody asked in that yard, and it took about four seconds.**

"**Did you put my name on it.**"

"No."

"**Right.**" He did not say anything else about it and he went and washed.

**And Milo Rook stood in a yard and could not say one word, and could not have said one word, and did not have one, and the sentence he did not say is the whole of the block and he has written it out in his own book and it is eleven words long and it is not *no*.**

*I did not put your name on it and I have known since Friday morning that it was going to be your name, and I have had four days to say something and I have not said anything, and there is no form in this city for saying something about a box on a printed side in an office about a mile away, and there is no form because nothing in this district is a party to anything.*

---

**And at about ten past seven, at a gate in a high wall four hundred yards from the yard, a man of forty-one on no list at all was loading a handcart and said nine words, and this is the twelfth time he has said nine words to Milo Rook since the twenty-eighth of December and there is no column anywhere for the number.**

"**You have got a man in a box now.**"

"That is what has happened."

"**That is what you said just now and not what I said.**" He got the handcart through the gap. "**I have never been able to work out why it is that a row of a district is a document and one man in one box is a different kind of thing, and I have been at this gate four times a day for two hundred days and I have not been sent and I am not going to be, and I would rather somebody in a good coat who writes things down worked that out than me, because I am not going to be the one who says it and have it in a book.**"

---

**And a name got into a piece of paper about this district in July and it was his, and he wrote it himself, in two inches of ruled space, face down, on a Sunday, in about four seconds, and it is the fifth of five things he has given up and the only one of the five he cannot get off.**

**And a name got into a piece of paper about this district in August and it is not his, and he did not do it, and he cannot get it off either, and the only difference between the two is that he can see his own.**

*Twenty-fourth of August. Two lines and then I will go and sleep.*

*One. **A typed name in a box that says a man signed it, and he did not, and it is the first time since the fourteenth of April that a person in this district has had his name on a printed side about this district, and I did not do it, and I knew on the Friday, and I said nothing for four days, and there is no form.** And I have got a name of my own on a card in a building about a mile away from the same nineteen lines, and the difference between the two is that I can see mine, and that is the whole of what a field book is and it is not worth much.*

*Two. **Nothing has moved. The pairs are twenty-nine. Eleven remembered routes, ten crossings, the tally is ten lines, and I have not looked at it since the thirteenth of January and I am not going to look at it in August, and the end of July was four days ago and I did not do it and I am going to break the promise about the ninth crossing on the last day of this month, in writing, which is the only tool I have and the worst possible one, and a promise kept in a book and broken in a book is not a promise and is not a break and is a third thing and I do not have a word for the third thing and I have not had one since January.***

*And the standing position: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.**
