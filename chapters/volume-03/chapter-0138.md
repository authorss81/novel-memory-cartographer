# Chapter 138: A Sentence With A Number On It

It came off the same machine on Saturday morning the twenty-seventh of June, in the second run, and it was a different piece of paper and it had a number in the top left corner that the first one had not had.

**The man of about fifty had put the first card down face up, and face up is not a state a card is in, and once a week at about four in the afternoon the day's cards are re-run so that a face-up one goes back through the machine in case the operator has left it the wrong way up, and there is a line at the foot of that run sheet that says *re-run — face up only*, and the line is on the form, and the form is about a printing error and not about a district.**

So there were two cards. **MIREWARD. Rateable units: nil. Returns received: nil. Reason: —** and then six words in a hand.

> *Asked. Nothing came.*

One of them had no number. The other one had a number, and the number is a reference in a series that runs from something like 41,000 upward, and about four hundred of those go out of that room a week.

"A face-up card gets re-run," said Milo Rook.

"A face-up card gets re-run," said the man of about fifty, "**and I have put a card face up twice in nineteen years and one of them was a card for the river core that had a coffee ring on it, and I did that in about 1981 and I have not done it since and I did it on Wednesday and I would like to go back to about 1981.**"

---

**And that is the escalation, and it is a document, and it took eleven seconds in a corridor at about ten past one on the Friday afternoon in a building about four hundred yards from a tram authority's district office, and it is the first time in seven months that anything has been put in front of Mara Quill that she wanted.**

"You have told me about the first one," she said, "and you have not told me about the second one, and the reason you have not told me is that the second one is the one with a number on it, and you worked that out about four hours ago and you have been carrying it ever since."

"Yes."

"**Say it.**

"A sentence is a thing a person can be sent to look at. A sentence with a reference on it is a thing a person can be asked for, and asking for it is done in a form, and the form goes in a file, and about four years from now somebody can ask a room for 41,000-and-something and a room can produce it, and the hand is not on the form. The hand is not on either card. The hand was on the paper and the machine has taken the hand over and there is nothing in this city that can get a machine to give a hand back."

"That took you about eleven seconds and it is correct and I had it."

"**Then you did not need me and you came anyway.**" She had a folder and she did not open it. "There is a second thing and I am going to say it once and I am not going to say it twice, and I am not going to say it in a room and I am not going to put it in a minute. **There is a line in a return that I reconcile on the last Thursday of every month which has got nothing to do with you and nothing to do with a tram, and I have looked at it, and I am not going to tell you what it says, and I am telling you that I have looked at it.**"

"What kind of line."

"That is the second time," said Mara Quill, "**and I said once.**"

---

**And in a yard at about half past six on the same Saturday there were about nine people and there was no coffee, and he said the thing out loud in about forty seconds and then did not improve it.**

"There are two cards off the machine for Mireward with six words in a hand in the reason column, and one of them has a reference number on it, and I did not know about the number until a quarter past twelve today, and the reason the number is there is that I wrote a card in a room and a man of about fifty put it down face up to look at it and a face-up card goes back through the machine once a week on a line at the foot of a run sheet that is about a printing error."

"And the six words."

"**Asked. Nothing came. And I am not going to say that in a yard twice and I have said it twice, and I am not going to say it a third time, and if it is on a card with a number on it then the number is the part that matters and the six words are the part that does not.**"

"Then who gets asked."

"That is the wrong question and you know it is the wrong question," said somebody.

"It is the wrong question," he said. "**The right one is who asks the room for the number, and it will not be anybody in this district, because anybody in this district who asks for it is on the form, and there are about nine people here and not one of them has a reason to be on a form in June.**"

---

**And nothing else happened on the Saturday, and nothing happened on the Sunday the twenty-eighth, and both of those are on the carbon of a week and neither of them is on a form anywhere in this city.**

Field Book, Saturday the twenty-seventh and Sunday the twenty-eighth of June.

*Twenty-seventh, 23.30. One day off the twenty-sixth.*

*Twenty-eighth, 23.25. One day off the twenty-seventh. A Sunday, and there is nothing in it, and it is on the page, and the list has not moved.*

*One. **Two cards, one number, and that is the whole of the escalation and it took eleven seconds.** A sentence is a thing a person can be sent to look at — I said that in April about a form in another building and I have not improved it since. A sentence with a reference on it is a thing a person can be asked for. The asking is done in a form. The form goes in a file. **And the hand is not on the form, and the hand was on the paper, and the machine took the hand over, and there is nothing in this city that can make a machine give a hand back.** The face-up card is the whole of the mechanism and it is a line at the foot of a run sheet headed *re-run — face up only*, and the run sheet is about a printing error. I have written a great deal in this book about the difference between a thing a person does and a thing an office does and I have never once watched the second one eat the first one in about four seconds over a coffee ring.

*Two. **And a line in a return.** She told me in a corridor that there is one, and that she has looked at it, and that it has nothing to do with me, and she would not say what kind of line it is and I asked what kind of line it is and she said *that is the second time and I said once*. **I have not asked her what is in that file with a coat in it for five months and I did not ask her about this for one second, and both of those are the same act and I have known that since the fifth of May and I have not done anything about it and a date is a column.** What is new is that she told me on her own. She has not told me a thing in six months and she told me that she has looked at a line, and I do not know what it means, and I am not going to find out, and if she tells me I am going to be a person who has been told and that is a different column.

*Three. **What I did not do.** I did not write the six words in this book. I have said them out loud twice — once in a yard on the seventeenth of June and once in a yard tonight — and I have not put them on the page, and there is a reason and the reason is that a Field Book is paper and graphite and lives in a coat and this coat goes into rooms. And that is the right reason and it is also a small one and I have got about nine of the small reasons in seven months and every one of them is correct and every one of them has the same shape, and the shape is a place where the sentence is safer than it is in the world. **The page home the two hundred and sixteenth day, blank, four marks in four hands and none of them mine.** The token cold, the two hundred and second day, in the inside pocket. Ten routes and ten crossings. The tally is ten lines and I have not looked at it since the thirteenth of January and I am not going to in July. The plate is eighty-two days in the grass and it is never reported and I have not told anybody and I am not going to. The round does not go today because a Saturday is not a Monday and a Saturday is not a Thursday and it is one man short. The condition column is empty and the reason column is empty and both are correct. The second tin has twenty-eight in it and there is a pad in a drawer in Crown Heights that nobody has rung up about since 1969. **And the sixth line of a maintenance return is fifty-four words long and nobody in this district has read it and I do not know what is in it and I am not going to ask, and it is now about five months since a clerk of forty-six wrote it in his own hand and signed it, and that is the only place I am going to put that and it is a book in a coat.**

*And the standing position: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.*
