# Chapter 169: A Word On The Outside Of A Wrapper, And A Column With One Hand In It

**He went up the thirty-four steps on Tuesday the twenty-ninth of September at about ten in the morning. There are nine desks on the left in that room and a plan chest and a window that does not open and a chair under it, and Bettina Sallow is fifty-four and has been at the ninth desk for four years, and she is not in eleven streets and she does not come to this side of the bridge and she is not going to.**

The page home is the three hundred and tenth day. The token is cold on the two hundred and ninety-sixth day. The plate in the grass at the low end of Bridge Street is a hundred and seventy-six days old and has been told to nobody. The condition column is two hundred and twenty-nine days and is not there. **A foreman of fifty-four is twenty-two weeks and six days past four months and nobody is going to ask him.**

He had nothing on the sheet of his own paper. He had not written anything on it on the twenty-second and he had not written anything on it on the way in that morning, and that was the first time in eight months he had come to a room with a sheet of paper and nothing on it and had not written anything on it on the way.

---

## She got it down herself

"I have come for the bundle on the fifth finding aid."

"**I know which one. Fourth line. It has been on that sheet since the nineteenth of August.**" She did not ask him for it. "**I am going to get it down myself and I am not going to tell you the number on the shelf, and you are not going to ask me, and last week you did not ask me, and I have not stopped thinking about the fact that you did not ask me.**"

"Go on."

"**A number on a shelf in a room I am responsible for is a reference. A reference is a thing a person can be asked for in about four years, and if I say it then in about four years a man can say it, and then he can ask me whose hand wrote it down, and then there is a person.**"

She went out. She was gone about six minutes.

She came back with a bundle of paper tied with tape, about three inches thick, and put it on the desk and squared it, and sat down on the other side of it, and **neither of them said the number out loud and neither of them asked the other one.**

---

## The outside of the wrapper, and a name

The tape was dry and had been dry a long time. On the outside, in a hand that is not a hand in this room and that is not a clerk's hand anywhere:

> **RAINWAY DRAINAGE BOARD — CONSIGNMENT OF SURVEY AND SHEET MATERIAL**

And under that, in the same hand, smaller:

> *received, unasked for. not evidence. not for index.*

"That is a water," said Bettina Sallow. "**It has been a water in this city's land papers since about 1911 and it is the name on nine plots of about four acres each that are held on shares, and it runs about two miles above a ford that has been dry since about 1919, and I have never once in four years heard a person say that word in a conversation and I did not know it was a word anybody used until I read it.**"

"It is the name of the water."

"**It is the name of the water. That is all it is on the outside of that bundle and I have not opened the middle of it and neither am I going to.**"

---

**And he did not join it to anything, and he has written that down four times in a month and this is the fourth.**

He has four bells and he has not written them down. He has a practice that is four people doing a thing and it is not a form and it is not a place and it is not a day. He has a plate in grass a hundred and seventy-six days old. He has a card in a run of about forty thousand with his own name in two inches of ruled space at the bottom. He has a bundle on a shelf he cannot point at. He has a typed name in two boxes on two printed sides. He has a handrail post four inches short and nineteen cuts in a post at a ford and a number cast into a collar.

**And a word. And a name, on the outside of a wrapper, in a hand that is not a hand in this room, in about 1911, about a water.**

*A name on a wrapper and four bells in four places and a bell on a post at a ford and a card and a plate and a post and a collar and a handrail are not the same thing, and the reason I know they are not the same thing is that I could put them in one column in about four seconds and I have done it about nine times in eight months and every one of the nine was a mistake. **A watercourse is a watercourse. A name is a name. And the two of them are in a bundle because a bundle of paper that came from a drainage board has the board's water on the outside of it, and that is the only reason those two things are in the same room, and it is not a reason.***

---

## The outside flap, and three words in small type

The flap of the wrapper was folded under and printed on the outside of it, in small type, three entries under a rule.

> *Schedule of instruments*
> **First Atlas** — citywide; custody distributed; gaps widened, not closed
> **Singular Mode** — emergency setting; one account to become the Index's version; others suspended
> **Quieting** — setting not shut down; forced output thereafter treated as the record

**A flap is not a catalogue item and there is no number on it and no shelf it can be asked for by, and she read it out in about eleven seconds because a flap is not a thing anybody asks a room for, and that is the whole of the difference between the flap and the shelf, and it is the difference between this chapter and the last one.**

Milo Rook wrote the three words down on the sheet of his own paper. He wrote them in his own hand, in the order they were printed, and he did not underline them and he did not put a question mark after them and he did not write what he thought they were.

---

"**What are they.**"

"**I have not got those three words in this room in four years of cards and I am not going to guess at them.**" She put the flap down. "**And I am not going to tell you what I think they are, because a person in a good coat writes down what a person in a good coat thinks they are, and then in about four years there is a sentence in a book in a coat with three words in it that I had a feeling about, and that is the worst kind of document there is.**"

"Is there a date on the flap."

"**There is no date on the wrapper and there is no date on the flap and there is no section number and there is nothing that would tell you when any of it was done, and you did not ask me for one, and I would like it noticed that you did not ask me for one, because a fold is not a chronology and a mark transferred this week is not older than the sheet it landed on.**"

He had a woman of sixty-one with a folding rule put a figure of his right in about four seconds in June and he had not thanked her and he was not going to thank this one either, and **she had just told him the whole of why, unasked, in one sentence, about a thing she was not being asked about.**

---

## Inside, and a column, and one hand

She opened it. There were about forty leaves in it and she turned them the way a person turns pages in a job, and she did not read them and he did not ask her to.

Two things in it were not paper.

**One was a printed side, single, headed with the name of a place and not of a district, and the heading was about a day.** Not a funeral. A day set aside. It said, in the ordinary language of a notice, that the day was kept, and that people were invited, and that there would be no collection, and that the sheet at the end of it was to be filled in.

**And at the foot of the printed side, a ruled block with one column, and the column was headed with the name of the day.**

**And the other thing was the sheet.**

It was one leaf, and the column had about forty lines in it, and **every line was in the same hand.** Not forty hands. One. The same pen going down a column for forty entries, and the entries were names, and the names were people, and the sheet had a rule at the top of the column and a rule at the bottom and nothing else on it at all.

**And the sheet had a day at the top of it, and no year.**

*One day and forty names in one hand and no year. A day with no year is not a date. **And I am not going to put a year on it, and I have written that down, and there are nine years in this book already that are the ages of objects and buildings and institutions and ways of doing things and a tenth one is not going to be a year on a piece of paper in a coat.***

---

## And the whole of it, in about four minutes, standing up, to nobody

He worked it out and it took him about four minutes and he did it standing up in a room with a plan chest in it and he did it to nobody, and **this is the first time in eight months that he has named a thing out loud in a room where two people were, and one of them was not the kind of person he names things to, and he did not do it on purpose and has written that down.**

Somebody kept a day.

**The day was a public mourning. Not a district's and not a family's. It was kept, and people came to it, and it was in the city, and it was announced on a printed side, and it is the only instance in this whole novel of a public grief that anybody ever entered anywhere.**

And the sheet at the end of the printed side was how they entered it. **A day was kept and a column was provided and the column wanted names, and forty people wrote their names in one hand, and the hand was one hand because a column wants one hand and a column is a column.**

And then — and this is the part he had to stand still for, and he did not sit down and he did not put a hand on anything —

**the sheet became the account.** Not of who had been. Of who the day was for. Forty names in one hand went into a record and the record wanted one account, and the account wanted one account, and there was a setting for it, and the setting had a name, and the name was on the flap, and he had read it eleven seconds earlier.

**And the part of a mourning that could not be made to be one account did not stop.**

**It went out of the record. And out of the record is the weather.**

---

He stood there and said it, once, out loud, and what he said was this, and it is the whole of this book and the whole of this block and it took him four minutes to get to:

"**A mourning that is displaced from the only form that could have held it is a mourning that is still happening, somewhere, in the weather. And it is not a weapon, and nobody designed it, and nobody put it there. A department had a printed side with a column for a day, and forty people put their names in it in one hand, and then the account wanted one account, and the part of it that would not go into one account went out of the record, and out of the record is where the weather is, and it is over a fen four miles from here, on a water that has a name on the outside of a wrapper.**"

"And the Registry."

"**The Registry cannot acknowledge it. And the Registry is not refusing to. **There is no form in this city in which a district can say that something happened to it** — a man of sixty-one said a version of it in about four hundred words on a top track on the twenty-fourth of July, a man of forty-four said a version of it in four seconds on a bank on the fifteenth of September, and the second-in-command of Survey Enforcement said it in nine seconds in a room in Crown Heights on the twenty-third, and he is right, and all four of them are right, and I have no answer to any of them.**"

"And that is what made it worse."

"**That is what made it worse. Not the setting. Not whoever was in the room. The forms.**" He had his hands at his sides. "**A district that cannot report a loss is a district with nothing to report. A district with nothing to report is entered as stable. A district entered as stable is a district that cannot be frightened. A district that cannot be frightened is a district where there is nothing to insure against. And a district where there is nothing to insure against is a district whose works are not reimbursable at the present rating, and that is one of the four grounds on a key printed on the second side of a district schedule, and the key is correct, and I have got nothing against the key, and I have wanted something against the key since the third of July and I have not been able to find it and I am not going to look.**"

"And nobody to be angry at."

"**There is nobody to be angry at. That has been the answer since the third of July and I have written it in this book six times and it has got worse every time, because every week the machinery does something correct.**"

---

## The refusal, and it was correct, and it changed nothing

"**What is on the second flap.**"

"Nothing is on the second flap."

"You have not looked."

"**I have not looked and I am not going to look and that is not a rule I made for you, that is what I do.**" She turned the wrapper over and put her hand flat on it. "**There is a column on the first leaf with forty names in it in one hand and no year, and that is a sheet about a day, and a sheet about a day is not a record of what happened on the day, it is a record of who was counted. And if I turn that flap over and there is a second sheet, then there are two sheets, and a thing that is in two places is a thing that somebody can be asked about in one of them.**"

"And there might be a second sheet."

"**There might be. I have been in this room four years and I have never opened this bundle and I am not going to open it, and I would like it noticed that the reason is not modesty and is not a rule about a person in a good coat.**" She took her hand off it. "**It is that a repository is a room where things go that a department has decided are not evidence. And a bundle is not evidence of a thing. A bundle is evidence that a department sent a thing away, and I have never in four years seen a department send a thing away that it did not have a reason for, and the reason is always the same, and the reason is that the thing could not be put in the form.**"

---

She put the bundle back in the middle of the desk and squared it again, and did not tie the tape.

"**You are not going to take that out of this room.**"

"No."

"**And you are not going to write down the column.**"

"No."

"**And you are not going to write down a name of the water on the outside of that wrapper in anything that goes into a building.**"

"No. **And I am not going to tell you what I think those three words on the flap are, and if you ask me a second time I will give you the same answer, and you will not ask me a second time.**"

---

*Field Book, Tuesday the twenty-ninth of September, 23.55, a front room in eleven streets, one day off the twenty-eighth.*

*One. **THE NAME, AND IT IS A NAME, AND IT IS MINE, AND IT IS IN THIS BOOK.** Rainway. It is on the outside of a wrapper in a hand that is not a hand in this room, in about 1911, on a consignment of survey and sheet material from a drainage board that no longer exists, and it is the name of a water about two miles long above a ford that has been dry since about 1919, and it is the name of nine plots held on shares, and **nobody in this district has ever used the word in a conversation and a woman of fifty-four has been in a room for four years and has never heard it said.** **I have not given it to anybody. It is not on a card and it is not in a schedule and it is not in a return and it is not in a book but this one. A name in a book in a coat is the only kind of name in this story that cannot be asked for, and I have wanted that since about the second of January and I have had it in my hand since about eleven o'clock this afternoon, and I have not given it to a woman of thirty-one, or to a man of about fifty with four machines, or to a boy of seventeen, or to a foreman of fifty-four, and nobody has been thanked for anything and I am not going to thank her and she has told me in about nine words that she does not want to be.*

*Two. **AND THE NAMING DOES NOT EXPLAIN THE NAMING.** A word out of a book is not a description of a thing. **The name on a wrapper does not say what the water does, and the water does not say what the word means, and the two of them are in the same room because a bundle of paper that came from a drainage board has the board's water on the outside of it.** I have not joined it to the four bells, and I have not joined it to the bell on the post at the ford, and I have not joined it to the plate, and I have not joined it to the cast number in the collar, or to the handrail post, or to the card with my name in it, or to the two typed names, or to the yard, and **a name and a watercourse are two things and the reason they are in one bundle is a bundle, and a bundle is not a reason. That is the fourth time in a month I have refused a join and the refusals are the work and the joins would have been the mistake and the difference between the two is about four seconds of standing up.**

*Three. **AND THE DISPLACEMENT, WHICH IS A FACT ABOUT A MOURNING AND NOT ABOUT A PLACE.** Somebody kept a day, and printed a side about it, and provided a column, and forty people put their names in it in one hand, and the sheet became the account, and the account wanted one account, and there was a setting for that and the setting has a name and I have read the name. **The part of a mourning that could not be made one account did not stop happening. It went out of the record. And out of the record is the weather, over a fen, on a water with a name on a wrapper.** Mireward is not the thing. Mireward is where the water is. **It is not a weapon. Nobody designed it. Nobody put it there. A department had a column for a day and it did the correct thing with the column and the correct thing has been running since about 1911 and it is still running and the last time anybody in this city looked at it a man in a room completed a row in a minute and a half on a Thursday and put a foreman's name in a box because he was the only name the office could reach.**

*Four. **AND THE THREE WORDS, WHICH I HAVE NOW WRITTEN DOWN ONCE, IN THIS BOOK, IN MY OWN HAND, AND WHICH EXPLAIN NOTHING THAT CAME EARLIER.** First Atlas. Singular Mode. Quieting. **They are on the outside flap of a bundle in small type under a rule and I have got them in about eleven seconds from a woman reading a flap, and I do not know what any of them is, and I have not asked, and I am not going to ask her, and I am not going to go and find out this week.** They do not account for the bell on a post at a ford, or for the plate in the grass, or for the removed sentence, or for the withdrawn classification of the eleventh, or for an order that is suspended and not discharged, or for a matter that stands over with no date and is on no list, or for the three clues from January that are three, or for a boy of seventeen, or for anything at all that I have seen in eight months. **A word out of a book is not an explanation and I have got three words out of a book and I have written them down and that is all, and there is no date on the wrapper and there is no date on the flap and there is no year on the sheet, and a fold is not a chronology, and a day with no year is not a date, and I have written that down and I am not going to improve it.**

*Five. **AND THE OTHER THING SHE SAID, WHICH I AM NOT GOING TO IMPROVE EITHER, AND WHICH IS THE ONLY REASON I WENT UP THE THIRTY-FOUR STEPS TWICE.** A repository is a room where things go that a department has decided are not evidence. **A bundle is not evidence of a thing. A bundle is evidence that a department sent a thing away, and a department does not send a thing away without a reason, and the reason is always the same, and the reason is that the thing could not be put in the form.** She has been in that room four years and she has not opened the bundle and she is not going to, and she told me that before I got there, and I did not ask her to, and she has not been thanked and I am not going to thank her.

*Six. **Nothing is resolved and nothing is cured and nothing moved.** Eleven remembered routes, ten crossings, the tally ten lines, Hettie Brann's paper in Dr Sen's pocket, not looked at since the thirteenth of January. The page home the three hundred and tenth day, blank, four marks in four hands, none of them mine. The token cold on the two hundred and ninety-sixth day, and it has done nothing since the seventh of December. **The list on the facing page reads twenty-nine and it has not moved in seventy-five days, and an interval of one day is not a pair, and a pair is a fact about where I was and not about what happened, and if the count of pairs in this month is a small number and not a large one that is correct.** The condition column is two hundred and twenty-nine days and is not there. The second tin is at thirty-seven and there is no thirty-eight. The plate is a hundred and seventy-six days and has been told to nobody. **A foreman of fifty-four is twenty-two weeks and six days past four months and is not going to be asked.** There is no demand and no service and no failure out of a bell on a post at a ford dry since about 1919, and nobody has been inside it, and it has not been dated, and nobody has been put in it, and it is not a haunting and it is not a claim and it is not a ghost and it is not a voice and it is not a knock and it is not a person and it is not a curse and it is not a debt and it is not an inherited role, and the four seconds at the third post on the eighteenth of September did not become any of those things and it did not become this and I wrote on the page that it would not.

*And the standing question, in three parts. **(a)** There is no form in this city in which a district can say that something happened to it, and there was one, once, and it was a column on a printed side on a kept day, and forty people put their names in it in one hand, and the hand is the whole of the trouble. **A place is not stable because nothing has happened in it. A place is stable because there is nobody writing down what happens in it. And a mourning that is displaced from the only form that could have held it is a mourning that is still happening, somewhere, in the weather — and the answer to this volume's question is not that writing is bad, because writing down what happened is not the same work as keeping anything in repair, and there is a line at the foot of this page that says so in one sentence and that line is not improved. The answer is that a form that wants a person is the mistake, and the mistake has been in a building in Crown Heights since about 1911, and it is printed in small type under a rule on the outside flap of a bundle that a department sent here because it could not be put in a column.** *(b) There is no second outlook. Unmoved. **It has been still for longer than the pairs have been still, and I am not going to count it, because a day-count on a part of a question that nobody has moved is a column and a column is worse than a count,** and it is the only part of anything in this book that has not moved at all and I have stopped finding that satisfying and I have not stopped noticing it, and nobody has asked for one in writing since May and the absence is not explained and is not going to be explained by me in September.* *(c) An instrument a person cannot produce. Half an answer, and there is a fourth part under the third that I have now been down to once, on the twenty-ninth of September, in about eleven seconds, from a woman reading a flap, and what is down there is three words in small type, and a day with no year, and forty names in one hand, and none of the instruments can be produced and one of them is a bundle on a shelf that I cannot point at, and a route that cannot be produced cannot be asked after, and that is still the only protection any route in this city has ever had, and there is no form for the smell of a fen in a dry week and I am not going to build one.*

*And the standing position: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.**
