# Chapter 136: A Man Walks Him In And Nine Rings On A Post

The gang was on the back length at half past seven on Thursday the twenty-fifth of June, eleven men and a foreman with a clipboard, and it was the twenty-fourth walk since the first of April, and the reason column was empty and the condition column was empty, and both of those are correct, and for the second time in a fortnight he was not on it and nobody telephoned and the sheet is right about that as well.

**A lorry comes into this district on a Thursday morning to a yard at the north end and it does not wait either, and between it and a flatbed on a Friday there are two motor vehicles a week in a place that has a hundred and forty people in it, and there is one tractor, and the tractor belongs to a man who will not be named on anything.**

He was on the bank of the drain at the old ford at about nine. **Ola Petrossian got off the lorry at the yard at about nine, and she is twenty-three, and she is a nurse, and nobody asked her, and the man of fifty-eight had told her on the Saturday at the top of the path in about nine words, and she came on her day off and she walked four miles from a yard at the north end to get here and she is going to be paid by a clinic that has no record of where she went.**

---

**The four conditions are the same four words they have been since October and he said all four of them out loud at about ten past two on a bank of a drain at the old ford, in front of about six people, and he said them slowly and in the order.**

"A person walks me in and walks me out and the person is not me. **Garn Ollerton, and she is doing her run and this is the second of four landings and I have not asked her and she has not offered.**

"A count is held by a person and not by a box. **Sabra Trewin, and she is holding it in her head, and the count is minutes and there are nineteen children at Cobb Lock who are the only roll of those children that exists, and she has told me she is not writing this one down either, and that is hers and not mine.**

"The time is stated before I go. **In at twenty past two. Out by five past three. Forty-two minutes, and I am saying it now so that if I am not out at five past three there are six people who know where I went and what time I said.**

"And the clinic in the morning. **Ola Petrossian, on this bank since about half past ten, unasked, and she has a bag and she has not looked at my hand.**"

Garn Ollerton said, "That is four conditions and you have said them to a drain."

"They are four conditions and I have said them to a drain, and they have been said to a gang and a counter and a yard since October, and the drain is not going to mind."

---

**In at 14.20. Out at 15.02. Forty-two minutes inside, from the old ford, on a Thursday afternoon in the fourth week of June, in a dry week, in a district entered as nil.**

**It was not a street.**

He has been in and out nine times and every one of them was a place with a hard edge on it and something at the end of it, and this had no edge, and the far side of it was not a far side, it was more of the same.

**The ford was dry.** That is the first thing. There has been no water over this ford since about 1919 and there is a stone on the far side with 1919 cut into it and the ford is bone dry and hard, and it is not dry because of the week. **The water is up.** It was up to a mark on the left-hand parapet, and the mark was about two feet below the top of the parapet, and the parapet is four feet above the ground, and the mark is a line of tidemark and it is a line of somebody having stood there and looked at it, and there were three of them and they were in a row and they were not the same age, and the top one was newer than the bottom one by about nine years.

Then the ground went on being the same ground, and the water went on being where the water was, and the two of them were together.

**And the smell was in there and it was not a smell in a place. It was the fen in a dry week and it was about four times what it was outside, and he has lost a sound and a taste and this is neither of those, and it came from underneath the whole length of it rather than from anywhere, and he wrote nothing down inside because he has never written anything down inside, and the reason he has never written anything down inside is that he could not see his own hand.**

**And at about four hundred yards along it there was a post with a bell on it.**

It is a school bell. It is about five inches across the mouth and it is on a post of about seven feet and there is no school attached to it and there is no school anywhere near it. It is a hand bell on a post and the post has nineteen marks cut into it, low down, in a row, and somebody has cut them fresh, because two of them were still pale on the Wednesday when she said so.

**And it had been rung nine times.**

Not while he was there. He stood under it for about eleven minutes with his watch out and it did not ring, and he wrote the times in his own head the way he has written nine times in a yard in April, and then he made himself write them in the working book afterwards and he wrote all nine and he could not put a ring on any of them.

**And he did not ring it, and he did not touch the post, and he did not go and look at the marks from underneath, and there was nothing else in the whole of those forty-two minutes, and he came out.**

---

**And on the bank at five past three there were six people and a woman of twenty-three with a bag, and Garn Ollerton said nine words.**

"You are two minutes over."

"I am two minutes over."

"That is not the point. **The point is you said five past three and you came out at five past three and I have been sitting here since twenty past two doing a job I do every Thursday, and you did not ask me to and I would have said yes and I am glad you did not, and those are two different things and I am not going to mix them up in a yard.**"

"Twenty minutes' worth is a pair," said Sabra Trewin, from about four feet away, "**and I did not write it down, and I am not going to, and there are nineteen marks on that post and I put four of them in on the Wednesday and I am not going to tell a man in a coat which four.**"

---

**And the sound went on the Wednesday morning of the twenty-fourth of June at about ten past eleven at the water clinic at the water steps, and it was gone for about four seconds and it was there again and it did not come back.**

No face. No place. No person. No name. **He decided nothing about that, in the clinic, and he decided nothing about it on the lorry, and he has decided nothing about it since the first week of January and he is not going to decide anything about it in a volume where it happens four times.**

He wrote the time down at the clinic counter, in his own hand, and Dr Sen did not ask him to describe it and did not ask him to find it and said the thing she said in June, again, for the second time, which is that a routine about a sound is the fourth thing.

---

**And in the middle of it, once, in about nine words, on the bank at about half past two, in his own book, given to nobody and to be given to nobody:**

> *A dry week is a promise about water.*

**He wrote it down and did not act on it and did not know what it was about and it is the eighth time anything like it has appeared in a book in seven months and it is not going to be handed to a woman of sixty-one at a counter, and it is not going to be said in a yard, and there are six people on a bank and none of them has it.**

---

**The Field Book, Thursday the twenty-fifth of June, 23.20, a room in Cobb Lock above a shop, and there is an entry in this book on this night and there has not been one since the eighteenth of June, and the interval is seven days, and seven days is a pair.**

*Twenty-fifth, 23.20. Seven days off the eighteenth.*

*One. **The list on the facing page reads twenty-eight.** Two sixes, seven threes, one nine, four twos, three fives, one eight, one twenty-five, THREE sevens, six fours. It read twenty-seven on the eighteenth of June and it read twenty-seven on the twenty-fifth of April and it had not moved in seventy-seven days and it moved tonight, and it moved because I was asleep for six nights in a place with no address with the book in the bottom of a bag and I did not open it once, and **a pair is a fact about where I was and not about what happened, and I have known that since the twenty-sixth of April and I have had it printed in here since the second of June and it is the first time I have had one that was about me moving instead of about me not moving, and I do not like it, and it is true, and I am not going to rub it out.** I did not work the interval out on the Friday and I did not work it out on the Saturday and I did not work it out on the Sunday night or the Monday or the Tuesday, and the Friday was a decision and the other five were not, and the decision was for the wrong reason, which is that I was not going to sit down and write a book entry about a piece of paper.

*Two. **Ten remembered routes. Eleven.** I have said the eleven out loud to nobody. The original eight are intact and none has been removed and all ten of the old ones are marked by somebody who is not me, and the tenth is the one from the seventh of December and the eleventh is this afternoon, and the ninth one's failure to cost anything is still unexplained and is not going to be explained before the end of July. **Every crossing cost one route. Nine times, and one of them did not, and I have written that sentence down in this book four times this year and I have not got a word more on it than I had in January.** The tally has ten lines in it. The paper is Hettie Brann's and the pocket is Dr Sen's and I have not looked at it since the thirteenth of January and I am not going to look at it in July.

*Three. **The seam, and I am writing down what it was and not what it was for.** The old ford at Mireward, in at 14.20 and out at 15.02, forty-two minutes. Four conditions said out loud to a drain. Garn Ollerton walked me in and walked me out and is not me. Sabra Trewin held the count in her head and is not a box. The time was stated before I went. The clinic in the morning was a woman of twenty-three on a bank from half past ten, unasked, on her day off, paid by a clinic with no record of where she went. **It was not a street. It had no edge. The far side was more of the same.** The ford has been dry since 1919 and the water was up to a mark two feet under the parapet and the mark was a tidemark and there were three of them and the top one is about nine years newer than the bottom one. The smell was about four times what it was outside and it came from underneath the whole length of it and I have lost a sound and a taste and this was neither of them and I have written three times in a working book now and no explanation and I am not going to look for one. **A post with a school bell on it, five inches across the mouth, seven feet up, and nineteen marks cut into it low down in a row, and two of the nineteen were still pale on the Wednesday. It had been rung nine times and not while I was there and I stood under it for about eleven minutes with my watch out and it did not ring, and I wrote all nine times down afterwards and I could not put a ring on any of them. I did not touch the post. I did not go and look at the marks from underneath. There was nothing else in forty-two minutes and I came out.** And that is the whole of it and I have got no demand and no service and no failure and I have been in and out nine times and this is the first one I have come out of with less than I went in with.

*Four. **And the two halves of the missing record, which I am not going to join.** Percy writes down what he finds once a year and nobody has ever read it. Coll Sarrowe holds the water down every day of his life and there is no paper in this city with his name on it. Neither of them has been asked about the other. A village of a hundred and forty people and nineteen children and a schoolroom has been off every list since about 1911 and has never been on one, and **that is not a place that has been forgotten, because a place that has been forgotten was on something and came off it, and this was never on anything, and the second of those is easier to leave alone and much harder to put right, and nobody did this to them and there is no meeting at which anybody decided it.** And a forecast of weather-driven seam events was issued for a season in which a district with no roll and no recorded loss was entered as nil on the ground that there is no resident witness, and I have had that ground in this book since February and I have never turned it over, and it turns over like this: **a resident witness is a person on a list, and there has not been a person on a list in that district since about 1911, so the district has entered as containing no persons, and it has been wrong about a place for sixty years without once being wrong about a fact.** And the smell is the only instrument in this whole business that a person produces and cannot produce on demand, and I have got no form for it, and I am not going to build one, and that is not a resolution.

*Five. **And what has not moved.** The page home the two hundred and fourteenth day, blank, four marks in four hands and none of them mine, and I am not writing that it is there. The token cold, the two hundredth day, in the inside pocket of this coat, on a bank, at a ford, at half past two in the afternoon. Ten routes and ten crossings now. **The order is suspended and is not discharged and is not varied and is not withdrawn, and the public name is not on the street name index, and the removed sentence cannot be restored and a desk has been asked twice and has not answered twice, and the matter stands over and is not adjourned and has no date and it is not on anything and the twenty-fourth of June came and went on a Wednesday and nobody in eleven streets stood in the weather and there is no column in this city for a date that passes.** The round is one man short. The plate is eighty days in the grass and is never reported. The condition column is empty for the twenty-fourth walk. **I have not told Mara Quill about the season for eleven weeks and a date is a column and I have not asked her about the seventh of January since the fifth of May and I am not going to in July, and a woman of sixty-eight on nothing is not named in this entry and the count stands at eight and I am adding nothing to it in here.** And the eighth seventh column is in the bottom of this bag face down with a hundred and ten words in it that I gave to a ferry pilot and she said no to and then told me who to ask, and I asked, and he said no in four seconds, and the ninth resolution was a person, and it did not work.

*And the standing position, which is the last thing in this book and which does not improve because a volume has started: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.*
