# Chapter 185: A Sheet In A Coat, And A Woman Of Forty-Six Who Looked At It, And A Man Who Cannot Be Asked

**The round went out at about ten past eight on Monday the sixteenth of November and came in at about four with the bag empty, and it is a Monday and a Thursday round and it has been since the second of January and it is one man short, and the bag has been empty since the twenty-eighth of August.**

The page home is the three hundred and fifty-eighth day. The token is cold on the three hundred and forty-fourth. The plate in the grass at the low end of Bridge Street is two hundred and twenty-four days old and has been told to nobody. The condition column is two hundred and seventy-seven days and is not there. **A foreman of fifty-four is twenty-nine weeks and six days past four months and nobody is going to ask him.**

---

## What he had done on Saturday, and why, and the shape of it

He had done it on the Saturday morning at about eleven with a pencil and about forty minutes, and he had done it because he was frightened, and **the fear is the whole of the history of this book and it has never once improved and it is never going to be a reason for anything except a sheet of paper in a coat.**

What he had written was the route of this district's paper since the middle of January.

*Out of a room with four machines on a Wednesday morning, about nine inches by five, four columns and two inches of ruled space at the foot. Into a bundle on a Thursday at about eleven. Into a satchel that went out with the works' papers. Down a gate. Across four hundred yards. Into a handcart. Along a road. Into a firm's yard. Onto a counter. Back into a satchel. Back down four hundred yards. Into a bin.*

Eleven lines. Seven places and four times and no name on any of them.

**And one name, on the eighth line, written smaller than the rest and in the same pencil: a man of about fifty in a room with four machines, who was given a use in September and did not know the purpose of it, and who had a card of his own come back in a bin on a Friday in September and had not rung anybody, and who has not been asked anything since by anybody including me.**

He had written the sheet on the Saturday and had put it in the inside pocket of the coat, and **he had read it on the Sunday and had not torn it up, and on the Monday morning he carried it upstairs, and the reason he carried it upstairs is the reason a man carries a thing upstairs.**

---

## Four flights, and a counter, and a woman of forty-six

The quay section office is four rooms above a fitter's shop and there is a rack in the corridor outside it, open at the front, about four feet high, with nothing in it in it, and a mark on the shelf about nine inches from the left edge and about two inches wide that has not changed. **Nell Crale is forty-six and she is the works clerk and she has been on that counter for about nine years and there is a rack outside her door and she is not the man of about forty-one with a handcart and she is not the man of about fifty with the four machines and she is not on any list of anything either.**

He put the sheet on the counter, flat, and did not give it to her, and he said:

"**Look at that and tell me if I have it wrong.**"

**That is the whole of the failure and it took about four seconds and he had the sentence ready since the Saturday and he knew while he was saying it, and he has written down that he knew, and a man who writes down that he knew is not the same as a man who did not know and the writing does not make it the first one.**

She looked at it. She did not touch it. She read it the way she reads everything on that counter, which is quickly, and once.

"You have got the satchel wrong."

"I have not."

"**You have. The satchel did not come back on the Friday. It came back on the Monday, and it came back empty, and it went out again on the Thursday and it has not come back since the twenty-eighth of August.**" She put a hand flat on the counter about four inches from the edge of the sheet. "**Everything else on that is right and I have watched all of it.**

"And the yard."

"**The yard is a yard. It is not a place anybody can post to. Nobody audits a firm's yard. I have been trying to get a card to a man at that yard for about two months and I could tell you what that is like and I am not going to.**"

Then she stopped, and looked at the sheet for about four seconds longer, not at the places.

"**You have got a man at the end of that.**"

"Yes."

"**Take it out of the book.**"

He did not take it out of the book.

"**Then leave it out of the book, and do not tell me what it is for, and I will not ask you, and neither of us is going to write anything down about it now, and if you bring that sheet up here again I am going to put it in that bin, and that bin is a bin and it is not a file and it has already had one card in it this year.**"

He put the sheet back in the inside pocket of the coat. **He did not tear it up and she did not ask him to and she would have been right to ask him to and she did not, and he has written down that she was right to ask him to and did not, and that is twice in three days that a woman has been right about a document and not said so.**

---

## The wrong-shaped thing, and it is the worst-shaped one he owns and it is the oldest

He got the sheet out at about half past eleven in the lane and looked at it in the flat grey light and it took him about four minutes to be able to say what was wrong with it, and then he said it out loud to nobody, in about eleven words, and the eleven words are the finding and he wrote them down that night and he is not going to improve them.

**A route is not a sequence. A route is a thing you walk. A route does not have a first thing and a second thing.**

**A sheet with places and times on it is not a route. It is an order, and an order has a first thing and a second thing, and a thing at the end of an order is a thing that was got to, and this man was not got to, he was in a room and a card came back in a bin, and the difference between those two is the whole of what I have been doing since the sixth of April.**

And the second half, which is worse, and which he wrote in his own hand:

*A route that cannot be produced cannot be asked after, and that is the only protection any route in this city has ever had. I produced one. It is in an inside pocket, on a Saturday, with a pencil, and there is no form in this city for taking a produced route back out of production, and I am not going to build one, and I did not build one when I was given it in the spring and I am not building one now. **And the man at the end of it cannot be asked. He cannot be asked whether it was all right, and the only way to ask a man whether you should have written something down about him is to come back and ask him, and coming back is the second ask, and the first one was made on the twenty-first of September by a man of thirty-one who gave him a use and did not ask him whether he wanted it, and he was right to refuse the second and I am not going to put it to him a third.***

---

## And the three doors, and the one that was on a top track, and none of them moved on the Monday

**He did not go up the top track.** It was Monday and he was in eleven streets and the track is about four miles the other way and there is a man of sixty-one at the top of it with a jar on a table and nineteen years of dates in his head, and Milo Rook went up that track on the thirteenth of October with a sentence he had been carrying since the Friday before, and the only way to ask that man whether it was all right is to go again, and going again is the thing that was asked not to be done.

**He did not ask the woman of thirty-four a second thing.** He asked her one on Tuesday the third and it was two questions wearing one coat and she told him so, and there is no instrument in this city for converting a decision back into a question and he has not gone looking for one and he is not going to build one.

**He did not ask the woman of fifty-four at the ninth desk on the left a second question.** She told him on the twenty-ninth of September that a second question would get the same answer, and a letter would make her a correspondent of this district, and a correspondent of this district is a party, and a party is a person.

**And he has not put a name in a box in this district in eight months and he did not put one in a box today, and the reason he is telling you on a Monday in November rather than in a book is that a sheet of paper in an inside pocket is a name in a box that has not got a box round it yet, and that is worse, and there is no column for the difference.**

---

## The rest of the day, and a lane, and about four inches of difference

At about half past two he was at the end of the lane again, because the round goes past it, and there was about four inches of standing water in the bottom forty yards of it where there had been about nine inches on Tuesday morning, and it had not gone anywhere and it had not gone down; it had spread, and there was now a line of dead grass along about eighty yards of it, and a man of about sixty with a shed was at the end of the lane and did not look up.

**Milo Rook did not stop. He stood about four feet off for about two minutes and then went on, and he did not ask him anything, and this is the fourth time in seven days.**

At about ten past four he came in and washed his hands and did not write anything for an hour, and then he wrote a Field Book entry, and the last thing in it is the standing sentence, and it stands.

Nobody thanked anybody. **A woman of forty-six looked at a sheet of paper she was not asked about and told a man of thirty-one what was wrong with it and did not touch it, and she is not thanked, and she told him she would not be thanked in advance in effect, and she was right.**

---

*Field Book, Monday the sixteenth of November, 23.45, a front room in eleven streets, one day off the fifteenth.*

*One. **I put a route on a sheet and I carried it up four flights and asked a woman of forty-six to look at it, and I had the sentence ready on Saturday, and I knew while I was saying it, and that is now three times in eight months that I have arrived somewhere with the words already in the order they are going to be said in.** The first cost a boy of seventeen four seconds in a yard in front of about nine people. The second cost a man of sixty-one the only thing he has got, which is that nobody can produce him. This one cost a man of about fifty a thing I cannot give back, and I do not know that it cost him anything at all, and I am not going to find out, and I have written that down as the reason and it is not the reason. **The reason is that I was frightened on Saturday and a sheet of paper is the only thing I have ever been able to do about being frightened.**

*Two. **THE FINDING, AND IT IS THE ELEVEN WORDS AND I AM NOT IMPROVING THEM: a route is not a sequence. A route is a thing you walk and it does not have a first thing and a second thing. A sheet with places and times on it is not a route, it is an order, and an order has a first thing and a second thing, and a thing at the end of an order is a thing that was got to, and he was not got to.** The man at the end of that line is about fifty and is in a room with four machines and was given a use in September and did not know the purpose of it, and his own card came back in a bin on a Friday in September, and he has not been asked anything since by anybody including me. **A route that cannot be produced cannot be asked after, and I produced one on a Saturday in about forty minutes and there is no form anywhere for taking a produced route back out of production, and I am not going to build one, and he cannot be asked whether that was all right because the only way to ask is to come back and the coming back is the second ask.**

*Three. **Three doors and a track and none of them moved.** I did not go up the top track and I cannot ask the man of sixty-one whether the thirteenth of October was all right and there is no form for that. I did not ask the woman of thirty-four a second thing and there is no instrument in this city for converting a decision back into a question. I did not ask the woman of fifty-four a second question and I have not written to her. **A sheet of paper in an inside pocket is a name in a box that has not got a box round it yet and that is worse than a name in a box, and there is no column for the difference and I have not drawn one and I did not draw a fifth column and I did not draw a sixth.**

*Four. **Two women were right about a document this week and neither of them was thanked and one of them told me not to and one of them told me not to in advance by saying she was not going to be told anything, and they are right twice each and I am not going to make a list of it.** A woman of sixty-one will not let a counter be a post. A woman of forty-six will not let a bin be a file. **And a bin is a bin and a file is a file and a sheet in a coat is a sheet in a coat and the only one of the three that is a party is the one I did.**

*Five. **About four inches of water in a lane, spread, with about eighty yards of dead grass along it.** It has not gone down. A man of about sixty was at the end of it and I stood four feet off for about two minutes and I did not ask him anything, and that is four times in seven days, and I have not started a count of it and I am not going to and if I find myself keeping one I shall stop keeping it.

*Six. **Nothing has moved.** Eleven remembered routes, ten crossings, the tally ten lines, not looked at since the thirteenth of January. The page home the three hundred and fifty-eighth day, blank, four marks in four hands, none of them mine. The token cold, the three hundred and forty-fourth day, and it has done nothing since the seventh of December. **The list on the facing page reads twenty-nine and it has not moved in one hundred and twenty-three days, and an interval of one day is not a pair, and a pair is a fact about where I was and not about what happened, and if the count of pairs in this month is small and not large that is correct.** The condition column is two hundred and seventy-seven days and is not there and the reason column is not there and no new figure is to be printed for either. The second tin is at thirty-seven and there is no thirty-eight. The plate is two hundred and twenty-four days in the grass and has been told to nobody. A foreman of fifty-four is twenty-nine weeks and six days past four months and nobody is going to ask him.

*And the standing position: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.**
