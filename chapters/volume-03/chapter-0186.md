# Chapter 186: Thirty-Four Steps, And A Woman Who Is Not Going To Come Here, And A Coat That Is The Wrong Shape

**The flatbed came in from the city on Friday the twentieth of November at about a quarter to seven with the week's post and the sacks on the bed, and it went back on the afternoon and was in the yard at the city end at about half past four, and it does not wait either way, and he was not on it, and being in a place is not a day off, and a bus goes past the end of this street every twenty minutes on a weekday and he has not used one since the second of January and he has used one twice this month.**

The page home is the three hundred and sixty-second day. The token is cold on the three hundred and forty-eighth. The plate in the grass at the low end of Bridge Street is two hundred and twenty-eight days old and has been told to nobody. The condition column is two hundred and eighty-one days and is not there. **A foreman of fifty-four is thirty weeks and three days past four months and nobody is going to ask him.**

---

## Four hundred yards to where the bus stops, and about forty minutes, and a coat

He walked the four hundred yards at about half past nine and got on at about ten past ten and was in the city at about ten to eleven, and **the bus is not a day off and its timetable is a document he does not have, and he did not look at one at the stop and he did not ask the driver anything.**

**A coat is a coat.** A coat is for a person and it is the only thing a man in this city has ever used as a repository, and he has used it for one in September, and he used it on Monday for a route in eleven lines with a man's name at the end of it, and **he worked that out on the bus in about four minutes and it is the wrong shape of the oldest thing he owns and it is this: a coat cannot be asked after, and that is the only reason anything survives in it, and it is the only reason anything in it can be produced, and the two of those are the same property and they cut in opposite directions, and there is no instrument anywhere in this city that can take a thing out of a coat and put it back where it cannot be asked for.**

He did not take the sheet out. It is in the inside pocket and it is going to be in the inside pocket on Monday and it is not going to be posted and it is not going to be a file and it is not going to be a record and it is not going to be anything, and that is the whole of what is going to happen to it.

---

## Thirty-four steps, nine desks on the left, a plan chest, a window that does not open

**Bettina Sallow is fifty-four and she is on the hand index at the ninth desk and has been for four years, and she is not in eleven streets, and she has never been south of that bridge, and she is not going to be.**

He had been in the room four times since the seventeenth of December and told her nothing and been told nothing. **She told him on the twenty-ninth of September that if he asked her a second time she would give him the same answer, and he has not asked her a second time and has not written to her, and the reason he has not written to her is on the page: a letter would make her a correspondent of this district, and a correspondent of this district is a party, and a party is a person, and a woman of thirty-one went through a register of correspondents standing up in about nine minutes in August and entered nothing and she was right.**

**A hand index is a card for one named thing, and the named things in that room are rateable things with a street on them. About nine hundred drawers of street numbers from 1911, and a plan chest, and a window that does not open, and a chair under it.**

He stood at the ninth desk from about ten past eleven until about twenty past and he did not ask her anything.

**And there is a drawer about nine feet away that has a floor of street numbers in it, and a bundle on the bottom shelf that has not been opened in four years, and about nine finding aids that nobody will ever find, and he looked at the drawer once, for about two seconds, and did not go near it, and did not open it, and did not put his hand on it.**

---

## What she said, which was two sentences, and neither of them was an answer

She worked for about nine minutes and then she said, without turning round:

"**You are not going to ask me.**"

"No."

"**Then don't stand at my desk.**" She went back to the card she was on. "**I have said the same thing twice and you have not asked me a third time and I want you to know that I have noticed that twice, and I have noticed it both times, and I am not going to thank you for it and I would rather you went down the stairs.**"

**And that is everything that was said in that room in about ten minutes by either of them, and he has written the whole exchange down in about four lines and the four lines are the best four lines he has written this year and he is not going to improve them and he is not going to do anything with them at all.**

---

## And the recovery, which is eight weeks old, and is hers, and is not a word

He came down the thirty-four steps and stood at the bottom of them in the street for about nine minutes.

**Here is what he got, and it is this, and it took him about nine minutes on a pavement in the city to be able to put it anywhere:**

*For eight months I have been trying to get things out of people. A roll in a woman's head. A depth of water on a bank. A landing that is not a rateable thing. A yardage a man of sixty-one will not say. A length of frontage that eleven streets cannot produce. A plate off a fence. A name in a box. **And the only thing in this district that has ever held anything is a refusal.***

*A woman of thirty-four has kept a schoolroom off paper for nine years and has said no to four people this year and answered a question she was badly asked anyway. A woman of thirty-seven wrote one word in pencil in a box and put a date in the date box that I did not read. A man of sixty-one has a jar with thirty-one years of wages in it and said that if he says a yardage to a man in a good coat then in about nine years there is a man in a room who can produce him. A woman of sixty-one will not let a counter be a post. A woman of forty-six will not let a bin be a file. A man of fifty with a mower has said a thing to me three times and has said nothing at all the third time. **And a woman of fifty-four at the ninth desk on the left said the same thing twice and I did not ask a third time, and that is the whole of what I have got, and it is eight weeks old, and it is hers, and it is not a word, and she is not going to come to eleven streets, and I am not going to thank her, and I am not going to ask her anything else.***

*The method has changed and the method is this, and it is not mine, and I did not go and get it, and I am not going to put it in a column: **I have stopped asking and I have started being refused, and a refusal is the only instrument in this city that cannot be asked after.** Everything I have wanted in eight months is producible and everything that has held anything is a person saying no.*

---

## And the thing that is permanent, and it is not a reward and it is not a punishment

**He did not find out.**

That is the sentence. He went up thirty-four steps to find out what is in a drawer of street numbers from 1911, and he did not find out, and he is not going to, and there is nobody he is allowed to ask, and **in about four years somebody may go and get it out and put it back and that village may be a place with a number inside a fortnight, and it will not be because of anything I did on any day, and it will not be because of anything I did not do on the twentieth of November either.**

*Going there might have saved that village and going there might have put a number on it in every column of every schedule in this city for ever. I do not know which. I have had one Tuesday and I have spent it eleven miles the other way in a schoolroom and I came up these stairs this morning to see whether I was allowed to know, and I was not, and that is the correct answer, and it is the permanent one. **There is no instrument in this city for finding out whether the right errand was the one you did not do, and I am not going to build one, and nobody is going to be told what I gave up today and what I did not get, including me, in a book that goes into a coat.***

He got on a bus at about ten past one and was at the end of the street at about half past two and did the rest of the day in eleven streets.

**Nobody thanked anybody. The flatbed went back at ten to four and he was not on it. The bus came every twenty minutes and he got on one twice and neither of them was a day off. A bus is a vehicle and a ladder is a ladder and thirty-four steps are thirty-four steps and none of the three of them was built for what I used it for and I am not going to improve that into anything.**

---

*Field Book, Friday the twentieth of November, 23.30, a front room in eleven streets, one day off the nineteenth.*

*One. **I did not ask her a second time. That is all I did in that room in about ten minutes and it is the second time I have not done it and the first time was the twenty-ninth of September and she told me the consequence of doing it and I have now not done it twice and she noticed both times and thanked me for neither.** I did not open the drawer. I did not go near it. I did not write to her and I am not going to. She is not going to come to eleven streets and I am not going to ask her anything else this year, and nobody is going to thank her, and I am not going to either, and a person who has thanked somebody is a person who has asked.

*Two. **THE RECOVERY IS SOMEBODY ELSE'S WORK AND IT IS EIGHT WEEKS OLD AND NEITHER OF US SAID A WORD ABOUT IT.** For eight months I have been trying to get things out of people and the only thing in this district that has ever held anything is a refusal. A roll in a head. A word in pencil in a box. A jar on a table with thirty-one years of wages in it. A counter that is not a post. A bin that is not a file. A man who says nothing the third time. And a woman of fifty-four who said the same thing twice and was not asked a third time. **I have stopped asking and I have started being refused and a refusal is the only instrument in this city that cannot be asked after.** That is not a resolution and it is not a rule and I am not going to draw a column for it, and there is no facing page for it, and the sixth of his own is still the sixth of his own and the six of the other shape are still six and I am not putting this on either of them and I am not making a third.

*Three. **I did not find out and I am not going to.** Whether that floor of street numbers covers the fen country is a thing I will not know, and in about four years somebody may go and get it out and put it back and that village may be a place with a number inside a fortnight, and it will not be because of what I did on any day and it will not be because of what I did not do this morning. **There is no instrument in this city for finding out whether the errand you did was the right one and I am not going to build one and nobody is going to be told what I gave up today and what I did not get, including me.**

*Four. **A coat is the wrong shape of the oldest thing I own.** A coat cannot be asked after and that is the only reason anything survives in it and the only reason anything in it can be produced, and those are one property and it cuts both ways, and there is no instrument anywhere that can take a thing out of a coat and put it back where it cannot be asked for. There is a sheet in the inside pocket with eleven lines on it and one name at the end of it and it is not going to be posted and it is not going to be a file and it is not going to be a record and it is not going to be anything, and that is the whole of what is going to happen to it, and I have written that down on a Friday night in November and I am not going to write it down again.

*Five. **Nothing has moved.** Eleven remembered routes, ten crossings, the tally ten lines, not looked at since the thirteenth of January. The page home the three hundred and sixty-second day, blank, four marks in four hands, none of them mine. The token cold, the three hundred and forty-eighth day, and it has done nothing since the seventh of December. **The list on the facing page reads twenty-nine and it has not moved in one hundred and twenty-seven days, and an interval of one day is not a pair, and a pair is a fact about where I was and not about what happened, and if the count of pairs in this month is small and not large that is correct.** The condition column is two hundred and eighty-one days and is not there and the reason column is not there and no new figure is to be printed for either. The second tin is at thirty-seven and there is no thirty-eight. The plate is two hundred and twenty-eight days in the grass and has been told to nobody. A foreman of fifty-four is thirty weeks and three days past four months and nobody is going to ask him. **The four unshowable things in five months are four and there has been no fifth and I am not counting them and I am not going to start, and nobody has stood in the weather on a Wednesday in November and I am not going to print a total of anything.**

*And the standing position: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.**
