# Chapter 162: A Return About A Person Who Holds Water, Sent To A Firm

**There is no bus and there has not been one since about 1961, and there are two motor vehicles a week in Mireward, and both of them are on Fridays or Thursdays. He came out on Tuesday the fifteenth of September in a van that belongs to a firm, and he was not driven by anybody he could name afterwards, and he was in Mireward at about ten and in the city at about half past three.**

The man of about fifty with the mower had the van. It is a white panel van with a rack on the back for mowers and a company name on the side in a colour that has gone the colour of the weather, and the mower man had it outside the gate at ten to eight in the morning and did not ask him whether he wanted to come.

"You are going to the top track."

"I am going to the top track."

"**It is not a day off for you and I am not going to pretend it is.**" He put the rack in. "**Get in the front. I have got to be back at the yard before the light goes and I am not doing this twice.**"

Nobody thanked anybody. It is a van and it is a Tuesday and the driver had four stops and two of them were on the way.

---

## What a bar is for

They were on the top track at about ten past ten and the ground was soft because it had rained on the Sunday, and Coll Sarrowe was working the bar and there were three men with him and the fourth was away at his father's.

He is forty-four. He has held the water down on this bank for thirty-one years, and the sluice at the top of the track belongs to a body that was abolished in about 1968, and the water authority cannot bill him for the water he is holding and cannot employ him to hold it and cannot write a letter to him that would arrive, and he is paid for the Tuesday out of a farm wage by the hour for ditching, and **the farm pays him for the Tuesday and the Tuesday is not a ditch and the farm does not know that either.**

There were three of them and not five, and Milo Rook stood at the top of the bank for about fifty minutes and understood none of it and worked out all of it, the way he worked out the five of them in July, and the difference is that this time he was not surprised by any part of it and that is the whole of what nine weeks does to a man.

**And the order had changed, and Coll Sarrowe had not mentioned it, and it took him about a fortnight to work out and get it right.**

In July the run was set left to right with the youngest at the wrong end and the foreman — the wrong word; there is no foreman, there is a man of forty-four who has been getting four men ready for nine years and has never once told them he was getting them ready — walking the boy up and down the ninety feet four times by the arm without a word before putting him on the other end and going back to the bar.

On the fifteenth of September the three were set **facing out** and not facing along, and the near man took the short throw and the far man took the long one, and the man who had been on the wrong end in July was on the near end and had been on the near end for about a fortnight and had not been told that he had been moved.

"He put you on that end on purpose."

"**He did not put me on this end. There is no end. There is a run of ninety feet and the water goes where the water goes and I take the short one because I am quicker at the short one and I have been quicker at the short one since I was sixteen.**" The man straightened up. "**I have never asked him why I take the short one and I am not going to. If you have been switched round since July then you have been switched round since July and there is nobody in this district who is going to tell you why and there is nobody in this district who wants to know.**"

Milo Rook did not ask.

---

## The printed side, and the ordinary refusal

It came out of a bag at about half past eleven and the man of forty-four had it in his hand before it was offered, and he did not look at it, and he said:

"**That is the second one.**"

It was a single side, printed on one, and it had come to the farm, and the farm has a rateable address and a letterbox and a name on a rate roll, and **a district with no address is not a place anybody writes to, and a man with no address is not a person a form can be sent to, and the way this city gets hold of a man who holds water is that it writes to the firm that pays him for ditching.**

Four columns and a heading.

> **HOLDERS OF DRAINAGE APPARATUS — RETURN OF CONDITION**
> *To be completed by the person responsible for the apparatus named. State the depth of water held at the time of inspection, in feet. Where no inspection has been carried out, state nil.*

And at the foot, in the same small type, the sentence that turned it into a document:

> *A return not received within the period stated will be completed by this office.*

---

**And here is the whole of the refusal, and it took him about four seconds, and it is the most ordinary thing that has happened to Milo Rook in a month.**

He read it. He turned it over. He put it in his pocket.

"What are you going to do with it."

"**Post it back.**"

"There is a figure on it. You could put four in that box and it would be true."

"**It would be true on the day.**" He did not look up from the bank. "**That is the whole of what is wrong with it and there is a form for that in about four places in this city. A depth of water is true on a day. If I put a number in that box then in about four years a man in a room can come and ask me what the number was, and I will have to say a day, and there is no column anywhere for the day, and he will go away and write down that the return was completed by the office, and it will not be wrong, and that is the part of it that matters.**"

---

"And it is not yours to give them."

"**It is not mine to give them because I am not a party to it. That is the answer and it is a good one and it has been the answer for thirty-one years and nobody has ever printed it.**" He straightened up and put the bar in the ground and left it there, which is what he does. "**A named ditch keeper is a ditch keeper that can be got rid of. There have been four of us named in this district in thirty-one years and three of them left inside two years and the one who is still here is the one who is not.**"

"Will you fill in nil."

"**No.**"

"Why not. Nil is a figure. It is the safest figure there is."

"**Nil is a statement that somebody inspected it and found nothing, and nobody has inspected it, and if I put nil in that box then this office has got a return saying that a drain on a bank that nobody maintains has been looked at.**" He got the bar back up. "**They will not send another one. That is what I am frightened of, and it is not a fear, it is a Tuesday.**"

He posted it back on the Friday afternoon from the shop at the north end, in an envelope that belonged to somebody else, and he did not use the farm's franking and nobody at the farm knew and nobody was told and Milo Rook was not told either until the following Tuesday.

**Nobody thanked him for any part of it, and there is no sentence in a book about it, and there is a man of forty-four who has refused a figure once and it is another time in eight months that somebody in this district has refused me a figure, and I have not asked for one of them and I have written that down more times than I am going to keep counting.**

---

## The rest of the day

He was back in the city at about half past three and the page home is the two hundred and ninety-sixth day and the token is cold on the two hundred and eighty-second and the plate is a hundred and sixty-two days in the grass and the condition column is two hundred and fifteen days and is not there. **A foreman of fifty-four is twenty weeks and six days past four months and he is not in this block and nobody is going to ask him.**

He had not got a figure. He had not got a name, and he had not got a date, and he had not got a form, and he had not got a statement, and the four that he has were not written down.

**The two of the other shape are still on the bench where he left them on the twenty-fourth of July and the fourteenth of August and the thirty-first of August, and he has not added a third, and he is not counting, and he is not going to start in September because it is the middle of the month and that is the worst possible reason to stop and he has written that down too.**

*Field Book, Tuesday the fifteenth of September, 23.15, a front room in eleven streets, one day off the fourteenth.*

*One. **Four bells in four places is four bells in four places, and it is not a form, and it is not a place, and it is not a day, and the fifth thing is the one that would turn it into a row, and the fifth thing is somebody writing down the four, and that is the last thing that has to happen in this district. I have not written them down and I am not going to, and there is no column on this page for them and there is not going to be one.** A man of forty-four has now refused a figure on a printed side, and the refusal is correct, and it changed nothing, and he posted it back on the Friday in somebody else's envelope, and I have got nothing.*

*Two. **A return about a person who holds water, sent to a firm, because a man with no address is not a person a form can be sent to. That is the third time in eight months that this city has reached a man in this district by going to a document it could reach** — and the first two produced a fifth column that was not asked for and a typed name in a box on a printed side, and this one produced nothing at all, and I have to write down that I do not know which of those I wanted. **A return not received within the period stated will be completed by this office. That sentence is printed at the foot of a form about drainage apparatus and it is the same sentence that is printed at the foot of a form about a district, and the two forms were not written for each other, and the office that completes the row does not have to know which form it is holding.** Nobody lied. I have written that sentence four times in eight months and this is the fourth time and I have improved it none of them.*

*Three. **The run on the bank is set facing out and not facing along and the near man takes the short throw and nobody arranged that, and the man who has been getting four men ready for nine years has not said one word about it.** A changed method that is not a word. I have been helped twice now on a Tuesday morning by a man of forty-four and I have got no sentence for either of them and I am not going to get one, and I am not going to do the thing I do, which is sit down and write down what a man of forty-four has arranged.*

*Four. **Nothing has moved. Eleven remembered routes, ten crossings, ten tally lines, not looked at since the thirteenth of January.** The page home the two hundred and ninety-sixth day, blank, four marks in four hands, none of them mine. The token cold, the two hundred and eighty-second day. **The list on the facing page reads twenty-nine and it has not moved in sixty-one days and the reason is the same reason and it is a pair is a fact about where I was and not about what happened, and an interval of one day is not a pair.** A foreman of fifty-four is twenty weeks and six days past four months and nobody is going to ask him and that silence is in my handwriting on a page I cannot get it out of.*

*And the standing position: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.**
