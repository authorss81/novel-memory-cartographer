# Chapter 140: No Seventh Column Before The First Of September

It was an ordinary Wednesday, the first of July, and that is the whole of what happened on it and it is worth about four hundred words, and the four hundred words are the machinery of a city that has not been told to stop.

At nine o'clock the allocation sheet came off the machine. Five rows, four columns, one line under the words *officer responsible*. **A man of about fifty transferred five figures out of it with a pen, the way he has done every week since the twenty-sixth of January, and the fourth column is still empty and has been filled in three times in nineteen years, and the line under *officer responsible* has never been filled in at all.** The zero in the row for the fen is a zero and has been a zero every week since the twenty-sixth of January and nobody has reduced it and nobody is going to.

The round did not go. **It goes on a Monday and a Thursday and it has gone on a Monday and a Thursday since the second of January, and it is one man short and has been one man short since the twenty-eighth of December, and the second side of the sheet of who does what and on which day has a blank on it for today, and that blank is the thirteenth one since the eighth of April, and the sheet is right about it.**

The sheet is right about it because the only person who stood in the weather in this district today was a man of about sixty at a shed at Cardew Docks, eleven minutes away, who has been there since before the war and who does not know that a piece of paper exists and is not going to be told. **And that is the only reason there, and there is a second reason eleven miles away, and the second reason has been there since the seventeenth of June, and a sheet that is right about a day for two reasons is not more right than a sheet that is right about a day for one.**

The four things in five months are four. A rectangle of standing water on the twenty-ninth of January. A hundred and ten yards of frontage in the rain on the nineteenth of February. A plate in a field on the sixth of April, and about nine people in eleven streets standing in the weather for nothing at all on the fifteenth of April. **It is two and a half months since the fourth of them and there has not been a fifth, and a fifth thing needs somebody to be surprised, and about nine people in this district were not surprised on Wednesday when a man of fifty heard that a district of a hundred and forty people had a card with a sentence on it, and they are not going to be, and that is the correct condition of a district that has no chair.**

**And the three nils about a place entered as nil are three, and he saw all three of them inside four days for the first time, and he is not going to write a fourth, and there is a rule about that somewhere and the rule is not his and it is not a form.**

---

**And at about half past four on the Wednesday afternoon, in a front room, in his own hand, on the facing page, above the list, he wrote one line and then wrote four more under it, and it is the first thing he has ever put on that page that is not a measurement.**

> **No seventh column before the first of September.**
>
> *This is not a resolution. The seventh column is the only thing I am good at and I get to be good at it about four times a year and every time somebody else has to hold the paper. I am stopping it for two months and I am not stopping it because I have learned anything. On the thirty-first of May I wrote at the top of an entry that the not-telling was not a rule, and on the fourth of June I chose a thing about myself that can be produced against me on purpose, and neither of those was a resolution either, and I have three of them in this book now and they are not three habits, they are three places where I put something down and did not do anything. I am writing the fourth one down at half past four on the first of July and I am not going to be able to help it and I have written that sentence before and I am writing it again because I have not written it since the fourth of June. Nobody is to be told about this line, because a line that is on a page is a thing somebody can be sent to look at, and I have been in this district seven months and I have learned that and I have not learned the other thing, and the other thing is that not being sent is not the same as not being sent.*

**And the cost of it, said out loud in the same entry, about nine minutes later, and it is a cost and not a virtue.**

*What stops on the first of September is me, and nothing else stops on the first of September.* Roy Pryce's condition column will be empty for another eight weeks. On about the eleventh of July a man of fifty-four is going to be eight weeks past the four months and he is not going to be asked, and that silence was the yard's and it has now got me in it in my own handwriting, and I cannot get it out of my handwriting. On the third of July a man of about fifty is going to transfer five figures off a sheet with a pen and the zero in the fen row is going to be a zero, and there is no column in this city, in that room, in that building or in this book for the man who could have gone, and I am not going, and I am not going because I have put a line on a page about a thing I am good at, **and that is the most self-serving sentence I have written in seven months and it is dressed up as a discipline and it is not one, and I know exactly what it is, and the knowing has not stopped me writing the line.**

---

**And on Thursday the second of July the gang was on the back length at half past seven and it was the twenty-sixth walk since the first of April with an empty condition column, and the round went out at about ten past eight and came in at about four, and a Friday had not been and a rack day is a Friday, so the sheet for that week was still in a drawer in the quay section office at half past eleven that night.**

The man of about twenty-six in a high-visibility jacket was on the length and did not say anything and did not look at the condition column, which is a thing a man of twenty-six has never once done and has never been asked to say he was not doing. The plate in the grass is eighty-seven days old and nobody has reported it and nobody is going to and the grass on that side is being cut by somebody who is not the works and not the council and is not anybody this district has a name for. **The plate is a decision and not a refusal and he chose it on the first of June and a woman of sixty-three with a hand barrow asked him in a doorway to tell the works, and she was not asking what it was, and he did not tell the works, and the asking did not make it into a refusal, and in about four years this is the only thing anybody in this city can produce about him, and he chose it, and nobody asked him to.**

---

**And in a book in a coat, on the night of the second of July, the volume's first block came to an end, and the conditions were written down in the order he has used for two months, and none of them moved, and there are thirty of them and about fourteen people and none of them has been asked to be on it.**

*Second, 23.50. One day off the first.*

*One. **The list on the facing page reads twenty-eight and it has not moved since Thursday the twenty-fifth of June, and the line above it is four lines long and it is mine, and I have not broken it and I have not broken anything by it.** A pair is a fact about where I was and not about what happened, and I had that printed in this book on the second of June and I had it the wrong way round for fifty-two nights, and the run of fifty-two consecutive nights from the twenty-sixth of April to the sixteenth of June is over and there is no run now, there is a habit, and a habit is not a discipline and a habit is not a method and it is the only thing I have got that I built on purpose in seven months.

*Two. **Ten remembered routes and ten crossings. Eleven marked, one unexplained.** The ninth crossing's failure to cost one has been in this book since January and I have written it down four times this year and I have got no further with it than I had in the first week, and I am not going to get further with it in the next four weeks, and a thing I cannot get further with is not a puzzle and is not a debt and is a thing about a man. The tally is ten lines, the paper is Hettie Brann's, the pocket is Dr Sen's, and I have not looked at it since the thirteenth of January and I am not going to in July. **The page home the two hundred and twenty-first day, blank, four marks in four hands and none of them mine, and it is not cured, and nothing that has happened since November is going to cure it, and a woman of sixty-one wrote nine lines of it on a counter on the first of April in about four seconds and refused to put it in the envelope, and it is a line somebody else can walk me home by and that is all it is and all it is going to be.** The token cold, the two hundred and seventh day, in the inside pocket of this coat, and it has now been cold for two hundred and seven days and it has been in eleven streets and in a fenland and on a bank at a ford and it has not done anything since the seventh of December, and a street that opens because of something in a man's coat is a place kept by that man.

*Three. **The conditions, and there is no seventh column and there is not going to be one for two months, and the first month of that is over.** The order is suspended and is not discharged and is not varied and is not withdrawn. The public name is not on the street name index. The classification of the eleventh is withdrawn and is not to be relied on by any party and stays withdrawn. **The removed sentence cannot be restored, and the department cannot account for its removal, and a desk has been asked twice and has not answered twice, and a query came back down a second time in April and stopped asking about a sentence and started asking about a box, and the box went up empty a second time with a second line through it and a second date, and in about four years somebody will read two ruled lines and will not be able to tell whether a clerk refused twice or whether the section ran out of answers.** The matter stands over and is not adjourned and has no date and was not on the business of the fifth of May and is not on anything, and the twenty-fourth of June came and went on a Wednesday on which nobody in this district stood in the weather. The line of the twelfth of January stands and its condition is the answer of the fourth of December and the condition is not the department's, and it was used once in May as a shield, and a woman of thirty-one said in a corridor that she does not know whether it is the shield or the gag and has not softened it. The levy is two and four a head a week, in advance, week by week, with no term, for two hundred and fifty-two people in a building with no number, and it is not curable, and a woman of about thirty-four knows all twenty-six names and has deliberately not written them down for about four years, and the woman of sixty on the second landing is the ninth of them and is not named and is not going to be told anything by anybody in this district. A round is one man short and cannot be cured from inside here. A boy's own blank card is under a brick with two other cards and has been blank since the nineteenth of January. A plate is eighty-seven days in the grass and is never reported. A cover has been flush since the sixth of April and there is no form for a fault that stopped. An annual maintenance return goes in every January with its fifth box filled nil and a box on it asked about twice and a sixth line of fifty-four words that nobody in this district has read and that I do not know the contents of. Four things in five months cannot be shown to anybody and there are four. Thirteen blank Wednesdays are on a sheet that is right about all thirteen. There has been no second outlook in six months and nobody has asked for one in writing and the absence is not explained. A foreman is ten weeks past four months and is not going to be asked, and on the eleventh of July it will be eleven weeks, and that silence is now partly mine and I put it in my own handwriting this afternoon. A timetable clerk was not asked anything and no working person was sent anywhere and the division's sentence has not been said since the twenty-fourth of April and it will keep. Tern is not found and the twenty-two days are unasked and unsaid. **A woman of sixty-eight on nothing is not named in this entry and is not referred to in this entry and the count stands at eight, and the eight were all in the ten before this one, and I am adding nothing to it here and I am not going to add anything to it in July.**

*Four. **And the thing I gave up, and it was the fourth of four and it is on the facing page in four lines and it is the only one of the four that was made about a habit and not about a person.** The plate was about a person. The seventh column on a table was about a person. The not-telling was about a person. **This one is about the only thing I am good at, and stopping it does not make anybody's life better, and I wrote it at half past four on a Wednesday and I have not been asked to by anybody and I have not told anybody, and the sentence underneath it says that a line on a page is a thing somebody can be sent to look at, and I have been in this district seven months and I have learned that and I have not learned the other thing.** A reader of this book in about four years will be able to see that on the first of July I knew exactly what I was doing, and that is not the same as it being the right thing to do, and I have not got anything in seven months that would let me tell the difference.

*Five. **And the standing question, which is where the volume starts and which I am going to write down in full one more time and then leave alone for four weeks.** *Why does weather memory threaten a region the Registry claims is stable.* **(a)** Because a place is not stable because nothing has happened in it, and a district entered as nil is entered as nil because there is no maintained seam, no recorded loss, no resident witness and no schedule, and a resident witness is a person on a list, and there has not been a person on a list in Mireward since about 1911, and a hundred and forty people have been in it the whole time, and a woman of thirty-four keeps the only roll of nineteen of them in her head, and a man of forty-four is not on a rate roll, and a man of fifty-eight writes four lines once a year into a drawer in Crown Heights and a woman of sixty-one keeps three pieces of paper face down under a counter, and a boy of seventeen's own card is under a brick blank since the nineteenth of January, and the eleven streets have exactly the same list a fortnight later and a foreman and a rack and two tins. **Both of those places are the same kind of place and one of them has stationery.** **(b)** There is no second outlook, and a man of about fifty-one has been in a room nineteen years and has never known anybody who has seen a second one, and I am not going to ask him in July, and I have written that down four times now and improved it none of the four. **(c)** An instrument a person cannot produce: a punch roll about nine inches long that knows how many and not who, a yard man's flag and a book of times, a field book, a pad, and now a smell in a dry week — **five things that are not one, and half of an answer is not an answer, and there is a fourth part under the third that I have never once been down to, and I am not going to go down it in the first month.** And under the first: a book on a shelf four feet from a desk at Alder Quay with a terminus column that says a place that is not on the diagram and a ruled margin headed *actual* with thirty-one names in ink in the order the people got on, and **a destination that does not exist is a fact about a place and not about a person, and there is nobody in it, and nobody came back, and nobody inherits that page, and the printed part is the false part and the handwritten part is the true part, and I went there twice and asked a depot clerk nothing at all, and the thirty-one are at home, and I have not told them, and there is no form on which I could.**

*And the standing position, which is the last line of this book and of this fortnight and which does not improve because a volume has begun: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.*
