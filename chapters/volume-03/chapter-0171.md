# Chapter 171: Two Boards In One Corridor, And A Thing That Is Either Maintained Or Not Maintained

**The round went out at about ten past eight on Thursday the first of October and came in at about four with the bag empty, and it is a Monday and a Thursday round and it has been since the second of January and it is one man short, and the bag has been empty since the twenty-eighth of August, and it is not a walk and nobody has called it one.**

The page home is the three hundred and twelfth day. The token is cold on the two hundred and ninety-eighth. The plate in the grass at the low end of Bridge Street is a hundred and seventy-eight days old and has been told to nobody. The condition column is two hundred and thirty-one days and is not there. **A foreman of fifty-four is twenty-three weeks and one day past four months and nobody is going to ask him.**

**And the return goes to a board today, because the first Thursday of a month is when the return goes to a board, and it has gone on the first Thursday of every month for about two years, and he has known that for about two years and has never once gone to look at it.**

He went at about ten past ten. A bus goes past the end of the street every twenty minutes on a weekday and he has not used one since the second of January, and he used one on Thursday the first of October because the board is in a building in Crown Heights and there is nothing else in this city that will tell a man what is happening to him.

---

## Two boards, in one corridor, and neither of them knew about the other

The corridor is about forty feet long and it is not a nice corridor. There is a bench that four people have sat on since about 1968 and a fire notice and a floor tile with a corner out of it.

**There are two boards on the walls of it and they were put up within about twenty minutes of each other on the same morning by two offices that do not speak.**

The left-hand board is the return. A grid of ruled lines on a sheet of stiff paper about two feet by three feet, headed with the name of a month, and the rows on it are districts, and the columns are the Fridays of the month.

He did not go near it. He stood about six feet off and looked at the shape of it and then looked at the wall opposite.

**The right-hand board is new today.** The paper on it has a crease down the middle where it was folded into three to come out of a room, and the crease has not been pressed flat, and there is a thumbprint of grease on the bottom corner from whoever unrolled it.

He read it standing up. It took him about four minutes and he read it twice.

---

## What a schedule is

> **SCHEDULE OF DISTRICTS — SEASON 4 (PROPOSED)**
> *Surface and drainage maintenance. Column 3: districts at the present rating. Column 4: districts proposed to be withdrawn from maintenance. **A district is either maintained or it is not maintained.** This schedule is printed from the district list, which is a hundred and forty years old and has never been printed from a list of offices.*
> *A district with no correspondence address will be completed by this office.*

Six columns and a key along the foot of it, and the key is the same key that is printed on the second side of a district schedule in the middle of May, and the four grounds are on it, and the four grounds are the four grounds, and he has had nothing against the key since the third of July and is not going to look for anything against it now.

**A schedule is a thing that is either maintained or not maintained.** That is the sentence. He had it in about eleven seconds standing in a corridor and he wrote it on the back of his hand in pencil, which he has not done since June, and then he rubbed it out with his thumb about four seconds later because a thing on the back of your hand is a thing on your hand.

---

## The district that is not a district on it

Nine hundred and something rows and it is a hundred and forty years old and it is a list of names.

**The eleven streets is not one of the names.** It has never been one of the names. It is not a district on this schedule and it is not a district on any other instrument in this city, and it has not been one since a street list was compiled in about 1911 by people who had a reason and have all been dead for fifty years.

And it is on the schedule anyway.

He found it about a third of the way down, in the fourth column, in a line that is not in the same typeface as the rest of the line and is a shade darker, and it does not begin in the name column at all. It begins in the second column, and the second column is headed *Remarks*, and it reads:

> *No district number. No correspondence address. Works contract 3rd qtr (R. PRYCE), expired 31 August. Ground (d). **NOT TO BE MAINTAINED.***

**Nobody put a lie on that board.** A district with no correspondence address cannot be sent a schedule and cannot be asked about one and has been exactly that since about 1911, so the office went to the document it could reach, and a contract names the supervisor, and a contract has a date on it and the date has gone.

**That is the same mechanism as the box in column six in August and the same mechanism as the second box in September, and it is the fifth time in eight months, and nobody lied in any of the five, and the fifth is on a board in a corridor and it is going to be there on Monday.**

He stood and looked at it for a while.

And here is the thing he could not get round for the rest of the morning:

**Nobody has ever looked at that line and thought it was wrong, because there is nothing in it to be wrong about.** It is not a claim about a district. It is not a claim about a place. It is a note about a set of works, and a set of works is not a place, and a length is a third kind of place and is not a remnant, and **a thing that is not a place cannot be asked whether it should be there.**

---

## The form that goes out with it, about something a department can count

There was a second sheet. It was not on the board; it was on a counter at the end of the corridor in a wire tray with about forty other forms in it, headed, and he took one, and it took him about eleven seconds to work out what it wanted.

> **RETURN OF SCHEDULED WORKS — SEASON 4 (PROPOSED)**
> *To be completed by the district. State the length of frontage, in yards, proposed for maintenance in the season. A district with no correspondence address will be completed by this office.*

**A length of frontage in yards.** A season's work goes out with a form about something a department can count, and the something is a length, and a length is countable, and that is the whole of why a length is in it.

He turned it over. There was nothing on the back. There was a box for the district and no room to write a district in it if you did not have a name, and a box for a figure and no room for a day, and a date at the foot in the small type that everything in this city is printed in.

**It will be completed by the office.** Not this season's. Every season. And the office will complete it out of whatever it holds, and what it holds is a contract, and a contract holds a schedule of quantities, and a schedule of quantities holds a length of frontage in yards, and a length of frontage in yards is a figure about a set of works.

**The eleven streets is entered on the board at two thousand three hundred and forty yards.**

Two thousand three hundred and forty yards is a mile and a bit over. He knows what a mile and a bit of frontage looks like; he has measured a hundred and ten yards of it in the rain and been unable to show anybody. **It is the length of surface works executed under a third-quarter contract, it is not the length of anything, and no one on this board is going to be able to tell the difference, and neither is he, because he has not been able to find out where it came from and he is not going to ask.**

Cobb Lock — a name in the list, a hundred and forty years old, with a district number in the second column, and a figure of four hundred and ten yards in column three, at the present rating —

and he stopped, and stood there, and read that twice as well.

**Four hundred and ten yards is a quarter of a mile.** A village of a hundred and forty people, a schoolroom, a hall, about nine houses along a raised road and forty behind it, is not a quarter of a mile of frontage. **And the office has got four hundred and ten yards for it and cannot be asked where it got it, and neither can he, and there is no column anywhere for the question.**

---

## And a place, a yard, a rack, and a road with the barriers still up

He got the bus back at about eleven and was in eleven streets before twelve.

The Row was there. The man of about forty who sweeps it was on it with a brush and did not stop and has still not been asked for his twenty-eight undated words. The fishmonger of about forty-five had her trays out by nine and was not thanked and was not asked and did not say anything.

The barriers were still out on the back length and the road was open, and both of those are correct, and a site is not cleared by a date.

**And a rack, four rooms above a fitter's shop, about four feet high, open at the front, against a wall, with nothing in it in it.** It has had nothing in it in it since the fourth of September. **Nobody empties a rack. A rack is furniture. There is a mark on the shelf about nine inches from the left edge and about two inches wide, and it has not changed, and there is no instrument in this city for a mark that has not changed.**

**The second tin is on a bottom shelf in a plan room in another building, under a repair schedule, at thirty-seven, and there is no thirty-eight, and the tin is not going anywhere, and the tin does not know that and neither does the repair schedule.**

---

## The count, which is three now, and he did the count before the bus

He did it on the bus, in about two minutes, with a pencil and the back of a sheet of his own paper, and it is arithmetic and it is not interesting and he did it because it is the kind of arithmetic he is good at.

One. **The twentieth of August**, column six of a district schedule, a box that says he signed it and he did not, *not to be maintained after 31 August*.

Two. **The seventeenth of September**, a maintenance responsibility return for Season 3, a second box with the same man's name in it, and beneath it the office at which returns for the district are held, and the office is a description of a place, and a description of a place is how a person gets found.

Three. **The first of October**, a board in a corridor, column four, in a line that does not begin in the name column because the eleven streets is not a district, in a darker typeface than the line it sits in, with a length in it.

**Three printed sides in nine days, and two of them are in a building in Crown Heights and one of them is on a wall, and both of the first two say the works are not going to be maintained, and the third says the same thing in the language of a set of works, and the second of them was the first time this district had been on a printed side of anything since the fourteenth of April.**

He wrote the three dates on the sheet and then, because he has been wrong about his own arithmetic in this exact way before, he counted the days out loud on a bus.

Six days from the twentieth of August to the seventeenth of September to the first of October. Fourteen days, then. **Fourteen days for three of them, and a hundred and forty days of nothing before the first one, and there is no column in this district that wants a number for either figure.**

---

*Field Book, Thursday the first of October, 23.30, a front room in eleven streets, one day off the thirtieth of September.*

*One. **THE PROPOSAL HARDENED AND THE HARDENING IS A DOCUMENT AND THE DOCUMENT IS CORRECT.** A schedule is either maintained or not maintained and a schedule is printed from a list of districts a hundred and forty years old and the eleven streets is not on that list, and the office went to a contract that expired on the thirty-first of August and entered it in the remarks column in a darker typeface, and nobody lied, and this is the fifth time in eight months and there is nobody to be angry at and there has not been since the third of July.*

*Two. **A SEASON'S WORK GOES OUT WITH A FORM ABOUT SOMETHING A DEPARTMENT CAN COUNT, AND THE SOMETHING IS A LENGTH.** Two thousand three hundred and forty yards for eleven streets, and four hundred and ten for a village of a hundred and forty people, and **a length is a third kind of place and is not a remnant, and a length of frontage in a schedule of quantities is a figure about a set of works and is not a figure about a place, and I have written that down and I am not going to improve it, and it is the reason this block is going the way it is going.** A season's work cannot go out without a quantity. That is not the design working against anybody. That is a quantity.*

*Three. **The choice is not one I have made and it is not one I have avoided. It is two places.** I am not writing the two places down in this book in the same entry as this one, and the reason is that a date is a column and a column is worse than a count, and I have been dating things since the seventh of September and I know what that is.*

*Four. **Nothing has moved and nothing is cured.** Eleven remembered routes, ten crossings, the tally ten lines, Hettie Brann's paper in Dr Sen's pocket, not looked at since the thirteenth of January and I have written that I am not going to look in August and September and I am not looking. The page home the three hundred and twelfth day, blank, four marks in four hands, none of them mine. The token cold, the two hundred and ninety-eighth day, and it has done nothing since the seventh of December. **The list on the facing page reads twenty-nine and it has not moved in seventy-seven days, and an interval of one day is not a pair, and a pair is a fact about where I was and not about what happened, and if the count of pairs in this block is a small number and not a large one that is correct.** The condition column is two hundred and thirty-one days and is not there and the reason column is not there and no new figure is to be printed for either. The second tin is at thirty-seven and there is no thirty-eight. The plate is a hundred and seventy-eight days in the grass and has been told to nobody. A foreman of fifty-four is twenty-three weeks and one day past four months, he is on a firm's books and not on a district's, and nobody is going to ask him, and the date of that silence is in my own handwriting on a page I cannot get it out of.*

*Five. **The four bells are four bells in four places and they are not a form and they are not a place and they are not a day, and there is no column on this page for them and there is not going to be one, and the fifth thing is somebody writing down the four.** I did not go near a bell today and I am not going to and there was a handbell on a nail in a house that is not made of brick that I have not been to since the twenty-eighth of August and I have written that I am not going to, and the day I write it the second time is the day a sentence written twice stops being a sentence.*

*And the standing position: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.**
