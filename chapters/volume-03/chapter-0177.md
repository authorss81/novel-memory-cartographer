# Chapter 177: Five Cells, And The Same Fact In Two Offices

**Friday the twenty-third of October, and Mara Quill is thirty-one and has been on the return for about two years, and the return is a grid, and the district she is responsible for reconciling is on the fourth page from the bottom, and entries are not moved.**

She had been at the counter since about half past eight. There was a queue of the ordinary kind at ten past nine and none of it was unusual and none of it was hers, and at about twenty to ten the woman from the cross-reference side put a single sheet of stiff paper on the corner of her desk without a word and went back to her own desk, and Mara Quill did not touch it for about nine minutes because the woman had put it down the way you put something down that is not urgent and then is.

---

## A cross-check, which is a thing that happens about four times a year

**CROSS-REFERENCE — SCHEDULES AGAINST THE DISTRICT LIST. To be initialed by the records side.**

Nine hundred and something rows. Four columns. Column four was new to her and she had a schedule on the corridor wall of the other building in her head before she had read the second line of it, and she had walked past it on Thursday morning without going near it, because a corridor is not her counter and a board is not a return and there is no reason in the world to stop and read a thing on a wall.

She read it now, at her own desk, because it was on her desk.

**Column three: districts at the present rating. Column four: districts proposed to be withdrawn from maintenance. And in the fourth column, a name she had written a dash against on the fourth page from the bottom every month for two years.**

> *COBB LOCK — District 214. No correspondence address. No maintained works of record. Ground (d). Length of frontage 410 yd. **PROPOSED FOR WITHDRAWAL.***
> *Person responsible for maintenance up to the date of withdrawal: **none of record***

She read it twice and then she got out the return for September and put it flat on the desk beside it, and there was the row, fourth page from the bottom, and there were four cells on it and every one of them was a dash, and she put her finger on the first one and kept it there for about eleven seconds.

*A dash is a statement about the post and not about the district. **And a district that returns nothing is a district with nothing to report. And a district with nothing to report is a district where there is nothing to insure against. And that is ground (d) on a key printed on the second side of a district schedule in the middle of May, and the key is correct, and I have read the key.** Two offices. One district. One fact, seen twice, in two buildings, three days apart, and neither office has ever sent anything to the other, because there is nothing to send it with.*

She initialled the sheet. She did it in about four seconds and she did not put anything in the remarks box, and there is no remarks box.

**And she did not telephone anybody, and there is no number on the sheet, and a district with no correspondence address has no number, and the reason there is no number on that sheet is the same reason the row on her return is four dashes.**

---

## He came at about eleven and had about four minutes

He came at about eleven with his coat still on and he did not sit down, and he said it in the order it had happened and he had it in about nine seconds, which she has noticed about him and has never mentioned.

"Say it back to me," she said, "in the order, and do not put anything in it that is not on the paper."

He did. The board on the first of October, a hundred and forty years of names, the eleven streets in the second column where the numbers go, with a contract and a supervisor's name and an expiry. The amendment on Thursday. Cobb Lock. District 214. Four hundred and ten yards. *None of record.* And the office at the foot with a rule and nothing on the rule.

"**Ground (d) on Cobb Lock,**" she said.

"Yes."

"**No maintained works of record. Nothing to insure against.**"

"Yes."

"**And Cobb Lock has returned nothing to me for two years and has four dashes in September and will have five in October because there are five Fridays in October, and I put them in with a pen, and the pen is the wrong instrument and I have used it twice in about two years.**" She put her hand flat on the cross-reference sheet. "**I have got the same fact. You have got it off a wall. I have got it off my own return for two years. And neither of us can do one single thing with it.**"

---

## And then the part that was not about the paper

"You have not gone to the drawer."

"No."

"**Say the rest of it.**"

"I have not been in that building on a street-numbers floor since the second of January. There are about nine hundred of those drawers. There is a bus past the end of this street every twenty minutes on a weekday."

"**And the drawer is the only thing in this city that can put a number on a place that does not have one.**" She said it at the same speed she read forms out loud. "**I worked that out in about nine seconds in July and I have been carrying it since, and you have been carrying it since the seventh of September, and neither of us has put it down in a corridor. A district with a number is a district with a correspondence address. A district with a correspondence address is a district whose returns are held somewhere. And a district whose returns are held somewhere is a district somebody can ask a card from, and I have four of those places on a list and you made the fifth one in nine seconds.**"

"Yes."

"**And a place with a number can be entered in a column. A place with a number can be objected to. You have been telling me for two months that a schedule is a thing that is either maintained or not maintained, and that is true, and you have not once said to me what stops a thing being not maintained, and the answer is an objection, and an objection has to be filed against something, and Cobb Lock cannot be filed against because it is not addressable, and the only thing in this city that can make it addressable is a sheet of street numbers from 1911 in a drawer in a building about a mile from where that schedule was drafted.**"

She stopped. She had not raised her voice at any point and she was aware that she had not.

"**So you have got a village that can be protected and a building that can make the protection work, and you cannot do both, and it is not a question of a bus. I want to be exact about that, because you will do the arithmetic and get the wrong answer.** Going and not going are not two halves of one thing. **Going is what un-does it.**"

---

## The rule, applied against him, about a village

"And you have not asked anybody in it."

"I have not asked anybody in it."

"**In two years you have asked a man of sixty-one on a track for a number and a boy of seventeen in a yard for a hand and a man of about fifty in a room for a use, and you have put a contractor's yard on this district as its correspondence address, and every one of those was you deciding what a place was for.**" She looked at him. "**And in about four weeks you are going to work out what is best for a village of a hundred and forty people, and you are not going to ask anybody in it, and then you are going to go down there and tell them.**"

"That is not what I would do."

"**I know it is not. That is the part I am objecting to.**" She squared the cross-reference sheet to the edge of the desk. "**A woman of thirty-four has been keeping a schoolroom of nineteen children off every piece of paper in this city for nine years. She has said no to four people this year. She has said, without being asked, that she is not going to be the woman who hands a district its first piece of stationery. And you have not asked her one word about any of it, and I have not asked her one word about any of it, and there is a bus past the end of this street every twenty minutes and neither of us has got on it.**"

---

**And he did not say his half.**

He had said it four times. *It is the only place in this city where an objection is a thing a man with a box can ask for in about four years.* He had it ready. It took him about four seconds to get to the place in it where he usually said it and then he did not say it, and he stood there, and she watched him not say it.

"**You were going to say something,**" she said.

"Yes."

"**Then do not.**"

He put his hands in his coat pockets, which he has never done in this room.

"**No. I am not going to say it a fifth time in a corridor with a bag over your shoulder. And I want it recorded that I noticed you not saying it and I am not going to supply it for you this time either, and that is a different thing from last month and I have thought about why it is different for about four minutes and I have not got a word for it and I am not going to look for one.**"

---

## What was not said on either side, in a corridor, on a Friday

**He has not told her about the season. She has not asked since July. She noticed some months ago that the number he used to give had stopped going up, and she has not told him she noticed, and there is no column for that and there is not going to be one.**

**There is a page in a drawer with a coat in it, and it is hers, and it has been in a drawer for eleven blocks, and neither of them said one word about it in that corridor and neither of them was going to.**

**She did not describe the line. Nobody asked her to describe the line, and he did not ask, and a cell and a line are two different things in a return and paper is not a reason, and that has been the arrangement for two years and it is not going to change on a Friday in the third week of a month.**

He said, at the door: "The twenty-ninth is a Thursday."

"**The twenty-ninth is a Thursday,**" she said, "**and you are not going to be in this room, and you have not been in this room on a last Thursday in about two years, and I am not going to ask you why and you are not going to tell me.**"

---

**Neither of them was warm and neither of them won it and nobody was thanked, and a person who has thanked somebody is a person who has asked, and she had initialled a cross-reference sheet in four seconds and had not told him she had it, and he had said four dates and three boxes and a yardage in about nine seconds and had not told her which man, and the corridor was about forty feet long and they both had somewhere to be and neither of them said so.**

**And there were five Fridays in October, and the fifth of them was on the thirtieth, and on the twenty-ninth she would put five dashes in five cells on the fourth page from the bottom, with a pen, in about four seconds, and the row above Cobb Lock's would say the same thing, and the row below, and the one below that, and there is no remarks box and there has never been a remarks box and there is not going to be one.**
