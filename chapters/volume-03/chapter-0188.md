# Chapter 188: Going Is What Un-Does It, And A Woman Who Will Not Let Him Call It The Cost Of Anything

**Tuesday the twenty-fourth of November, and there is no van at the gate this morning because a man of about fifty with a mower said *then I shall not stop* on the tenth and has not stopped since, and a bus goes past the end of this street every twenty minutes on a weekday and he got on one at ten past ten and was in the city at about twenty to eleven, and a bus is not a day off.**

The page home is the three hundred and sixty-sixth day. The token is cold on the three hundred and fifty-second. The plate in the grass at the low end of Bridge Street is two hundred and thirty-two days old and has been told to nobody. The condition column is two hundred and eighty-five days and is not there. **A foreman of fifty-four is thirty-one weeks exactly past four months and nobody is going to ask him.**

---

## The room, and what was on the table when he came in

Her room is on the second floor of a building about a mile from where that schedule was drafted. Six desks, a counter, a window that opens, and about nine people in it on a Tuesday morning. **It is not the repository, and he has not been in the repository since Friday, and the two rooms in this city are not the same room and the difference between them is about a mile and a floor.**

She had a sheet face up on the corner of the desk and it was not turned round, and he did not ask.

"**Say what you came with,**" she said, "**in the order, and do not put anything in it that is not on a piece of paper.**"

"There is no piece of paper."

"**Then I am not going to pretend it is a piece of paper.**" She put her hand flat on it. "**But say it.**"

---

## He said it, and it took about nine seconds, and it was four things and none of them was a fact about a village

"One. There was water in eleven streets yesterday afternoon. It came out of a drain at the end of a lane and it filled the low end in about eleven minutes and it was over in hours and about a hundred and forty people watched it and nobody was hurt.

"Two. The gates are the city's and nobody in this district built them and nobody in this district can be angry with anybody on them, because not one of them has ever heard of that street.

"Three. **I spent the only day I had, on the third, eleven miles the other way, in a schoolroom, with a woman of thirty-four, and I did not go to the drawer.** And I do not know whether that floor of street numbers covers the fen country, and I went up the thirty-four steps on Friday to find out and I did not ask her and I am not going to, and I will not know.

"Four. **And I am not going to tell you what that cost, because I do not know, and because the day I know is a day I have turned a Tuesday into a bill.**"

She looked at him for about four seconds.

"**No,**" she said. "**You are not going to tell me what it cost, and I am not going to let you, and I want that on the record in this room, because you have been in this city for eight months turning every wrong thing you have done into a price, and a price is a thing that goes on a schedule and gets a rate put on it, and you know that, and you did it on purpose in June with a yard of about nine people and then you told yourself it was the only honest thing available.**"

"It was the only honest thing available."

"**It was one honest thing and you had about nine of them and you picked the one that made you the author.**" She had not raised her voice at any point. "**And now you want to do it with a village of a hundred and forty people.**"

---

## The disagreement, the sixth time, and both halves of it are right, and he loses

"They cannot be objected to," he said. "**That is the whole of it. A schedule went into force on Friday the thirtieth of October and it says not maintained and neither of them has been asked and one of them cannot be. An objection is the only thing in this city that can stop a schedule, and an objection has to be filed against something, and something has to be addressable, and a number comes out of a drawer on a floor of street numbers from 1911. **Going to the drawer is the only errand in this city that can stop that schedule and I am the only person who could have gone, and I went the other way, and I knew that on the Monday night before I got on the bus.**"

"**Say the rest of it.**"

"**And I could have found out whether it would have worked, and finding out is the only part of it that was any use, and I went and asked a woman of thirty-four instead.**"

"You did ask her."

"I asked her."

"**You asked her a question you had the answer to. She told you it was two questions wearing one coat. You have not said one word to me about what she said and I have had four days of not asking you and I have got very good at it.**" She turned the sheet a quarter of a way round, which was not for him. "**So here it is, and I have said this in four corridors and I am saying it in a room and it is the same sentence. Going and not going are not two halves of one thing. Going is what un-does it.**"

"I know."

"**You do not know, because you have decided that the thing you did was a sacrifice and a sacrifice is a thing that has to be paid for by somebody, and you have nominated that village so that you do not have to pay it yourself.**" She put her hand off the sheet. "**In two years you have asked a man of sixty-one on a track for a number, and a boy of seventeen in a yard for a hand, and a man of about fifty in a room for a use, and a man of about fifty in a room with four machines for nothing at all, and a woman of forty-six to look at a piece of paper you did not need her to look at. Every one of those was you deciding what a place was for, in about eleven seconds, standing up. And on Tuesday you decided what a village was for, and got on a bus at eight in the morning, and asked one question, badly, and came back with a sentence about yourself.**"

"That is fair," he said.

"**It is not fair. It is correct, and it is not fair, and you have just said it is fair because it is the shortest way out of the room and I have watched you do that four times.**"

---

## And the part where each of them put their own half on the table and neither of them could carry it

"**Then here is mine, and it is not a counter-argument, it is what I do all day.**" She turned the chair about so that she was not facing the door. "**I have a return with a row on the fourth page from the bottom and that row has a village in it and four hundred and ten yards and a box that says none of record, and I cannot write in that box. I cannot write *objection* in it, because it is not a box for an objection, it is a box for a person responsible for maintenance, and there is nobody, and the truth of that box is the truest thing on the sheet and I have said so out loud in about nine seconds in a room with nine people in it and the room did not move.**

"**And there is no box in this city in which I can put *this district objects*. There is one in a form about districts in which works have been discontinued, and this district is not a district in which works have been discontinued, it is a district in which nothing has ever been maintained, and the two of those are the same on the paper and they are not the same in the world, and the paper is the instrument I have.**"

"**So on Friday the twenty-sixth I am going to sit at that desk and put a pen on a return with four cells in it and fill four of them with a mark that means nothing came, and I am going to do that in about nine seconds, and it will be correct, and I will be the only person in this city who knows that the row above it and the row below it are doing the same thing for the same reason.**"

"And after that?"

"**After that I will go home, and nobody will have told me anything, and I will not know any more on Friday than I know now, and that is a Tuesday and a Thursday and a Friday in an ordinary month in a job I have had for two years.**"

He did not say anything for about eleven seconds.

"**You could have gone up those thirty-four steps on Friday,**" she said. "**I know you went up them. The stairs are inside a building and a building is not a person and nobody in it heard you go. And you went up them and you came down, and I am not asking you what you did up there and I am not going to, and if you tell me I will not be able to do anything with it and I have nowhere to put it, and that is the whole of what I want and it is the whole of what I have wanted since July.**"

"You knew I went up them."

"**I knew somebody went up them. That is a different sentence and you are allowed to have noticed the difference and I am not going to explain it.**"

---

## And neither of them said the word, and there is no form for it

He did not say *because*. She did not say it either, and she was the one who had spent about four minutes in September working out how to stop him, and she has said that in a room and not in a corridor and **she has not told him she has said it, and there is no column for what a person has worked out about somebody and has not told them.**

"**Say the other half of it,**" she said, at about twenty past twelve.

"There is no other half."

"**There is always an other half and you have got one and you are not going to give it to me because giving it to me makes it a thing we have both got, and then one of us has to be wrong.**"

"Yes," he said. "**That is the reason.**"

"**Then it can stand there and it does not have to be wrong on its own.**"

---

## And then he said the other thing, out loud, for the first time in eight months

He had it ready. He had had it ready since about half past six the previous evening and he had built it in about nine seconds and it was the first time in four months that he had arrived at a place with a sentence ready that he actually intended to say rather than to arrive with.

"**I stopped counting the weeks in August.**"

She did not look up.

"**I have not been able to tell you that and I have not wanted to say it in a corridor, and I have said four sentences to you in four corridors and I have not said that one in any of them, and that is the reason you watched me not say it in October and I do not know that you watched me and I am not going to ask you whether you did.**"

"You did not say it in October," she said.

"**I know I did not.**" He was standing with his hands at his sides. "**I stopped counting the weeks and I started dating them. I have a date for every night since the seventh of August and the dates are in a book. A count is a number and a number is a thing about a person, and I have been trying to stop having things about people. **A date is a column. And a column is worse than a count, and a count is a number somebody can be given, and a date is a place on a line where a number used to be, and I have made it worse and I did it on purpose and I did it because I did not want to be asked how many.**"

He stopped there. **He did not say the second half, which is that the number had been eleven something and he could not say it, and that is why he stopped, and he is not going to say the second half, and there is no instrument in this city for asking a person how many weeks it has been since a thing they did not tell you about.**

She wrote something on the sheet and it was not about him.

"**Right,**" she said. "**Thank you. That is all it is and I am not going to do anything with it and I want you to know that I am not going to do anything with it, because you have just handed me the only number-adjacent thing you have had since January and I am not going to use it.**"

"That is your right."

"**It is my right and I have had it for about four months and you have not given me anything to do it with and that was your decision and it was not mine and I have not complained about it once.**" She put the pen down. "**I noticed in September that the number had stopped going up. I have not told you that I noticed and I am not going to tell you now, and I have not written it anywhere, and there is no column for it.**"

And that was the end of it, and neither of them was warm, and neither of them won it, and **he lost**, and he knew that he had lost inside about four minutes and he did not write it down and she did not say it.

---

## What was not said, in a room, on a Tuesday

**There is a page in a drawer with a coat in it and it is hers and it has been in that drawer for twelve blocks now, and it was not produced, not named, not confessed to, and neither of them said one word about it in about fifty minutes.**

**He did not tell her about the sheet in the inside pocket of the coat.** Eleven lines, seven places, four times, and a name at the end of it, written on the Saturday with a pencil in about forty minutes, and a woman of forty-six looked at it and told him to take it out of the book, and it is in the book. **He did not say it and she did not know it existed and that is the second thing this month that he has carried about in a coat and not said, and a coat is a coat and a coat cannot be asked after and that is the only reason anything in it survives.**

**She did not describe the line.** Nobody asked her to describe it, and he did not ask, and a cell and a line are two different things in a return and paper is not a reason, and that has been the arrangement for two years.

**And the last Thursday of this month is the twenty-sixth and she reconciles the return on it, and he was not going to be in that room on the twenty-sixth and he is not, and he did not ask to be, and there is no column for what is in a line she has never described to anybody in about two years, and a promise about not asking is the only kind of promise this book has ever held.**

---

## And nobody thanked anybody, and he got the bus back, and the river did not go up

She said at the door, "**I have not been thanked and I am not going to be, and you have not been thanked either, and I have not rung anybody, and I have put nothing in a box, and if you go and find out what is in that drawer you will come and tell me and that is the whole of what I want.**"

"I am not going to find out."

"**Then do not come and tell me,**" she said, "**and that is not a relief and it is not a punishment and I have not got a box for it and I am not going to have one.**"

He got the bus at about half past three and was at the end of the street at about ten past four. **The low end of Bridge Street was about two inches of grey silt and four inches of water in the grating, and there was no van at the gate and there was a smell of drains in the whole street and a man of about forty with a brush was on the Row and did not stop.**

**He did not write a Field Book entry about this day and the reason is in the entry for the twenty-sixth and it is not a rule and he is not going to make it one.**
