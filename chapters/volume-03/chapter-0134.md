# Chapter 134: A Column That Wants A Name And There Is Nobody To Put There

Percy came back into the city in his own van on the Thursday and went back out again on the Friday morning, because the first dry week is four days and a man of fifty-eight sleeps on the Sunday. **And he got on a flatbed at a quarter to seven on the Friday and he was on the far side of a bridge by ten past seven and there is nothing to write by on that path, and he did not open the book for four nights, and in those four nights he slept in four rooms in a place that has no address, and not one piece of paper came into existence about any of the four nights, and there is no register anywhere that would have a line in it, and that is not a trick anybody played. It is what a place with no address is. He had never noticed it from the inside before and he noticed it on the Sunday morning when he tried to work out who could say he was there.**

On Sunday the twenty-first of June he wrote on a form, in his own hand, in about four seconds, a seventh column, and then he filled it in, and it is the best thing he has written since the third of June and it is the same mistake for the eighth time in about six months.

The form is a district returns card. **DISTRICT RETURNS — MIREWARD (no rateable unit).** Four columns across the top: *district. rateable units. returns received. reason.* The reason column on a card where nothing is wrong prints a dash. He has seen that card come off the machine with six words in it in a hand and he has the thing in the inside pocket of his coat and he has not written the six words in his own book and he is not going to.

And he wrote, at the top, in a ruled space at the bottom of the card that is about two inches deep and is for the district's own use:

> *Why this district is entered as nil.*

**And he wrote about a hundred and ten words in it, and every word in it is true, and there is no name in it, because there is no name to put there.**

---

**The man of fifty-eight found it on the Monday morning at about seven, at the top of the raised path, where he goes on the first dry week and where he had been since Friday morning.**

He had it in his hand, folded once, and he read it standing up, and it took him about eleven seconds, and he did not read all of it.

"You have written a seventh column."

"Yes."

"And it is about a place." He handed it back the way you hand back a thing that is not yours. "**It is the first one you have written that wants a name. Every other one wanted a person to put their name against the why. This one wants somebody to be the reason, and there is nobody, and there has not been anybody since 1969, and you have written a hundred and ten words about a hole in this city where the people who would be the reason are not.**"

"That is the finding."

"That is the finding," he said, "**and you have got it the right way up for once and it has not made any difference, because a column that wants a name and has nobody to be given one is the same thing as a column that wants a name and is refused one, and both of them are a form that wants a person, and the person is not there, and you have got about nine of these in seven months and every single one of them has been about a person who exists.**"

---

**And the seventh column is the eighth of them, and the diagnosis has not moved since the fifteenth of March, and it is in the working book and not in the Field Book because the Field Book is in a bag on a floor.**

*The seventh column is the only thing I am good at, and I get to be good at it about four times a year, and every time I get to be good at it somebody else has to hold the paper, and in seven months I have not once found another way.*

**The eighth time is the worst of the eight, and it is the worst of them for one reason: for seven months the problem has been that there is a person on the other end of the paper who does not want their name on it. This time there is nobody on the other end of the paper, and the paper does not know that, and I knew that on Sunday morning before I finished the hundred and ten words.**

---

**And the resolution, and it is the ninth of his own, and it is the first one that is not a place.**

He went and found Garn Ollerton on the Sunday afternoon at about two o'clock at the landing and he put the card down on the bollard in front of her, face up, and he told her what was in the last two inches of it, and he did not explain it and he did not apologise for it.

"That is a form from this city," he said, "and it is the only form it has ever had for your district, and I have put a column on it that wants to know why the city says there is nothing here, and I have written the answer, and it is a hundred and ten words and it is true. **I am not going to send it. I am going to give it to the person it is about, and if you want it you can have it, and if you do not want it then it goes in my book and nobody sees it, and either way it does not leave this landing in my hand.**"

She looked at it for about nine seconds.

"No."

"That is your right and I am not going to argue with it."

"**It is not my right and you know it is not my right, and you have handed it to me because you have run out of people.**" She did not touch it. "I am a ferry pilot. I have a licence that is a piece of paper with a name on it and it does not say anything about a district and it does not have a space on it for what you have written in those two inches. And if I take that card and I keep it, then in about four years there is a woman in a room in Crown Heights who says *I have never seen it*, and there is a man in a room somewhere who says *I gave it to her*, and one of those two is going to be a document about me and I am not going to be able to get out from under it by being right."

"Who should have it."

"**The man who goes out in the summer.**" She said it without any difficulty at all. "And he will tell you no as well, and I want you to go and ask him, and I want you to hear the no, and I am telling you now so that you cannot go and say that I did not tell you."

---

**He asked on the Monday morning at about ten past seven at the top of the raised path, and the answer was about four seconds, and it was the same no in the same voice in the same order as the last one, and it was the correct one.**

"No."

"You have not heard what it is."

"**I know exactly what it is. It is a form with a space on it and you want me to put something in the space, and in about four years somebody is going to produce that card and ask me what is in the space, and there is no form in this city that can tell them whether the man who wrote it in was the man who was there.**" He had the rod over his shoulder. "I have got one of these. I have had one since 1969. It is four lines and it is a pad and it goes in a drawer on a Sunday. **You have been here one day. If I take that off you, then in about eleven years there are two pads and one of them has a stranger's hundred and ten words in the bottom of it, and neither of us can tell anybody which one is the record, and that is the whole of the reason I said no to a copy in a car park on Thursday and I am saying it again because you have come back on Monday as well.**"

**So the eighth seventh column went into the bottom of a bag with a Field Book in it, face down, and it is the first of the eight that has been kept by a person rather than by a place, and a person is not a custody.**

**And the ninth resolution is this, and it is in the working book, and it is the first of the nine that he made on purpose about another thing and not about the paper: a thing that is about a place gets handed to a person and not to a form, and when the person says no, the thing is not a document and it does not get better by being kept, and the keeping is a third thing and the third thing is the one he actually does.**

---

**On the Monday morning at half past seven the quay section gang was on the back length, eleven men and a foreman with a clipboard, and the reason column was empty and the condition column was empty, and it was the twenty-third walk since the first of April.**

**And for the first time in seven months he was not on it, and nobody telephoned, and nobody noticed, and Roy Pryce wrote nothing in the hours column and put the sheet in the rack on the Friday at about ten past ten, and the duplicate went into a tin, and the duplicate went into a second tin on a bottom shelf, and the twenty-third walk is on the carbon and the condition column is empty and the reason column is empty and both of those are correct and nobody has ever been asked about either of them and the reason column is now fourteen weeks.**

**A length is a third kind of place and it is not a remnant. It is a gang, a number, an inspection interval, a weekly sheet, a boundary at a wall, and a form with no column that wants to know why. It cannot be sealed or pinned, because there is no edge on it to seal. A length remains a distinct kind of place for exactly as long as the people who walk it keep walking it, and the people who walk it walked it on the twenty-second of June, and they were on it, and he was not, and the sheet is right about that as well.**

---

**And at about four in the afternoon on the Sunday, Garn Ollerton took him out in the boat to the second landing, which takes eleven minutes, and the third, which takes nineteen, and the fourth, which is the end of the water, and on the way back she said the thing that the crossing on the Thursday is going to be built out of, and she said it as a job and not as a favour and she was not asked and he did not know he had come here to be asked.**

"I do not go to places," she said. "**I go from places. There are four of them and I start at whichever one the day starts at, and the day starts where the boat is, and the boat is where it is, and that is not a decision, it is a boat.** If you want to know whether somebody can get from the fourth landing to your office you do not ask the fourth landing. You stand on the fourth landing and you watch them come in, and you go backwards from the boat, and the last thing you do is the first thing they will do, which is get off it."

"That is a return route."

"That is a return route," said Garn Ollerton, "**and there are about nine of them on that water and every one of them was written by a person who was not going to be on it, and there is not one piece of paper about any of them, and if you put one on paper you will have killed about four of them inside a year, because a route on paper is a route somebody maintains and a route somebody maintains is a route somebody can be asked about.**"

---

**And that night, in a room in Cobb Lock above a shop, he did not take the Field Book out of the bag, and there is no entry for the nineteenth, the twentieth, the twenty-first or the twenty-second of June, and the interval that runs from the eighteenth of June to whatever night he next opens this book is not a one-day interval, and the page home is the two hundred and tenth day tonight and the two hundred and eleventh on the Monday, and the token is the hundred and ninety-sixth and the hundred and ninety-seventh, carried and not printed, and the plate is seventy-six days old and seventy-seven on the Monday, and there is a hundred and ten words face down under a Field Book in a bag that he has not shown to anybody and is not going to, and the resolution was a person and the person said no, and that is the ninth one, and it is the first one that was not a place, and it did not work, and I am not going to write down what the next one is going to be.**
