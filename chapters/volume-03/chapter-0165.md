# Chapter 165: Finding Aids, Which Are Not A Catalogue Item And Which Nobody Asked Her To Write

**The Survey School's repository is beneath Crown Heights and it is thirty-four steps up and there are nine desks on the left and a plan chest and a window that does not open and a chair under it. Bettina Sallow is fifty-four and has been on the hand index at the ninth desk for four years and she is not in eleven streets and she has never been south of that bridge and she is not going to be.**

He went up the thirty-four steps on Tuesday the twenty-second of September at about ten in the morning with a sheet of his own paper in his coat and he had not written anything on it, and he had written nothing on it on purpose.

---

## What she does four times a day and has never been thanked for

A person comes in and asks for a document, or asks whether a document exists, and the ordinary thing she does, and has done four times a day for four years, is that **she will not tell you a document does not exist unless she has read the whole card**, and she will tell you what she looked at, how far she went, and what she did not open.

And a card in a hand index is one card for one named thing, and the named things in this room are rateable things with a street on them, and there is no card in it for a drain, a cover, a length, a row of shops, a fence, a woman, a market, or a hole, and the building is two hundred years old and the street list is a hundred and forty.

**And the index is not a record of what happened. It is a record of what is where. A card in an index is a claim and not the document.**

He had heard all of that in December. He had been in the room four times since the seventeenth of December and told her nothing and been told nothing.

---

## The changed thing, which she did not do for him and did not mention

He noticed it in about ninety seconds, standing at the ninth desk, because he is good at reading a room that is made of paper and the room was not the room it had been.

**She had gone and looked before she wrote it down.**

The old order is the order he watched her use in December and the order anybody uses: the request goes on the pad, with the day and the desk and the hand, and then the person goes and finds the thing. There were four pads on the shelf behind her and there was writing on the top of all four and the writing had dates on it in a column at the left.

On the desk, under a sheet of blotter, there was a fifth pad and the top of it was clean, and the clean part of it was a set of headings, and the headings were not *request* and *date* and *desk*.

They were: *what I looked at. how far I went. what I did not open.*

"She has changed the order of two things she does."

"I have," she said, "**and I have not told anybody and I am not going to and you did not ask me about it and I would have said it anyway.**"

---

"Since when."

"**Since about the middle of August.**"

"Why."

She turned a card over, and put it down again, and did not hurry it.

"**Because a request on a pad is a person who wanted something.** A request on a pad is a person who wanted something and the day they wanted it and what they called it, and a request is a party, and a party is a person, and in about four years somebody can come and ask me who wanted what, and I will have to say a name, and there is no column anywhere in this room for who wanted what, and there is not going to be one, and I am not going to be the woman who puts a column in.**"

"So what is on the fifth pad."

"**What I found.**" She said it flatly. "**Not who asked. What I found, and where I stopped, and what I did not open. If a person wants the same thing twice then I have written the same description twice and there are two pieces of paper about a place and not about a person, and two pieces of paper about a place is a thing that can be asked after, and one piece of paper about a person is a thing that can be asked for.**"

She looked up for about a second and a half.

"**And that is not yours. That is a method, and it is four years old, and the change is five weeks old, and I have not written it down anywhere, and you are the first person who has been in this room since December.**"

Nobody thanked anybody. **A person who has thanked somebody is a person who has asked, and that is a conductor of thirty-eight in April and it is not improved in September, and she has never been thanked for anything in four years and has never asked to be and did not ask for it now.**

---

## The recovery, and it is a sheet of paper with no headings on it

She had nine of them. She said that as though she were saying how many chairs there were.

"Finding aids. About nine, since about the middle of August."

"What is a finding aid."

"**A description of what is in a room.**" She did not smile and she did not look as though she was enjoying it. "**An index is a description of what is where and it is a claim. A finding aid is a description of what is in the room and it is not a claim, and it is not a catalogue item, and nobody asks the office for a finding aid because there is no shelf number on one and no one has ever been able to look one up.**"

"Then nobody will ever find it."

"**No. That is the point of it and I did not invent the point.**"

---

He read the first four on the desk. They were not in his hand and not in a clerk's hand. They were a list of what was physically in a room, in the order it was on the shelves, and about a third of the items on them were on the index and about two thirds were not.

And on the fifth one, fourth line, in the middle of a page of drainage apparatus and land drainage notices and a run of notices of meeting that had been minuted in about 1968 and never looked at since:

> *Bundle, paper, tied with tape. Not on index. Not opened. Came in with a consignment of drainage material about 1911 from a body that no longer exists and was not asked for by anybody. Wrapper has a watercourse on the outside of it in a hand that is not a hand in this room. **Not a survey. Not a schedule. Not an index card. Nothing in it is a rateable thing.***

He read it twice.

"That is the only thing on nine sheets that is not a survey and not a schedule."

"**It is.**"

"Where is it."

"**On a shelf.**"

---

## The refusal, which was correct, and which changed nothing

"Tell me the shelf."

"No."

"It is a number on a shelf in a room you are responsible for."

"**It is a number on a shelf in a room I am responsible for, and I have had that number in a book for four years, and if I say it to you then in about four years a man can come and ask me for the number, and I will have to say whose hand wrote it down, and then there is a person, and a person is a party, and that is the third time this month that the same thing has happened to this room and twice it has happened to men who were not in it.**"

"And if I write it down myself."

"**Then it is on a piece of paper in a coat, and a piece of paper in a coat is the only kind of paper in this story that is not in a building, and I have not got an opinion about that and I am not going to be asked for one.**"

He did not write it down.

He stood at the ninth desk for another three minutes and did not ask a second question, and she did not offer a second thing, and neither of them mentioned the drawer in Crown Heights, and neither of them mentioned a bus, and neither of them mentioned a woman of thirty-four in a village four miles the other way who has said no to four people this year.

---

## And then she said the other thing, because she had decided to and not because he got there

**And it is the seed of everything that happens on the twenty-ninth of September, and she gave it away in about eleven words on a Wednesday in between two other things, and she has never mentioned it since and she is not going to.**

"Every card in here is a rateable thing with a street on it."

"Yes."

"There is no card in it for a drain, a cover, a length, a row of shops, a fence, a woman, a market, or a hole." She put a card down. "**And there is no card in it for a funeral either.**"

He did not move.

"**I have checked. Twice. In about four years, and the second time was about two years ago and I did it because I could not think of an example and that is not a thing anybody does in a repository.**" She drew a sheet towards her and squared it to the edge of the desk. "**It is not a card that is missing. It is that a funeral is not a rateable thing, and it is not in a street, and it does not have a plan, and there is nobody to hold a card about it, and a person dies and there is a paper for the person and the paper is in a town eleven miles away, and in this room, in a building two hundred years old, with a street list a hundred and forty years old, there is nothing at all.**"

"That is not an oversight."

"**No. That is the design. That is what an index is.**" She looked at him. "**I did that on my own time and nobody knows I did it and I am not going to tell anybody else and you did not ask me for it, and I would like it noticed that I have not been thanked and I am not going to be, and I have said that in about nine words before in a different room and I am not going to say it again.**"

---

*He went down the thirty-four steps at about twenty past eleven and out into Crown Heights and he did not write anything on the sheet of his own paper, and it is still blank, and it is in a coat, and a sheet of his own paper in a coat is the only kind of paper in this story that is not in a building.*

**The page home is the three hundred and third day. The token is cold on the two hundred and eighty-ninth day. The plate in the grass at the low end of Bridge Street is a hundred and sixty-nine days old. The condition column is two hundred and twenty-two days and is not there. A foreman of fifty-four is twenty-one weeks and six days past four months and nobody is going to ask him.**

*The pairs are twenty-nine and they have not moved in sixty-eight days and an interval of one day is not a pair and a pair is a fact about where I was and not about what happened, and if the count of pairs in this month is a small number and not a large one that is correct. Eleven remembered routes, ten crossings, the tally is ten lines, and he has not looked at it and is not going to look at it in September. He did not thank her, and he did not ask her a second question, and he did not write the number down, and on the way down thirty-four steps he worked out that he now has a bundle on a shelf that he cannot point at, and that this is the first time in eight months that he has wanted a piece of paper more than he has wanted not to have one, and that the two are not the same want and only one of them is about a person.*
