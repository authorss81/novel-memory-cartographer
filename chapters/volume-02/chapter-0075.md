# Chapter 75: A Hearing As Of Right

The hearing was set on Wednesday the seventh of January at about half past four in the afternoon, twenty-six days after the order was suspended, and the three weeks it took were not anybody obstructing anything, and everybody in this district found that harder than obstruction would have been.

The notice went up in the Hall of Wards on a sheet of the department's own paper with a date on it in a hand that is not a hand.

> **HEARING — district continuity order CO/6/BELL (suspended).**
> *Monday the twelfth of January, eleven o'clock, Hall of Wards.*
> *The district is entitled to this hearing as of right and not as of favour. It has been claimed as of right and it is listed as of right and the entry is on the register and the register is not a departmental document.*
> *Reporting officer: **I. Sarratt**, Survey Enforcement.*
> *Second-in-command: C. Ardery, Survey Enforcement.*

The last line of that notice is the entire reason any of the last three weeks happened and it is on a wall in a hall and about forty people read it standing up in the rain on the Friday morning.

---

**Why it took three weeks, which is the answer to a thing a man of about forty-four said in a corridor on the twenty-eighth of December, and he was half right, and he knows which half.**

Cass Ardery had said: *a hearing as of right that nobody has set is a thing a department can leave on a shelf, and the shelf is the department's and not mine, and it is not going to be set in January either.*

The department did not leave it on a shelf, and he was wrong about January and he knows he was wrong about January, and nobody has told him so and nobody is going to.

The department did something worse and more ordinary than a shelf, which is that it passed the file down.

Paragraph four of the letter of the eleventh of December says the reporting officer is to be a person other than any person named in the present letter or in the order. That is one sentence and it took three weeks and four floors, because to comply with it you have to know who is named in the letter (there is a name at the bottom of it) and who is named in the order (there is a name on the first page of it), and then you have to go through every officer of Survey Enforcement in three districts and cross out the two, and the crossing out is done by a clerk of about thirty-four who has never heard of the case and does it correctly, and then somebody has to decide which of the remaining ones will do, and that somebody wrote two lines on a form and sent it up, and the form came back with a name on it on the seventh of January at about eleven in the morning, which is twenty-six days after the order was suspended.

Ivo Sarratt is fifty-one and has been in Survey Enforcement twelve years and is not a man anybody in this district has ever seen, and he asked for two things before he would accept it and both of them are in a file and both of them are about the same objection.

He asked to be given, in writing, that the reporting officer is not the officer who made the order and is not the officer named in the letter, and he asked for the order of reference on the letter to be produced to him, which is the only document in the whole of this case that would let him apply to anybody in another department, and the department produced it on the morning of the seventh, in twenty-two minutes, and it is in the file.

**The name at the bottom of the letter of the eleventh of December is not on the notice, and no one in the hall said it, and the notice is correct about that and the notice is not the document.**

---

**Monday the twelfth of January, eleven o'clock, the Hall of Wards, the same room as the fifteenth of December, about forty people, and the same clerk at the same table.**

Milo Rook did not speak. He was asked on the Friday by Mara Quill whether he was going to speak and he said no and was told that was the correct answer, and he has said since that he wanted to speak and was told no twice and agreed twice, and that the second agreeing was harder than the first, and that on the Monday morning at about ten o'clock he was in a corridor for fifty minutes with a Field Book shut in his hand.

**What the hearing was about, and it is three items and none of them is the neighbourhood, and it is on the notice and it is in the terms of reference and the terms of reference are on a sheet of the department's paper in a bundle with a tie on it.**

> *The grounds of the making of the order.*
> *The classification of the site dated the eleventh of the current year, which has been withdrawn by the department and is not to be relied on by any party.*
> *The removal from the original safety report of the sentence: **no evidence was found of any hazard to persons at the service court.***

**And the third of those is the whole of the hearing, and it took four hours, and the answer to it is a sentence of about forty words, and the answer is that nobody can produce the reference.**

Ardery had it and produced it himself, from a case of his own, and it is in the file, and it is the fourth paragraph of the letter of the eleventh of December and it says the withdrawal reference against the removed sentence *is not a district reference and is held in the office of the First Surveyor.*

"The office of the First Surveyor is a working desk in an instrument," said the reporting officer, reading it out, "and it has produced nothing to this department in twenty-two minutes, and the department has asked twice since the twelfth of December, and the second request was on the sixth of January and it is on the file and it was acknowledged by a desk on the seventh and it has not been answered."

"Does the department contend that the sentence was properly removed?" someone asked.

"The department makes no contention about it," said Ivo Sarratt. "**The department's position is that it cannot account for the removal and has never been able to, and it is not the department's document and it is not within the department's custody, and I am not going to sit here for four hours and have six men ask me what a sentence means to me.**" He turned a page. "What I find is this. A sentence was removed from a safety report six years ago. The reference for its removal is not a district reference. The department has asked twice for it and has been acknowledged twice and has had no answer. The department cannot produce the page that was replaced and cannot produce the reference for replacing it. That is the finding and it is a finding about a document and it is not a finding about a person, and I am going to read it out and then I am going to go and sit down."

---

**And the classification, and the finding is the department's own hand against itself, and it is not softened and it is not a criticism.**

"The classification of the eleventh of the current year," said the reporting officer, "was made by an officer of this department. It has been withdrawn by the department. The department has recorded in writing that it was made correctly on the day it was made and that its withdrawal is not a criticism of the officer who made it. **I confirm both halves of that.** It was correct on the eleventh. It is not correct now. A document that says *minor seam, unstable, not a public route; persons affected: nil* was correct on the eleventh of a year in which the district had a reach with a gang on it at ten to two every working day, and the reason it was correct on the eleventh is that there is no instrument in this city that will put a person on a reach, and the reason it is not correct now is that there is one, in the same city, on a piece of accountancy paper with a rule at the foot of it, and it has been in use since the seventh of December."

Nobody said anything in the room for about four seconds.

"The reporting officer does not restore the classification and does not uphold it," said Ivo Sarratt. "It stays withdrawn. That is the department's decision and I have no power over it and I would not use it if I had."

---

**And then the order, and the district asked for three things and got none of the three, and the fourth thing it did get is a sentence in a minute.**

The district, through Advocate Quill, asked for the order to be discharged; asked for a finding that the classification was not correctly made; and asked for the removed sentence to be restored. All three were refused, each in a different way, and two of the refusals were right and the third was procedural and the reporting officer said so and asked that the third be recorded as procedural.

**And what was granted is this, and it is in the minute, and it is the reason the district turned up.**

A party that has taken no part in a passage cannot be heard on the conduct of it. The department's position was that the district's account of the seventh and eighth of December is a collection of documents and a schedule of persons, and the district had produced neither, and the district had in fact certified on the fourth of December, in a named officer's own hand, that it is **unable** to compile a schedule of persons resident at a place that is not a rateable place and has no address and no street.

That answer stands. It is on the file, dated the fourth, signed, and it says in terms that a nil return is capable of being read as a representation that no persons are resident there, and that she does not know that, and that the schedule is short by twenty-one and that thirty-three rooms are on no list at all.

"Advocate Quill," said the reporting officer, at about half past one in the afternoon. "If the district has a document that describes the passage, I will receive it. I cannot receive a document that describes a household, and I will not ask you for one, and I am going to say that on the record so that if anybody in this room wants a schedule of persons they can see what I have just done with it. **What I am asking for is what you did.**"

---

**And she had it with her, and she had it in a folder, and she said the price out loud in the room before she gave it over, and that is on the page in her own words.**

It is four pages on Registry paper in her own hand and it is headed *Return of persons resident at the withdrawn alignment, the seventh and eighth of next month*, and it has ten entries in the words of the people, and twenty-six names, and no floor, and no number in the column headed *the choice of the person*.

"There are five conditions on the face of it and I am going to read all five into the record before I hand it over, and I am going to do it in that order deliberately, and I am doing it because I have been thinking about it since Saturday and because I would rather this room had them in this order than have me in a corridor afterwards saying that there were conditions."

She read them. And the room heard the third one, which is that no person's name has been entered in any schedule, return, roster, roll, index, or register by the district, the works, or any party, and that the page is not such a document and is not to be entered in one; and the fourth, which is that no person who went is to be asked to go again and no person who did not go is to be asked why, in any proceeding, hearing, or room, by the district or by anyone acting for it.

Then she put the four pages on the clerk's table.

"Now the thing I have to say, and I am going to say it in about eleven words because I have said a longer version of it to myself since Saturday and the long version is for me. **Twenty-six names go into a file.** I have told about nine of them that their names are not in any schedule, return, roster, roll, index or register, and one of those nine is a woman of sixty who has been off every list for nine years, and I am about to make that untrue in one line of a minute, and I am doing it because the alternative is that there is no hearing at all, and I would like it recorded that I did it knowing."

She said it at volume, in a hall, in front of about forty people, and nobody said anything, and about nine of those forty looked at each other.

---

**And the reporting officer ruled on it, and the ruling is four lines, and it is the one thing this district has won in four months that nobody can take back on a Tuesday.**

> *The document is received.*
> *The conditions on its face are read into the record of this hearing and are part of it.*
> *The district's answer of the fourth day of the current month stands, and is not to be disturbed by this department in this proceeding.*
> *No schedule of persons resident at the withdrawn alignment is to be obtained from the district by this department, in this proceeding or in any proceeding while that answer stands.*

"The last of those is mine and not the department's," said Ivo Sarratt, "and I have not cleared it and I am not going to, and I have written it that way on purpose so that there is a line in it with a name on it and not a line with a department on it, and I would like the department to understand that I have done that."

Somebody in the second row said, "You have just undertaken something you may not be able to deliver."

"I have undertaken a thing I can deliver for about two years," said the reporting officer, "and in two years I will either be in a different job or I will not be, and if I am not, this is a line with my name on it in a file and somebody can look at me, and that is the only kind of undertaking in this city that is worth anything."

---

**And the order is suspended and not discharged, and the matter stands adjourned, and the date is in the minute, and Milo Rook heard it read out at about twenty past three in the afternoon and understood it in about four seconds.**

> *The order CO/6/BELL of the sixth year back remains suspended. It is not varied, not withdrawn, and not discharged. Its schedule remains suspended.*
> *The public name is not restored to the street name index.*
> *The hearing is adjourned to **Tuesday the fourteenth of April** and the reporting officer is adjourned with it.*

"The fourteenth of April," said Milo Rook, in a corridor, at about twenty past three, to nobody in particular, and about four people were in it and all of them heard him.

"It was not the reporting officer's date," said Cass Ardery, who had been in the room for all of it and who had said nothing in it. "I asked. I asked in the room and I was told it is a departmental date and it has to be the next available list day after the department's own list, and the department's list runs to the middle of April because of a quarter. **I am going to find out who drew that list and I am going to write it down when I find out, and I have asked for that to be minuted and it has been minuted.**"

Nobody said anything.

"It is the fourteenth of April," said Milo Rook, "and it is the second week of April, and a foreman of fifty-four said in December that in about four months a man in an office is going to ask him whether he witnessed thirty-one persons. The twenty-second of December was three weeks ago. Four months from the twenty-second of December is about the twenty-second of April, and the twenty-second of April is in the same fortnight as a list day on the fourteenth, and the list day in the middle of April is the same list." He had the Field Book shut under his arm. "I am not saying it is a conspiracy and I have not got a figure for it. I am saying that the department has a quarter and a list and the quarter ends in April, and I have got a hearing date and a foreman's date about two hundred yards and eight days apart, and I am the only person in this city who can see both of them at once, and I have not got a use for it, and I am writing it down in a book tonight and I am not going to do anything with it."

---

**The Field Book, Monday the twelfth of January, 23.15.**

*Twelfth, 23.15.*

*The hearing is set, and it is on a register, and it is as of right, and there is an officer's name on it, and a name on a register is the only thing in this city that a department cannot unsay, and that is a man of fifty-one's doing and not mine and I have written it down as his.*

*Nobody died on the seventh and the eighth of December and nobody will die of it. That is the sentence in the file that nobody can argue with and Mara Quill said it in a yard on the twenty-third and I have heard it four times since and it is the best sentence anybody has said in this case and she did not invent it, she said *nobody died* in about two words and then spent four minutes on the rest of it.*

*What it cost: twenty-six names, in a file, in Crown Heights, for good, and she said so in a hall at about half past one in the afternoon before she did it, in about eleven words, and about nine of the forty people in that room looked at each other, and the woman of sixty was not in the room and does not know yet and will be told and will not be thanked.*

*What it bought: a line with a name on it that says no schedule of persons is to be obtained from this district, in this proceeding or in any proceeding, while her answer of the fourth of December stands. Two years, he said. Possibly less.*

*And the order is suspended and not discharged and the name is not on the index and the classification stays withdrawn and is recorded as correctly made, and the sentence in the report about no evidence of any hazard to persons at the service court cannot be restored by anybody in this city because a desk has never answered a letter, and there is no name in front of the desk, and there has not been a name in front of that desk for longer than anybody in the hall has been alive.*

*The fourteenth of April. A foreman in a hut said in December that in about four months a man in an office is going to ask him whether he witnessed thirty-one persons, and I have got a hearing date eight days after that, and I said the whole of it out loud in a corridor to four people who did not ask.*

*Twenty-three days. The book has not written. I have started to notice that I check it in the morning now and not at night, and that is a change in a habit and not a fact, and I have no idea which it is and I have decided not to write that down either, because I have got two things I do not understand in this book already and one of them is me and adding a third is how a man ends up with a theory.*
