# Chapter 91: The First Two Blanks

The sheet of who does what and on which day has a second side, and the second side is a week, and the week runs Monday to Sunday because the first side runs Monday to Sunday, and on Friday the twentieth of February that second side had four lines on it and every one of the four was on a Monday or a Thursday.

That is the whole of it and it took a man of about thirty a Saturday morning and a Sunday morning and a Monday morning to find, and nobody in eleven streets had looked at that side since the twelfth of February.

The four lines are the second, the fifth, the twelfth and the nineteenth of February. The second is in a man's hand in high-visibility jacket with his initials against it, and he put them there because the first side is about a person and this side is about a day, and the fifth, the twelfth and the nineteenth were put in by whoever was standing in the weather on those days, and the nineteenth has got something on it about nine people and rain.

**And the seventeenth, the eighteenth, the twentieth, the twenty-first and the twenty-second of February are on the same piece of accountancy paper and there is nothing on them at all.**

---

The reason there is nothing on them is four sentences long and all of them are about a satchel.

The round is a round of seven stops in eleven streets and it goes out at eight and in at four on a working day. It has gone out at eight and in at seven on seven days twice this winter, once in the last week of January when a woman with a plastic wallet decided it, and once in the first week of February because a man of about fifty in a drainage section had come back from a sheet with a line missing on it and the round had been told about it in a yard, and both of those are on the record and neither of them is a schedule.

The sheet goes where the round goes. The sheet is three feet of accountancy paper with a heading in a fishmonger's hand on one side and a second heading in the same hand on the other and a rule at the foot of each. The round carries it, and the round carries it on a working day, and on a Saturday and a Sunday the round does not go, and so the sheet is in a satchel or it is on a nail or it is under a bed in eleven different houses, and nobody in this district has ever agreed which.

**So the second side of the map of this district is a record of the days that the round runs, dressed up as a record of the days that things happen.**

That is the whole of the finding and it is the finding of a season and not of a month, and it was found on a Saturday by a man who was not sent anywhere and was not looking for it and was in a yard on a Saturday for no reason anybody could establish afterwards.

---

"**A side with nothing on it is a day when nobody stood in the weather.**"

Rue wrote that at the foot of it on the morning of Monday the second of February in about eleven minutes, and it is a good rule, and it is the rule that made the two blanks the worst thing on the paper.

"They are not," said Milo Rook.

"They are exactly what they are," said Rue. "I wrote it. I wrote it because a man of about twenty-six told me a thing in about nine words and it was the best thing anybody said to me that month and I wanted it on the paper before I lost it."

"You wrote a rule about the days somebody stood out in the weather and you did not write a rule about the days the round runs, and the round is a working day, and the second side is a working day."

Rue put the sheet down on the trestle table, squared, which is a thing she has watched a man of thirty-one do, and she looked at it for about four seconds.

"**I did not think about it,**" she said. "I have been thinking about it for nine days and I did not think about it in eleven minutes, and I would like it noticed that I did not, and I would like it noticed more that you have been sitting on it since Saturday and it is a Saturday and a Sunday and everybody in this yard is out in it and there is nothing on the paper."

"I have been sitting on it since Saturday," he said, "and I did not bring it here to be got out of me, and I have not written a form about it, and I want that said first because in this district that is now a thing that gets said."

"Then say the rest of it."

"The rest of it is that the second side is a week and the practices on the first side are daily. The fishmonger calls those prices at ten past five every day. The sweeper sweeps that row at ten past six every day. The coffee man puts his hand on that corner at half past five every day and has done for nineteen years. Six irons go on a rail in an order every evening. **Those four lines on the second side are on a Monday and a Thursday because a gang with a clipboard and a woman with a satchel happen to be on this district on a Monday and a Thursday, and the second side is counting the week by the two days the district's own two instruments happen to run on.**"

"And on the seventeenth and the eighteenth and the twentieth the fishmonger called those prices and the sweeper swept and the coffee man put his hand on that corner."

"Yes."

"**Then the second side has been wrong since the second of February,**" said Rue, "and it is wrong in a way that makes it say something true, which is the worst way for a piece of paper to be wrong, and I want to know what you are going to do about it, and I am telling you now that if you say *a column* I am going to walk off."

"I am not going to say a column."

"Then say what you are going to say."

"I am going to say that I do not know, and that I have known since Saturday morning, and that I have not written anything down about it since Saturday morning, and that I have not slept particularly well and that is a personal matter and I am not going to make it a form."

---

**And the fifth of those sheets came off the same machine in the same room on the Friday morning the twentieth of February at about ten o'clock, and it is for a week that nobody could have told you the date of in January, and the man of about fifty who transferred the four pages' own figures into its second column with a pen on the Monday the twenty-sixth of January put it face down on his own desk and it was still face down on the Monday the twenty-third of February at about half past eight in the morning, and it is the first time in nineteen years that one of these has been left face down over a weekend, and nobody noticed, and he noticed, and he has said since that he decided to leave it and could not afterwards have told anybody why he decided it.**

*A man of about fifty took it off the run on the Friday the twentieth of February at about ten in the morning and put it face down on his own desk and it was still face down on the Monday the twenty-third of February at about half past eight in the morning, and it is the first time in nineteen years that one of these has been left face down on his own desk over a weekend, and nobody noticed, and he noticed and said so to himself and did not say so to anybody.*

The figures in the second column are his handwriting, because that column is nobody else's job and never has been. He writes them out from the four pages every week with a pen, and the four in the river core is the same four it was in January, and the one in the Outer Reach is the same one, and the zero against Mireward is the same zero, and **the whole of that row has been the same five numbers since the twenty-sixth of January and a man of fifty has written them out five times and is holding the fifth face down on his own desk.**

---

**And that is the second thing that went wrong in this district in February, and only one of the two is anybody's fault, and the one that is not anybody's fault is the one the man of about fifty is going to be asked about in April.**

The river core row on that sheet carried one hand-entered line, and it had carried it since Friday the thirtieth of January, and the line is five words and a time and two initials and says *no persons*.

The four in the second column had not been reduced to three by anybody. That is in his own book and it has been in it for four weeks and nobody has taken it out and nobody is going to, and **a printed figure of four in a hand-entered column that is not reduced is the only arithmetic in this district that anybody has ever refused to do, and it is on a sheet in a drawer, and a drawer is not a record and a sheet in a drawer is not a decision.**

"You have got one line in that row," said Milo Rook, on the Monday morning at about half past nine, in a room with a machine in it, "and there have been two things in this district since the twenty-ninth of January."

"There have."

"Do you want me to say what they are."

"**No,**" said the man of about fifty. "I would like to say something and I have been not saying it for four weeks and I am going to say it now and then I am going to do a job. Sit down if you are going to sit."

Milo Rook sat down.

"The sheet says four events in the river core between the second of January and the fourteenth of April. I have written the four out nine times. Two of the four have gone somewhere and one of the two is on this sheet in my hand and the other one is on a hundred and ten yards of frontage that is not a street, and I have written down in a book that I have been in this room nineteen years that I am not going to be the man who writes a market into a district row."

"Nobody is asking you to."

"Everybody is asking me. That is what I have got and I want to say what it is in one sentence. **A sheet is a claim that somebody was told. That is the whole of what a sheet has ever been for. And there is a form in this city that goes out about a quarter and a form that goes out about a section and a form that goes out about a week, and every one of them wants a number under a heading, and the only number a district can give anybody in the second month of a season is a number of days that it did not have anything.**" He turned the sheet round and put his finger on the second column. "**That is what is wrong with it this month. In January the wrong thing on it was a hole. A hole has a shape and you can look at it. This month the wrong thing on it is a four.**"

---

Nobody wrote it down.

About four people in that room at about half past nine on the Monday morning of the twenty-third of February noticed that nobody had, and that was the seventh time in eight weeks, and the reason was always the same and the reason is that there is no form for it.

And then, later in the day, at about ten past one, a woman of sixty-one came in with a cup of tea, because it is the half hour between one and half past one that her counter is shut for, and her counter is not in this building, and she was here about a different thing entirely, and she put her bag down on the end of a bench and said the only sentence anybody in that room said that day which was not about a form.

"You have all got the face you had on the twentieth of January," said Hettie Brann. "You have all got it on the Friday. And I have got it too, and I have had it for a month, and I would like somebody to say out loud that it is not going to be a different face by the fourteenth of April, because I have been telling about nine people a week that a side with nothing on it is a day when nobody stood in the weather, and on Saturday and Sunday the side had nothing on it and about four people stood in the weather, and I have been the one telling them, and I have been telling them for four months and I am not going to stop, and **a rule that has been right four times and wrong once is not a rule anybody can stand behind, and I would like it noticed that I said *notice* twice.**"

---

**The Field Book, Monday the twenty-third of February, 23.40, a front room, standing up, and the standing up is the fourth of those in six weeks and he has not decided what it means.**

*Twenty-third, 23.40. Standing up. Fourth time in six weeks and I have written down that I do not know what it means and I have now written that down twice, which is twice as often as I have written down what anything means. **The twenty-first and the twenty-second of February have no entries in this book and I am not writing a pair for them, because two days of nothing in a week in which I spent the two days finding out that a week sheet is a week sheet is two days of nothing, and that is the whole of the reason and it is not a reason a book can use.** The pair in front of this one is three days off the twentieth and it is the sixth three in this book, and the four that were here in December are the four that were here in December, and the fifth was added in the first week of February, and I am not collapsing them and I am not going to write the word six twice.*

*The finding of the month so far is about a piece of paper I did not write and have not corrected and am not going to correct. **The second side of the sheet of who does what and on which day has four lines on it and every one of the four is on a Monday or a Thursday, and the fishmonger calls those prices at ten past five every day and the sweeper sweeps at ten past six every day and the coffee man puts his hand on that corner at half past five every day and has done for nineteen years, and none of the three has ever missed, and the second side has been blank on five days out of every seven since the second of February, and the reason is that the sheet is carried by a round that goes out on a working day.** That is not a fault. That is a fact about a satchel. And the rule at the foot of that side, in a woman's hand, says a side with nothing on it is a day when nobody stood in the weather, **and so for five days in every seven the district has been telling the truth about a person and lying about a day, and it has been doing it with the best rule anybody has written in four months.**

*And here is what I am not going to do about it. I did not write a form. I did not write a column. I did not write the word *why* anywhere and I have caught myself three times in two days starting a sentence with it. **I have had six ideas in six months and every single one of them has been about a page, and a fishmonger made a rule in eleven minutes on the second of February and it has been more use in three weeks than anything I have written since the sixteenth of December, and I have not worked out yet what that ratio is telling me, and I am not going to write it down this month because a ratio is a pattern and I have four months of not making patterns.**

*And the other thing. A man of about fifty left a printed sheet face down on his own desk over a weekend for the first time in nineteen years and said out loud that he was not going to write a market into a district row, and then he said the sentence of the morning, and I am putting it here before I put it anywhere else: **in January the wrong thing on that sheet was a hole and a hole has a shape, and this month the wrong thing on it is a four.** He did not reduce the four. Nine Mondays and five hand-copies of the same five numbers and the four in the river core is still four and the zero against Mireward is still zero, and **I am not going to be the man who asks him to reduce it and I am not going to be the man who puts a line under the two that have happened, because a line under a thing is a column and I have made three of those in five weeks and the district knows the shape of them now before I finish the sentence.**

*The page home the ninety-second day, blank, three marks in three hands, and there is a fifth day of this month on which nobody has asked anybody to fill it in. Ten routes, ten, none removed, all marked by somebody who is not me, and I have added nothing and lost nothing and the ninth crossing still did not take one. The token cold, seventy-eighth day, and I have not taken it out. The tally has nine lines and I have not crossed and I am not going to, and I have written that down in this book since the nineteenth of January and I have decided this month that I am going to keep doing it, and that is not a decision either, it is a habit, and I noticed the word as I wrote it and I am leaving it.*
