# Chapter 65: The Custody Register

The thing about a two-year note is that it does not stop you doing your job, and the thing about having no preferred access is that you find out what your access was only when you try to use it.

On the morning of Friday the nineteenth of December Advocate Quill was in an alcove off a records corridor in the Registry of Continuance in Crown Heights at about ten past nine with a request for a route custody register page, and the request was made by a clerk on her behalf because she has not been permitted to make her own requests since the fourth, and the clerk was a man of about thirty called Rudd who did not know what she wanted it for and asked no questions, and she has thought since that if he had asked she would have told him, and that she is glad he did not, and that both of those are true.

"Survey Enforcement holds route custody," said Rudd. "It is not a continuity record and it is not in your division."

"It is not in my division," said Advocate Quill.

"I could ask."

"Do not ask," said Advocate Quill. "Ask the enforcement clerk on the fourth floor to say the record is not held in this division and let her say it, because if you ask her whether it exists then in about four months somebody can produce a note that says Advocate Quill's clerk enquired after a record outside her access, and I have got a two-year note and I am not going to hand anybody a second page with my name at the top of it in the same month."

Rudd wrote that down in his own book, because he writes everything down, and she has never asked him why.

---

**And what the river core was doing on the Friday while she was in an alcove in Crown Heights, which is on the page because the district did not stop, and has not stopped, and is not going to.**

There are two hundred and fifty-two people in eleven streets on a per-head additional schedule that a woman of about thirty-four signed for in a hall on the fifteenth of December, at two and four a head a week, in advance, with no term, and she signed the intake for seven children in the same hand in the same minute, and two children lost in October are still lost and she has said so twice in a room unasked.

There is a hundred and forty feet of named frontage behind a fence with a settlement plate on it and a date on the plate, out of true by eleven inches over forty feet, with a gang on a fortnightly inspection against it, and a works clerk in a portacabin has cost about four hundred words mostly about insurance.

There is a gate on a stop-screw through the stem with a padlock on the wheel, and a barrel silted to about a third of its diameter, and it is not the culvert that was there before the order and it is not going to be.

There is a woman of sixty-eight on nothing. There is a man of forty-one behind a boatyard on no rate book, no index, no tenancy and no schedule, and never has been, and he is the reason a reachable road exists. There is a mechanic of sixty-one on a drainage record and in a file in Crown Heights forever who will not swear. There is a conductor of thirty in a printed rule book who will not be on that reach again. There is a woman of fifty-four with a plot number and no bill in six years. There is a foreman of thirty years who has been reinstated twice for certification work and who certified a level he has written on a form that says he cannot stand behind it, and the level is not in his file and may not be put back. There is a man on the third floor of a building with no number who has not been on that street since the night of the closure, and who has not been asked, and whose name is still on five copies of a sheet of paper with a contractor's head on it and has not been taken off any of them, and there is a foreman of sixty-three with a flag who walked past a rail that came up four feet on the sixth of December and got his hand up a fifth of a second before it and says he did not decide to. And there is a surveyor's own front door with three chalk arrows on it and a nine-line blank in his own book that is the twenty-sixth day of it, and he has not asked anybody to fill it in for three weeks and nobody has offered.

And there is a hearing on the Bellwether order that has not been set, on an order that is suspended and not discharged, investigated by a department that has withdrawn its own classification and kept its own reason for making it, and seven children go back to the school in the spring on a referral signed in the same hand that signs the levy, and two children do not.

And there is a woman asleep on the third floor of a building with no number, who is not written down, and whom nobody has woken, and whom a clerk of the Recovery office read out correctly in four seconds in front of thirty people in a hall a fortnight ago.

**And there is now a second gang.** Six hundred yards of tram route off the water stairs has a quay section gang on it twice a week and a foreman of thirty-one years and a rack with a tin in it and a schedule in a drawer and a boundary nobody has looked at.

Milo Rook wrote the two of them in the same paragraph in his own book on the Thursday night and then struck the paragraph out and wrote it again as two, and the reason is in the book and it is the whole of what he has learned in four days: **a hundred and forty feet of made ground is kept by a district drainage book and a works schedule and a mechanic who asked for it, and six hundred yards of tram route is kept by a gang and a wall and a schedule in a drawer, and the two things have got exactly one thing in common and it is that a man walks them, and I am not going to write that down as one sentence until a mechanic has said it out loud and I have got it from his mouth.**

He asked Tomas Rusk on the Friday afternoon. Tomas Rusk said he would come on the Saturday and that somebody would have to walk him, and did not say where.

---

She had the number of the standing instruction in a notebook of her own that is not the department's, and the notebook had been in her bag since Tuesday afternoon.

**11/D/6.** It was on the works' notice of the sixteenth of November, which any person can read, standing at the bottom of a page about a flood-gate test, in the line that gave it its authority. It was on service amendment 12/44, which any person can read, in the line that took six hundred yards of tram route off the map of Alderquay. And the district continuity order of six years back, which is the only one of the three that cannot be read by any person, **cited a standing instruction of the office of the Prefect and did not give the number on the face of the order.**

She wanted the number. That was all. On the Wednesday she had begun, at about eleven o'clock at night, in her own flat, with a pencil, to write out every document she had ever seen in three years that cited an authority, and there were nineteen of them, and the number was on two of them and absent from the third, and **that is not a fact about anybody. It is a fact about a filing system.** She wrote that in the notebook too, because she has been made once already this month for reading a filing system as a motive.

---

The code is one line and she can recite it and has recited it to herself about four hundred times since the fourth of December, which is a thing she has never told anybody and would not find funny if she had.

> *An officer who copies, or removes, or causes to be copied or removed, a record held outside their access shall be suspended.*

Suspended. Not dismissed — suspended, and then a hearing, and then whatever a hearing decides, and a two-year note is already on her conduct and is going to be on her record for the rest of her working life whatever happens at the hearing, and the hearing is not set either.

She read the line at about twenty past ten on the Friday morning. She read it twice. She did not read it a third time, because the third reading is where a person begins looking for a way round it, and there is no way round it, and she has known that since the fourth of December and has been getting very good at doing things she has known since the fourth of December.

And then she asked Rudd to bring her the page.

That is how it was done. Not a break-in and not a midnight copy and not a woman in a corridor with a bag over her hand. She asked the clerk on the fourth floor, who brought a page to a counter because she asked for it in a normal voice in a normal way, and she stood at a table in a records alcove with the door open because the alcove has a door and it was open, and she copied four lines and a continuation sheet onto a sheet of paper of her own with a pencil, and it took about ninety seconds, and a man in Survey Enforcement walked past the alcove at some point in the middle of it and looked at the page and kept walking.

He did not stop. He did not speak. He was about forty-five and she did not know him and has not seen him since and would not know him again.

Mara Quill put the pencil down and did not follow him, and the reason she did not follow him is the reason she has thought about most in six days, and it is this: **if she had followed him, then in four months the thing in the file would be a woman who was seen taking a page out of a custody register and then went after a man to ask him about it, and that is a document about a state of mind. She did not follow him, and so the only thing in the file, if there is one, is a woman in an alcove with a door open.** She is not proud of that reasoning. She has written it down anyway, because it is the reasoning and it is what she did, and a person who will not write down the reasoning they used is a person who has a second one.

---

**The page. Four lines and a continuation, and she has it in a notebook and the notebook is in her bag and the bag has been in her flat in a drawer with a coat in it since Friday.**

> *Route custody register, quayside, Line 3.*
> *Length: back length, Alder Quay interchange to water stairs, approx. 600 yd, comprising the down loop and the quay service access.*
> *Withdrawn from running diagram: 13 Oct, service amendment 12/44, under standing instruction 11/D/6. Requesting body: PW quay section.*
> *Route custody after withdrawal: **nil.***

And the continuation sheet, which is a separate sheet and is a different size, and which is filed on the back of the page and is not in the same hand as the page:

> *Length: as above. Instruments, routes, or objects recorded on the above at the time of withdrawal: **one.***
> *1. Route token, brass, hand-stamped **LARK ST.**, no number. Issue not in current series. Held by: **ROOK.** Authority for issue: [no entry]. Returned: [no entry].*
> *2. [no entry]*

She read *LARK ST.* four times on the Friday and then she went and got a sheet of paper and wrote four lines on the back of it in biro, because that is what she did to a carbon relocation schedule on the fifteenth of December and because a document she has read without a note on it is a document she has not read.

> *This is a copy of a page of a record held by another division and I have no access to it and I have taken it knowingly, and the code has one line about this and I know the line.*
> *I am not saying what the instrument is. I am saying that a brass object stamped with the name of a street this city does not have was recorded as being on a length of tram route on the day that length was taken off the running diagram, and that it was recorded as **one**, and that the word "one" is a word a person uses when they have counted, and that the page says it was never returned and does not say who signed it out.*
> *The surname is one I know. I am not writing anything else about him on this side of this paper and I am not going to, and if this page is ever produced I will say what I did and I will say why and the why is the four lines above and not one word about the name.*
> *I am filing this because a document in a file is a thing somebody can produce, and I have read it, and I would rather be the person who put it there. — M.Q., 19 Dec, own hand, own date.*

---

**What she told the river core on the Friday afternoon, and what she did not, and the difference was made in about four seconds and it was the only decision of the day that cost her anything.**

She came down at about half past three and she stood in the yard because there is no other room, and about nineteen people were in it, and she said four things.

"One. The tram amendment and the works' notice and the order all stand on the same standing instruction of the Prefect's office, and the number of that instruction is on two of the three and not on the third, and I have now got the third, and the number is 11/D/6. That is three documents carrying one reference number. It is three documents. It is not a pattern and I am not going to call it one this week, and when somebody comes to me and says *advocate, three documents* I am going to say *three documents*, and if I am still saying that in a year then I will believe it, and if I am not then I will be a woman who has decided that a number means something because she wanted it to."

"Two. The order is suspended and opened for investigation and the reporting officer has to be a person who is not named in the letter or in the order. The amendment of the thirteenth of October is not part of that investigation. It is a service amendment made by a tram authority under a standing instruction, and the tram authority is not the Registry, and nobody in Crown Heights can put the two in the same room at the same time, and I have tried in about four days and I have not found the door and I have not found the desk."

"Three. That has to be fixed, and it has to be fixed by somebody with standing, and the only person in this city who could stand up in a room and say *these two documents are the same instruction* is me, and I have a two-year note, and I am going to do it, and I am telling you I am going to do it so that you can stop me if you think I should not, and I would like somebody to try, because I would rather be stopped by nine people in a yard than by a panel in a building in about four months."

"And four," she said, "which is the one I have to say last and it is short. Nothing in this case is going to be settled by a person going in with a lamp. I have read what the district has done since the fourth of Sazur and I have not read it kindly and I am not going to, and the whole of it rests on three documents and about nine people and a man of sixty-one who will not swear, and on Tuesday a box that was not in a piece of tram route because the piece of tram route was not on the diagram. So the next four days are going to be about paper and I am going to be slow at it and I am going to be thorough, and the car is not going anywhere that four days will make any difference to, and if it is, then it was not a car that was going anywhere."

Somebody asked her, in about nine words, whether the department would say where the car had gone.

"No," said Mara Quill. "The department will say, in writing, on a date, that it has no record of six hundred yards of tram route, because that is what the record says, and the record is not lying, and the record was written by a man who was asked for a reason and gave a filing number, and there is no face in it, and I have spent three days looking for a face and I would like it recorded that I did not find one and that I am not going to go on looking for one in a form."

Then she went back up to Crown Heights and did not tell anybody in the river core what she had copied, and did not tell Milo Rook, and has not told him.

---

**The difference between that and the fourth set of initials, which she wrote out in full on the Friday night because she needed to know which of the two she was doing.**

There is a fourth set of initials in the annex box of an intake routing slip of six years ago on the Bellwether file, and she read it on the fourth of Sazur and has read it four times since and has not read it aloud to anybody, including him, and there is a review on the third of December that found she ought to have declared it and did not.

That is one thing. That is a thing she has not done and has not chosen every day for four months, and it is the kind of not-doing that gets worse the longer it goes on, and she has a review about it.

This is not that. This is a thing she read on Friday, in ninety seconds, at a table, with the door open, in full knowledge of one line of a code she can recite; and a man's name in a column with nothing after it; and a fact about him that has nothing to do with the tram, and she has had it for a day and a half now and has not used it, and is not going to, and would not use it if the car came back tomorrow.

She wrote that at the bottom of the page and dated it and put the page in with the rest of it, and then she wrote four lines more that are the only four lines in eleven months in which she has written down a thing about a person for any reason other than a file.

> *He has not asked me. He did not ask me on the Wednesday and he has not asked me since and I know exactly why he did not ask and so does he and so does a man of sixty-three in a basement with a day book, and neither of them said it out loud and I am not going to now.*
> *That is a third thing. A thing I have not done, a thing I have read, and a thing I am not going to use. There is no version of my job next month in which the first and the second of those are as heavy as the third, and I have written them down in that order and I am going to go on writing them down in that order whatever anybody finds, because the order is the only part of this I am actually good at.*
> *And I am not going to be thanked for it and I do not want to be, and if he ever finds out that I know, he will not thank me, and he will not be angry either, and that is the part I would like written down, because I have worked out in advance that I will not be able to bear it and I would like there to be a piece of paper in which I said so first.*

She did not know, at about eleven o'clock on the night of Friday the nineteenth of December, that a man was going to walk into a route in the morning and that the route would be moving, and that she would write out a way for him to come back, and that she would do it in about forty minutes on a Saturday morning in a front room with the door shut without being asked and without telling anybody, and that the card would have three hands on it and none of them would be his.

She thought about that for about ten minutes and then she stopped thinking about it, because there are nineteen documents with a number in them and the fifth working day is somewhere after the twenty-eighth and it will not be set by anybody in this case and the hearing on the Bellwether order **has still not been set** and the reporting officer on the investigation **still has to be a person other than any person named in the Prefect's letter or in the order**, and neither of those things is going to move because a woman of thirty-one is tired on a Friday night.
