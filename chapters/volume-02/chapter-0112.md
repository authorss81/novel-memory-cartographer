# Chapter 112: Nineteen Calls

The working is out of Crown Heights at seven minutes to eight and it is a figure of three, and the first of the three is a car with a number on it, and on Tuesday the twenty-eighth of April Milo Rook got on it at the bottom of the ramp at ten past seven in the morning because he had decided the night before that he was going to, and he told nobody and nobody asked.

She was about thirty-eight and she had a bag with a strap and a roll in a wooden case and she said, before the doors were shut, "Mind the step, it's the second one, everybody forgets it's the second one," and he had not known that and now he did.

"Line three," she said. "Nineteen calls out and nineteen calls back and if you are going past the water stairs you have to be on before the water stairs, and I am telling you that now so that you can decide now, because after the water stairs I cannot stop this car."

"Thank you."

"That is not a favour. That is the job. **I say the next one out loud before I have to, and I have said the next one out loud every run for sixteen years, and on a Tuesday morning in April this year I said the next one out loud and I got a car and thirty-one people back and I have been asked about it twice and both times by somebody who wanted it to be a thing that happened to me.**"

"I am not going to ask you about it."

"**Good. Then get on.**"

---

**She is not a device and she has said so, in about nine words, on a ramp, at ten past seven in the morning, before anybody had got a pen out.**

"I am not a symbol and I am not a survivor and I am not going to be called that. I am a conductor on line three and I have done it for sixteen years and there are about nine hundred people a day who get on my car and go somewhere because of a thing I say out loud in a particular order, and that is the whole of what I am and it is a job and it is a good one and I would like it left as a job."

"And the shed."

"Was shut. I asked for it to be shut and it was shut and it is shut now, and I asked for the carbon to stay in the drawer and it is in the drawer, and I asked for the names not to be read out in a yard in the rain and they were not read out in a yard in the rain, and I got all three of those in about nine seconds on a day in December and I have never thanked anybody for them and I am not going to start, because a person who has thanked somebody is a person who has asked."

She said the first call at about two minutes past eight, which is four minutes before it is due, and she said the second one before the doors had finished opening, and by the fourth he had stopped listening for the words and started listening for the shape, because the shape was the same every time and the shape was her deciding something about nine hundred times a day and being right about nine hundred times a day, and there was nothing on the printed sheet that said so.

---

**And at about twenty past eight, at the fourth call, he asked her a question and it was a bad question and he knew it was a bad question before he had finished it and she let him finish it anyway.**

"You fill the alighted column yourself."

"I do."

"Every one."

"Every one of nineteen, out and back, thirty-eight numbers a day, and I have done it for sixteen years, and the only reason I do it is that if I do it wrong somebody upstairs finds out in about a fortnight and comes down and stands behind me, and that costs about nine minutes of my morning and I would rather do the thirty-eight myself."

"And the roll."

"The roll is a machine and the roll is right," she said. "**The roll is the only thing in this city that knows how many people were on that car and it knows it because a hole went through it, and it is a piece of paper about nine inches long and it goes in a case and it comes off the machine at the end of the run and it goes in a tray, and nobody has ever asked me what is on it and nobody is ever going to, and that is correct and I want that said before anybody in this cabin decides it is a small sad thing.**"

"I was not going to say that."

"You were going to write it down at about half past six this evening and you were going to put it next to a plate in a field," said Nessa Bray. "I have watched four men do that in sixteen years. You get in a car and you get a fact and you take it home and you make a column out of it and then in about four years there is a piece of paper with a fact about a person on it and your name on it and neither of you wanted that."

The doors opened. She said the next one out loud and neither of them said anything else for about eleven minutes.

---

**And at about half past nine, at the ninth call, he asked her the other question, and this one was not bad, and he had thought about it on the ramp.**

"You do not need the sheet."

"I have never needed the sheet."

"You have it in your hand."

"I have a card in my apron with the times on it because the manager wants times and because the times are not in my head, and the times are the department's. The stops are in me." She looked along the length of the car. "**I could tell you now where the doors are on this line in the order they come, in the dark, backwards, and I could not tell you why the seventh is before the eighth on a Tuesday and after it on a Friday, and I have never once needed to be able to.**"

"Nineteen."

"Nineteen out and nineteen back and the twentieth is a siding and it is not a call and it does not have a door on this side. **And there is no form in this city for a place a car goes to be looked at, and that is not a small thing, and about nine hundred people a day get on that car because a woman of thirty-eight tells them where it is going to stop, and neither of those two facts is on anything at all.**"

**Milo Rook wrote nothing down. He has written that down four times since and it is the only rule he has made in a book in a coat in about seven months, and he has not improved it and he is not going to.**

---

The car came back in at about twenty past three and stood at the ramp for eleven minutes and he got off it and she shut the doors and said the next one out loud to an empty car, because it was a habit, and because the next one is due at twenty-six minutes past three, and because a thing that is said out loud on a schedule stops being a car about a year later, and she knows that, and she has never once been asked how she knows it, and it is the same sentence a woman of about forty-five said in a doorway on the Row on Wednesday the twenty-second of April and the two of them have never met.

---

**And in four rooms above a fitter's shop at about ten past four that afternoon, a works clerk of forty-six was told about it by a man of thirty-one who had not been asked to be told anything, and it took about nine seconds, and the clerk said one thing and then went back to his four returns.**

"You rode a car."

"I rode a car."

"And you came back with a fact about a roll of paper."

"I came back with a fact about a piece of paper about nine inches long that knows how many people were on a car and does not know who any of them are, and it is the only instrument I have come across in seven months that is exactly the right shape for the job it does, and it is right about nine hundred times a day, and **nobody in this city can produce it and everybody in this city has it.**"

"That is not a fact I can put anywhere."

"I know. **That is why I am telling you and not writing it down, and I have worked out in about four seconds, standing on a ramp, that those are the same sentence and about six months apart, and I did not write it down, and you did not write it down, and about nine hundred people today counted a hole in a piece of paper and were right.**"

---

**The Field Book, Tuesday the twenty-eighth of April, 22.50, a front room, one day off the twenty-seventh, and I am not pairing it, and I have stopped writing that clause, which is its own finding.**

*Twenty-eighth, 22.50.*

*One. **A conductor of thirty-eight, sixteen years on line three, and the ordinary thing she does thirty-eight times a day is decide about nine hundred times a day and be right about it, and there is nothing on the printed sheet that says so.** Nineteen calls out and nineteen back. She says the next one out loud before she has to, and she said the next one out loud to an empty car standing at a ramp for eleven minutes at twenty past three, because the next one is due at twenty-six minutes past three, and she told me on the ramp that a person who has thanked somebody is a person who has asked, and I have written that down and I am not going to use it on anybody.*

*Two. **The roll.** About nine inches of paper in a wooden case, and a hole goes through it for every person that gets on, and it comes off a machine at the end of a run and goes in a tray, and it is the only thing in this city that knows how many people were on that car and it does not know who one of them is, and that is not a defect and she said so before I could and she was right and I would have got it wrong. **It is the right shape for a job that has to be true about nine hundred people a day without asking any of them, and there is no seventh column on it, and it has never wanted one, and a machine counts it and a person does not.** And then the thing underneath it, and I did not have it before about half past nine this morning on a ramp: **the instrument a person cannot produce is the thing that a person produces about nine hundred times a day, and the reason nobody in this city has ever asked her to make it is that they already have it.** That is the third part of the question I put at the top of this book in April and I have got one half of an answer to it and the half is that it is not an instrument, it is a Tuesday.*

*Three. And I did not ask her about the sixteenth of December and I did not ask her about the thirty-one and I asked her two questions about a roll and a card in an apron and she answered both and neither of them was the one anybody in this district would have asked, and she has been asked about it twice by somebody who wanted it to be a thing that happened to her and she is not going to be asked a third time by me or anybody else in this yard. **And I did not ask her about the printed sheet and I am not going to and I want that written down, because there is a rule about a timetable division and it says ask nobody, and she is not that division, and if I had asked her about a printed sheet then in about four years there would be a man in a corridor who had been asked about a morning in December and he would be one of about five, and he is not going to be one of anything.** And the twentieth is a siding, and a siding is not a call, and there is no form in this city for a place a car goes to be looked at, and she told me that in about eleven seconds and she is not going to be thanked for it and she is not going to be asked about the twentieth either.*

*Four. And the two blanks that are not faults. **A conductor's card in an apron with the times on it is the department's, and the stops are in her, and she has never needed the sheet, and the sheet is a print of a person and not the person, and the print is the wrong shape for the thing because the print cannot go out and come back.** I have written that sentence once and I have read it back and I am not going to write it a second time, and a man of forty-one on no list at all told a yard in April that a list is a thing you read and a minute is a thing you are in, and a printed sheet is a thing you read, and a car at a ramp with the doors shut is a thing you are in, and I have spent seven months not being able to say that and a woman of thirty-eight said it in about nine words on a ramp while I was getting on.*

*The page home the hundred and fifty-sixth day, blank, and it is there. Ten routes, ten. The token cold, a hundred and forty-second day, and it was in the inside pocket of this coat at ten past seven this morning at the bottom of the ramp at Crown Heights and it was cold and it did nothing, and it has done nothing every day since the seventh of December, and I have written that sentence down six times and improved it none of the six times, and today it was in a place where about nine hundred people got past it and I want it written down that I noticed it and did not think about it.*
