# Chapter 86: Where The Paper Is Soft

The first working day of the month is the second of February, and a woman of sixty-one at the water clinic counter has a rule about it in her own hand at the foot of a sheet, and the rule has four parts and one of the four parts is an exclusion and the exclusion is about two people who are not in the room.

> *First working day of the month: the four copies are taken out of the four places and looked at, by the person who is holding them, and the version and the date are compared and nothing else is read. If one of them is not the same, the person who is holding them asks the person whose hand it is and nobody else. The holder is not M.Q. and is not M.R. and will not be.*

She did it at about four o'clock on the afternoon of Monday the second of February with the counter shut for the half hour between one and half past one that it has been shut for since before anybody in that room was born, and she had the envelope, and nobody was in the room but about four people waiting, and she did not ask them to leave and she did not ask them to stay.

**Three of the four copies said version one. One said version two.**

"And the date," said Hettie Brann, to a surveyor standing at the counter, who is the only one of the four people who has ever put a date on that card. "I am not reading anything else. Not the route. Not the gate. I have been asked to compare the version and the date and I am comparing the version and the date."

"Two of them are dated the nineteenth of January," he said. "One is dated the nineteenth. One has a corner on it with your date on the outside, which is — " He stopped. "I do not know which of the four is the one you are holding."

"That is my question to you," said Hettie Brann, "and I have asked it and I am going to wait, and I want it noticed that I have waited."

"I do not know," said Milo Rook. "I wrote a version number on one card in this building and a date on it, because I am the only one of the four of us who writes dates, and I did not look at the other three, and **a version number is a claim. A version number with four copies in four buildings and nobody reading any of them is a rumour with a date on it.**"

"Then that is the answer and thank you," said Hettie Brann, "and I have learned one thing this afternoon, which is that a person can be the only one who knows the date and still not know which is right, and I did not expect that, and I have been at this counter nineteen years."

---

**And at about ten past five on that same Monday afternoon a man of forty-one in a wet coat was in the corridor, going to the clinic about the water at his own yard, and a woman of sixty-one asked him one question in a corridor, and it is the only question anybody in this district asked anybody that week, and the whole of the recovery is in his answer.**

"How do you know a paper has been read."

He stopped, which people do not expect, because a question like that is not a question anybody asks.

"You fold it back wrong," he said.

"Explain that to me."

"Paper's soft where it's been handled. That's all it is. It's not a mark and it's not a hand and it's not a date. **You take a sheet out of a drawer and you open it and you read it and you put it back, and where your hands went it goes soft and lighter and the fold stops being a fold and starts being a dent.** A sheet that's been in an envelope on a shelf since the nineteenth of January is stiff in the same three places every time. A sheet that's been opened and read and put back is soft all the way down the middle and it folds across instead of along. I've been handling other people's paper since I was nine."

"How long would it take to look at four of them."

"About an hour," said Doreen Sankey. "If I can get at them. There's a gate on that quay I can't get through and a flat I can't get into and I don't know both of those and I'm not going to be told them, and if somebody gives me the two I can't get at then it takes about an hour and ten minutes and I go on the water when the water's down, which is now, and it is now, and I have got two hours before the tide."

---

**And he did it, and nobody sent him, and he said afterwards that nobody sent him, and that if anybody had sent him he would have done it differently.**

He came back at about half past six and put a wet cap on the counter and did not sit down.

"Three of them," he said.

"Three."

"The one in the shed at Cardew Docks has been out. It's soft all down the middle and it folds across. That's the one a man of about sixty takes out in a shed about half past five most nights and puts back before six and has never once been asked to."

"That is the second copy," said Hettie Brann. "Thank you."

"The one in a building by the school has been out and put back and then been out again, because there are two soft lines in it and one is older than the other by about a week, and I am telling you that a week is my best estimate and it could be four days, and **I am not going to be asked to swear to it and you are not going to write it down.**

"And the one on top of a pile behind a counter in a building on the third floor has not been out since it went there. It is stiff. It has got a slight bow in it where it has been sat on, and I have not touched it."

"That is three," said Hettie Brann, "and I know where all three of those are, and there are four, and you have not told me about one."

"No," said Doreen Sankey.

"Do you know where the fourth is?"

"I know which one it is by what's done to it and I'm not going to say where it is, and you did not ask me, and I have noticed that you did not ask me, which is a thing I have not been on the end of before and I have not decided what I think about it."

There is a rule at the foot of a sheet in this district that says if one of four copies is not the same, the holder asks the person whose hand it is and nobody else. The hand on all four cards is the same hand, which is his, and so the question was not a question and both of them had it in front of them for about nine seconds, and **neither of them said the thing.**

He did not ask her where the fourth copy was. He has written down that he did not ask, and that not asking was the correct procedure and not a courtesy, and that in December he watched a woman look at a table for about four seconds before she answered a question in a room, and that he wrote down something else about it at the time and has not gone back to that page.

"There is a fourth copy that has been read," said Hettie Brann, "and the person who is holding the envelope is not going to be told where it is by a man who has been kind to me twice this month, and the rule at the foot of that sheet says *nobody else*, and **the rule was written by me and I have just found out what it is for, and it is not for stopping two people disagreeing. It is for not making a person admit where they keep their papers.**"

---

**And what she did about it took about forty seconds and it is the whole of the recovery, and the recovery is that somebody now knows something, and the nothing that came out of it is the finding.**

She wrote the date on the outside of the envelope, in her own hand, in her own ink, in about two words, and she put all four copies back in the four places they came from, and she did not alter any of them, and she did not read the route on any of them, and she did not copy any of them.

"So they still don't agree," said Milo Rook.

"Three of them say version one and one of them says version two, and that will be true on the second of March as well, and the second of March is the day I do this again." She put the envelope in the drawer. "**I have not fixed anything and I want that said in front of about four people. I have not found out which copy is right and I cannot, and I am not going to be able to, and in a year somebody is going to give that envelope to somebody and they are going to be told it is a record and it is not a record, it is four pieces of paper with three hands on them in four buildings and a woman of sixty-one at a counter. And a man of forty-one has just told me three of them have been out and one of them has not, and that is one more fact than there was at eight o'clock this morning, and one fact is what a season is made of.**"

"And the fourth."

"The fourth has been read and the person who read it did not tell me and I am not going to find out, and if I find out I will not use it, and I would like the four people in this room to know that I said that in that order."

---

**And there is one more thing that happened on that Monday and it is in the Field Book and it is about the card, and it is the last time anybody in this district tried to make four pieces of paper into a route.**

Doreen Sankey said, in the corridor, going, with his cap in his hand:

"There is one more thing and it is not about the paper. **On Friday morning, when the boards were under at the low end of my yard, the only way round was the wall.** And I have been up and down between the boatyard and the hard on foot for nine years and I will tell you how many ways round there are: two, and the other one is a fence with a lock on it that is a company's, and on Friday the other one was water. **So the card you have got in four places with a version number on it says how to turn and it does not say whether there is a way to turn.** And I am not saying put it on the card, because a man of fifty-four wrote on that card in January that it is not a route and he is not redrawing anybody's route and he was right."

"I know," said Milo Rook.

"I know you know. That is why I am telling you and not writing it down," said Doreen Sankey, "and I am going to be at the boatyard at about eight and the water will be back over the boards by Thursday."

---

**And a boy of seventeen was in the yard at about half past six that evening and he had two things to say and he said both of them and neither of them was the one anybody expected.**

"The card under the brick with the number in it is still under the brick. Nobody has taken it. **Hester Brann looked at four copies of a route this afternoon and did not read one word of the route, and she is going to do it on the second of March, and there is a card on a table in this yard that nobody has read for six weeks, and those are the same sort of thing and one of them has a version number on it and the other one has a sentence about a number on it, and I have noticed that and I have not written it down anywhere and I am saying it out loud instead.**"

"And?"

"And there are three things under that brick now and one of them is mine and it is blank and it has been blank since the nineteenth of January and it is going to be blank on the second of March as well." He did not look at anybody. "I am not going to say anything about where I was. Nobody has asked me. Nobody is going to ask me. **I have been back thirteen days and there is a card under a brick that is mine and it is blank, and a man is going to come and read it one day and it is going to say nothing, and I have decided that is what I am going to be able to live with and I made that decision up the south and I have not been able to think of anything better since.**"

---

**The Field Book, Monday the second of February, 19.30, the sixth of the ten in this month, and it is the one that says what the winter is actually about and it is six lines.**

*Second, 19.30. Six lines and I did none of it.*

*One. The first working day of the month. Four copies out of four places. **Three say version one and one says version two and the one who knew the date could not say which was right, because a version number is a claim and I made the claim and checked nobody.** A woman of sixty-one wrote the date on the outside of an envelope in her own ink and put the four back and fixed nothing.*

*Two. A man of forty-one, who is on no list at all, was asked one question in a corridor at ten past five and answered it and then went and did a thing nobody sent him to do: **you know a paper has been read by where it is soft.** And he told me three of the four had been out and one had not and he would not say which the one was and he was right not to and I have written that in this book and I have not written down which one I think it is and I am not going to, because a crease is not a date and a fold is not a chronology and I have been caught on that once in December and I am not going to be caught on it twice in the same winter.*

*Three. And here is the thing I have got to write down honestly, and it is a thing I did not expect to be the finding of a day: **every method this district has for keeping a place — a gang, a round, a rota, a card, an envelope, a tin on a bottom shelf — has one failure and they are all the same failure and I have watched four of them happen in five months.** They all answer the question they were built to answer and none of them answers whether a person is on a page. A card tells you which way to turn and not whether there is a way to turn. A sheet tells you who does what on a day and not whether the day was safe. A return tells you what eleven men did and not why. A tin tells you the sheet was not lost and not what is on it. **And a man who handles other people's paper for a living looked at four of them and told us in ninety seconds which had been touched, by looking at where they are soft, and he is not on any of them and there is no box anywhere in this city for what he knows.***

*Four. So: is there an instrument? And the honest answer, which I have written down now so that I cannot improve on it in April, is that **there is, and it is not a document, and it is a person who is on no list, and it does not scale, and I am not going to build a version of it and I am not going to put a column on a sheet saying somebody should do it, because the second I do that it is a job, and a job is a thing a company can ask about.***

*Five. And a boy of seventeen stood in a yard tonight and said a card of his has been blank for thirteen days and that a man is going to read it one day and it is going to say nothing, and that he decided that up the south, and that nobody asked him and nobody is going to. **He is seventeen. I am thirty. I have got a book with two hundred and something entries in it and a line home that is blank and has been for seventy-one days, and he has a blank card and he has made a decision about it and I have not made one about mine, and I have been not making one for seventy-one days, and those two facts are the same fact and I do not know what to do with it.***

*Six. The page home the seventy-first day, blank, three marks in three hands, and nobody asked since the twenty-seventh of December. Ten routes, ten, none removed, all marked by somebody who is not me. The token cold, fifty-seventh day. The tally has nine lines on a piece of Hettie Brann's paper in Iona Sen's apron pocket and I have not crossed and I am not going to and **I have written that down eleven nights running now and I have noticed the eleven, and I am going to stop writing it, and I am also not going to stop writing it, and both of those are true, and that is the ninth thing in this book I do not understand and one of them is still me.***
