# Chapter 90: The Fourteenth Of April

The run number was looked up in the last week of January, in about four minutes, in a book on a shelf four feet from the desk, by a depot clerk of fifty-one who had said in January that she would not do it and then had done it, and nobody asked her twice.

It came back as four characters, and a run number in this authority belongs to a crew and not to a car, and the working timetable for the week commencing Sunday the fourteenth of December carries that crew's number against **two** things on the morning of Tuesday the sixteenth, which are the 07:52 booked working off Crown Heights, which is the car that went round for five days and two hours, and a depot move to the works at a quarter to eight, which is a light engine and a two-car set going to a gate.

Milo Rook wrote both down on the back of the card in a yard, with a date, and did not choose between them, and the card went back under the brick on the trestle table, where it has been since the twenty-first of January, and it has two lines on the back of it now and the line on the front of it has not been taken off, because a foreman of fifty-four wrote a rule in grease pencil about that in December and it applies to every sheet in this district including a clerk's.

He asked one question about it in that yard and got the answer he was given by a man of forty-one with a wet cap, and the answer is the whole of it, and about four people have said since that it is the only rule this district has ever made that actually works.

"You are not going to move it," said Doreen Sankey.

"It has been under that brick for thirty days."

"It has been under that brick for thirty days and a man of about thirty has walked past that table about four times a day and read it every time and has not moved it, and about nine people in this yard know what is on that card and nobody has put it in a pocket, and that is not because it is not important. **That is because a card under a brick is a thing and not a job, and about four times a day somebody looks at a job and decides it is not theirs, and four times a day is a very different instrument from once.** And if you want to know whether that is a good rule, I will tell you what happened to the one you made in a coat, and you already know."

---

**And there is a letter, and it came on the Tuesday the seventeenth of February, and it is four paragraphs, and every paragraph of it is correct, and the district is no better off for having received it.**

A company secretary of about sixty, in the tram authority, answering a nine-minute letter of the sixteenth of January signed in her own hand by an advocate of thirty-one with a two-year note on her conduct and no preferred access, who asked that the quay section's annual maintenance return for the current year be produced to the reporting officer at the adjourned hearing **in original and not as an extract**, and who had begun the letter *I have no right to the document I ask for and I do not assert one.*

> *The return for the current year was completed in January and counter-signed. A return is a record of work and is not a document of proceedings. A copy of it may be supplied to a reporting officer in proceedings; it may not be supplied to a party; and the officer's election is not a facility the authority is able to extend to an authority that is not a party to the proceeding in which the document is sought.*

Three refusals, three correct sentences, and in the fourth paragraph, which is the one Mara Quill read out in a corridor at about half past four on the Wednesday to about nine people who did not ask her:

> *In order that the request may be answered accurately, the authority confirms that the return for the current year is filed under its own reference and bears the date of completion. The authority does not treat this paragraph as a disclosure of the contents of the document and the request is accordingly refused in full.*

"So she has told us it exists," said Rue.

"She has told us it exists and where it is and when it was done, and she has said in writing that she is not telling us what is in it, and she has refused the request in full so that nobody can say she partly granted it." Milo Rook was standing at the end of the table with the letter in front of him and his hands flat, the way Lena Marr does, and where he learned it. "**A reference and a date and no copy. That is the seventh door again and it is a different door and it is the same door. The thing in a file in another body's building is in a file in another body's building, and the only difference is that a works clerk of forty-six put fifty-four words in a sixth line in January and nobody in this district will ever know he did it, and now there is a number with the words *filed under its own reference* against it, and that number is a hole with a heading on it, and it is going to sit there until somebody wants to know.**"

"And the fourteenth of April," said Mara Quill.

"The fourteenth of April," he agreed. "She will refuse the same request in the same form and file the same paragraph, and by then the return will be two years old and the file will be in a store and there will be a reference to a reference."

"I have written a second letter," said Mara Quill.

"You have written a second letter."

"I wrote it on the eighteenth and I have not sent it and I am not going to tell you what is in it, and I have written down in my own book on the eighteenth that I am not going to send it, and I am going to read that back to myself on the day, and the reason I have said that out loud in this corridor to nine people who did not ask me is that **there is now a letter in this city that nobody has sent, and a woman with a two-year note who wrote it, and the only way that stays safe is if about four other people know about it.**"

Nobody wrote it down, and this time four of them noticed that nobody had, and that is the sixth time in seven weeks.

Then she said the other half of it, which is the only reason the two of them spoke at all that week, and she said it without being asked and in about eleven words.

"You have not crossed since December."

"No. The tally has nine lines on it and it is in Iona Sen's apron pocket and it is my paper under hers and I have not looked at it since the thirteenth of January."

"I am not asking you to," said Mara Quill. "I am saying it out loud in a corridor because in about two months the first weather-driven event in this city that anybody allocated will be on a printed sheet with a time in a man's own hand, and there will be a great deal said in offices about what the Atlas can and cannot do in a season, and **none of it will be true, and the truest thing about the whole winter happened because two women in this district put seven days into a counter and a satchel on their own signatures, and one of them is a woman of sixty-one at a clinic counter who is not going to be thanked and one of them is going to be leaned on, and I have written those two things down in a book of my own with a date on them and my name at the bottom, and that is all I have done this winter that anybody could call mine.**"

"Mara—"

"And I am not going to ask you about the seventh of January," she said, "and I have not asked you, and I am going to write down in my own book on the day that I am not going to, and I would like it noticed that I have now said that in two rooms and a corridor in a month and that both times there was a clerk within about thirty feet and that I have started doing it on purpose."

**And this is what is on the schedule on Friday the twentieth of February, and it is a list of other people's handwriting and other people's decisions, and there is not one line of it in his, and he has said so at the top of it, and it is the answer to a question he asked on the first of January and it took seven weeks to get and it is partial.**

> **To the fourteenth of April.** *Mon 23rd, Thu 26th, and then every Monday and Thursday at 07.30 — the quay section gang, eleven men, a foreman, a clipboard, six columns, the reason column empty, the condition column carrying three words about a cover that rocks. Sheet signed off and into the rack on the Friday; duplicate into a tin; duplicate into a second tin on a bottom shelf under a repair schedule. **By Friday the twentieth of February that tin holds nine weeks of gang carbon and one page that is not a gang sheet, and the page has not been read.***
> *Friday the twenty-seventh of February and every Friday after it — a form with six boxes, one of them headed **persons observed on the length during the year**, filled nil, in a clerk's own hand, and a counter-signature in another building. **And the sixth line filled once, in fifty-four words, on the fourteenth of January, and there is no copy, and the district has no standing, and Milo Rook does not know it and there is nobody in this district he could ask.***
> *Daily — a water clinic counter, and a courier's round, and neither of them is on this list because neither of them is on anybody's list, and both of them are unpaid, and the woman who keeps the round has said out loud that she would rather be wrong on a Tuesday.*
> *Tuesday and Friday — the counter's own hours, and the card in the frame by the door.*
> **Monday the second of March — the first working day of the month: four copies out of four places, version and date compared and nothing else read, by a woman of sixty-one who is neither of the two people who made them, and if one of them differs, ask the person whose hand it is and nobody else.** *And it is known, since the second of February, that three of the four will say version one and one will say version two, and that the fourth has been read and will not be described, and that the person holding them is not going to find out.*
> *Wednesday the first of April — the same thing, and the last time before the fourteenth.*
> **Tuesday the fourteenth of April — the Hall of Wards. The hearing on CO/6/BELL, adjourned, and a reporting officer's name on a register, and a line in a minute with a name on it and not a department on it refusing any schedule of persons from this district while an answer of the fourth of December stands, and the sentence removed from a six-year-old report that nobody in this city can produce, and a department that cannot account for its removal and has never been able to. And it is a departmental list day and not the reporting officer's, and that was asked for and minuted.**
> *And the same day, on four pages from an office that signed no name, the end of a season. **The second of January to the fourteenth of April. Eleven weather-driven seam events. Four in the river core, one in the Outer Reach, the rest unallocated and allocated on the day.** And it is a printed card for the same machine that prints the district returns, and it has five rows, and one of the five rows has a zero in it and a line under the words *officer responsible* that nobody has filled in, and the query about it comes back out of the same machine seven days later with a sentence in the reason column where there used to be a dash.*
> *In January — a form, again, in a different building, with the same box on it, and it will be filled in again in January and in every January after this one, and the only true answer to it is nil.*
> *On a bottom shelf, under a repair schedule, in a building in this city — a page with six lines about six people on it and a woman's name with a line through it and no date and no reason, and a rule in grease pencil about not taking a line off, and a man of thirty-one who has not read it and never will.*
> *Under a brick, on a trestle table — a card with two lines on it and a date, about a number that belongs to a crew and not to a car, and a number that can mean two things, and nobody has chosen between them, and nobody has moved it, and about four times a day somebody reads it.*
> *On the second side of a sheet of accountancy paper, in a frame at a clinic counter and in a satchel — four lines on it so far and about nine blanks on it by the fourteenth of April, and a rule in a fishmonger's hand that a side with nothing on it is a day when nobody stood in the weather, and a first line with a man's initials on it that are new, and about eleven blanks, and the standing of a blank is a record of a distinction that cannot be made.*
> *In eleven streets, carried, and not one of them moved this month, and none of them refunded: **a woman of sixty-eight on nothing**; a hundred and forty feet of named frontage behind a fence with a settlement plate and a date on it and a gang on a fortnightly inspection; a man asleep on a third floor who is not written down and whom nobody has woken; a man of forty-one behind a boatyard on no list at all; a mechanic of sixty-one on a drainage record forever who will not swear; a conductor of thirty in a printed rule book whose own hearing has no date; a woman of fifty-four with a plot number and no bill in six years; two children lost in October and still lost; two and four a head a week, in advance, week by week, with no term, for a building with no number; and a woman of thirty-one with a two-year note who has a page in a drawer with a coat in it that she has not used and is not going to.*

---

**And the question, and it is a Tuesday, and it is the same question as on the first of January with two more things on it, and it is not answered and nobody in this district is going to answer it this year.**

Four pages from an office that signed no name say that between the second of January and the fourteenth of April there will be eleven weather-driven seam events in this city, that weather does not make a seam and tells you where the one that is already there is going to open, and that a region entered as stable is a region about which somebody has written down that there is nothing there.

There are three nils about Mireward in nine days: a dash and three blanks on a printed card out of a machine, a zero typed by a man with a pen in a row of a sheet, and a nil in a column entered by a desk that did not sign its name on the ground that there is no maintained seam, no recorded loss, no resident witness and no schedule.

There is a man of fifty-eight who goes out into eleven square miles of fen on the first dry week of every summer with a rod and a chain and writes four lines about about nine feet of drain into a pad that is in a drawer in Crown Heights from this year and every year back to about 1969, and nobody has ever asked him for one of them, and **in forty-one years nobody has ever written the word office next to that name on a piece of paper, and nobody has gone there this winter, and nobody in this district is going to, and the going is the part that has been going wrong for forty-one years.**

And there are four of the eleven in the river core, and two things have happened in this district in nine days, and one of them is a line in a man's own hand in a printed sheet with a district in the row, and **the other one happened on a hundred and ten yards of frontage in the rain in front of about nine people and there is no row anywhere in this city for it, and nobody has asked for one, and there is a column on a printed form that is a line under words where a name would go, and it is a line that is a hole with a heading on it.**

So: eleven events, four of them here, and ten left, and no calendar, and no district offices, and a machine in a room in Crown Heights that will allocate them on the day to five rows of which one has a zero in it, and the only thing anybody in this city has ever been able to put under a heading is a thing somebody has been to look at, and **nobody has been, and a place is not stable because nothing has happened in it, and a place is entered as nil because there is nobody writing down what happens in it, and those two facts look identical on a card out of a machine and they are not the same, and the second one is worse, and a man of about fifty said that in a room in January and a woman of sixty-one wrote it on the outside of an envelope in February and neither of them can do one single thing about it.**

---

**The Field Book, Friday the twentieth of February, 21.15, in a front room, standing at a table, and it is the last entry of the month, and it is the tenth of the ten, and he has said that at the top of it because he has learned something this month about numbers he has not counted.**

*Twentieth, 21.15. Tenth of the ten since the twenty-ninth of January, on nine dates, and the twenty-first of January has none, and I have gone back and checked twice and there is no entry for the day the clinic shut at six and the round came in at seven, and I have not decided whether that is a habit or a way of not looking at something, and I am writing the count down because I have learned this month that a number I have not counted is a number I get wrong.*

*One. Seven weeks ago I wrote down seven doors and a place nobody could name, and I said I would not improve on it in a fortnight and I have improved on it in seven weeks and here is the improvement and it is smaller than the original: **there are eleven doors now, and the original seven are all still there, and four more have come out of the same seven weeks: a sheet that comes out of a machine in a drainage section with five rows on it, a printed card out of the same machine with a zero and three blanks on it, four pages on a table that has not been in a register since the twenty-eighth of December, and a form in another body's internal file that I now have a reference and a date for and no copy of. I have been to all eleven and not one of them is a lie and not one of them is ever going to be anything else.** A thing happened on the twenty-ninth of January and this city has no instrument for showing anybody that it happened, and a second thing happened on the nineteenth of February and this city has no row for it, and both of those are the same hole, and the hole is that the city's paperwork is built out of places, and the first of January said that and I did not believe it for four months and now I have two pieces of evidence and one of them is a rectangle of standing water.*

*Two. What I have got, and it is a partial answer and it took nine working people and not one of them is me: **you stand in it on the day it does not come as well as on the day it does, and if you only stand in it for the ones that happen then you have not been keeping anything, you have been collecting.** And the cost of it, in nine words from the same man: **a blank is not a record of nothing, a blank is a record of a distinction that cannot be made.** That is the whole of this winter's answer and it is not enough and it is not going to get enough, and I have written it down and I have not improved on it and I am not going to.*

*Three. What I have got wrong this month, all three, and I am going to write them as sentences and not as a list because a list of my mistakes is a form with a column about me: **I made a form for a season and a column of days and I put another woman's week in it out of a card on a wall. I got the right answer about a thing built for one purpose on the Thursday night and I was on the wrong side of it by the Saturday evening. And I made the case for a zero against my own district in a yard in about eleven seconds and said it out loud, and it cannot be unsaid, and I did not know I was doing it.** All three are the same mistake and the same mistake is a column that wants to know why, and it is the thirteenth of October, and it is the seventh column, and it is me, and it is not cured, and the repair I got was four words from a fishmonger and seven from a man of about twenty-six and I am not going to be thanked for either of them and I have not been.*

*Four. And the thing I am going to be asked for at some point this year, by a man in an office, in a room, with a form, and I have written it down now so that I cannot be surprised by it: **is this district stable?** And the answer, and it is mine and it is not a surveyor's: **there is no such question about this district, and there never has been, and the reason it is not being asked yet is that eleven streets with a clinic counter and a round and a gang and about fourteen people who are not paid for any of it cannot be entered in a column, and a district that cannot be entered in a column is not a district that can be asked to be stable, and if this city ever gets round to asking it then somebody is going to have to open a drawer and find a man asleep on a third floor of a building with no number, and that is not a joke and it is not mine, and nobody in this district will ever have written that name down.***

*Five. So: the fourteenth of April. A season ends and a hearing sits and neither office has told the other and a woman of sixty-one found it out from a card on a wall, and the second of March is four copies of a card out of four places, and the first of April is the last of those before the fourteenth, and on the fourteenth I will be in a hall or I will not and I have not been asked and the yard is not asking me. **And a run number is a number that belongs to a crew and can mean two things, and a card about it is under a brick with two lines on it, and I have not looked up which one, and I am not going to, and about four times a day somebody reads it and does not move it, and that is the only instrument in this district that has never once produced a wrong answer.**

*Six. The page home the eighty-ninth day, blank, three marks in three hands, and nobody has been asked to fill it in since the twenty-seventh of December, and the district has not asked and is not going to, and **that is not a kindness and nobody has said it is a kindness, it is a refusal, and I have written it here as a refusal because a thing everybody has quietly agreed not to ask about is a thing that has quietly become nobody's, and this district has lost two rooms and a school place and a grave in six years to things nobody asked about.** Ten routes, ten, none removed, all ten marked by somebody who is not me, and the ninth crossing did not take one and nobody has explained it and I have stopped writing down that I am waiting to be told.

*The token cold, seventy-fifth day since the seventh of December, and I have not taken it out of the coat, and I am not going to, and the coat is going to be the reason for one or two things that are not in this book and I have written that down and I have not written the other thing down and I have not told anybody and she has not asked, and **I am going to end this month's writing where I have ended it six times and I am not going to be the author of the back length's maintenance, and I have not been for five weeks, and a man of fifty-four walks it twice a week and a woman of about forty-five puts her thumb in a crack twice a day and a man of about sixty puts his hand on a corner for four seconds twice a day and none of them knows me and I have known all three of them for five months and I have not asked one of them for anything, and that is not modesty and it is not discipline and it is the only thing I have done this winter that I would be willing to write the word method next to.***
