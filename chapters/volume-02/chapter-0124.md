# Chapter 124: What Day Is The Figure Of

On Friday the fifth of June, at about ten past ten in the morning, the weekly sheet for the week of the first went into the rack in the quay section office, the duplicate went into a tin, and the duplicate went into a second tin on the bottom shelf of the plan room of the Civic Atlas Office under a repair schedule, and **that tin now holds twenty-five weeks of gang carbon and one page that is not a gang sheet.**

The second side of the sheet of who does what and on which day went out in the satchel that morning for a week that runs Monday to Sunday, and it is the day-side that travels, because the day-side is the one anybody can hold up, and there is a block of figures on the first side under a rule at the foot, and a person has to turn the sheet over to know whether a thing is a day or a count.

---

**And in a room with a counter in it, a woman of sixty-one did not stop, and she read the four returns out loud, in order, standing up, at about a quarter past ten, to a man of thirty-one who had not asked her to.**

She reads them because the figures on them are a week old, and she has read them out loud to the person they are about since about 1971, and nobody has ever asked her to and nobody has ever thanked her and nobody ever is going to.

"**You are going to ask me why I do it and I am going to answer you before you do, and it is not a favour and it is not a kindness and if you thank me for it I will stop.** In about four years these four sheets are going to be produced against somebody who lives in one of the buildings on them, and the person they are produced against is not going to have been in this room, and being asked about a figure is a different thing from having heard a figure read out in a room by a person who was there when it was wrong. **One of those two you can answer. The other one you can only be asked.** So I read them. In order. The post came in on Friday and the post comes in on Friday and I do them in the order they came in and not in the order of the days, because the days are somebody else's order and the post is mine."

"What day is the figure of."

That stopped her. She put the first one down and looked at it.

"Say that again."

"What day is the figure of."

"**Saturday.**" She turned it round and put her thumb under a line. "Saturday before last. Every week, the figures on these are the count of the Saturday, and they go out on the Monday, and there is no column anywhere on this piece of paper for the day the figure is of, and there is no column for it on any of the four of them, and there has not been one since about 1971, and the figures are a week old every single time anybody in this district reads one of them."

"How long have you known that."

"Since the first one." She squared the pile. "**Nobody has ever asked me. Not you, not a woman of about thirty-four, not a man of about thirty-one in four rooms above a fitter's shop who has been in this counter four times this year. You have all got a figure and not one of you has ever asked when it is of, and that is not because you are all fools, it is because every figure in this district comes on a sheet with a date on the sheet and the date on the sheet is the Monday and the Monday is when it went out.**"

---

**And it took him about nine seconds and then he walked out into a street and stood there, and the thing he had in his hand was his own book, and he had been carrying it since the twenty-fourth of November.**

He did not open it in the room. He opened it at about half past eleven on a front step four streets away and went down the entries, and the entries go back to the sixth of December, and there are about two hundred and forty of them, and **every single one of them is dated the day he wrote it, and the day he wrote it is very often not the day the thing was of.**

The plate in the field. He wrote it down on the sixth of April and it had gone down on the sixth of April and that one was right. The rectangle of standing water on the twenty-ninth of January he wrote down on the thirty-first, and the hundred and ten yards of frontage in the rain on the nineteenth of February he wrote down on the twenty-second, and the last walk that went into the condition column was the twelfth of February and he wrote it down on the sixteenth, four days, and he had believed for four months that he had been exact about it.

**He has been dating a book to the day he noticed things. Not one entry in about two hundred and forty is wrong. Every one of them is a day late or a week late or a month late, and there is no column for the day a thing was of, and there is no form in this city that has one, and he has just found that out at a quarter past ten on a Friday morning from a woman of sixty-one who has been printing a week-old number on a Monday-morninged sheet since 1971 and has never once been asked about it by anybody.**

He did not go and re-date anything. That was the decision and he made it in about four seconds on a front step and it was the correct one and it cost him nothing, because a corrected book is a second book and a second book is a document with two dates in it and a man in about four years would read both and not be able to tell which one he had been carrying.

**So the thing he got is a knowledge and not a record, and the knowledge is this: the figures in this district are about four days old, and about six weeks old, and the two hundred and fifty-two is a count of a night in the middle of December, and he has been writing them down as though they were figures of the day he wrote them, and he is the only person in eleven streets who has that wrong, and there is nowhere to put being right about it.**

---

**And at about half past four the same afternoon, in a yard, with about nine people in it, a man of forty-one on no list at all was told a fact and did not write it down, and a man of thirty-one told it to him.**

"Cyril Sale has three sentences on the backs of three forms," said Milo Rook. "His name and the date. In the correspondence tray and not in a drawer, and he has said three times that he is not going to stop."

"And?"

"He is going to stop."

Somebody said, "Why."

"Because a sentence on the back of a form with a man's name and a date against it is a document, and he has three, and he has worked out in about four seconds that a document is a place a person can be asked about, and he has decided he would rather have said it than have written it." He put his hands flat on the trestle table. "**So here is the method and it is his and it is nine words long and he said them to me in a corridor on Wednesday and did not write them down either, and I am not going to say them in a yard, and I have just said what he said, which is that he is stopping, and you can all have that.**"

Nobody wrote it down.

"And the other half of it," said Rue.

"**The other half is that a fact is the only thing in this city with no reference, and I have spent seven months trying to build a place where nothing can be produced against anybody, and I have built six of them, and every one of them is a place, and a place has a reference in it the moment anybody looks for it, and he has not built anything at all.**"

That is the whole of the change and Milo Rook did not make it and did not improve it, and it was forty-one words long in a yard and it is in a book in a coat and it is in no form, and a practice is not a place and is not a day, and nobody in that yard is going to be asked about it in a fortnight.

---

**And eleven men walked six hundred yards at half past seven on Thursday the fourth and the condition column was empty, and on Monday the eighth the sheet for that week goes into the rack, and nobody has asked a gang what a length was like, and there is no column on that sheet that wants to know why a column is empty, and a works clerk of forty-six said the whole of that once in May and is not going to say it again.**

**And on Wednesday the third of June the second side had a blank on it for the ninth time since the eighth of April, and the sheet is right about it, and there is a plate in a field at the low end that is fifty-nine days in the grass, and a man of seventeen who is one man short on a round, and a form about a round on the top of a table in an office that anybody can walk into, and none of those four things has a column and none of them is going to get one in June.**

---

**The Field Book, Friday the fifth of June, 23.05, a front room, one day off the fourth, and the thing I am writing at the top of this entry is the only thing in it that matters and I have put it first, which is new, and I am not going to be sure it is a good idea and I am doing it anyway.**

*Fifth, 23.05. One day off the fourth.*

*One. **Every figure in this district is a week old and nobody has ever asked, and I found out at a quarter past ten this morning from a woman of sixty-one who has been printing a Saturday's count on a Monday's sheet since 1971.** The figures go on a Saturday, they are written up on the Friday, and they go out on the Monday, and the date on the sheet is the Monday, and the Monday is the day the post came in and not the day the number is of. **And the finding that is the whole of it: there is no column on any of those four returns for the day a figure is of, and there is not one in this city, and a form that carries a number without the day the number is of is not wrong, it is a form about work.** She read the four of them out loud standing up because in about four years they are going to be produced against somebody who was not in the room, and one of those two things you can answer and the other one you can only be asked. **That is the changed method and it is hers, it is nine words long, and I did not make it and I am not going to improve it, and a practice is not a place and is not a day and I have written that sentence down and I am not going to put it next to anything.**

*Two. And what it did to me, which is the cost and it is not curable and nobody is going to help. **I have been dating this book to the day I noticed things and not one entry in about two hundred and forty is wrong and every one of them is late, and some of them are four days late and some of them are a week, and the last walk into the condition column was the twelfth of February and I entered it on the sixteenth and I have believed for four months that I was exact.** I did not go and correct anything. A corrected book is a second book, and a second book is two dates against one fact, and a man in about four years reads both and does not know which one I was carrying. So the thing I have got is a **knowledge and not a record**, and the knowledge is that the two hundred and fifty-two is a count of a night in the middle of December, and that the seven stops are a fact about a Monday in a month I have not been here for, and that I have been the only person in eleven streets with the day of a figure wrong, and there is nowhere to put being right about it, and being right about it is not going on a sheet.

*Three. And the eight words a man of forty-six said in a corridor on Wednesday and did not write down, which is the other half of it and which I have put in a yard as a fact and not as a sentence. **He has three sentences on the backs of three forms, with his name and the date, in the correspondence tray and not in a drawer, and he is stopping, and the reason is that a document is a place a person can be asked about.** A man of thirty-one has spent seven months building places where nothing can be produced against anybody, and he has built six, and every one of them is a place, and a place has a reference in it the moment anybody looks for it, and a man of forty-six has built nothing and lost nothing and is giving up three pieces of paper that nobody has ever asked for. **That is not a lesson and I did not give it to him and he did not give it to me and it is in a corridor and not in a yard and it came out the other side of a conversation about a return on a Friday morning in June.** I am not going to be better at this. I am going to keep drawing the seventh column about four times a year and the diagnosis has not moved since the fifteenth of March and it is still true and it still does not reach this, and there is a form on the top of my own table about a round with eighty words in it that anybody can walk in and pick up, and that is the arrangement and I am not going to describe it as a cure.

*Four. **The second tin at twenty-five on Friday the fifth, and I wrote the figure, and I said in this book on the fourth that I was not going to write it again and I have written it, and that is twice now I have said a thing about not writing a number down and then written the number down, and a sentence about not writing a number down is a column in everything but the paper.** I am going to let that one stand. The rack is a Friday and the gang is a Monday and a Thursday and the walks in these ten days are the twenty-fifth and the twenty-eighth of May and the first and fourth of June, and there is one tomorrow morning, and the condition column is empty on all four of them and the reason column is empty on all four of them and both of those are correct.

*Five. And the rest. **Ten remembered routes, ten. Nine lines in the tally, the paper is Hettie Brann's, the pocket is Dr Sen's, and I have not looked at it since the thirteenth of January and I am not going to look at it in June, and that sentence is now in this book six times and I have improved it none of the six.** The token cold, a hundred and eightieth day, and it is in the inside pocket of this coat. The page home the hundred and ninety-fourth day, blank, four marks in four hands and none of them mine, and I am not writing that it is there. The Lark Street grievance is not named and not resolved and not softened in ten days, and there is a page in a drawer with a coat in it and I have not asked about it for five months and I have stopped keeping the number. The run number has not moved since the twenty-fifth of April. Tern is not found. The three things under the brick are three. **I have added nothing in these ten days about a woman of sixty-eight on nothing and the count stands at eight and her name is not written in this entry, and the woman on the fourth floor of Cedar Court is not in this month's account either, so that the two sixty-eights cannot be folded.**

*And the standing position, which does not improve: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.**
