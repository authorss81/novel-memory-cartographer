# Chapter 82: The Allocation On The Day

A seam event has to be allocated on the day, and that is on the four pages, and nobody in the city has ever asked the only question about it, which is what allocated *to*.

The sheet came out of the same machine as the district returns cards, in the same room, on the same Friday-morning run, and it came off it on the **Friday the twenty-fifth of January**, a day before the week it is for begins, and it is a single side and it is not a good sheet, and it is the only piece of stationery in this city that anybody has ever printed in order to have something to be given to on the day.

> **SEAM EVENT ALLOCATION — WEEK COMMENCING MON. XXVI.**
> *Issued with the seasonal outlook under the office of the First Surveyor. One event to one line. Unallocated events are allocated on the day of the event and returned the same day. An allocation which cannot be returned is not an outstanding allocation; it is a sheet in a drawer.*

| DISTRICT | EVENTS FORECAST | EVENT | ALLOCATED TO | OFFICER RESPONSIBLE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RIVER CORE | 4 | | | |
| CROWN HEIGHTS | * | | | |
| SOUTH SALT | * | | | |
| OUTER REACH | 1 | | | |
| MIREWARD | 0 | | | |

*To be returned to this office by Friday. — Ground and Seam Returns.*

**The column headed *events forecast* is not typed. It is the office of the First Surveyor's own figures, transferred by hand from the four pages, and a man of about fifty in the drainage section who has been in that room nineteen years did the transfer himself on the Monday the twenty-sixth, the day the week commences, with a pen, and he did not like doing it and he did it anyway, and about four people have said since that the two numbers nobody questioned in that room for nine days were the two he wrote out by hand.**

He is fifty-ish. He is not named here, and the reason he is not named is further down this page, and it is not modesty and it has nothing to do with him.

---

**The first entry in the whole city, of any kind, on anything, about an event that the four pages said would happen, was made on Friday the thirtieth of January at about a quarter past ten in the morning, in the river core row, in a man's own hand, with a time on it.**

He did it because nobody else in the building was going to and because he had been looking at the sheet for two days and because a printed form with a row in it and nothing in the row is worse than no form, and he had that at the bottom of a printed card about a district every month and did not want another one.

> *29.i. 02.20. Cover up in loop, ballast washed. Back length. No persons. — G.S.R., 30.i.*

**No persons.** He wrote that on the bottom line of a hand-entered row on a sheet that has a column on the left with four in it, and he has said since that he put it there because the form has no other way of being finished, and that *no persons* is the only answer anybody could honestly give on the twenty-ninth of January, and that it is the answer the sheet wanted and not the answer anybody wanted, and that a nil in a column on a length means a different thing from a nil in a column on a street and he knows that now and is not going to explain it to a machine.

That is the whole of the first event's record. It is five words and a time and two initials on a single side of printed paper that came out of a machine in a room in Crown Heights, and **there is no copy of it, and it is not on a return, and it is not on the gang's sheet, and the gang's sheet for the week of Monday the twenty-sixth of January has two dates in it and four words in a condition column and the reason column empty on both.**

---

**And then he came to the box that is a line under words, and he did not fill it in, and he was right, and it cost a week.**

There are five rows. Every row has a line under the words *officer responsible* and a line under words is a place a person can write anything at all, and he filled in four of them.

*River core — D. Farr, route room, Civic Atlas Office.* That one is easy and it is not even true: Dilley Farr is a route-room supervisor of thirty-one years and he has never been the officer responsible for a seam event in his life and he is the nearest thing to a name the room has.

*Crown Heights — S. Kestrel, district office.* South Salt — *A. Neve, district works.* Outer Reach — *J. Tarrant, district office, Outer Reach.*

Three of those four surnames came off a board on the wall of that room and none of the three has been confirmed this year, and about four people in the office have said since that the board is the oldest and worst thing in the building and has never once been wrong about a building.

Those are about nine people in a city of four hundred thousand, and every one of the nine is a name somebody wrote in a box under a heading on a form, and **not one of the nine was asked, and not one of the nine has been told, and about four people in that room have said since that this is the most dangerous piece of stationery that has come off that machine in nineteen years and it is dangerous because it is four lines long and it is right.**

The fifth row is Mireward, and the line under *officer responsible* on the fifth row is empty, and it is empty for the reason that is printed on a card from the same machine every month, which is *Officer responsible: [blank]*.

He put the pen down about a foot away from it and said the thing he said in January, in about nine words, to a surveyor who had come in without an appointment.

"I am not writing a name in that box and I am going to tell you exactly why and then I am going to go and do a job. **If I put a name in the box for a district, then in about four years somebody asks that person what they were doing on the night of the twenty-ninth of January, and there is no column on this form for the answer.**"

"Mireward has no officer to put."

"Mireward has no officer, and Mireward has never had an officer, and the box is on the form anyway, and that is not the fault of the form." He picked the pen up again and used it to point at the top of the sheet. "**The box is a promise that somebody is on the other end of it. Mireward is the only row on this sheet where nobody is, and the form does not know that, and the form cannot know that, because this list of districts was printed from a list that is a hundred and forty years old and has never once been printed from a list of offices.**"

---

**And he was right, and being right cost seven days, and the seven days are the machine's and not the finding.**

A sheet that cannot be returned is not an outstanding allocation. It is a sheet in a drawer. He had written that on the form himself, years ago, in the ordinary way, and on the Friday the thirtieth the sheet went back to his own office with a line missing off it, and on the Friday the sixth of February, at about half past ten, the internal post brought him a **district returns card**, printed, in his own room, out of his own machine, and it took seven days and it is not an answer.

> **DISTRICT RETURNS — CURRENT QUARTER**
> *District: **MIREWARD***
> *Return required: quarterly ground and seam return.*
> *Last received: —*
> *Reason outstanding: allocation sheet w/e XXVI, officer column uncompleted.*
> *Officer responsible: [blank].*

Nobody in this city had ever put the word *office* next to that name on a piece of paper, and the machine had now asked the question a second time in nine days without being aware that it had asked it the first time, and the reason column was not a dash this time. The reason column had a sentence in it, because there was a sentence to print.

"So it is a hole with a heading on it," said Milo Rook.

"It is a hole with a heading on it and now it is a hole with a **reason** on it," said the man of about fifty, "and a reason is a thing a person can be sent to look at. In about four years a fella in a room is going to read *allocation sheet w/e XXVI, officer column uncompleted* and he is going to go and find out why, and he is going to come back here, and I am going to have to say to him that a form printed a row for eleven square miles of fen because a list is a hundred and forty years old."

---

**And the row itself, and the zero, and the fact that nobody has been and that this is now on paper twice.**

Mireward's row on the allocation sheet carries a **0** in the events-forecast column, and he typed it himself, and it is the only zero on the sheet, and it is true, and it is the second time in nine days that a machine in the city has recorded about Mireward that nothing has happened there.

There is one on a printed card from the same room, from the sixteenth of January, *last received — , reason outstanding — , officer responsible [blank]*, which is a nil with three blanks on it.

And there is one on the four pages, in a district stability column, entered by an office that signed no name, on the stated ground that **there is no maintained seam, no recorded loss, no resident witness and no schedule**, and marked *entered as stable*.

"Three," said Milo Rook. "It is three times and the first of the three is five weeks old and the second is a fortnight old and this one is today, and it is a district that has not had a person go and look for a season in forty-one years, and I would like it recorded that one of the three is a machine and one is a man with a pen and neither of them is anybody going to Mireward."

"I am fifty-one next month," said the man of about fifty, "and I have been in this room nineteen years and I have wanted to say something about that column since I read it in December and I am going to say it now and then I am going to do a job.

"**Nothing about Mireward is a record of Mireward. A place is not entered as nil because there is nothing in it. It is entered as nil because there has been nobody to write down what is in it, and the two things look identical on a form and they are not the same thing, and the second one is worse.** Percy goes out there on the first dry week of every summer with a rod and a chain and writes four lines about about nine feet of drain into a pad that is in a drawer in this office from this year and every year back to about 1969, and nobody has ever asked him for one of those pads, and he does not go in a season, and he does not go in a storm, and he would not know a seam from a wet drain if he stood in one, and he is fifty-eight and he has a bad knee in the second week of every March.

"And he is not an officer. Nobody is. And there is a box on that form under a heading and the box is empty, and in about four years the box is going to be the emptiest box in this office, and the emptiness of it is going to be the only thing anybody knows about eleven square miles of fen."

---

**And on the Friday the thirtieth at about half past three in the afternoon, a man in a road with a chain over his shoulder asked the only question worth asking about the sheet, and the man who printed it answered it in about nine words and it was not the answer the surveyor wanted.**

"Allocated to," said Milo Rook. "Four in the river core. Where did those four get allocated *to*?"

"To whoever is the officer responsible."

"And the officer responsible for the river core is a route-room supervisor of thirty-one years in a building nine hundred yards from a length of tram route that is not on the diagram."

"Yes."

"So the four events in the river core are allocated to a man who does not know a drain is off its frame."

"**A seam event is not a gang going out," said the man of about fifty. "I have been in this room nineteen years and I have never seen an event allocated and I have never seen one close, and what I will tell you is what the sheet is for. It is not for knowing. It is for having something on the day to give somebody, so that a district is told, and so that if a district says it was told, there is a piece of paper with its name on it. That is all a sheet like that has ever done in this city. It has never once told anybody what happened. It has only ever been the thing that was given."**

"And the allocation is not a claim that anybody looked."

"No. **An allocation is a claim that somebody was told, and the gap between those two things in this city is about eleven months and a filing cabinet, and that gap is where this season is going to happen.**"

---

**The Field Book, Friday the thirtieth of January, 23.10, a front room, and it is five lines and he says so at the top of it and the five lines are longer than they look.**

*Thirtieth, 23.10. Five lines and I am not padding them.*

*One. A printed sheet came out of the machine in the drainage section with five rows on it and four of them had a name in a box under a line and one of them did not, and the one that did not is Mireward, and the name in the other four was written by a man with a pen on the Monday out of a four-page document that has no name on it, and nobody has been asked. **A box that wants to know why is the amendment, and a box that wants to know who is there is a worse one, because the amendment at least is about a thing that has happened.***

*Two. The first entry anywhere in this city on any form about the twenty-ninth of January is five words and a time and two initials in a man's own hand in the river core row, and it says **no persons**, and no persons is the only honest answer available and it is the answer the form wanted.*

*Three. Mireward is a zero on a printed sheet and a dash and three blanks on a printed card and a nil in a column written by a desk that signed no name, and that is three times in nine days, and **nobody has gone, and I am not going to, and the reason I am not going to is that a man of fifty-eight goes out there in the summer with a rod and a chain and writes four lines about about nine feet of drain into a pad in a drawer, and a man who goes out in a summer and writes four lines about a drain is not an office and does not become one, and in forty-one years nobody has ever written the word office next to that name on a piece of paper.***

*Four. The sheet goes back to an office that cannot answer it and a query comes back out of the same machine seven days later asking the question that machine asked nine days ago, and the second time it is not a dash it is a sentence, and a sentence is a thing a person can be sent to look at. **I have not got a way round this and I am not going to invent one, and I notice that wanting one is the seventh column again and I am putting that down in the same book in which I keep the other one so that the two are in the same place.***

*Five. The page home the sixty-eighth day. Ten routes, ten. The token cold, fifty-fourth day. The tally has nine lines and I did not cross and I am not going to, and the last thing I wrote in this book that was mine was on the twelfth of January, and that is eighteen days, and I have not written it down since.*
