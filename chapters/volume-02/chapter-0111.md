# Chapter 111: A Monday Like Any Other

On Monday the twenty-seventh of April, at half past seven in the morning, eleven men went down six hundred yards of reserved tram route with a shovel each and the trolley, and the weekly sheet for that week has two dates in it, the twenty-seventh and the thirtieth, and the reason column is empty on both and the condition column is empty on both.

**That is the seventh walk in April with an empty condition column, and it is the first one in which the foreman did not look at it.**

Roy Pryce signed at the top, wrote the date, wrote the number of men, and put the pen in his pocket. It took about eleven seconds and he has never claimed it took longer, and at about ten past ten the same morning a man of thirty-one asked him about the column and got four sentences and a correction, and he answered all of it in about a minute and then went and looked at something else.

"The last walk anything went into that column was the twelfth of February and I have not read it once since, and I did not not-read it. **Reading it and not reading it are two different things and the difference is about a second and a half and the second and a half is not a decision anybody makes.**"

"And when somebody fills it in."

"Somebody will, one day, and it will be a Tuesday in a wet week and a man will come along who does not know what the column is, and he will put something in it, and it will be wrong, and in about four years somebody will read a sheet and see a fault on a Tuesday in May and a gap from February to May and be perfectly entitled to say that nobody was watching." He squared the sheet on his knee. "**There is no column on this sheet that wants to know why a column is empty and I am not going to build one, and I have not built one in eleven weeks and I have had about four times in eleven weeks when I wanted to, and I did not, and there is no rule.**"

Nobody wrote that down. It was the thirteenth time in fourteen weeks and about four people in the office noticed that nobody had, and one of them was a works clerk of forty-six who has a rule about it that is a number and not a sentence and who did not push.

---

**And the round went out at about ten past eight and came in at about four, seven stops in eleven streets, and it is a Monday and a Thursday round and it has been since the second of January, and nobody in this district decided that this morning and everybody in it knows it was decided.**

The second side of the sheet of who does what and on which day runs Monday to Sunday and travels in the satchel. It now has three blank Wednesdays in it that nobody in this district is able to correct: the eighth, the fifteenth and the twenty-second of April. A blank on that side is a day when nobody stood in the weather, and the sheet is right, and it has been right in its own terms about ninety times a fortnight since the second of February, and it is right about all three of those days, because on every one of them the only person who stood in the weather was a man of about sixty at a shed at Cardew Docks, eleven minutes away, and the sheet has never once been able to see him and is not going to be able to. **On the fifteenth the sheet was right as well, and about nine people in eleven streets stood in the weather for nothing at all, and that is the fourth thing in five months in this district that cannot be shown to anybody, and a blank that is right about a day it cannot see is not a fifth thing and I am not going to let it be one.**

"And there is a fourth coming on Wednesday," said Hettie Brann, at about half past eight, without looking up. "I have not written it and I am not going to write it, and I have been not writing it since the eighth of April and I am quite good at it now, and it takes about eleven seconds a week and I have not got a column for the eleven seconds either."

**The twenty-eight words a man of about forty put under a rule on the second side in March are still under it, undated and uninitialled, and the man of about forty knows they are there and has read them, and the man of about sixty they are about was at a shed at Cardew Docks on the eighth, the fifteenth and the twenty-second, and is going to be at a shed at Cardew Docks on the twenty-ninth, and does not know that a piece of paper exists, and is not going to be told, and about four people know that and the number is not going up.**

---

**And in a yard at about a quarter to ten on the same Monday morning, with about nine people in it, the thing that was said out loud was not a sentence anybody had prepared and it was about forty seconds long.**

"It is an ordinary Monday," said Rue.

"It is the first ordinary Monday since the second of January," said Milo Rook.

"**It is the first Monday since the second of January that this district has treated as an ordinary Monday, and the reason is not that nothing happened on it. The reason is that the machinery is still printing and the chair is not.**" She put both hands flat on the trestle table, which is a thing she has watched a man of thirty-one do, and which he learned off her and has never once said he learned it off her. "A district that is waiting for a chair is a district with a date. A district with no date has to be in the street in the morning, because in the street in the morning is the only thing it has got, and it is not nothing, and it is eleven weeks of it."

"Eleven weeks of a season," said somebody.

"**A season ending is not an event,**" said Milo Rook. "I have written that down in five different places since the fourteenth and I am going to say it out loud once and then I am not going to say it again this month. A season ending is a column that stops being printed. Nobody has to announce that a column has stopped being printed. There is no meeting. On the fourteenth of April four pages went out of an office that signed no name and they were the last four pages anybody sent, and every piece of machinery in this city is still running this morning, and every one of them is running toward a thing that is not going to be printed."

"And they will go on running."

"They will go on running until somebody stops them, and nobody is going to stop them, because a machine that is running has not been instructed to stop, and **an instruction is a document, and a document is the thing this district is not able to produce.**" He put his hand flat on the table. "So this district is now in the position of a thing that is being maintained by machinery that nobody is maintaining, and the honest name for that position is a length, and I have written that down three times and I am not going to write it a fourth."

Nell Crale said, "You are all talking about a machine and none of you have said what happens on Thursday."

"Eleven men and a foreman and a clipboard go down six hundred yards at half past seven on Thursday morning, the same as they did this morning, and the sheet goes in the rack on Friday, and that is the whole of Thursday and it is the whole of every day from here to the end of May."

"That is not what I asked and you know it is not."

"Then I will answer a question you have not asked, which is the only kind this yard has, and it is this: **nothing happens on Thursday either, and the reason nothing is going to happen in this district for about a year is that there is nothing left to happen to, and a thing with nothing left to happen to is not in a hearing, and it is not on a list, and it is not in a column, and it is being kept, and that is the whole difference between this district and a length of tram route and I have had that in my head since the twenty-second of April and I have not got a way of saying it that a form would take.**"

Nobody wrote it down. That was the twelfth time in fourteen weeks.

---

**The Field Book has two entries for this weekend and this Monday and neither of them is a pair and he has written that down about the seventh time and has said he is going to stop writing it down, and he has not stopped.**

*Twenty-sixth, 22.40. One day off the twenty-fifth. I am not pairing it. A day is a day and an interval is an interval and a run of days is not a list of intervals, and I have known the difference since the twenty-first of April and I have been getting it wrong in a different direction since Friday, and I am not going to write a third paragraph about it because a woman of forty-five has told me in a doorway that a rule with a second paragraph in it is an instruction, and a person can say no to an instruction, and nobody can say no to a line.*

*Twenty-seventh, 23.05. One day off the twenty-sixth.*

*One. **A column that has been empty for eleven weeks is not an empty column. It is a column nobody reads.** And the difference is about a second and a half at the top of a Monday morning, and a foreman of fifty-four gave me the second and a half in one sentence and then looked at something else, and he has been asked about a seventh column four times in six weeks by a man of thirty-one and he has not built one, and there is no rule and he is not going to write one, and I am not going to ask him for the number either.*

*Two. And the finding of the morning, which I have not got to the bottom of and am not going to: **a season ending is a column that stops being printed.** There is no notice of it, there is no meeting, there is no form, and there are four machines in this city still running toward a document that is not going to exist. And a district that cannot produce a document is a district that cannot be instructed, and a district that cannot be instructed has to be in the street in the morning, and being in the street in the morning is what eleven streets have been doing since the second of January and it is not a habit and it is not a column, and I do not know what it is and I have not written it down and I am not going to.*

*Three. Three blank Wednesdays on the second side, the eighth, the fifteenth and the twenty-second, and a fourth on the twenty-ninth, and the sheet is right about all four of them, and the reason it is right about them is a man of about sixty at a shed at Cardew Docks who has been there since before the war and is eleven minutes away, and on the fifteenth of April the sheet was right as well and about nine people in eleven streets stood in the weather for nothing at all. **I have got four things in five months in this district that cannot be shown to anybody and that is the fourth of them and I am not going to get a fifth out of a piece of paper that is right, and I have written that down tonight in a book in a coat and nobody has asked me to and nobody is going to.** The twenty-eight words under the rule are undated and uninitialled and the man of about forty has read them and has not asked the man they are about, and I have not asked either, and the man of about sixty does not know there is a piece of paper and is not going to be told. **A blank on a day is not a fault and a thing that is not wrong cannot be struck out and there is no column on that paper for a man who is somewhere else, and that sentence is not mine, it is a man of about forty with a broom, and I have now written it down for the fourth time and I am going to leave it alone where it is.** I did not go near the north end of that row this month and I have not gone near it since the nineteenth of February and I am not going to on Thursday.*

*The page home the hundred and fifty-fifth day, blank, four marks in four hands and none of them mine, and the fourth is a woman of sixty-one at a counter in about four seconds on the first of April and she refused to put it in an envelope. Ten routes, ten, none removed, and I have worked out this week why it is ten and not eleven and it is because I did not cross, and that is the first time in my working life I have been glad of a number and I do not trust being glad of a number. The token cold, a hundred and forty-first day, and it is in the inside pocket of this coat and it has not been out of it since the seventh of December and I have not taken it out and I have not been asked and I am not going to.*
