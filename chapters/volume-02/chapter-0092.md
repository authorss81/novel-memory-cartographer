# Chapter 92: Allocated To

The sheet for the week commencing Monday the second of March came off the same machine in the same room on the Friday morning the twenty-seventh of February at about ten o'clock, and it is the sixth of them, and on the Monday morning at about half past eight a man of about fifty picked it up off the top of a pile, which means he did not put it face down, and there is a difference between those two things that he noticed and did not comment on.

The figures went into the second column with a pen at about ten past nine. The four in the river core. The four in Crown Heights, which is a figure that is not a figure and is transferred because the four pages carry it in the same place. The four in South Salt. The one in the Outer Reach. The zero against Mireward.

**Then he came to the fourth column, which is the one that says *ALLOCATED TO*, and it is a different box from the one he refused in January, and on the twenty-seventh of February it was the box he had never filled in in nineteen years.**

He has said since that the difference between the two boxes is the whole of the second month of this season and that it took him a fortnight to work out what it was.

---

The box under *officer responsible* is a promise that somebody is on the other end of it. He said that in January and he was right and it cost him a query with a sentence in it, and he has said the sentence four times since to about nine people, and the query came back on the sixth of February and it said *allocation sheet w/e XXVI, officer column uncompleted*, and it is in a drawer.

The box under *allocated to* is a different thing. It is not a promise. **It is a record of a promise being kept.**

*Allocated to* means: this event is in this district, and this person has been given it, and here is where it goes. It is the only column on the entire printed side that says a person has been told anything. The other four columns are about places and numbers and a form. That one is about an act, and the act is a person handing another person a piece of paper on a day.

**And nothing on that printed side has ever been filled in, in any week, by anybody, in nineteen years, because in nineteen years the season has not run twice.**

"I have never filled that column in," said the man of about fifty, to a surveyor who had come in without an appointment for the fourth time in five weeks. "I have signed the forms and I have typed the cards and I have written the figures out with a pen every Monday for nine Mondays, and that column is empty on every sheet in this room and it has been empty on every sheet in this room since before I came."

"Then what is it for."

"It is for **filling in.** That is what it is for, and I am the man who fills it in, and I have known since about ten past nine this morning what I am going to have to do about it and I have not done it and I am not going to do it in front of you."

---

**And what he had to do about it was three names, off a board on the wall of that room that has never once been wrong about a building and has been in that room longer than he has.**

The January sheet had four names in the fifth column, off the board, and none of the four had been asked and none of the four had been told. The river core box said *D. Farr, route room, Civic Atlas Office*, and a man of about fifty said out loud in January that it is not even true.

The fourth column is a different matter, and he said so before he picked the pen up, in about eleven words, and Milo Rook wrote them down and has not improved them.

"**A name in *allocated to* is not a mistake. A name in *officer responsible* is a lie about a person. A name in *allocated to* is a statement that a person was handed a piece of paper on a day, and if that did not happen then the person I have written down has been put on a piece of paper as having done a thing they did not do.**"

"That is a bigger thing than January."

"That is a much bigger thing than January and it took me a fortnight to see it and I am not going to pretend I saw it in ten minutes this morning, because I did not, and I have been not seeing it since the twenty-ninth of January and the reason I did not see it is that I have been looking at the box I already had an opinion about."

He filled in three of them. Crown Heights, South Salt, the Outer Reach, off the wall board, in a hand that is his own, and the fourth box in the river core row he left for about nine minutes and then he went and stood at the window for about four of them, which is a thing a works clerk of forty-six did on the twelfth of January and has since told about nine people.

The fifth one he did not fill in at all, and this is a different refusal from January's, and the reason is different, and it took him about twenty words and he said them in the wrong order first.

"No."

"January—"

"**January I would not put a name in *officer responsible* for a district nobody has been to, because in about four years somebody asks that person what they were doing on the night of the twenty-ninth of January and there is no column on that form for the answer. This is not that.** This is that there is nobody to hand it to. I could write *Percy* in that box and it would be a lie about a man of fifty-eight who goes out in the summer with a rod and a chain and writes four lines about about nine feet of drain into a pad in a drawer, and he does not know this sheet exists, and **if his name is on a piece of paper then somebody in about four years is going to ask Percy why Mireward was not allocated in the first week of March, and Percy is fifty-eight and has a bad knee in the second week of every March, and he would have to say that he has never heard of it, and then somebody in a room has written down that Mireward has no officer responsible, and I have just done the January thing to him in a different column and got further with it.**"

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds.

"That is a different refusal," said Milo Rook, "and it is better, and I am not going to say so in a room for about a year, and I am writing it down here and now so that the not-saying is a fact."

---

**And the fourth box, the river core one, is the one he came back to, and it took him the rest of the morning, and the thing that decided it was not a principle at all.**

It was that Dilley Farr is in this building, thirty-one years, on the second of the two tables under the north window, and walks to about eleven o'clock for a cup of tea, and the man of about fifty has watched that for nineteen years from a room with a machine in it.

"A name in that box is a claim that Dilley Farr was handed a piece of paper about a seam event on a day, and the day is the one the four pages say it is, and there is no day. **If I put him in and nothing is ever allocated, then I have put a man who is not on the diagram and does not know this sheet exists into a box that says somebody was told, and there is nobody to tell him, because telling him would be telling him there is a day, and there is not one.**"

"So the box is blank."

"The box is blank and the reason it is blank is a good one and I want it written down that it is a good one, because in about nine weeks somebody in an office is going to read four empty boxes on six consecutive sheets and conclude that nothing has been allocated anywhere, and they will be right about four districts and they will be wrong about this one, and **I am not going to be the man who makes that easier by putting a name in it.**"

---

**And on the Friday the sixth of March, at about half past ten in the morning, the internal post brought the sheet back into the room it came out of, and it is a printed card and it is the size of a postcard and about nine hundred of them a month come off that machine, and the reason column has a sentence in it again.**

> **DISTRICT RETURNS — CURRENT QUARTER**
> *District: **RIVER CORE***
> *Return required: quarterly ground and seam return.*
> *Last received: 30.i. (partial)*
> *Reason outstanding: **allocation sheets w/e XXIII and II, allocated-to column uncompleted.***
> *Officer responsible: [blank].*

Three sentences' worth of machinery in about two hundred years of forms: it knows the week commencing dates, it knows that two sheets went back, it knows which column was left empty on both, and **it has stopped printing a dash.**

"It has learned the word uncompleted," said Milo Rook.

"It printed that in February about a district nobody has been to and it took seven days to learn the word and about four weeks to get round to a district that has got a length and a Row in it." The man of about fifty put the card face down, which is what he does now. "**That is the eighth time that machine has said a sentence back to me since the sixteenth of January and every one of the eight has been about a box I did not fill in, and I would like somebody in a room in about four years to be able to read that sentence and understand that it is not a department being difficult with a fella in a room. It is a machine doing the only thing it can do with a hole, and a machine is a much worse thing than a man about being difficult, because a man stops and a machine does not.**"

---

**And at about ten past one in the afternoon of the same Friday, four days later than the Monday she had it written down for, in the half hour between one and half past one that the counter is shut, a woman of sixty-one did the second of the three things she has a rule about, and it took forty seconds and she has written the date on the outside of an envelope in her own ink every time and has never once read the route on any of the four.**

Three of the four copies said version one and one said version two and that is what they said on the second of February and that is what they said and nothing else was read, and the fourth has been read since before she held the envelope and she is not going to find out and if she finds out she will not use it.

What was different in March was a number. She had started writing the count on the outside of the envelope, in the corner, in pencil, in her own hand, on the second of February, and the count said *1 of 4 differs*, and on the sixth of March the count said *1 of 4 differs* and she had checked the two versions with her own hand twice and about four people watched her do it.

"One," she said, out loud, in front of about four people, "and I have said the number and not the finding, because the finding is somebody else's business and the number is mine, and I want that said because I have been in this building nineteen years and I have never before written a number on the outside of an envelope about other people's paper."

Somebody said, "Is it going to go down."

"No," said Hettie Brann. "**It is not going to go down and I have now written a number about it twice and the number is not going to get better, and a number that cannot get better is the only kind of number I have ever been willing to put on a piece of paper.** A person is a number that can get better. A round is a number that can get better. This is four pieces of paper in four buildings with three hands on them and a disagreement that started in January because a man of fifty-four wrote seven lines about a gate for nobody, and it is not going to resolve itself, and it is not supposed to, and on the first of April I am going to do it a third time and it is going to say the same number and I am going to want to write something else next to it and I am not going to."

---

**The Field Book, Friday the sixth of March, 22.50, a front room, and it is the first entry since Monday and the gap is eleven days and the gap is the largest since the twenty-five in January and he is not going to pair it and says so at the top of it.**

*Sixth, 22.50. Eleven days. I have not written since the twenty-third and that is the largest gap since the twenty-five of January and I am not writing the two ends of it down, because a silence is not a pair, and I have been told that by this book four times now and I have not taken it on trust from the book.*

*One. A column I have never seen filled in, in six sheets, in nineteen years. *Events forecast / event / allocated to / officer responsible.* **The first of those columns is a place. The last is a promise that somebody is on the other end of it. The second from the right is a record of the promise being kept, and it is the only one of the four on that printed side that is about a person doing an act, and the act is handing somebody a piece of paper on a day, and there is no day.** A man of fifty-eight with a rod and a chain and a bad knee in the second week of every March and a pad nobody has ever asked him for, and a refusal in twenty words that is not the January refusal and is better than it, and three names off a wall board in a hand, and a fourth box left blank because the man in it walks to the north window at about eleven for a cup of tea and has never heard of this sheet.

*Two. And the machine has stopped printing a dash. **The reason column on a district returns card out of a room in Crown Heights now carries sentences, and it has carried one every week since the sixth of February, and every one of them is about a box that was not filled in by a man who was right.** I want to write down the thing that is underneath it and I have not got a sentence: in about four years somebody in an office is going to read *allocated-to column uncompleted* on two consecutive weeks and conclude that nothing was allocated anywhere in the river core, and they will be right about four districts and wrong about this one, and there is no way to put the half of it that is true in a column, because the column is one cell in a printed side and **the half of it that is true is a cast-iron cover rocking about two inches proud of six hundred yards of reserved tram route and a hundred and ten yards of frontage that is not a street, and neither of those is a district.**

*Three. One of four differs, written in pencil on the outside of an envelope by a woman of sixty-one who was not asked to write a number, and who says it is the only kind of number she has ever been willing to put on a piece of paper. She is right. **A count of houses goes up. A count of people goes up or down. A count of documents that do not agree with each other is the only count in this district that is not a complaint about the world, and it cannot be got better by anybody working harder, and it is going to say the same number on the first of April, and she has said in advance that she is going to want to write something else next to it and is not going to.**

*Four. And the standing thing. I did not cross, I did not hold anything and I did not go in anywhere, and there is now no question in this district that needs me to. The page home the hundred and third day, blank, three marks in three hands, and nobody has asked anybody to fill it in since the twenty-seventh of December and the district is not going to. Ten routes, ten. The token cold, eighty-ninth day. **I have got a number in this book and a number on an envelope and a four on a printed sheet that has not gone down in nine weeks, and three of those three are written by somebody else, and I have noticed that, and I am not going to make a column out of noticing it.***
