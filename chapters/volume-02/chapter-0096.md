# Chapter 96: Nine Doors

Ola Petrossian is twenty-three, she has been in the job four months, and she has never been past the tram steps, and on Sunday the twenty-second of March she walked about four hours in eleven streets and found nine doors with standing water in them and she did it on her own time and she was not asked and she was not sent.

The reason she did it is eleven words long and she gave them to about nine people in a yard on the Sunday afternoon and they are on the page because she said them at volume, which she does not ordinarily do, and because she has said since that she has been a nurse for four months and has not done anything like it before and would like it noticed that she is frightened of having done it.

"**Then what is a water clinic for on a Tuesday morning?**" she said. "If it is not open at two o'clock in the morning, and Dr Sen is right that it cannot be, and the round comes past the door at about a quarter past eight, then who is standing in the kitchen at about nine o'clock when the water is two inches deep in it, and how does anybody know, and what is the clinic for."

Nobody answered, and about four people have said since that it is the best question anybody has asked in a yard in five months and that it is a question about a service and not about a street, and that nobody in the district had noticed it was a question.

---

**What she did was simple and it took four hours and it is the first piece of paper in this district that is about places and not about people, and that is the reason it matters and the reason it is frightening.**

She has a card in a frame at the clinic that says the counter is open from eight until six and that the round passes at about a quarter past eight and about a quarter to four, and it is there because a nurse needs to know when a woman with a satchel is going to walk past the door. On Sunday the twenty-second she took a clinic form to the eleven streets and wrote on the back of it, in her own hand, one line per door, and a line was a door and a number and a yes or no, and nothing else.

She had decided that before she left, and she has said so, and the decision is in four words and they are *doors and not people*, and the reason is that a nurse of twenty-three has watched a doctor refuse a claim twice in three months and has read a sheet out of her own book four times and has worked out that a sentence about a person is a record of a person, and **that a door is not a person, and that is the only difference that has ever been available to her.**

Nine of them. The water was in eight of them on the Sunday and in five of them on the Wednesday of the twenty-fifth, and the numbers were in no way in order and the distances were in no way in order, and about four people have said that the whole list could be drawn on the back of a fella's hand and that it is the first thing anybody in this district has produced that fits on a hand.

**And the ninth is a side door on a store that is shut, at the low end of Bridge Street, and a store is not a dwelling, and nobody in this district has ever counted a store.**

"The other eight are houses," said Ola Petrossian, "and the ninth is not, and I want to say why I put it on and I am going to say it badly. **It is a side door and there is water under it and it has got nothing to do with the front of the place, and I have been four hours in eleven streets and nobody else has been four hours in eleven streets, and if I had not written that door down then in a month somebody would have written it down as a house.**"

---

**And Milo Rook objected, in a yard, in about eleven seconds, and was refused by the person the objection was about, and has written that down and has not improved it.**

"You cannot keep that."

"I can keep it. It is on the back of a clinic form and it is doors and it is in my bag and it is not in the machine."

"**A list of doors is the first step onto a rate book.** A rate is built on a property and a person in a room is not a property and a rate cannot be built on a door, and I have watched a woman of about thirty-four sign her own name to a levy in a hall because there was no rateable basis for a block with no street, and I am not saying that to you, I am saying it because a list of doors is what a list of doors is the first of."

"I know what a list of doors is," said Ola Petrossian. "I have been in this district four months and I have been the person at the back of a counter since the middle of January and I have heard everything anybody in this yard has said about a list and a name and a schedule and I have been *taking the sheets*, Mr Rook. **I am not going to be the woman who holds the fourth copy of a card and does not know what is in it. I have watched that happen and I do not know how anybody lives with it.**"

Somebody said, "She has answered you."

"She has answered me and she is right and I am not going to argue with a twenty-three-year-old about her own bag in a yard on a Sunday," said Milo Rook. "**I am going to write down that I was right to object and that being right is not the same as being the person who decides, and I have written that sentence down four times since January and this is the first time I have been on the wrong side of it in public.**"

---

**And at about ten past four on the afternoon of the same Sunday, at the counter, a woman of sixty-one who has been on that counter nineteen years read nine lines on the back of a clinic form and did not put it in the machine, and said out loud, in front of about eleven people, what she had done and what it was the second time of.**

"There is no box on that machine for a list," she said. "I have looked, twice, and I did not know that until a month ago and now I know it every day. **And I made one of these in January, face down under this counter, and it is a piece of paper about a man with no rateable unit, and I said in front of nine people that I was not going to be the one asked about it in four years, and I have not been asked, and I am sixty-one, and here is the second one.**"

She put it face down under the counter, on top of the other one.

"It goes under, not in," she said. "**And I am going to say the thing that neither of the two of you has said, and it is the reason I have done it twice and not once. In nineteen years this counter has never had a piece of paper on it that is about a place. I have got nine names faces down under here and I have got a doctor who writes both facts in one line on purpose, and everything in this room is about a person, and a person is a thing you can be hurt with. A door is not. A door is a hole in a wall with an address on the other side of it that nine people already know. If that list goes anywhere it goes to a surveyor or a clerk or a rate, and a rate needs a property, and this is not a property, so the list will sit there until somebody in about four years asks what a door is a rateable unit for, and by then whoever wrote it has been doing it for four years and has about nine of them.**"

"You want it to be useless," said Milo Rook.

"I want it to be **wrong-shaped,**" said Hettie Brann. "The moment a thing can be used it is in use. I have said that a woman with a satchel says and I have got nothing against it and I have just watched a man of thirty-one say it in a yard about a rate book and be right, and **being right is the problem. I have made two pieces of paper in this building on purpose and I would like somebody to write down that I have made two and that I am not going to make three without saying so out loud, and I have said it out loud, and now it is on a Saturday's memory in eleven streets and that is the price and I have paid it in advance like a woman who knows what she is doing.**"

---

**And on Sunday the twenty-second of March at about ten past six in the evening, a line went on the second side of the sheet of who does what and on which day, and it is the only line on that side for that week, and it is in her own hand and it has her initials against it, and she did not tell anybody and told about four people on the Monday because she could not help it.**

And a man of about forty swept that row at ten past six on the Wednesday and put his twenty-eight words under the rule and did not initial them and has not dated them, and a man of about sixty was at a shed at Cardew Docks on that Wednesday and does not know that either of those two things happened, and will not be told.

---

**The Field Book, Sunday the twenty-second of March, 20.40, a front room, and it is four days off the eighteenth, and it is the first four in this book, and I do not know what to do with that, so I am writing the number down and nothing else about it.**

*Twenty-second, 20.40. Four days. The pair in front of this one is a four off the eighteenth and there is one four in this book until this minute and one six and it is a six, and I have looked at the two one-day gaps in the last fortnight and written down neither of them.*

*One. **A nurse of twenty-three went into eleven streets for four hours on a Sunday in her own time and came back with nine doors and no names, and it is the first thing anybody in this district has written down in five months that is about a place and not about a person, and she decided on the doorstep that it would be doors and not people and she has said that, and she is frightened of it, and she is going to do it again.** And when I said the true thing about it, which is that a list of doors is the first step onto a rate book and that a rate cannot be built on a door, **she said she had been taking the sheets since the middle of January and that she is not going to be the woman who holds the fourth copy of a card and does not know what is in it.** She is twenty-three. I have been not knowing what is in the fourth copy since the second of February and I have made a rule out of not knowing, and she has made a rule out of knowing, and both of those rules are about the same piece of paper in a drawer, and hers is better and she is younger than me and she has never been thanked either.

*Two. And this is the thing I have to write down and I have been putting it off since about half past four this afternoon. **A woman of sixty-one has now made two pieces of paper on purpose at a counter in this district and has said so out loud in a room of about eleven and has promised not to make a third without saying so, and that is a cost and it is hers and it is the first one anybody in this district has named in advance.** And she said a sentence that I have written out in full because I cannot do it justice and I am not going to improve it: she wants it wrong-shaped. **The moment a thing can be used it is in use.** And I have been in five months building things in this district that can be used, on purpose, out of the best possible material, with my own hands.

*Three. And the other thing, and I am going to write it as a sentence and not as a list because I have learned that about myself. **A blank on the second side of that sheet is a day when nobody stood in the weather, and it has been blank on every Wednesday since the second of February, and a man of about sixty has been standing in the weather eleven minutes away every Wednesday since before the war, and the sheet has been right, in its own terms, seven weeks running.** And there is no column for it and there never will be and a man of about forty put twenty-eight words under a rule in a fishmonger's hand about it and would not initial them, and a woman of forty-five would not add a paragraph to her own rule because the moment there is a second paragraph there is an instruction in it and a person can say no to an instruction. **That is the whole of what has changed in this district in three weeks and it was done by a man with a broom and a fishmonger and neither of them has ever met me at the top of that row at half past five and I am not going to be introduced and I am not going to ask.**

*Four. A rail at a shed. I have written that down and I am not going to take it back and I want it read to me in April: **I said out loud in a yard today that a list of doors is the first step onto a rate book, and I was right, and I was refused by the person it was about, and I have written down that being right is not the same as being the person who decides.** That is the fourth time in five months that I have been the fastest person in a room about a document and the first time that being the fastest person in a room has cost me nothing at all, and I have written down that I did not like it, and I have written down that not liking it is the price and I am going to pay it, and I am not going to be thanked for that either and nobody has offered.

*The page home the hundred and nineteenth day, blank, three marks in three hands, and there is now a fourth reason nobody in this district asks about it, which is that the only person who has offered to write it in the last month was the man of about forty and he would not initial it. Ten routes, ten. The token cold, hundred and fifth day. The tally has nine lines and I have not looked at it and I am not going to, and I have written that sentence in this book since the nineteenth of January and I have decided this month to keep it up for the rest of the season whatever it turns out to cost, and the season ends on the fourteenth of April and there is nothing printed for the day after that and I have just noticed that I know that and I have not said it out loud to anybody yet and I am going to.*
