# Chapter 115: A Boundary Needs Two Ends

There is a boundary at the low end that is a hundred and forty feet long and one of its ends is a corner in a street and the other end is a fence, and on Saturday the second of May, at about ten in the morning, Milo Rook put a tape against the corner and walked it on his own.

**A boundary is not a line. It is two men starting at two ends and walking towards each other, and the boundary is where they meet, and if they do not meet there is no boundary and there is a piece of ground that two departments will each believe they have.**

He had put two pegs back in on the twenty-eighth of April because the March returns had not been signed, and on the Friday afternoon he had gone back to look at them because he had a thing about a boundary, and one of the two was standing in the road surface and the other was about eleven inches out in a gutter, and neither of them was wrong and the one in the gutter had been in that gutter since February.

He wrote nothing on a form about it. He wrote the two distances in his own book against the date and nothing else, and the two distances are a number, and a number in his own book against a date is a fact about a boundary and not a fact about a person, and he has been unable to tell the difference for seven months and has decided for the time being to go on not telling it.

---

**At about eleven o'clock a works gang came on the fortnightly inspection and there were eleven of them and they walked the hundred and forty feet of frontage at about the speed of eleven men who are not looking for anything in particular, and they stopped at the fence, because a gang stops at a fence.**

Nobody told them anything. There was nothing to tell them. The fence is a fence and the plate that was on it is in the grass on the far side and has been since the sixth of April, and it is now twenty-six days, and the grass has closed over the place where it went down, and a man of about twenty-six in a high-visibility jacket walked past a place in the grass on the far side of a fence and did not look at it and was not asked to and would not have.

**And Milo Rook, standing on this side with a tape in his hand, did not say one word, and there is no column on a gang sheet for a fence, and there is no column for a plate, and there is no column for a field, and the gang's condition column on the weekly sheet for the walk they did on Thursday the thirtieth of April is empty, and it is the eighth walk in April and the first in May with an empty condition column, and the reason column is empty too and that is correct.**

Nobody wrote anything down. That was the fifteenth time in fifteen weeks.

---

**And here is what happened, and it happened by accident, and it took about four minutes, and it is the whole of what Milo Rook got out of a Saturday morning in May.**

The gang came to the fence and stopped, the way a gang stops, because the far side is not theirs and has never been theirs and there is no column that says it is. And the corner end of the boundary was at the shoulder of a man of about thirty-one with a tape in his hand. And so the two ends of a hundred and forty feet of boundary were at the same place at eleven o'clock on a Saturday morning in May, for about four minutes, because a gang stops at a fence and a fence is an end.

**A boundary needs two ends. It does not need two surveyors, and it does not need anybody to know what a boundary is, and it certainly does not need a plate.**

He wrote that down at about half past four that afternoon in the second of the two entries he has made for that day, and then he read it back and did not improve it, and then he read it back a second time and wrote one line under it, and the line is the only reason the whole of that Saturday is in a book at all.

*And the other end is also in the grass, about eleven minutes that way, face down, and a plate is a thing that a decision gets put on from the far side, and the decision is the one the two men made when they met, and the plate cannot tell you which end the fence is on, and there is nobody on the far side to put one up, and there has not been for six years, and a plate is the wrong shape for that, and so is a fence, and so is a field, and so is a woman of sixty-three with a hand barrow, and I have been trying since January to work out what shape the thing is that is left when you have taken the plate and the fence and the field and the woman away, and the answer is that there is no shape and that the boundary is still there and it is at the fence because the gang stopped there.*

---

**And at about ten past four on the same afternoon a man of fifty-four stood in a yard in four rooms above a fitter's shop and said nine words that were not about a fence.**

"Your second peg was in the gutter in February and it is in the gutter now and it is in the gutter for a reason, and the reason is that the low end floods about twice in four years and the gutter is the only thing on that length that moves, and everything that moves gets set again by whatever moved it, and there is nobody out here to set it, and there has not been since the eleventh of October and you know that."

"Who sets it."

"**Nothing does and nobody should, and the honest answer is that the boundary is now wherever the water puts it, twice in four years, and that is not a boundary, and a form that wants a chainage is going to be wrong twice in four years, and it will be wrong in the direction of the water, and a form cannot be wrong in a direction.**" He put the pen down. "I have written the same six words in the same column for thirty-one years and the six words are the length and the men and the date, and if you put a seventh column on that sheet wanting to know why a peg is in a gutter, then in about four years somebody reads a boundary that is wrong twice in four years and does not know which of the two times he is looking at, and he will pick the one that suits the return he is filling in."

"I have not written a seventh column."

"I do not know how many of them you have written and I am not going to ask you, and that is not politeness, it is that a man who has been asked how many of them he has written will tell you, and then it is a number, and I have watched about nine hundred forms come through this office and a number on one of them is worse than nothing on one of them." He picked the pen up again. "Go and do your boundary. **And do not go over the fence, and not because I have told you not to. There is nobody on the far side to stop you and there has not been for six years, and that is exactly why you should not go, and I have not got a better reason than that and I am not going to look for one.**"

---

**The Field Book, Saturday the second of May, 23.15, a front room, one day off the first, and there are two entries in this book for this day and both of them are about the same Saturday and neither of them is a pair.**

*Second, 23.15.*

*One. **A boundary needs two ends, and the two ends of a hundred and forty feet at the low end were at the same place at eleven o'clock this morning for about four minutes, because a works gang stops at a fence and a fence is an end.** A man of thirty-one with a tape was at the corner and eleven men who are not surveying came down the length and stopped where they always stop, and for about four minutes the boundary was walked from both ends by people who did not know they were doing it, and it is the only boundary I have ever seen that I did not have to explain to anybody.*

*Two. And the plate, and the third time I have written a sentence about it and the last: it is face down in the grass about eleven minutes that way and the grass has closed over the place it went down, and that is a fact about a field and not a document, and a plate is the wrong shape for a boundary because a plate is what a decision gets put on from the far side and the decision is made in the middle by two people walking towards each other and neither of them is at the fence. **There is nobody on the far side to put one up. There has not been for six years. That is not a reason to go over and it is the reason not to, and a foreman of fifty-four gave me that in nine words at ten past four this afternoon and said he had not got a better one and was not going to look for one, and I have written down that he had not got a better one, and I am not going to improve on his sentence with a hundred words of mine, and I have not.**

*Three. And what a boundary is for, and it is the plainest thing in this book and it took me seven months and a fence. **A boundary is a place where two people agreed to have stopped.** Not a line on a sheet. Not a plate. Not a peg — a peg is a point, and a boundary is not a point, and a peg only says where somebody started, and a man of fifty-four told me today that the low end floods about twice in four years and the boundary is now wherever the water puts it, and that is not a boundary, and a form that wants a chainage is going to be wrong twice in four years and it will be wrong in the direction of the water and a form cannot be wrong in a direction. There is no column for a peg in a gutter and there is no column for a peg anywhere, and I have made six of those mistakes since the fifteenth of March and today a man who has written the same six words in the same column for thirty-one years told me what the seventh one would cost and it is not my name on it, it is a man in about four years picking the wrong one of two floodings to suit a return he is filling in.*

*Four. And the pin went. He got a pin and drove it into the road surface about nine inches from the gutter peg and marked the road peg's true place, and the gutter peg he left where it is, and he has written both numbers in this book and nowhere else, and a pin in a road is a thing a man with a hammer puts in for a morning and it is not a boundary and it is not a record and it will be gone in about eleven years and by then there will be a hole in the road surface and a man will put a pin in the hole and not know why. **That is not a seventh column. I have checked it four times this evening and it is not a seventh column, and I am going to leave the sentence there and I am not going to add a why to it, and I am aware that the reason I am able to check it four times is that a woman of about thirty-four told me in a doorway a fortnight ago that the only custody in this city that cannot be produced is a head, and a head is not a column, and I did not understand that for two days and then I did and I have not been right since.** I have folded a sheet in at the back of this book with the thirtieth of April written on the fold and I am not going to take it out and I am not going to put it anywhere else, and that is the sixth of six and it is the worst of the six and I have said so out loud to a woman of sixty-one at ten past one on Friday and she has not thanked me and is not going to.*

*Five. The walks, and the only two there have been this month, and both of them are in April and both of them are empty. **The twenty-seventh of April was the seventh walk in April with an empty condition column and the thirtieth was the eighth, and I have written eight down in a fortnight and I have not written the word eight anywhere except here, and a man of fifty-four looked at the column on the twenty-seventh for about nine seconds and then did not look at it again, and I asked him about it and he said that reading it and not reading it are two different things and the difference is about a second and a half and the second and a half is not a decision anybody makes, and I have been using that sentence about everything since Monday.** The first walk in May is tomorrow. There is no column anywhere in this city for a walk that went normally, and that is not a fault, and I am not going to put a why next to it, and I have written four sentences this month about things I am not going to put a why next to, and four sentences about not doing a thing is a column, and I know it is a column, and I am writing it down anyway because the alternative is a book with one sentence in it.*

*The page home the hundred and sixtieth day, blank, and it is there. Ten routes, ten. The token cold, a hundred and forty-sixth day, and it is in the inside pocket of this coat and I stood about eleven feet from a fence this morning for about an hour and a half with nothing in my hands but a tape, and I did not think about it once, and that is the second time in four days and I have written it down both times and I am going to write it down once more and then I am going to stop, and I do not know what the rule is for stopping, and I have not got a woman of forty-five to ask.*
