# Chapter 129: Four Feet From A Desk

On Monday the fifteenth of June, at about half past eight in the morning, a man of thirty-one picked up a card from under a brick on a trestle table in eleven streets, read four characters off the front of it, and put it back under the brick.

**The line on the front of it was not taken off.** There are three lines on the back of it and the third is in a clerk's hand and begins *Checked by the timetable division. The run column is the 07:52. The move column is a light engine and a two-car set to a gate and it is not it. Not for index. — B.* He has not written down what that clerk told him and he has recorded that as a decision. There are three things under that brick and there have been three since the twenty-first of January, and the third of them is a boy's blank card, and it is blank, and nobody has taken it and nobody is going to.

He did not go to the timetable division. He did not ask anybody anything. Nobody was sent anywhere, and nobody in this district has sent anybody anywhere about a working timetable since the twenty-fourth of April, and the division has not said its sentence once since then, and the sentence is still true, and it is in a book, and it will keep.

---

**And at about ten o'clock the same morning, in a corridor in Crown Heights, a woman of thirty-one had nine words and then did not say any more about it.**

"You have not asked me for anything in three weeks."

"I have not."

"I have noticed, and I am not going to make anything of it, and I have got a page in a drawer with a coat in it that has been there five months and I have not produced it, and you have not asked, and that is the arrangement and I have not changed it and you have not changed it." She had a folder under her arm and she did not open it. "**I am not going to say anything else about the drawer. Not this week and not in June. That is not me being cold, that is me having said it in a corridor in May and it being the whole of what I had, and I have not got a second thing and I am not going to go and find one.**"

"I know."

"You do not know, and that is fine, and I have stopped requiring anybody in this district to know things." She turned the folder over. "**Now the other one. It is the ninth week and you have not told me about the season and you are not going to and I am not going to ask, and I want that in a corridor in June in exactly the words I am using and not in a room and not in a minute.**"

"Ninth week."

"Ninth week. And you have not asked me about the seventh of January since the fifth of May and you have stopped counting and started dating, which is worse, because a date is a column, and I have watched a man work that out about himself in about four seconds and then not do anything about it for four months." She looked at him. "**You are going to keep doing that. I am not going to ask you to stop and you are not going to stop and there is no column for either of us. That is the whole of what this is and it is not going to improve in a year and I have stopped waiting to see whether it does.**"

Then she said the other thing, which was work, and it took about a minute.

"You have had two returns out of a tram authority's district office in three weeks and you have told me neither of them, and you are not going to, and I would not have taken one if you had."

"You are a party to a proceeding in a building about four hundred yards from that office and there is no column on a return for a party, and I have not put it in my own book with a date on it, because it has a date on it, printed, in the wrong column, and I have said it in nine words at a gate to a man who is on no list at all."

"That is correct and it is a very cold way to be correct." She put the folder down. "**It is also the first thing in six months you have done that I would not have done, and I am not going to be warm about it in a corridor, and I want you to hear that the warmth is not coming and the not-coming is not a punishment, it is a Tuesday.**"

---

**And at about half past eleven, at Alder Quay, in a depot office, a woman of fifty-one with twenty-two years in the room said nine words before he had got the coat off.**

"You have not come here for six months."

"I have come here about nine times in seven months and I have not come for a book."

"Then I am going to do my work and you are going to stand there." She counted fares off a tray. "That is a working timetable and it is on that shelf and it is four feet from where I sit, and nobody has ever asked me for it, and the last man who asked me for it worked in this room before I did."

**The book is a working timetable for the division. It is bound. It is not a map and it is not a return and it is not a record of anything that happened. It is a printed list of the workings of a tram service, day by day, with columns.**

The columns are: *day. time. working. cars. run no. crew. terminates. remarks.*

A terminus is where a working ends. Every working in that book has one, and every terminus in that book is on the running diagram, and the book is printed from the diagram and the diagram is printed from a list, and the list is a hundred and forty years old, and there is no column in that book for a destination that is not on the diagram, and there has never been one, because a column does not check.

"I need the destination," said Hester Coombe, at about a quarter to twelve. "**I am booking a crew for Monday and the crew is a number and a number is not a car. A number is a body of people and they are booked to a working, not to a car, and the working is where they go, so I have to have the working.** That is my job. That is not you and it is not a favour and I have not done it for you and I would not."

"You are looking up the sixteenth of December."

"I am looking up whatever working that crew is on, and the crew is on the seventh and it is on the ninth and it is on the sixteenth of December, and the same four characters are on a card in this office with my hand on it that I put in a satchel in January and a district took out of a satchel and has had under a brick ever since, and I have not asked for it back and I am not going to." She had the book open on the desk. "**I have not been asked about a morning in December. Nobody has asked me about a morning in December. I have told you that on purpose in case anybody writes it down later: nobody asked me.**"

---

**And she turned to December, and December is a third of the way back, and it took her about eleven seconds, and then she stopped with her finger on the row and did not say anything for about four seconds.**

"Tuesday the sixteenth. 07:52 off Crown Heights." She read across it. "One car. Run number, four characters, and it is the same four characters. **And that is in the type, and it has been in that book in that type since it was printed, and it is not on a card under a brick anywhere, and it is on about a shelf in a room in this city, and it is not lost, and it has never been lost, and nobody in the world has to find anything.**"

"And the crew column."

"Four characters as well. Same four. It is the same crew and the same working and the same run and the same morning and it is all printed and it has all been printed for years." She moved her finger one column along. "And the move column, at a quarter to eight, a light engine and a two-car set to a gate, which takes nobody, and I have known that since the twenty-fifth of April because somebody wrote it on a card and I did not write it."

She stopped again.

"**And the terminates column for the 07:52 says Hollowgate.**"

---

**He had heard it. He wrote it in his own book on the Monday night and he has written it four times since, and it is four words and one of them is a place that is not a place.**

She said it again, which she was not asked to do, in the voice of a woman reading a column.

"Hollowgate."

"Have you got it on the diagram."

"No." She shut the book about halfway, which is how you shut a book you are not going to argue with. "**There is no Hollowgate. There has never been a Hollowgate. I have been in this room for twenty-two years and I have booked about eleven crews a week for twenty-two years and I know every terminus in this book, and it is not one of them, and it is in the column that only ever holds a place that is on the diagram, and there is a Hollowgate in that column in the type, and the type has been in that book since before I came.**"

"What is Hollowgate."

"It is a word with a H in it that is spelt H-O-L-L-O-W-G-A-T-E and it is a place, because it is in that column, and it is not a place." She squared the tray of fares. "**That is the whole of what I have got and it took me four seconds, and I am not going to look for it, and I am not going to tell you what I think it is, and you are not going to ask me, and if you do ask me I will say that the terminus column takes what it is given and that is a thing about a column and not about a place.**"

---

**He did not ask her.**

He stood in that office for about nine minutes after that and he did not ask her one question. Not whether the type had been reset, not whether anybody had ever queried it, not whether the working ran, not whether the book was printed or typed or written, not whether the diagram had ever had it and lost it, and not whether the timetable division had ever been asked, and she knew he was not going to and she said so.

"**You are not going to ask me anything and I am going to say one more thing and then I am going to do my fares, and it is not about the column and it is not about December, and if you write any of it down I will know and I will not stop you but I will know.**"

---

**And at the door, at about ten past twelve, she said it in nine words, and he did not ask, and that is where the day stopped.**

"There is a thing in the margin of that page."

"It is not mine," she said. "It was not there in December and it was there in January and I have not read it and I am not going to, and I have not told anybody and I am not going to tell anybody, and **if it is a woman's handwriting then I am not going to be the one who says so, and neither is the timetable division, and neither is the man from the round.**"

She shut the door. It is a room with a book on a shelf four feet from a desk and a person at the desk, and the person is a depot clerk of fifty-one with twenty-two years in it, and she was not sent, and she was not asked, and she has not been asked about a morning in December by anybody, and she is not going to be.

Nobody was sent. Nobody asked a timetable clerk anything. The man of forty-one on no list at all did not do it, and did not know about it until the Monday night, and has not been told.

---

**The Field Book, Monday the fifteenth of June, 23.20, a front room, one day off the fourteenth, and I have two things in this entry and one of them is four words and one of them is a person, and the four words are first because they are the fact and the person is second because I do not know what to do with her yet and I am not going to pretend tonight that I do.**

*Fifteenth, 23.20. One day off the fourteenth.*

*One. **A working timetable is a book on a shelf four feet from a desk, and it is bound, and it is printed from a diagram that is printed from a list that is a hundred and forty years old, and it has eight columns and one of them is headed *terminates*, and every terminus in the book is on the diagram, and the run column and the crew column and the working and the cars and the day and the time and the remarks are all printed type.** The occurrence of four characters in the run column on the morning of Tuesday the sixteenth of December is the 07:52 booked working off Crown Heights, in type, in a book, in a room, and it is not lost and it has never been lost and nobody has to find anything and nobody has been sent and the division has not said its sentence once since April and the sentence is still true and it is in a book and it will keep. **So the number has moved one step in seven weeks and it moved because a woman of fifty-one was booking a crew for a Monday in June and a crew is a body of people and a body of people is booked to a working and a working has a destination, and she needed the destination, and that is her job and not a favour and she has not been asked about a morning in December by anybody and she said so on purpose.**

*Two. **And the terminates column, and I am writing the four words and I am not going to explain them. HOLLOWGATE.** H-O-L-L-O-W-G-A-T-E. It is in the type. It has been in the type since the book was printed and the book was printed before December and the column only ever holds a place that is on the diagram. A depot clerk of fifty-one with twenty-two years in that room has booked about eleven crews a week for twenty-two years and knows every terminus in the book, and said in four seconds: **there is no Hollowgate, there has never been a Hollowgate, and it is in that column anyway, and the type has been in that book since before I came.** And then the sentence that is the whole of it and it is hers and I am not going to improve it: **the terminus column takes what it is given, and that is a thing about a column and not about a place.** I did not ask her one question in nine minutes. Not the type, not a reset, not whether the working ran, not whether the book was printed or typed or written, not whether the diagram ever had it and lost it, and not whether anybody in a building about a mile away had ever been asked. **I did not ask her because a clerk who is asked about a morning in December becomes one of about four people in this city who have been asked about a morning in December, and the four of them were not in that building, and I am not going to be a fifth of anything, and I have written that reason down in this book more times than I have got the patience to count, and I am not going to count it, because a count of the times I have decided not to be a fifth of anything is a column, and there is one of those on the top of a table in my own office.** A man of fifty in a works jacket said that in a corridor on the twenty-third of April and I did not know then that I would end up in a room at Alder Quay with the alternative in front of me and choose it, and I chose it, and nobody helped me.

*Three. **A thing in the margin of that page, in nine words at a door at about ten past twelve, and it was not said in answer to anything.** It is not hers. It was not there in December and it was there in January. She has not read it and is not going to. She has not told anybody and is not going to. And she said: **if it is a woman's handwriting then I am not going to be the one who says so, and neither is the timetable division, and neither is the man from the round.** I have not asked. I have not been to that page. I have not asked her to. And I am not going to write down in this book what I think is in the margin and I am not going to want to, and if I am told I am going to write it down and put it in a yard, and I would like somebody in about four years to be able to read this entry and see that on the fifteenth of June I did not ask.

*Four. And the rest, carried and not improved. **Ten remembered routes, ten, none removed. Nine lines in the tally, the paper is Hettie Brann's, the pocket is Dr Sen's, and I have not looked at it since the thirteenth of January and I am not going to look at it in June.** The page home the hundred and fourth day, blank, four marks in four hands and none of them mine. The token cold, a hundred and ninetieth day, and it is in the inside pocket of this coat and it was in Alder Quay at about half past eleven and it did nothing, and it has done nothing every day of one hundred and ninety of them. **The order is suspended and is not discharged and is not varied and is not withdrawn and the public name is not on the street name index, and the classification of the eleventh is withdrawn and is not to be relied on and stays withdrawn, and the removed sentence cannot be restored and the department cannot account for its removal and has been asked twice and has not answered twice, and the matter stands over and is not adjourned and has no date and was not on the business of the fifth of May and is not on anything, and it is not going to be on the business of the twenty-fourth of June either and nobody is going to put it there.** The line of the twelfth of January stands and its condition is not the department's and the answer of the fourth of December stands behind it. The levy is unchanged and is not curable and a woman of sixty on a second landing is the ninth of twenty-six names and is not named and nobody is going to tell her anything. I have not told Mara Quill about the season for the ninth week and she has said she is not going to ask and I have not asked her about the seventh of January since the fifth of May and I have stopped counting and started dating, and a date is a column, and I have not told her about a page in a drawer with a coat in it and I am not going to and I am not going to be asked whether I know about it.

*The page home is the hundred and fourth day. The pairs are twenty-seven and there is an entry on every night of this month and every night of last month and an interval of one day is not a pair whether or not either of the two days has anything on it, and the list has not moved since the twenty-fifth of April and I have not added to it and I am not going to add to it in a way that moves it. **And the standing position, which is not improved and which I am not going to improve in a fortnight: the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.**
