# Chapter 78: A Box About Persons

The quay section office is four rooms above a fitter's shop and on a Friday morning in January it is eleven men coming and going and a kettle on a ring and a works clerk of forty-six going down a returns sheet with a pencil, and it is the most ordinary working day in this district, and nothing whatever happened in it.

The gang's sheet for the week of the twelfth went into the rack at about ten in the morning on Friday the sixteenth, with the week before it and the week before that, and the two dates in it are the twelfth and the fifteenth and the reason column is empty on both, and the duplicate went into a tin at the end of the corridor and the duplicate for the week of the fifth of January and the duplicate for the week of the twenty-ninth of December also go into a second tin in a plan room in another building on the bottom shelf under a repair schedule.

**And that tin now has four weeks of carbon in it, and on the thirteenth of January a page went into it with six lines about six people on it and a woman's name with a line through it, and nobody in this city has read it and nobody in this city is going to.**

The form that caused the whole of the day came out of the authority's own head office on Monday the twelfth of January and came back to the section on the Wednesday, and it is a printed form and it is a single side and it has been in use since before anybody in that office was born.

> **PERMANENT WAY — ANNUAL MAINTENANCE RETURN, SECTION: ______ . Year: ______.**
> *Lengths held by this section: ______.*
> *Total length, chainage: ______.*
> *Condition summary: ______.*
> *Persons observed on the length during the year: ______.*
> *Action taken by the officer: ______.*
> *Officer: ______ . Date: ______ . Counter-signature: ______.*

---

**The box is the fifth line and it is the only one anybody in this district has been talking about for four days, and it is a box, and it is correct, and it has been correct since before the war, and there is nothing anybody can do about it at all.**

"It has been in the return since about 1968," said Cyril Sale, to about nine people in a yard in the river core on the Thursday evening, from the form itself, which he carried out of an office four rooms above a fitter's shop at about four o'clock and put on a table in a yard because the yard is where people are, and which he took back with him at about half past four, and which nobody in that yard copied, and about four people have said since that a man who could have copied it and did not is the reason this district still does not have a copy of a form with a box on it about persons. "There are about four hundred of them in this authority and every division fills one in every January and every one of them has had a line about persons on it since before I came into this office, and I have never once known why, and I have never once thought it was anybody's business, and on the twelfth of January I read that form twice with a pencil in my hand and then I put the pencil down and went and stood at a window for about four minutes."

"It is about a man on a road," said Lena Marr. "A drain, a cutting, a cess, a place where a man works. It is a health and safety return and it is asking whether anybody was in a dangerous place when you were working on it. That is not sinister. That is the most ordinary box in this city."

"It is a good box," said Cyril Sale. "I am not standing here saying it is a bad box, and I want that said before anything else, and I want the people in this yard to hear it from me and not from a man in a good coat. **It is a good box. Somebody thought of that box for a reason and the reason was a man in a cutting.** And I am going to tell you what the reason is, and then I am going to tell you what has happened, and then the two of you are going to work out between you what to do about it, because I have been four days and I am not the man who does anything about it, I am the man who fills the boxes in."

---

**What happened, in the order it happened, on the Monday and the Tuesday and the Wednesday, in about three hundred words.**

He filled the fourth line. The fourth line is *condition summary* and it took about nine words: *serviceable; quay cut rodded 12/x; boundary at wall checked; gang as scheduled.*

He came to the fifth line on the Monday afternoon at about half past four and he wrote **nil** and put the pencil down and sat there, and the two men who work in that office with him were in the room and neither of them said anything for about a minute and a half, and one of them has said since that the minute and a half was the longest silence of his working life.

"I want to say why and I want to say it once and I am not going to be asked," said Cyril Sale. "There were thirty-one people and a conductor and a driver on that length on the twenty-second of December at about ten past twelve in the morning and I know that because the conductor's own return says where thirty-one people got off and Mr Rook was in the road with a sheet of paper in his coat, and I have not put that in this box and I am not going to and here is the reason and it took me the whole of Monday afternoon to get it into words I would be willing to be produced.

"**If I write persons into this box then next January there is a return in a file in a building in Alderquay that says that persons were observed on the quay service lengths, and it has got a number in it, and somebody in four years reads that number in a room and asks a question about it. And the only name available to be asked is Mr Pryce's, because he is the officer of this section and I am the clerk, and he has already told me in front of eleven men that he will not be a man who witnessed a person. So I write a sentence about eleven men and a foreman into a box that says persons, and in four years a foreman of sixty-one is asked in a room what he observed on a length of tram route, and there is no column on that form for the answer, and he will have to stand there and say *a car was on my length and the gang is the crew on my length*, and that will be a record of a car on a length, and there will be thirty-one people in a room about it.**

"Nil is a true answer. On the day this return is filled in there is nobody observed on those lengths. I have put nil in it and I will put nil in it every January for as long as I am in this office, and I would like somebody in this yard to understand that **a nil on a length is not a nil on a street, and the reason it is not is that there is nobody on a length to be observed and there is a gang on it, and those are two different facts and they are in the same box.**"

Somebody said, "Then somebody has put two different facts in a box and the box has taken it."

"That is what a box is," said Cyril Sale. "I said that in this district in December and I have said it twice and I am saying it a third time because I have had four days and because it is the only thing I know."

---

**And then he came to the sixth line, and it is the one that cost, and it is not nil, and it is the only true sentence anybody in Alderquay has written about the thirteenth of October.**

*Action taken by the officer* is not a box about persons. It is a box about the officer, and it is filled in by the officer, and the officer of the quay section is a foreman of fifty-four and the clerk who fills the form in is a man of forty-six, and the return has a counter-signature line, and the counter-signature is the superintendent's and the superintendent's office is in another building and nobody in it has ever been in this one.

Cyril Sale wrote it on the Tuesday morning at about ten past ten and it took him about eleven minutes and it is nineteen words and it is in his own hand, and he read it back to the two men in the room before he signed it, because his mother taught him that a man in an office who cannot say what he wants in writing will be told he never came, and he is the fourth person in this district to say that sentence and it has never once been wrong.

> *Length PW/Q/4 (back length) added to this schedule at 4th edn., 22 Nov, per service amendment 12/44 (eff. 13 Oct), under standing instruction 11/D/6. Margin note in my hand. 3rd edn. (11 Sept) did not contain the length. **The amendment's lines for requested by and instructing officer are blank and I cannot supply either.** — C. SALE, clerk, 15.i.*

"That is my name," he said, "and that is my handwriting, and that is the initials that are in the margin of a schedule in a drawer in this office, and in about four years a man in a building is going to read two of my hands in one document and put them side by side and I will not be able to say I did not know."

Nobody in the yard asked to see it. There is no copy, because the return is the authority's and it goes into the authority's file and the return is not a minute and nobody reads it until somebody wants to read it, and the district has no standing to ask for a copy of anything in that file.

"You have put it in a place nobody will read," said Rue.

"Yes," said Cyril Sale. "**That is where a true sentence goes. Everywhere else it is a speech.**"

And the return was signed by the officer of this section on the Wednesday morning at about eleven with the sixth line not read out to him, because a foreman reads a return the way a foreman reads a return, which is the first, second, third and fourth lines, and the sixth line is a clerk's, and Roy Pryce has signed about nine hundred forms in his life and has never once read a clerk's line, and the counter-signature of the superintendent is in another building and will go on a copy of a form that nobody in this district is ever going to see.

**And Milo Rook does not know any of it. He will not find out in this volume. It is in a file in a building that the district has no standing to enter, and there is no copy, and the courier round cannot carry it because there is nothing to carry, and by the time anybody in the river core would want it the paper will be a year old and in a store.**

---

**And on Friday the sixteenth at about four o'clock in the afternoon, Advocate Quill did the only thing she can do with a form in another body's file, and it took her about nine minutes and it has her name at the bottom of it in biro and it is going to cost her.**

The district cannot demand the return. The district has no standing to demand a document in the internal file of a tram authority, and the proceeding of the seventh of January has established in four paragraphs of a minute that a party's standing is an effect upon a person, and the return is an effect upon nobody; and the Registry cannot reach it; and the Civic Atlas Office is a survey office and has never in its history been able to ask the tram authority for a page.

What Mara Quill can do is put it in front of the hearing.

> *To the company secretary, [the tram authority].*
> *I act for an interested party in the district. I have no right to the document I ask for and I do not assert one.*
> *I ask that the annual maintenance return of the quay section for the current year, being a form in your own custody, be produced to the reporting officer at the hearing of the twelfth of January past, or at the adjourned hearing of the fourteenth of April, at the reporting officer's election, and that it be produced in original and not as an extract.*
> *I ask for it because a form in your file carries a box headed **persons observed on the length during the year**, and because a length is a place with a gang on it and a wall at one end and no rateable unit, and because a nil in that box means one thing on a street and a different thing on a length, and nobody in this city has yet had to say which.*
> *I have signed this in my own hand and my own name and I have a two-year note on my conduct and I am aware that this letter will be produced against me. — M. QUILL, 16.i.*

"It is a good letter," said Rue, in a corridor in Crown Heights at about half past four on the Friday afternoon.

"It is a nine-minute letter about a box I cannot see, from a woman with a two-year note, to a company secretary, asking for a page be produced in April in a hearing I am not conducting," said Mara Quill. "And it may work, and it will cost me whatever it costs, and there is nothing else available and I would like somebody to tell me the eleven things I should have done instead so that I can write them down and not do them."

Somebody said, "Why is it nine minutes?"

"Because I have read the form and the letter is not about the form." She put the copy in her bag. "And I am not going to say the rest of it and I would like it noticed that I have not said the rest of it, and that the rest of it is about a piece of paper in a drawer in my own flat, and that it has nothing to do with a tram authority's annual return, and that I have not said it and I am not going to, and I have written down in advance that I was not going to and when the day comes I am going to read that back to myself."

Nobody noticed that except about four people, and one of them was not listening because he was looking at a barometer.

---

**The Field Book, Friday the sixteenth of January, 20.40, in a front room, standing at a table, six lines.**

*Sixteenth, 20.40. Six lines, and they are the only six I have written today and I have been in four rooms since eight in the morning.*

*There is a form in this city with a box on it headed **persons observed on the length during the year** and it has been in use since before the war and it is a good box and a man of forty-six stood at a window for four minutes on Monday and then put the word nil in it and gave the reason out loud in a yard, and the reason is that if he writes persons in there then a foreman of fifty-four is asked in a room in four years what he observed on a length of tram route.*

*He is right. I have thought about it for a day and a half and he is right, and I want it in this book that I tried to argue him out of it on Wednesday and I could not, and that the argument I had was **write the crew in it, write eleven men and a foreman and a wall, and then the box says a crew and not a person** — and it is a good argument and it is my argument and it is the argument of the man who wrote a seventh column on Tuesday, and it did not survive being written out loud, and the reason it did not survive is that the crew was not observed, the crew was the observer, and putting the observer in a box about what was observed is how a department ends up with a foreman who witnessed thirty-one persons.*

*Six lines then.*

*One. A clerk of forty-six has put nineteen words in a form in his own hand with his name on it and the form will not be read for about four years and there is no copy and nobody in this district will ever know he did it.*

*Two. Advocate Quill has written to a company secretary about a box she is not permitted to see, from a district with no standing, in a hearing she is not conducting, on the sixteenth of January, and it will be produced against her and she has told a corridor that she knew when she signed it.*

*Three. And the thing I am going to have to carry for the rest of this, which is that **the reason nobody can do anything about a box in a form in a file is that the reason the box is empty is a decision somebody in this district made correctly on the twenty-second of December and would make again.** A foreman refused to write a person onto a form. That was right. And the cost of it was right. And the cost of it is a box in a return in a file in a building that says a length of tram route has had no persons on it in a year in which a conductor of thirty-eight spent five days and eight hours on one.*

*And I am not going to write down that he was wrong. He was not wrong. I am going to write down that he was right and that the price of being right is on somebody else's form and that nobody told the form.*

*Twenty-seven days. The book is silent. Ten routes, ten, and nobody has asked me to add one and I have not opened the book for anything but this since Wednesday. The page home the fifty-fourth day. The token cold. I have not crossed and I am not going to and the yard on a Friday evening is about nineteen people and not one of them asked me for anything and that has now been true for a fortnight and I have not decided what it is and I am not going to decide it this month.*
