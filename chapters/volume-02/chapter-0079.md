# Chapter 79: Mireward, Nil

The gang was on the back length at half past seven on the morning of Monday the nineteenth of January and it was the first walk of a week that runs to Sunday, and there is no sheet yet for that week in any rack, and the sheet will have two dates in it when it is signed off on the Thursday, and on the Monday morning it had one.

Nobody wrote anything down about that. It is the third Monday in a month and it is the most ordinary fact in the whole of this.

---

**Three people acted on a four-page forecast in nine days, and two of them were right, and one of them did not know it was a forecast.**

Dr Iona Sen put the last week of January into the water clinic's opening hours on the Friday morning and told about nine people at the counter in about forty words, and she was not asked and she is not going to say it was a mistake.

"The first is expected in the last week of January and the last week of January is the week of Monday the twenty-sixth, and nobody has said which day and nobody is going to be able to say which day, because the four pages say the rest are allocated on the day. So the clinic is open every day of that week from eight until six, and the counter is staffed by somebody at all times and it has never once been staffed past four on a Sunday, and **I am not going to be told by a four-page document from an office that does not sign its name what day somebody in this district is ill.** That is a claim on my counter and I have had four claims on my counter in three months and I have refused three of them."

Rue put seven days into the round. The round is a round of seven stops and eleven streets and it goes out at eight and in at four on a working day, and Rue took it out at eight and in at seven on each of the seven days of the week of Monday the twenty-sixth, and said the price out loud in the yard on the Saturday morning in about eleven words, and it is on the page because she said it and about four people wrote it down.

"Seven days instead of five. It is not five extra stops, it is seven extra days, and the days are the expensive part and everybody in this yard knows it and nobody is going to say it for me. If somebody dies on the Tuesday of that week and I am out on the Friday with a satchel instead of a woman of sixty-three, then I have done this and got it wrong, and if I do not do it and somebody dies, then I have done nothing and got it right, and I would rather be the second one, and I am not going to be asked about that again this year."

And the tram authority did nothing, and the Registry did nothing, and the Civic Atlas Office did not do anything about the four pages at all until about two o'clock on the Monday afternoon of the nineteenth, when a man of about thirty opened a drawer and tried to enter four pages into a plan chest and could not.

---

**The four pages came to the Civic Atlas Office by hand on the twenty-eighth of December and they are not a notice and they are not a series and they have no reference number on them, and that is the whole of the machine in a drawer.**

A forecast enters this office properly through one of three doors. It comes on a circular with a number in the series and a distribution list, and a clerk enters it in a register and initials the register. Or it comes on a district returns form, and the district returns form has a quarter on it and a box for the officer responsible. Or it is asked for, by a person, by name, in writing, and the request is filed and the thing that arrives is filed against the request.

This had none of the three. It is four pages with a heading in capitals and a date on them, the twenty-second of December, and the office of the First Surveyor on the top of it, and no distribution list, and no number, and no return, and **it came into the building in the hand of a man of about fifty who put it on a table, said *it is for the route room*, said the name of the office, and left without waiting to be told whether anybody wanted it.** Which is how things arrive in this city when nobody is responsible for them arriving, and it is a good way to arrive and it is also the way a thing arrives that no department can be shown to have received.

"So it is not in the register," said Dilley Farr, at about twenty past two on the Monday afternoon. "It is on a table. And in about four years there will be a shelf of about nine hundred four-page documents in this room that are not in the register, and every one of them will have been on a table, and nobody will be able to say who put it there, and that is not a scandal, that is just what a table is."

---

**And on the Friday of the week before last he did the only thing anybody in the district could do with a name on the fourth page, which was to write a query, and the query is on the page because it is four lines and because he got it wrong and the getting it wrong is the finding.**

A district returns query goes to the district's own drainage section, on a printed form, and the printed form has a box for the district and a box for the quarter and a box for the *return required*, and the form is about ground and drainage and the boxes are about the ground. Milo Rook filled it in at about half past two on the Friday the ninth in the route room and it went into the internal post the same afternoon and the internal post is a man of about fifty with a bag who takes eleven streets of Crown Heights in about ninety minutes and Mireward is not eleven streets of Crown Heights.

What came back on the Friday the sixteenth was a **card**, and it is a printed card and it is the size of a postcard and it comes out of a machine that prints about nine hundred of them a month, and it is in the returns list, and it is not an answer to anything.

> **DISTRICT RETURNS — CURRENT QUARTER**
> *District: **MIREWARD***
> *Return required: quarterly ground and seam return.*
> *Last received: —*
> *Reason outstanding: —*
> *Officer responsible: [blank].*

"The column for *officer responsible* is not filled in," said Milo Rook. "Not by error. Look at the type. It is the same type as the reason column, which is the machine's own, and the officer column is a line under words and a line under words is a place a person writes anything at all, and the reason column is the machine printing a dash because there is no officer to print a dash against."

He had learned that about a line under words in December, from a form about a tram, and it took him seven days to get it back as a card about a district, and the seven days were not the part that was wrong.

---

**And the district office that does not exist, explained in about forty words by a man of about fifty in the drainage section, on the Friday afternoon, to a surveyor who had come in without an appointment because an internal query takes two days and a man in a corridor takes nine minutes.**

"There is no district office," the man said. "There has never been a district office. Mireward is not a district office, it is about eleven square miles of fen with a population on a card and a rateable figure on a card and a road that goes to it in the summer and a hard that goes to it all the year from Cardew Docks, and the thing that goes out there in the summer is a rod and a chain and a man from this room called Percy, and Percy is fifty-eight and he goes out on the first dry week and he has been going out since 1974, and he comes back and he writes four lines on a pad and the pad goes in a drawer in here and it is in a drawer in here from this year and every year back to about 1969, and nobody has ever asked him for one of those pads and he has never once been asked whether the four lines are a return."

"Is it a return?"

"It is four lines about about nine feet of drain." The man said it without any weight at all. "Mr Rook, I have been in this room nineteen years and I can tell you the thing nobody tells you about Mireward, and it is not a mystery and there is nothing behind it. **Mireward is on this city's returns because a return list is printed from a list of districts and that list is a hundred and forty years old and it has never been printed from a list of offices.** And there is no office because there was never anybody to make one, and a person who goes out in a summer with a rod and writes four lines about a drain is not an office and does not become one, and in forty-one years nobody has ever written the word *office* next to that name on a piece of paper, and so the thing about Mireward that is true — which is that a man goes out there every year and writes four lines — is not a return, and the thing about Mireward that is on the card, which is that nothing has ever happened there, is not a finding, **it is the absence of a signature.**

"That is the whole of it. It is not sinister and there is nothing to find and I have wanted to say it to somebody for about three years."

---

**And on the Monday the nineteenth at about half past four in the afternoon, in the route room, a man of thirty-one years said the thing that is the actual answer of anything anybody has said about Mireward, and it is the second half of a sentence he said in December and it is the other half, and the December half was right.**

"In December I told you that Mireward is entered as stable because there is nothing in it," said Dilley Farr. "I was right and it was not finished, and I have thought about the rest of it for three weeks and here it is. **Nothing is a very good answer and it is very hard to argue with, and I have watched it get written on four hundred sheets about this city. And what I have found out since is that the moment you put nothing in a *column* it stops being nothing and becomes a hole with a heading on it.** A hole with a heading on it is worse than nothing, because a heading is a thing somebody can look up. And Mireward is going to be looked up. There are eleven weather-driven seam events in a season, the first one is in a week and one day, four of them are in the river core, one is in the Outer Reach, and the rest are unallocated and will be allocated on the day, and there is a district on that list with a heading and no number under it, and when the day comes somebody in an office is going to have to put an allocation under that heading, and **the only thing anybody in this city has ever been able to put under a heading is a thing somebody has been to look at.**"

"And nobody has been."

"Nobody has been. That is not a decision. That is a row of cards." He shut the drawer. "Write your query down again, in your own book, and put on it the thing the man in the drainage section said, in his words, and then do not send it anywhere, because there is nowhere to send it, and the sending is the part that has been going wrong for forty-one years."

---

**And at about five o'clock on the Monday evening a boy of seventeen was in the yard and nobody said anything about it.**

He had gone up the south on the twenty-eighth of December and had come back on the nineteenth of January, which is twenty-two days, and he came back on his own money and he did not telephone and he was on the round at eight in the morning, out at eight and in at four, and he did not say anything about where he had been and about four people in this district have said that the not-saying was the whole of what he had come back with.

Rue said the sentence in the same yard on the Monday evening and asked for it to be written down by somebody who was not her, and nobody wrote it down.

"**He is back and he is on the round and he is not going to say anything about it and nobody is going to ask him, and I would like that written down by somebody who is not me, and I know who is going to read this in a year and it is not a person in this yard.**"

He put a card on the trestle table under a brick, face up, the way a card goes, and about nine people read it in about a minute and it is on the page because it is a card and because a card is a document and because this one is the only one in this case that is worth nothing at all.

It is blank. It is a courier's card, the same card he has had since November, and it is about two inches by three, and it has a line ruled across the middle and the line is blank, and there is nothing on either side of it, and it is dated in his own hand at the bottom in the way he dates everything, and the date is the nineteenth of January.

**A man of seventeen went twenty-two days up the south on a lead about a brother and came back with a blank card and put it on a table under a brick, and there is no document anywhere in Alderquay that says what that means and there never will be, and the list in the tin is not a finding and Ostrow confirms nothing and Tern is not found.**

---

**The Field Book, Monday the nineteenth of January, 21.20, in a front room, and it is the shortest entry in four weeks and he says so.**

*Nineteenth, 21.20. Eight lines, and it is the shortest I have written since the sixteenth of December and I am not going to pad it.*

*One. The first weather-driven seam event is expected in the last week of January, which is the week of Monday the twenty-sixth, and that is a week and one day. The four pages do not say which day and say that the rest are allocated on the day, which means the rest are not on any calendar anybody holds, which means a woman of about fifty has opened a clinic every day for seven days and a woman with a satchel is walking eleven streets for seven days, and neither of them knows which day it is.*

*Two. Mireward is entered as nil on a four-page forecast from an office that did not sign its name, on the stated ground that there is no maintained seam, no recorded loss, no resident witness and no schedule. A man of fifty in the drainage section has told me that there has never been a district office, and that a man of fifty-eight goes out there on the first dry week of every summer with a rod and a chain and writes four lines about about nine feet of drain, and that the pad goes in a drawer from this year back to about 1969, and that nobody has ever asked him for one of them.*

*Three. **A man who goes out in a summer and writes four lines about a drain is not an office and does not become one, and in forty-one years nobody has ever written the word office next to that name on a piece of paper.** That is the whole of the next two hundred square miles and there is nothing behind it and I am not going to go and look, and I am not going to enter it, and I want that in this book in my own hand as a decision and not as modesty, because in about nine days somebody in an office is going to have to put a number under a heading on a card and the only thing anybody in this city can put under a heading is a thing somebody has been to look at.*

*Four. I tried to send a query and it came back as a card, because a query asks a district office a question about a quarter and there is no district office and there is not a person to be rude.*

*Five. The office of the First Surveyor is a working desk in an instrument. A man of fifty put four pages on a table in this room, said the name of the office, and left. It is not in the register and it is not on the returns list and there is no address for it and no desk to put a question to and I have not asked anybody in this building whether one exists, and I am not going to, and the reason is the same reason as in December and it is a bad one: **I would be asking whether there is a man behind it, and if there is not, then the forecast is a machine, and if there is, then it is a man, and I do not want either answer this month and both of them are going to arrive anyway.**

*Thirty days. The book is silent. Ten routes, ten, and a boy of seventeen came back today with a blank card and went face up under a brick and there is no document anywhere in this city that says what that means, and I have wanted for four months to write down one sentence about a card, and I am writing this one, and it is not about his card.*

*Next walk is the twenty-second. The page home the fifty-seventh day. The token cold. I have not crossed this month and the tally says nine and I have not looked at it since the twelfth.*
