# Chapter 80: Who Keeps It

The card came back with a corner on it on Sunday the seventeenth of January and nobody had asked for it, and the thing in the corner is not a route and it is not a name and it is in grease pencil in a foreman's hand and it is seven lines.

> *The gate is shut Mondays and Thursdays 07.30 to about 08.30, for the walk, and the key is on a nail inside the hut and eleven men need it.*
> *The rest of the week it is on the latch until four and after that it is a padlock and the padlock is the company's.*
> *Do not go through that compound in a car. There is nowhere to turn.*
> *This is not a route and I am not redrawing anybody's route. R.P., 18.i.*

"Version two," said Milo Rook, and he wrote the version and the date in the top corner in his own hand, because he is the only one of the four people who has ever put a date on that card, and there are now four copies and **one of them is version two and three of them are version one**, and that is the first time the four copies have ever differed, and it is the entire argument of this book in about four seconds and he wrote it down before he did anything else.

Four copies is not a document. Three copies of a thing and one other copy of a thing is a thing two people disagree about. And a card that two people disagree about is a card that somebody will correct, and the person who corrects it is the one who is holding it, and if four people are holding it then it is four people correcting it and none of them is the person whose hand it is, and that is the failure that happened in Chapters 41 to 50 of this district's other life with a sheet of paper and a contractor's head on it and five houses and a man's name in a space somebody filled in on purpose.

Somebody has to hold it. That is all that has happened in the last four months in one sentence, and it took a foreman of fifty-four to write it down in a corner in grease pencil on a Sunday, for no reason, to nobody, having been told about a gate.

---

**And on Monday the nineteenth of January, at about ten past nine in the morning, at the water clinic counter, a woman of sixty-one was asked whether she would hold four pieces of paper and she said yes in about four words and then set a condition in about forty, and nobody thanked her, and she was not thanked on the Thursday, and she has said she would rather be a nurse.**

Hettie Brann, nineteen years on that counter, formerly a laundry hand, holds a tally with nine lines on it that is the record of his crossings, and she does not read it and has never read it.

"I am not doing it out of kindness and I want that said first," she said. "I have been asked for a route before by a woman of about thirty-one in a raincoat and I said no, and I was right, and I have not changed my mind about the raincoat and I am not changing my mind now. I am taking the envelope because there are four pieces of paper in four buildings and somebody has to know whether they are all saying the same thing, and if nobody knows then in about a year somebody finds one of them and reads out a date that is four months old and there is no other copy with that date on it, and then the whole thing is a rumour."

She wrote the rule at the foot of a sheet in her own hand in about eleven seconds.

> *First working day of the month: the four copies are taken out of the four places and looked at, by the person who is holding them, and the version and the date are compared and nothing else is read. If one of them is not the same, the person who is holding them asks the person whose hand it is and nobody else. The holder is not M.Q. and is not M.R. and will not be.*

"The first working day of the month was the first of January," she said, "and there was no holder then, and there were four pieces of paper in four buildings, and nobody knows whether they were the same. The next one is the second of February and I have written it on this and not in my own book, because a thing in my own book is a thing about me."

"And if you are ill," said someone.

"Then the round does it," said Hettie Brann, "and the round is a woman with a satchel and she has said in this yard that she is not going to be the person anybody leans on, and she is going to be anyway, and I would like that written down by somebody as the second cost of this card and not as a favour."

Rue said, "It is the first cost of anything anybody in this district has done in four months and I am not going to have it recorded as a favour."

---

**What is now on the schedule, which is the whole of the twenty-first of January and which is nine items and not one of them is a victory.**

> *Mondays and Thursdays, 07.30 — quay section gang, back length, eleven men, foreman R. Pryce. **Next walk Thursday the twenty-second.** Sheet signed off and into the rack on the Friday; duplicate into a tin; duplicate into a second tin on a bottom shelf in a plan room in another building.*
> *Every Friday from this week — a returns form with six boxes, one of them headed **persons observed on the length during the year**, filled nil, by a clerk of forty-six, in his own hand, and signed by a foreman of fifty-four who does not read the clerk's line.*
> *Week of Monday the twenty-sixth of January, daily, 08.00 to 18.00 — water clinic counter open, staffed at all times, by a decision of one person, on a four-page forecast that cannot be entered in any register in this city.*
> *Week of Monday the twenty-sixth of January, daily, out at eight and in at seven — the round. Seven days instead of five. Unpaid. Stated aloud.*
> *Second of February, second of March, first of April — first working day of the month. Four copies, one envelope, one holder, nothing read but a version and a date.*
> *Nineteenth of January — a returned card from a district that has no office: return required, last received —, reason outstanding —, officer responsible [blank].*
> *Sixteenth of January — a letter to a company secretary, nine minutes, from a woman with a two-year note, asking that a form in another body's file be produced in original at a hearing in April, and which may work.*
> *In a drawer in a flat in Crown Heights — a custody register page copied in ninety seconds with a door open, and a second thing in a file about a named officer's own hand, and a third thing she has not done and has chosen every day for four months, and she has written all three down in that order and is going on writing them down in that order.*
> *On a bottom shelf, under a repair schedule, in a plan room in this building — three weeks of gang carbon, and one page that is not a gang sheet, with six lines about six people on it and a woman's name with a line through it and no date, which nobody will read, and which is there because a foreman wrote a rule in grease pencil about crossing lines out and not taking them off, and because a man of thirty-one would not sign a deletion, and because a doctor said that a thing you have hidden has happened twice.*
> ***Tuesday the fourteenth of April, in a hall — the hearing, adjourned, with an officer's name on it and a line that says no schedule of persons is to be obtained from this district while the answer of the fourth of December stands, and a quarter and a list in another department, and a foreman of fifty-four in a hut with a clipboard who has said that in about four months a man in an office is going to ask him what he saw.***
> *In the week of Monday the twenty-sixth of January — eleven weather-driven seam events, four of them in the river core, one in the Outer Reach, the rest unallocated, and one of the eleven in a region entered as stable by a desk that signed no name because a man of fifty-eight goes out there in the summer with a rod and writes four lines about a drain and nobody has ever written the word office next to his name.*

---

**And at about ten past ten on the morning of Wednesday the twenty-first of January, in a depot office at Alder Quay, a surveyor asked a clerk a question he should have asked in the first ten days of January, and a clerk of fifty-one answered it in about nine words and then did not go and look.**

The working manifest is the conductor's and the depot keeps the carbon, and it has six columns, and one of them is *run no*, and the 07:52 off Crown Heights of Tuesday the sixteenth of December is a booked working and every booked working has a run number against it and a run number is not a destination.

"Thirty-one names and a run number," said Hester Coombe. "It is on the carbon in the second drawer, second sheet, and I have not looked it up, and I have been a clerk in this office for twenty-two years and I can tell you the number without looking and I have not looked it up, because a run number is a number and a number is not a question and I have had four requests for that carbon in three weeks and every one of them wanted the alighted column and nobody has ever asked me about the second sheet."

"You can look it up."

"I can look it up in about four minutes in a book that is on a shelf in this room," said Hester Coombe. "And I have not done it, and you have not asked me to, and I want it written down that I did not do it before you asked and I have not done it since you asked, because I have got a pile of returns in front of me and the fifteenth is the current one and it is off my desk on Friday the twenty-second."

He asked her to look it up on the Friday, and she said yes, and he wrote it on the back of a card and put the card on the trestle table in the yard that afternoon, under a brick, face up, the way a card goes, and about nine people in a yard read it, and it says:

> *The run number on the carbon of the 07:52, 16 Dec. To be looked up in the working timetable. Not before Friday. — H.C.*

**And the card went under the brick on the Wednesday afternoon and it was still under the brick on the Wednesday the eleventh of February, and nobody in this district chased it, and for three weeks in which it sat there a man of thirty-one walked past it about four times a day and read it and did not pick it up, and the reason is on the facing page of the Field Book and it is eleven words long and it is the last rule this district has made and it is not a rule.**

---

**The Field Book, Wednesday the twenty-first of January, 16.10, the route room, the second of the two tables under the north window, the light going, about four people in the building.**

*Twenty-first, 16.10. First entry in three days. I have not written anything since the nineteenth at about nine at night and I have looked at this page about nine times and there was nothing to say and that is not a thing I have been able to say out loud to anybody so I am writing it down.*

*Nine items on the schedule and the first of them is a walk at half past seven tomorrow morning and it is a Monday and a Thursday week and the sheet will be signed on the Friday, and I have not been asked to be there and I am going to be there anyway at about ten to seven with a chain I will not use, because I stood at a north end in December and I am not going to be a person who was on that length once.*

*A foreman of fifty-four wrote seven lines in grease pencil in the corner of a card on Sunday for nobody, about a gate and a key on a nail and a padlock, and a version number went to two, and one of four copies says version two and three say version one, and a woman of sixty-one at a clinic counter has taken the envelope and written a rule at the foot of a sheet in eleven seconds, and the rule is that the holder is not M.Q. and is not M.R. and will not be. **That is the third time in five months that the sentence at the bottom of a sheet in this district has been the whole of the change, and the first two times it was me, and this time it is a woman at a counter and I am not on the sheet and I did not ask to be.***

*Four things this month and one of them is mine and I have written it down in the order they happened, which is the only order I am allowed.*

*One. A clerk of forty-six put **nil** in a box headed persons observed on the length during the year, and gave the reason out loud in a yard, and the reason was that if he writes persons in it then a foreman is asked in a room in four years what he observed on a length, and there is no column on that form for the answer. He is right. I tried to argue him out of it on the Wednesday and the argument I had was write the crew in it, and the crew was not observed, the crew was the observer, and he beat me in about nine words and he beat me because he works in the building and I have been in it three years.*

*Two. I held a market for about ninety seconds on a wall at seven minutes to six on a Wednesday evening and I do not know whether I did anything. It was the first thing I have held that was not a wound. A tram gong went off at half past four and I could not tell what it was and I needed to and I did not get it, and that is twice, and there is no date in this book on which I expect it back, and it is a sound and a taste and I still do not know whether that is one thing or two.*

*Three. The thing I got wrong, on Tuesday the thirteenth, in a form with seven columns, and it is in a tin on a bottom shelf in this building with a woman's name under a line through it and nobody has read it and nobody is going to. **I have not taken it out and I am not going to and I have not asked the man who owns the shelf whether it is still there, because I know that it is, because a rule in grease pencil says you do not take a line off, and because I wrote the rule's author a card in December and he is the one man in this district I would not put in a position of choosing.***

*Four. A card under a brick with a date on it and nothing on it, about a run number on a carbon in a drawer in a depot, and a clerk of fifty-one who has not looked it up, and a book on a shelf four feet away from her desk.*

*And a man of fifty in the drainage section told me that a man of fifty-eight goes out into eleven square miles of fen on the first dry week of every summer and writes four lines about about nine feet of drain into a pad, and that the pad is in a drawer from this year back to 1969, and that nobody has ever asked him for one of them, and that **a man who goes out in a summer and writes four lines about a drain is not an office and does not become one.** And a woman of sixty-eight on nothing. And a hundred and forty feet of frontage with a settlement plate on it. And a man asleep on a third floor who is not written down. And a shed in the last week of January and a doctor who has opened a counter for seven days on a forecast she cannot enter anywhere.*

*Ten routes, ten, and the list has not moved since the ninth of December, and it is the seventh week in which I have not stood in a corridor and let anybody read them to me, and nobody has asked, and I have not asked, and I have written the imperative half of a sentence down on the twenty-seventh of December and I have read it back twice this month and it has not worked once.*

*So here is the question and it is the question and it is not going to be answered this month and I am not going to pretend to have got near it.*

*On the first of January I wrote seven doors and a place nobody can name. There are nine items on the schedule and every one of them is somebody else's handwriting or somebody else's decision, and four of them are in this city because a man of fifty-four keeps walking six hundred yards of tram route twice a week and a woman of about forty-five puts her thumb in a crack in a wall twice a day for nineteen years and a man of about sixty puts his hand on a corner for four seconds twice a day for nineteen years and neither of them knows the other one does it.*

*That is the answer to the question on the first of January and it took three weeks and a refusal and a form and a doctor, and the answer is: **whoever turns up. A length is a distinct kind of place for exactly as long as the people who walk it keep walking it, and the whole of the machinery in this city is designed to find out who is responsible, and there is nobody, and there has never been anybody, and the only reason six hundred yards of tram route is still a place is that about fourteen people in this district are not being paid for any of it.**

*And the eleventh seam event is in a week and one day and four of the eleven are in the river core, and there is no form for that either, and there is no register for it, and there is no office to write to, and I am not going to be the man who goes and looks. **I am going to be at a north end at ten to seven tomorrow morning with a chain I will not use, and I am going to be at a counter on the second of February, and I am not going to write any of this down again until it has moved.***
