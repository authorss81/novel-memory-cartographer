# Chapter 89: The Second One, In Daylight

It rained on the afternoon of Thursday the nineteenth of February from about half past one, hard, off the south-west, and it did not stop until about ten to five, and about four people in eleven streets have said since that this is the only weather of the season that anybody in the district enjoyed.

The round was out at eight and in at four, as it is on a working day, and the counter was shut for its half hour between one o'clock and half past one, which is the only half hour it is ever shut for. The gang was on the back length at half past seven that morning, eleven men, and came back up at about twenty past eight, and the cover was still rocking and the ballast round the frame was tamped and the reason column was empty. **The gang walked six hundred yards of tram route seven hours and forty minutes before a hundred and ten yards of frontage did whatever it did, saw nothing, told nobody, and wrote four words about a cover, and about nine people have said since that this is the most useful thing anybody in this district did all winter and it was walking a length at half past seven in the morning and then going to lunch.**

The fishmonger of about forty-five called the lot prices from her doorway at about ten past five, as she has done for nineteen years, and Milo Rook was at the top of a hundred and ten yards of frontage that is not a street and cannot be given an address and has been standing in a doorway since about half past four because it had been raining sideways since half past one and there is nowhere on that row to stand out of it.

**And the paving at the top of that row was wet, and it was wet in a way that paving does not get.**

It is stone, set in a fall to a gutter, and it is a hundred and forty years old or near it, and it has been swept badly by a man of about forty every morning at ten past six for as long as anybody has been keeping the tally. It had not been wet like that in nineteen years, because in nineteen years the top of that row has had a market on it twice a week and a market is a hundred and ten yards of wet stone and a hose and fish.

There were scales on it. Not fish, not whole, not a box spilled — **scales, about the size of a thumbnail, scattered, in the run of the gutter at the north side where the stone dips, and they had come out of the stone and not off anything, and there were about nine thousand of them and the man of about forty confirmed it at ten to six on the Thursday morning and said he had swept that row every morning since he was twenty-one and there had not been scales on it since before the market shut.**

---

**And there was coffee on that row at about ten to five, which is forty minutes early, and it is the part of it that is slightly wrong, and it has been the slightly wrong part since the twentieth of December and this is the second time it has been said out loud in this district.**

There has been no coffee sold within a hundred and ten yards of that row since the year before the war. That is on the page and it is not a mystery, it is a shop that closed, and a man of about sixty has walked eleven minutes back to a shed at Cardew Docks every evening for nineteen years to make coffee for a stall that opens at six, and the smell of it is in a hundred and ten yards of frontage at half past five and has been for nineteen years and nobody in this district has ever been able to account for it, and the man himself has said twice that it is not his.

On the afternoon of the nineteenth of February, in rain, at about ten to five, there was coffee on that row, and it was not coming from a cart and it was not coming from a window, and about nine people were in it and six of them have said that they could not have told you where it was coming from, and the man of about sixty, who was not there yet, was not asked.

---

**And at about half past five on the evening of the nineteenth, six flat irons were on a rail in an order, in a doorway, in the rain, and the rail had dripped a mark on the wet stone under it, and there were seven marks.**

That is the whole of it. There are six irons and they are in the order they are always in and the order has not been written down by anybody and is not going to be. Under the rail on the wet stone there are seven marks the size of an iron foot, and the irons were six, and **the seventh mark was not under an iron, and it was not a mark of an iron that had been taken away, because there is no record anywhere of an iron having been taken away and there is no person who would take one.**

He put a rule on it. He is a surveyor and it is what he is for, and it took about four minutes, and it is on the page because he did it and wrote it down and it is the only measurement he has taken in this district in five months.

> *Mark, north side, iron foot, about 5 in. by 3 in., standing water inside it about 1 in. Seven marks in a run of about 3 ft. Six irons above, in the usual order, none displaced. No iron absent that anybody can name, and no iron has been taken, and there is no list of irons anywhere in this city because nobody has ever needed one, which is the seventh column again and I have written that down three times now and I am not going to write it a fourth.*

**And then he went to the north end at about ten to five, because that is where a person goes, and there is a scale at the north end of that row, and he went toward it and he slowed down about nine feet short of it, and then he went back up the Row, and nobody saw him do either of those two things except a woman of about sixty who was in a doorway and who has not said anything about it and is not going to.**

The beam was down on the low side and the poise was at nothing.

It was not at nothing on the twentieth of December, and it was not at nothing on the seventeenth of January, and he has looked at it four times since the sixteenth of December and he has never once been able to say that he saw the poise move, and **he did not write it down, and the reason he did not write it down is the entire standing consequence of the tenth of January and he has said so in one line in his own book and that line is the only repair this district has got out of a page in a tin.**

*There is a poise off nothing on a beam scale at the north end of a row that is not a street. I have got one sheet of accountancy paper in a building in this city with a name on it, undated and unreasoned, and I am not going to be the reason there are two. If that is the only thing that keeps me off a piece of paper then it is the best thing anybody has done for me in five months and it was done by accident by a foreman writing a rule in grease pencil about not taking a line off.*

---

**And then, at about ten to six, on a wall, in the rain, with a rule in his hand and about four people in a market of a place that is not there, the book wrote, and it wrote seven days after it wrote the last time, and the line is six words.**

> *Weather uses whatever door is open.*

*What it does not say, and I am writing this down before I have done one single thing with it, including a yard, including a form, including a column:*

*It does not say whose door. It does not say what is behind it. It does not say whether the door was open before today. **And it is a sentence about a door and not about a seam and not about a market, and a hundred and ten yards of frontage that cannot be given an address is not a market and is not a street, and I have four things in it tonight that I did not go looking for and I am not going to find out what a door is by shutting one.***

---

**And the second half of that afternoon, in a room in Crown Heights, on the same day, a man of about fifty had a printed sheet in front of him with one hand-written line in it and four printed numbers, and he did not write a second line, and the reason is about four hundred yards of frontage and not about the weather.**

The sheet has five rows. Four of them are districts. The fifth of them is a district with a zero in it and a line under *officer responsible* that nobody has filled in since the Friday the thirtieth of January.

There were two things in the river core in nine days: a cover up out of a frame at about twenty past two in the morning with the ballast washed from under it, and a hundred and ten yards of frontage that put fish scales out of a wet stone floor at about five o'clock in the afternoon in the rain, with coffee forty minutes early and seven marks under a rail with six irons on it.

**The first of those got a line in the sheet, in his own hand, with a time, on the Friday morning the thirtieth, in about ten minutes, because it is on a length that a gang of eleven men walks twice a week with a clipboard.**

The second of those did not and cannot, and the reason is nine words long and it is the hinge of this entire volume.

"**I have got one row and it is a district. A row of frontages that is not a street and has no address is not a district and it is not a length, and there is no box on that form for it, and if I write it in the river core row then in about four years somebody asks me what was in the river core on the nineteenth of February and I have to say a market that has not been here for nineteen years.**"

"Nobody would ask that."

"**Everybody asks that. That is the whole of what allocation sheets are for. A sheet that says *a thing happened here and nobody has been* is the only document in this city that has ever been useful to anybody, and I have made one this month and I have not made one in nineteen years and I am not making one about a hundred and ten yards of frontage that has never been on a sheet, and that is not caution, Mr Rook, that is a man who has one child and a mortgage and about eleven years to go.**"

So the second one is not allocated, and the river core row still has one line in it in a man's own hand, and the four printed underneath it still say four, and **a thing has happened in this district in the last nine days that there is no row for anywhere, and the district index has one entry in it that might have been a row, and that entry was put in by a different department in the autumn in the only box there was, and it says a works frontage, and a hundred and ten yards of a market is not a works frontage, and nobody is going to have that entry tidied and nobody is going to add a second one.**

---

**And on the same afternoon, in a yard, a boy of seventeen put a fourth thing face up under a brick and told nine people that he was not going to make a column out of it.**

"There is a man in a works jacket at about ten past two this afternoon, about forty yards off the north end, in a doorway out of the rain, with a notebook out, and he wrote once, and he did not come down the Row." Pell Harrow did not raise his voice and did not look at Milo Rook. "That is four times. Two of them were in December and one was on the fifth of January, and this is the fourth, and he was not there on the fifth of February, which was a Thursday and a walk day, and I was on the round and I saw him not be there.

"**And there are now two cards on that table about a man in a raincoat, and there is a third about a number in a book in a depot, and a man I am not going to describe put a fourth one down this afternoon, and I am going to say the thing I have been saying since the twenty-ninth of January and I would like it written down by somebody who is not me: four sightings is not a pattern, two cards on one brick is not a pattern, and if I put a line under any of them I will have made a column out of a doorway and a book on a shelf, and a column is a case, and I have watched two men in this district make one and I am not going to be the third.**"

Nobody wrote it down. About four people noticed that nobody had, and that is the fifth time in six weeks that nobody has, and the reason is always the same and it is that there is no form for it, and a card under a brick is a thing and not a job.

---

**The Field Book, Thursday the nineteenth of February, 23.15, and it is the longest entry in this book in a month and he has said so at the top of it, and it is not one of the reasons it is long.**

*Nineteenth, 23.15. The book has not stopped since the fourteenth of January. It has been written nine times since, on eight dates, and this is the ninth, and the gap in front of this one is seven days off the twelfth. Where the ten were, and they are still the ten they were on the fourteenth and I have not gone back over them: one six, four threes, one nine, one two, one five, one eight, and one twenty-five. And what this month has added is three more, which are a three and two sevens, and the three is a fifth three and it is not one of the four. **That is thirteen, and the four threes are not collapsed, and the fifteen days before the twenty-ninth of January was a silence and I did not write the two ends of it down then and I am not going to write them down now, and the one-day interval from the tenth of December is still looked at and is still not written, and I am joining none of them to any other and I am not going to write down what thirteen of a thing means because the whole of this book is a record of things I have not understood and I have never once been wrong about the count and I have been wrong about everything else.***

*What happened on a row of frontages that is not a street, in the rain, at about five o'clock in the afternoon, in front of about nine people, and nobody was hurt, and nothing fell down, and there is no seam, and there is not one thing in this afternoon that I could put in a box:*

*One. Fish scales out of a wet stone floor for the first time in nineteen years, about nine thousand of them, in the run of a gutter, confirmed by a man of about forty who has swept that row every morning since he was twenty-one.*

*Two. Coffee on that row at ten to five, forty minutes early, in rain, and there has been no coffee sold within a hundred and ten yards of it since the year before the war, and six people could not tell me where it was coming from, and the man it belongs to was not there and has not been asked.*

*Three. Seven marks under a rail with six irons on it. I put a rule on them: about five inches by three, standing water in each, in a run of about three feet, no iron absent that anybody can name. **And there is no list of irons anywhere in this city because nobody has ever needed one, and a thing with no list cannot have a thing taken off it, and that is a thing I have wanted to write about for five months and I have finally got one piece of evidence for it and it is seven rectangles of standing water in the rain.***

*Four. A beam scale at the north end with the poise at nothing when it was at nothing four times before, or at something four times before, and I do not know which, and **I have not written it down and I am not going to, and the reason is a page in a tin, and that page is the only thing in five months that has stopped me writing something down, and a stopping that works by accident is the only kind that lasts.***

*Five. A man of about fifty in a drainage section did not put a second line in a printed sheet, in his own hand, in a row that is a district, and gave me nine words for it, and I have written his nine words out in full because they are better than mine, and because the whole of this winter has been arrived at by people who do not have my job.*

*Six. And the thing that this is and I did not know it was going to be when I came out in a wet coat at half past four. **The Row is a remnant. It is on the page, it was read in December by a man with a chain, it is a Keepsake remnant and it has eleven habits in it and about four people, and I am not going to stand here in the rain and invent a fourth kind of place because a form has no row for it.** A length is a third kind of place and that is on the page and it is not being moved. What has happened tonight is much smaller than either of those and it is the same thing that happened on the first of January and on the seventh, and the same hole: **there is no row anywhere in this city for a place that people use and that no department can enter, and there is not going to be one, and the district index has one entry in it that might have been a row and that entry was put in by a different department in the autumn in the only box there was, and it says a works frontage, and a hundred and ten yards of a market is not a works frontage, and nobody is going to have that entry tidied and nobody is going to add a second one.** So the second one of the eleven is not allocated, and the river core row still has one line in it, and the four printed underneath it still say four, and **a thing has happened in this district in nine days that there is no row for, and the honest statement of that is not a mystery and is not a category, it is that the city's paperwork is built out of places and this is a place and it does not fit, and the seventh of January said the same thing about a length in nine words and I did not understand it for four months.**

*Six words, on a wall, at ten to six, in the rain, with a rule in my hand. It does not say whose door. It does not say what is behind it. It does not say whether the door was open before today. **I am not going to find out what a door is by shutting one, and I have got one idea in this book about anything and I have had six in five months and every one of them has been about a page, and I am not going to let this one be about a market.***

*The page home the eighty-eighth day, blank, three marks in three hands. Ten routes, ten, none removed, and I have added nothing and lost nothing, and the ninth crossing still did not take one and nobody has explained it and I have stopped expecting it to be explained. The token cold, seventy-fourth day since the seventh of December, and I have not taken it out, and a book that counts a coat is a book that has run out of other things, and I am writing that down so that I have written it.*
