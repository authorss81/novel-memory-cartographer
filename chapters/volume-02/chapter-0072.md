# Chapter 72: There Is No Box For It

The gang was on the back length at half past seven on the morning of Monday the fifth of January and it was the first walk of a week that runs to Sunday, and the sheet for that week has two dates in it and two entries in the hours column and the reason column empty on both, and the foreman wrote it standing at the wall the way he does.

A foreman's week is not a walk. A walk is fifty minutes on a Thursday morning and everybody in this district has the shape of it in their heads wrong, because a walk is the part you can see, and a week is eleven men in four rooms above a fitter's shop from about nine in the morning and the requisition for the week's stores and the returns for the previous week and a kettle and a radio and a man of about fifty-four going through about four hundred small things in his own order, none of which is a decision and all of which is the job.

Milo Rook was in the quay section office from about ten until about four on the Monday and he was not on the round and he had not come for the walk.

He had come with a piece of accountancy paper on which he had drawn, in his own hand, a proposed entry.

---

**The entry he wanted was four lines long and it was for the district's own index, and the four lines were a heading, a nature, a reference and a signature space, and he had spent the Sunday evening on them and had gone backwards over them twice.**

> *Proposed entry, district index, quayside section.*
> *Name: **[to be supplied]**.*
> *Nature of place: maintenance length, tram, reserved, no booked service.*
> *Rateable reference: **none — not a rateable place.***
> *Maintained by: quay section gang; inspection Mondays and Thursdays.*

He got the first three and a half lines of that right on the first attempt and it took him four attempts to stop, and the thing that stopped him was not an argument, it was that he had written the word *Name* in a box and had nothing to put in it, and he had gone round the kitchen twice looking for something.

What he had instead was a reference, and the reference was the number of the fourth edition of the quay section maintenance schedule, **PW/Q/4**, and he had a very good reason for the reference, and the reason was written on the second line in his own hand and it is the reason he gave to two people in four rooms over the course of the day and then struck out of his own document on the Monday afternoon at about twenty past three.

*The reason: a maintenance length that exists in two buildings is a maintenance length that somebody can find. The record of the week of the twenty-first is in a rack in the quay section office and a carbon of it is on a bottom shelf in a plan room in this building under a repair schedule, and a fire in the quay section office in March would be a fire in a building nobody has insured properly since about 1961, and if that happened then the twenty-second of December would be gone, and there would be no way of showing anybody it had not been a thing we made up.*

---

**The first refusal was from a works clerk of forty-six at his own desk, standing up, at about ten past ten on the Monday morning, and it was nine words and then eleven more.**

Cyril Sale read it all the way through twice, which nobody had expected, and then he put it down.

"Refused," he said. "And I am going to tell you why and I am not going to be pleasant about it, and then I am going to go back to my desk because I have four lengths and I have not done Wednesday's."

"Go on."

"You have written a box with *rateable reference: none, not a rateable place* in it." He put his thumb on it without touching the ink. "Do you know what that line does in this building? It is the only line in your four that does anything, and it does the opposite of what you want it to do. It says: this is a thing that exists and it is responsible for nothing. Every department in this city reads that line and reads it as a hole. And a hole is a thing you can put something in, and the first department that puts something in a hole is the one that has found a place with no person attached to it, and that is a Tuesday in March and a form with a name on it, and there will be a man in an office saying *there was nothing there before and now there is.*"

"I have written that it is not a rateable place on purpose."

"You have written it on purpose and it is the reason I have refused you." Cyril Sale did not raise his voice and neither did he soften it, and the two men who work in that office with him were in the room and both of them have said since that they were not enjoying it. "Mr Rook, I sent a copy of that schedule down to a building nine hundred yards from this one on the eighth of October because a man telephoned me on a Thursday and asked for it *for index*, and I have thought about that telephone call every day since you first asked me about it, and I have not stopped thinking about it, and I have got a wife and I go home at five. That is what a reference is. **A reference is a place other people can write into.** I have spent four years writing a number at the top of a piece of paper so that about nine people in four buildings can find the thing I have written, and every one of those nine has a form, and not one of the nine has a shovel."

He turned the sheet round and put it face down on the desk, which is the quay section's way of refusing something without throwing it at anybody.

"You want the length to be findable so that the record of the twenty-second of December survives a fire. I understand the whole of that and I have not got an argument against it and I have got an answer, and the answer is that in about eleven years this office will be closed, and the racks will go to a store, and the racks in the store will be cleared on a schedule that is not mine, and I have seen it happen to a section in the north of this city and I was told about it afterwards by a man who is not here. **The sheet for the week of the twenty-first is not lost in a fire. The sheet for the week of the twenty-first is lost in about eleven years because somebody clears a rack, and the person who clears the rack is not a villain either, and there is not one thing you can put on a piece of paper that will stop it.** And what you have put on your piece of paper will not stop it and it will make it worse, because a length that can be found is a length that can be written on."

---

**The second refusal was in a different building from the first one and it came from a man who had not read the application and would not.**

Dilley Farr was in the route room with a repair schedule on the board and about four hundred sheets in a plan chest, and he refused it standing up in about six sentences without being asked to read more than the heading.

"No," he said. "And I am not going to read the other three lines and I would like that minuted in whatever you are keeping."

"It is a public index entry. It is a place on a map with a note that there is not a building there. It is the only honest way to put it in a book."

"You have come to the wrong building," said Dilley Farr, "and I have been in this one for thirty-one years and I know what is in it, and there is one thing in this building that has never once been asked of it and I am not going to start today. **A public map is not a place to put a length.** A public map is a place people go to find out where they are. The day there is a mark on the public sheet for a length of tram route that is not on the diagram, then a man reads the sheet and comes and looks for it, and he comes on his own time and he brings a dog, and he is standing on six hundred yards of reserved length on a Tuesday evening with a shovel in his hand, and there is nobody on it, and it is not a street and it is not a road, and he does not know that. And then what."

"Then what," said Milo Rook.

"Then in about four years there is a report about a man who went onto a reserved length and there is nothing in it about a gang, because a public sheet does not carry a gang, it carries a mark, and the mark is the map's and the gang is nobody's." Dilley Farr put the repair schedule straight. "You have a map of practices on three feet of accountancy paper in a drawer somewhere and it is a good piece of work and I have read it and I have said so to a foreman. It is not going in a press. It is a sheet that eleven men can walk routes off. That is all it is for. **If it goes in a press it is a mark and a mark is a claim and about four people in this district have been eaten by a claim in six years.**"

---

**And the third refusal came from a man of about fifty-four at the wall, on the Thursday morning, at half past seven, at about eleven minutes in, in the cold, with eleven men standing about him with shovels, and it was the shortest of the three and it was the one Milo Rook wrote down in his own book in full.**

"No," said Roy Pryce. "You are not putting a reference on my schedule."

"I am not putting a reference on your schedule. It is a cross-reference from the index to—"

"You are putting a reference on my schedule and it does not matter whose paper it is on, because a cross-reference is a reference and I have four lengths and a number against each of them and the numbers are mine and they mean one thing, which is the drainage and inspection of that length. If your number goes next to my number then a man in four years reads a row with two numbers on it and a column headed *reference* and the only question that row will ever be asked is *what is the second one for*, and the answer is going to be a length of tram route that cannot be given a street, and there is going to be an officer in an office who reads that row and writes *maintenance length* in a box, and *maintenance length* is a phrase that means the same thing as *not a place* for about four years, and then it means the same thing as *not yet a place*, and then one of my eleven men is going to have a car on a length and a document about it, and I am not having that on a Monday in January."

He looked at the clipboard and then at the wall, which is the last pitch on Saltmarket Row on the other side of it.

"Ask me again in about four months," he said, "when you know what a fella in an office is going to say to me, and ask me then and not now, because now it is a Tuesday practice and in four months it is a document and I have been a foreman for thirty-one years and I know the difference between them to the day."

---

**What a foreman's week is, and what it is not, in about two hundred words, on the Tuesday and the Wednesday, with the kettle on.**

It is the requisition. It is the returns, and the returns are eleven rows and one of them is the week of the twenty-first of December and the reason column in it is nine words in a foreman's hand and **it is the only entry in four years of that return in which a reason is written at all, and nobody has queried it and nobody queried it in December and nobody queried it in January and it is going to be in a book in a rack in eleven years and a man in an office is going to read it and ask about it and there is not one word in it that belongs to a person.** It is the stores: eleven shovels, four brooms, a length of line, and coal. It is a man of about twenty-four who wants the day off in February and is told yes in about four seconds. It is a fitter who has sent up a piece of gear that is not working and cannot be made to work and has to be written off, and the write-off has a reason box, and the reason is a sentence, and the sentence is about a cost and not about a feeling, and the whole batch of paperwork from a gang foreman in this city is that: reasons that are about money.

And it is a man of about twenty-six who stops in the down loop.

He stopped in the down loop for about two minutes and a half on the Monday morning and for about two minutes on the Thursday morning, and the rest of the gang carried on round him, and this has been going on for two years on every walk of that length, and nobody in this district has ever asked him and Doreen Sankey has said he is not going to start and there is a standing note about that.

On the Thursday morning at about twenty-five minutes past seven, the foreman said to him, in front of eleven men, in the flat ordinary voice of a man giving an instruction, **"Do not stand in the loop in the cold, we will do it in a minute."** And the man of about twenty-six said he would, and stood in the loop for two more minutes, and then came round.

And Milo Rook was on the north path at about half past seven that morning because he had been told where to stand, and he wrote it down, and he did not ask.

---

**And the fourth way, which nobody refused, and which was the answer, and which cost nothing and was the worst of the four.**

There was one more place the record could go and Milo Rook found it on the Tuesday afternoon at about half past two, and it was not a building and it was not a reference and it was not an entry, and a foreman of fifty-four told him about it in a hut with a kettle on in about ninety words, standing up, and then went back to his week.

"The sheet is the gang's," said Roy Pryce. "That is what I have been saying to you since the seventeenth of December and I did not think I was going to be thanked for it. The sheet is not a record of the length. The sheet is a record of what eleven men did, and the reason it is in a rack is so that a foreman can see in March what a foreman did in December, and it is a record of work, and it has no opinion in it and it is never going to have."

He put the kettle down on its ring.

"So where is the length's record."

"There is no such thing."

"There is. **I am the record.** Eleven men, twice a week, since the year before the war, and if I stop coming then the length is not kept and it is a piece of track with a fence round it, and I have told you that in front of nine people and I would like it written down that I have told you it twice. The paper is a courtesy to the gang. The paper is how a man in a new job in 2051 knows that the shovels were counted. It is not the length and it is not a record of the length and it is not a thing anybody can lose, because a thing that a man walks twice a week is not kept in a rack."

"And in about four months a man in an office asks you whether you saw thirty-one persons get off a car."

"Then I will say yes, and I will not have a piece of paper next to it, and that is exactly how it should be, and you can tell him that if you like and I will not put my name to you saying it." Roy Pryce looked at the clipboard. "**Mr Rook. You have spent four months finding out that a place is kept by people and documents, and that is true, and it is the half of it. The other half is that a place is kept by people, and the document is how the next people find out. And the second half is worth about eleven men and a wall, and the first half is worth a rack in a building, and you have spent thirteen days trying to make the first half do the second half's work.**"

---

**The Field Book, Tuesday the fifth of January, 23.40, a front room, standing up.**

*Fifth, 23.40. I have been refused three times in two days by three people who were right three times and I want the three in the book with who did them and what they said and I do not want them softened.*

*A works clerk of forty-six: a reference is a place other people can write into, and not one of the nine people who can find my four numbers has a shovel, and the number I have wanted to put on that length is a hole with a sign over it.*

*A man of thirty-one years in a room with three atlases on the wall: a public map is not a place to put a length, a public map is a place people go to find out where they are, and the day there is a mark on the public sheet for six hundred yards that is not on the diagram, then a man comes on his own time with a dog and there is nobody on it.*

*A foreman of fifty-four at the wall, in the cold, in about nine words, and he told me to ask him again in four months and not now, and I am going to do as I am told and it is going to be harder than being refused now.*

*And the answer, which I did not go out and get and which was sitting in a hut the whole time, from a man who has been a foreman for thirty-one years: **I am the record.** The sheet is a courtesy to the gang. Eleven men twice a week since the year before the war. If I stop coming the length is not kept and it is a piece of track with a fence round it.*

*So there is no record of the length and there never was, and the thing I have been trying to make is not a record and cannot be made into one, and what I have actually wanted, since the twenty-second of December, is for a piece of paper somewhere to say that a thing happened, and that is a different want and it is a want about me and not about a tram, and I have known that for about two days and I have not written it down until now.*

*Sixteen days. The book is silent. The twentieth of December to the fourteenth of January is twenty-five, and if it writes before then that is a pair, and I am not going to help it and I am not going to write the number down twice.*

*The token cold. Ten routes, ten. The page home the forty-third day, three marks, blank, and I have written the imperative half at the front of today's page and I have read it back and reading it back does not work and I am not going to keep reading it back.*
