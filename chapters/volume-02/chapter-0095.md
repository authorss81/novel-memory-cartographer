# Chapter 95: He Is Not There On A Wednesday

On Monday the sixteenth of March a gang of eleven men and a foreman of fifty-four tried to report a cast-iron cover, and the report could not be made, and the reason it could not be made is correct, and it is nine words long, and it was said by a works clerk of forty-six at his own desk at about twenty past eight in the morning with a form in front of him.

The cover is in the down loop of about six hundred yards of reserved tram route that has been out of use since the thirteenth of October and has been rocking about two inches proud of its frame since the second of February. Eleven men have walked past it on a Monday and a Thursday for seven weeks. A foreman has written three words about it in a condition column every Thursday and the reason column is empty every Thursday, and the correct entry has not changed since the twenty-ninth of January.

**Nobody in the city can report it, and nobody in the city can be wrong about why.**

---

The form is the authority's own. It has been in use since before anybody in that office was born and it is a single side and it is the correct shape of the thing it is for, and it has five boxes.

> **PERMANENT WAY — REPORT OF DEFECT**
> *Location. Chainage. Description. Risk to passengers. Action taken.*

"Location," said Cyril Sale.

"**Back length,**" said Roy Pryce. "Down loop, north side, about nine foot short of the drain the lad puts his ear on. Chainage — "

"You have not got a chainage."

"I have a chainage off the schedule. PW/Q/4, length two of the third edition, and it runs from the interchange at about three hundred and ten to the water stairs at about nine hundred and ten, so the loop is about one hundred and forty to one hundred and sixty on the section's own reckoning, and it is on my sheet and it is right."

"That is a section's reckoning and not a chainage," said Cyril Sale, "and I am going to write that on the form and it is going to come back."

He wrote in the first box: *Back length (unnumbered), down loop.* And that is the whole of the problem and it took the rest of the morning.

"There is no place called that," he said, at about a quarter to nine, with a pencil in his hand. "**There is a number and there is a schedule and there is a length, and a length is not a place, and there is not a box on this form for a thing that is not a place.** If I write the interchange, then somebody comes out and looks at the interchange, and the interchange is about three hundred yards north of where he is standing and the report is a report about a different place. If I write the water stairs, it is six hundred yards further. And the box does not want a length. The box wants a place it can send a man to, and **that is not a complaint about the form. That box is a man with a barrow on a Tuesday and he cannot find six hundred yards of track because it has not got a name, and he will not find it on a diagram either, because the diagram took it off in October and the diagram was right to take it off.**"

"Then put the schedule number in the chainage box," said somebody behind him.

"I could put PW/Q/4 in the chainage box and it would be accepted on the Monday and rejected on the Thursday," said Cyril Sale. "**I know that because I have been doing this form for four years and I have watched a fella in a shed do it, and the second time it comes back it comes back with a sentence on it instead of a dash, and the sentence says *chainage not in section series*, and that is nine words and all of them are correct and none of them are about a drain.**"

---

**And the fourth box is the one that finished it, and it is a good box, and it is the same good box that a clerk of forty-six stood at a window about four minutes over in January.**

*Risk to passengers.*

"Nil," said Roy Pryce, at about nine o'clock, without being asked, and it is the same answer he gave in January in about the same words, and it is true.

"No," said Cyril Sale. "I cannot put nil in that box and I will tell you why and then I am going to do a job. **That box is asking whether a person is at risk. And the answer to that box about a length is that there is nobody at risk because there is no booked service and there are no passengers, and if I put nil in it, then in about four years somebody reads a nil on a risk-to-passengers box in the river core and concludes that no passengers have ever been on any of the quay lengths, and there were thirty-one of them on one of them in December with a conductor of thirty-eight and a driver of fifty-one, and the whole of that is because a length is not a place.**"

"What do you put in it?"

"Nothing," said Cyril Sale. "**You leave it. And I am going to write on the back of it, in my own hand, with my name at the bottom and the date, that the box is left empty and why, and I am going to put it in the section's correspondence tray and not in a drawer, and then it is a piece of paper in a tray in a building nine hundred yards from that cover, and there is no copy and nobody will read it for about four years, and I have done that before in January and I am doing it again in March, and I would like it noticed that I have noticed.**"

Roy Pryce read what he wrote, and signed nothing on it, because he signs the first, second, third and fourth lines, and he said the other thing, which is the thing he has said about three words in a condition column for seven weeks.

"**The cover rocks and the sheet does not and the sheet is the truer of the two,**" he said. "And I have got eleven men walking past it twice a week and I have not got one box in this city that will take a sentence about it, and you are the fourth person in four months to come at that with a piece of stationery, and I am going to say this once and then I am going to go and do a walk."

---

**And at about ten to six on the evening of the same Monday, on a wall at the top of a hundred and ten yards of frontage that is not a street, in the dark, with a chain over his shoulder that he did not use, Milo Rook's book wrote, and it is six words.**

> *A habit is not a calendar.*

*It does not say which habit. It does not say whose calendar. It does not say what a calendar is for. **And I have a piece of accountancy paper in a yard in the rain that is about six people's Saturdays and a column in it with a why at the end, and I am not going to find out what a habit is by putting it in a column, and I have had six ideas in six months and every one of them has been about a page, and I am not going to let this one be about a market.**

---

**And on Wednesday the nineteenth of March at about ten past six in the morning, a man of about forty put a broom down in a hundred and ten yards of frontage and said nine words, and the nine words are the whole of this chapter and he is not a surveyor and he has never been asked for anything in his life.**

He had been sweeping that row badly at ten past six for as long as anybody in this district has been keeping the tally, and there was no coffee on it, and there never is on a Wednesday.

"He is not here on a Wednesday," he said.

"He is here at half past five—"

"**He is not here on a Wednesday and he has not been here on a Wednesday since before the war,**" said the man of about forty, "because there is a stall at Cardew Docks that opens at six and it is open on a Wednesday and that is the day it is busy, and he makes the coffee for it and he carries it up here at half past five on the other six nights and puts his hand on that corner for four seconds and walks off at four miles an hour, and on a Wednesday he does not do it, and he has never told anybody that and I have never told anybody and I have swept this row since I was twenty-one."

Milo Rook stood on a hundred and ten yards of frontage and worked out what had been written on a piece of accountancy paper for seven weeks, and it took him about eleven seconds and it is the longest eleven seconds of his working life.

"**The second side has got a blank on it every Wednesday since the second of February,**" he said. "A blank on that side is a day when nobody stood in the weather. And it has been a day when a man of about sixty stood in the weather eleven minutes away, at a shed, where a stall opens at six, and the four of us who have ever been on this row in the evening could not see it and did not know it."

"Yes."

"And there is no way to correct it with the rules on that sheet, because the rules are about a person and the blank is about a day, and **a day that is blank is not wrong, and a thing that is not wrong cannot be struck out, and there is no column anywhere on that piece of paper for a man who is somewhere else.**"

"No," said the man of about forty, and picked his broom up. "**That is the first time anybody has said it and I have been saying it to myself since the war and I could not make it into a sentence, and you have made it into a sentence in about eleven seconds and I am not going to pretend I am pleased about it.**"

---

**And what was done about it took about two minutes and it was done by the man with the broom and Milo Rook was not asked and had no advice and says so.**

He did not strike anything. He did not cross anything out. He went and got the second side out of the satchel — which was in the yard, because the satchel was in the yard — and turned it over, and wrote on it, underneath the rule, in his own hand, in about twenty-eight words.

> *Wed — he is at the shed at half past five and has been since before the war. He has not stopped. The side cannot see a Wednesday and a side is not a person.*

He did not put a date against it and he did not put his initials against it and he said why, when Milo Rook asked him for a date in the ordinary way, and the reason is nine more words and it is about the first side of the same sheet.

"**The first side has got a column for whether I am wrong and I would have to initial it, and the second side is a day and I was not on the day,**" he said. "So I have not initialed it and I have not dated it, and if anybody ever asks me I will say I wrote it and I will not have to say why, which is the same as what he says about the day, and neither of us has said that to the other one and I have not noticed it until this morning and I am not going to."

Then he read out the whole of what he had written, once, in the voice a man uses when he is not sure he has said a thing right, and a woman of about forty-five who had come out of her doorway at about ten past five to ask what was going on heard it, and said one thing about it and then went back in.

"I am not adding a paragraph to my own rule," she said. "**The moment there is a second paragraph there is an instruction in it, and a person can say no to an instruction, and nobody can say no to a line. I have had that rule up for six weeks and about nine people have found ways round it and I have not lifted it and I am not lifting it now, and a man's twenty-eight words in a row at the foot of my side is a line and there is nothing under it and nothing over it and it does not tell anybody they have to do anything.**"

---

**And at about twenty past six that morning the coffee man of about sixty was not asked anything by anybody, and does not know that a piece of accountancy paper exists, and has not been told, and the man with the broom has not told him and said why, in a yard, at about nine, on the Sunday, without being asked.**

"He has said twice it is not for anybody and he means it every time," the man of about forty said. "**I have swept this row since I was twenty-one and I have never once told him what time I am out here and I am not going to start by telling him about a piece of paper.**"

---

**And what did not happen, and is on the page because it is on the page: a tram gong went off at the end of Cardew Street on Monday the sixteenth of March at about ten to six in the evening and went on for about eleven seconds, and at four seconds into it Milo Rook could not tell what it was, and he needed to, because a Line 3 car comes down that street at about that time and the coffee man walks against the traffic from Cardew Docks at half past five, and he wanted to know whether a car was coming so that he could say so.**

He did not get it. It is the third time and it is a sound and it is not a taste and neither of them has a face on it, and he has decided nothing about whether that is one thing or two, and he wrote the time down twice and used a watch.

The man put his hand on the corner at four seconds and walked off at four miles an hour, and he was not in the road, and nothing happened, and Milo Rook has written in his own book that **he wanted to say so and could not, and that a man of about sixty is owed a shout by somebody, and that the somebody is now going to be somebody else, and that this is the third time in five months and the pattern of it is that it is always on a day when he is standing in the dark on a wall writing things down, and he has written that down and is not going to do anything about it.**

---

**The Field Book, Tuesday the eighteenth of March, 23.20, a front room, and the sixteenth and the seventeenth of March have neither of them got an entry, and he has written down that they have not and has not written down why, and the pair in front of this one is three days off the fifteenth.**

*Eighteenth, 23.20. Three days off the fifteenth. The sixteenth and the seventeenth have no entries and the reason for the sixteenth is that I spent the evening on a wall and did not write it up, and the reason for the seventeenth is that it was a Sunday and I had nothing, and a two-day run of nothing is a two-day run of nothing and not a pair. **The pair in front of this one is a three, and it is the seventh three in this book, and the four that were here in December are the four that were here in December and none of them is one of the other three, and the fifth was added in the first week of February and the sixth on the twenty-third.** And there is one six in this book and it is a six.

*One. **A man of about forty swept a row at ten past six on a Wednesday morning and said nine words, and the nine words are the whole of the finding of this month, and I did not find it, and I was not even in the room when it was said, and I have not been able to stop thinking about the fact that he said it to me and not to a surveyor.** He has swept that row since he was twenty-one. He did not tell anybody and there was nobody to tell. And a blank on the second side of that sheet is a day when nobody stood in the weather, and there has been a blank on it every Wednesday since the second of February, and on every one of those Wednesdays a man of about sixty stood in a shed at Cardew Docks eleven minutes away and made coffee for a stall that opens at six. **And there is no way to put that in a column, because the column that is for a fault is a column about a person and a day is not a person, and the side that is for a day cannot hold a fault, and so the sheet has been saying, correctly, in its own terms, that nobody stood in the weather, on a day when somebody did, seven weeks running.** That is the same sentence a man of about twenty-six said in a yard in February and it is the same finding and it is worse in a way I have not got a word for, and I am not going to say the worse thing out loud in a yard for about a year.

*Two. A form with five boxes came back to a section office four times in one morning and it was right four times. *Location, chainage, description, risk to passengers, action taken.* **There is no box for a length. There is no box for a number that a man can stand at. The risk box wants a nil and a nil is a lie about the twenty-second of December and a word is a sentence about a section, and a clerk of forty-six has now put one of those sentences on the back of a form in a correspondence tray with his name on it and the date, and he has said out loud that he has done it before in January and has noticed himself doing it again, and nobody has thanked him and I am not going to.** That cover has been rocking for seven weeks and there is no box in this city that will take a sentence about it and the form is not going to be changed and the reason is a man with a barrow on a Tuesday, and the man with the barrow is right and the box is right and the cover is a hazard and there is nowhere to put the three of those facts together.

*Three. Six words on a wall at ten to six on Monday. *A habit is not a calendar.* It does not say which habit, it does not say whose calendar, and I have not got a use for it that is not a page, and I have written down that I do not have a use for it, and that is now six ideas in six months and every one of them has been about a page, and I am not going to let this one be about a market.

*Four. A sound on Monday at ten to six, at the end of Cardew Street, eleven seconds, and I could not tell what it was at four seconds, and I needed to, and I did not get it, and a man of about sixty put his hand on a corner and walked off at four miles an hour and was not in the road, and the man of about forty who sweeps that row was not there because it was a Monday, and I was standing in the dark on a wall writing things down while it happened. That is the third time and I have written down the shape of it, which is that it is always the same and I am always doing the same thing, and I have got no better at it and I have decided this month that I am not going to get better at it and I am going to be a man who is standing in the dark on a wall when it happens, and that is a worse sentence than the first one and I am keeping it.*

*The page home the hundred and fifteenth day, blank, three marks in three hands. Ten routes, ten. The token cold, hundred and first day. The tally has nine lines. I did not cross and I did not hold anything and I did not go near the north end of that row and I have not written down why and I am not going to, and I want it read back to me in April that I did not go, and I want somebody else to have written it down by then.*
