# Chapter 71: The First Of January

The works did not shut for the first of January either.

The gang was on the back length at half past seven on the morning of Thursday the first, and it was the second walk of that week and both of them are on one sheet, because the week runs from Monday the twenty-ninth of December to Sunday the third of January and the sheet covers Monday to Sunday, and there is no version of a calendar in this city in which Monday the twenty-ninth of December and Thursday the first of January are in different weeks. It was nineteen degrees below freezing at the water stairs and eleven men were on that length with a shovel each and a trolley between them and a clipboard, and a foreman of fifty-four wrote something on the clipboard at the wall and came back up.

Nobody watched. That is the thing about a machine and it is the first thing anybody in this district understood about a machine, and it took a year and a reach and a wall to learn it and it was not learned for free.

Roy Pryce's sheet for that week has two lines in the date column and two lines in the hours column and **the reason column is empty on both of them**, which is correct and which is the only correct thing about it, and he wrote it that way himself and would have written it that way if nobody had ever asked him.

The sheet went into the rack on the Friday the second at about ten in the morning, with the week before it and the week before that, and the duplicate went into a tin at the end of the corridor, and the duplicate for the week of the twenty-first of December also goes into a second tin in the plan room of the Civic Atlas Office on the bottom shelf under the repair schedule, and **Dilley Farr has signed for that tin once and has not been asked to sign for it again, because the signature on the second tin is a standing instruction and the foreman wrote it in a box on the side of the tin in grease pencil on the Friday the second with a signature under it, and the foreman's initials are R. PRYCE and there is no column on a tin.**

---

**And the city did not stop, and this is the sentence the first of January is actually about.**

At ten past six in the morning a man of about forty swept a hundred and ten yards of row badly from the top end to the bottom end, because he sweeps it. At about twenty past five the evening before, a woman of about forty-five called the lot prices from a doorway in about two minutes with no fish. At half past five the evening before, a coffee man of about sixty put his hand on the corner of the same wall for about four seconds and took it away again and walked eleven minutes back to a shed at Cardew Docks in his own time for a stall that opens at six.

Hettie Brann opened the water clinic counter at eight on the morning of the first with a queue of about nine people in it, and about four of them had come about the same thing and two of them were children and one of them was a woman who wanted a form, and she gave her the form.

Seven children were to go back to the school in the spring on a Continuity referral signed in a woman's own hand in the same minute and with the same signature that puts a levy on her. **Two of the nine are still lost and there is no third and nobody in this district has said the word back about it since the fifteenth of December.** A hundred and forty feet of named frontage is behind a fence with a settlement plate on it and a date on the plate and a gang goes on it every fortnight. A mechanic of sixty-one is on a drainage record forever and will not swear. A conductor of thirty is in a printed rule book and his own hearing has no date. A woman of fifty-four has a plot number and no bill in six years. A man of forty-one behind a boatyard is on no list at all and never has been. **A man asleep on the third floor of a building with no number is not written down and has not been woken.** A woman of sixty-eight is on nothing.

**None of it moved because it was the first of January, and that is the correct behaviour of all of it, and about four people in this district have noticed that the correct behaviour of all of it is not the same thing as it being all right.**

---

**The offices opened at nine on the morning of Thursday the first of January, and there was a queue.**

The Registry, the tram authority and the offices in Crown Heights had shut at noon on Wednesday the twenty-fourth of December and had been shut four days, and they opened again on the twenty-ninth, and the twenty-ninth and the thirtieth and the thirty-first were the three days in which everybody in this city who had spent four days not being able to find anybody came at once, and by nine o'clock on the first of January there was a queue at the district index section counter on the ground floor of the Civic Atlas Office of **about eleven people**, and every one of them wanted something that could not be given, and every one of them was told so correctly.

Milo Rook was in that queue at about ten past nine with a sheet of accountancy paper in his coat and a Field Book in the other pocket and no expectation whatsoever of getting anything, and he has said since that the queue was the most useful thing that happened to him in January.

"Four days is not a long time," the woman at the counter said to the fourth person in the line, and it was the day before yesterday from where she was standing and it was the fourth day from where they were, and both of those were true and she said it once. "It is a long time when you have been told to come back." She did not look up again until the person had gone.

He asked the second question in the line and got a clerk, because the first question had been about a sewer and the second question was about whether a person could be entered.

"Can a person be entered against a maintenance length."

The clerk was about thirty-four and she had the district index open in front of her in a book the size of a family bible, and she did not say *no*, and that is the thing that is on the page and it is the reason the day went the way it went.

"Which maintenance length."

"About six hundred yards of tram route between the Alder Quay interchange and the water stairs. It is on the quay section's schedule. It came off the public diagram in October and it is not a street and it is not a road and it has got a gang on it twice a week and a wall at the south end."

"That is not in this index," said the clerk. "That is not a place. I could not enter it if I had it, because there is a column here for the name of the street and a column here for the nature of the place and a column here for a rateable reference, and a length of tram route has not got a street and has not got a building, and the rateable reference is the only one of those three that the book will not do without, and I have tried it before for a drainage catchment and I have been told no by a man in a different department with a better claim to the truth than me." She turned the book round and showed him the row. It was the row for a hard with no frontage on it and there were four such rows and one of them had a name and the other three had numbers in a different column. "If somebody gives me a rateable thing, I will enter it, and I will enter it in about four minutes, and I have wanted to."

"Thank you," said Milo Rook.

"You have not asked me the question you came to ask," said the clerk. "Everybody asks me that question and then they go away looking pleased. Ask me the other one."

---

**He asked her the other one at about a quarter to ten in the same corridor, and it took about two minutes, and it is the whole of the first of January.**

"On the twenty-second of December," said Milo Rook, "a gang of eleven men walked that length a second time at about eleven o'clock in the morning, with a foreman, and there were thirty-three people on it — thirty-one passengers, a conductor and a driver — and a conductor's word was said at four points on the length by people who were not on a tram, and the car came off the north end at twenty to five that afternoon and ran its whole round and came into the shed road, and thirty-three people got off it, and one of them had been on that car since seven fifty-two on the morning of the sixteenth of December, which is five days and two hours."

"Where would you like it entered," said the clerk.

"Anywhere it would be true."

"That is not a place either," said the clerk, "and I am being serious, and I have been a clerk in this section for eleven years and I have learned the one thing about this book, which is that it is not a record of what happened. **It is a record of what is where.** Those are not the same and this book has wanted the difference for about a hundred and forty years and it is very good at it and I have never once seen it hold a fact about a person."

He stood in the corridor for a while.

"Try it with me," he said. "Just — run through it. What would I have to do."

"You would have to have a place," said the clerk. "Everything in this book hangs off a place. That is not a policy, it is a hinge, and it is underneath everything on this table. A name on this index is entered against a rateable thing, and a rateable thing is a building with a street. **A maintenance length is not a building and it cannot be given a street, and the second one is not a matter of administration.** You could put a name against it this afternoon if the district council passed a resolution making a rateable place out of a piece of track, and there is a form for that, and on the form there is a box for the rateable value and a box for the person who certifies the level, and about four months ago a man in this city tried to fill the second box in and could not and wrote *no person available* in it and it is in a file in Crown Heights, and I have read about it and I have not been asked."

Milo Rook said, "Thank you," again, and meant it differently the second time.

---

**And then he did what he always does, and it took him eleven days, and this is the first of the three days.**

He spent from about ten until about four in the afternoon on the first of January going round four counters in one building and one in another, with the same seven lines on the same sheet of accountancy paper, and he wrote down where each of them had to go and what each of them said, and the sheet is the only thing he made on the first of January that anybody could use, and it is a sheet of paper with a list of doors on it, and here it is in full, and there is nothing in his hand in it.

**One. The tram authority, timetable division.** A running sheet has a box for a car number, a box for a time in, a box for a time out, and a fault box. *Ran through a length not on the diagram.* He had been told this in December by a works clerk of forty-six and he has written it down twice. A man in the division of about fifty said it again on the first of January and then added something the works clerk had not said, and this is the new thing and it is the whole chapter: **the fault box is completed by the shed, and the shed completes it from the car's own record, and the car's own record on the twenty-second of December is a conductor's return that says thirty-one alighted at three places, and there is nowhere on that return for where the car was.** So the fault box cannot be completed from the record and the division cannot complete it from anything else, and the sheet is held open, and it has been held open since the twenty-second of December, and **a sheet of paper in this city can sit half-filled for a long time without anybody noticing and that is not a complaint, it is how the machinery works.**

**Two. The Civic Atlas Office, incident and casualty book.** The Atlas Office is a survey office. It keeps a book of accidents for people who fall off scaffolds and cut themselves on the handles of their own instruments, and it has one page for the twentieth of October and one for the twenty-sixth, and there is no page for the twenty-second of December and the clerk who keeps it said the correct thing in about eleven words: *If nobody was hurt, this is not a book for it, and if you put it in here it will be a thing about a person who was not hurt and there is a box here for the name of the person hurt and I am not going to leave that box empty with a name in the next one.*

**Three. The Registry of Continuance, continuity file for the suspended order.** The order is suspended and opened for investigation into three things and the three things are on a page: the grounds of its making, the classification of the site dated the eleventh of the current year, and the removal of a sentence from a report six years old. **A tram service amendment is not within any of the three**, and a clerk of about thirty named it in about four seconds and was right and would not soften it, and the file has a line in it in the department's own hand that says *matters outside the scope of this investigation are not received.*

**Four. The gang's weekly sheet.** There is no column for thirty-one and there is no column for the thirty-three and there is no column for a car and the foreman said so in advance and has said since that he will be asked about it in about four months by a man in an office and will have to say yes.

**Five. The route custody register**, which is a Survey Enforcement record, which is the only page in the whole of Alderquay with a column for what was on a length when it came off the diagram, and which says **nil**, and whose continuation sheet says **one**, and which is the one document in the world that joins this case to the standing instruction, and which is in a drawer in a flat with a coat in it and which the man who would need it does not know is there.

**Six. The man of thirty-four at the district index counter**, who could enter it in about four minutes if she were given a rateable thing.

**Seven. Nobody.**

He wrote the seven out on the sheet and then wrote under it, in his own hand, and it is nine words: *Seven doors and a place nobody can name, and none of them is a lie.*

Then he went and stood at the north window of the route room for about a minute and a half and Dilley Farr said something to him from the other table, and Dilley Farr was thirty-one years in that room and had not crossed a thing in his life and did not want to.

"Put it in a drawer."

"There is no drawer."

"Then it is a thing you know and not a thing anybody else knows, and in about four months a man in an office is going to ask a foreman of fifty-four whether he saw thirty-one persons get off a car, and the foreman is going to have to say yes, and there is going to be nothing in this building to sit next to that yes." Dilley Farr shut a drawer. "**I have signed no deletion in this room for about three months and the reason is still the same and it is still not principle.** Write it down. Not in here."

---

**And the thing that made the day, at about ten past ten at night, in a front room, in a book.**

*First, 22.10. Thursday the first of January. The offices were open and there was a queue and I have got a piece of paper with seven doors on it and I have been in this trade eleven years and every one of the seven was correct.*

*The clerk at the index has been there eleven years and told me the difference between a record of what happened and a record of what is where, in the corridor, at about a quarter to ten, unasked, and she wanted to, and I have written it down in her words because I would have got it wrong. **The book is not a record of what happened. It is a record of what is where.** And every document I went to today is that book. The running sheet is where a car ought to be. The index is where a place is. The continuation file is what a department agreed to look at. The custody register is what somebody is responsible for. The gang's sheet is what eleven men did with a shovel.*

*And the thing happened and it is going to stay a thing that happened.*

*So here is the whole of the first of January, and I am going to write it in nine lines and I am not going to improve on it in a fortnight.*

*There is no place in this city that the twenty-second of December can be entered on, because the only thing that can hold it is a length, and a length is not a place, and a length has no street and cannot be given one. The seven doors are all the doors there are. **A thing happened on the twenty-second of December and this city has no instrument for showing anybody that it happened, and in about four months a department is going to say that no passage occurred, and we will not be able to prove it, and there is no appeal from that and no hearing would help, and I have now been to four counters in one building and one in another and I have the paper to prove that I went.***

*That is the first of January. It is a weekday. I did not cross and I am not going to, and the tally has nine lines on it and the number is nine, and I have stood in a corridor holding a piece of paper with seven doors on it and I have not been asked to be the author of anything today and that is the best I have had since the twenty-second.*

*Ten routes. Ten since the ninth of December and the eighth of the nine is a hundred and one steps and the tenth is a walk, and the page home is the thirty-ninth day of it and is blank with three marks on it in three hands, and I have written the imperative half of that sentence at the front of this page because a rule is what a man writes when he wants one, and I have read it back, and it does not get better, and the line is still blank and it is going to be blank in March.*

*The book has not written since the twentieth of December. I looked at it at about four this afternoon and about nine this evening. The twentieth of December to the first of January is twelve days and the twentieth of December to the fourteenth of January is twenty-five, and if it writes in between then that is a pair, and twenty-five would be the largest interval in this book, and I am going to write it down when it happens and I am not going to write it down before it happens, because the last time I wrote the two ends of a silence down before the thing in the middle of it had happened, the thing in the middle of it did not happen, and I have never told anybody that and I am not going to now.*

*The token was cold this morning and it is cold now and I have not taken it out since the seventh of December, and that is twenty-five days, and I am aware of what the number is and I am not going to be a man who counts his own coat.*
