# Chapter 104: Thirty-Four Steps

On Wednesday the fifteenth of April, at about ten past eight in the morning, a courier's round of seven stops in eleven streets went out, and at about seven in the evening it came in, and both of those were four hours longer than they needed to be, and neither of them was paid, and nobody had decided to do either of those things and everybody in eleven streets knows it was decided.

A woman of forty-five called the lot prices from a doorway at about ten past five, with no fish behind her, for a minute and a half, and then put her thumb in a crack in a wall at the south end for about half a minute.

A man of about forty swept a hundred and ten yards of frontage that is not a street at about ten past six, badly, and has swept it badly since he was twenty-one.

There was no coffee on the Row, and there has never been coffee on the Row on a Wednesday, and at about half past five that evening a man of about sixty was at a shed at Cardew Docks where a stall opens at six and it is open on a Wednesday and that is the day it is busy.

**And about nine people stood in the weather in eleven streets between about five and about seven in the evening on Wednesday the fifteenth of April, for no reason at all, out of a guess, and they were the only people in this city who did that.**

Milo Rook was not one of the nine and was not asked and did not go, and he has written down that he did not go, and the reason he gave in his own book is nine words and it is the reason he is not going to improve:

*I was the only one who would have had a reason.*

"If you had stood in it," said Rue, on the Thursday, in a yard, "then on the Wednesday after that you would have had a reason, and on the Wednesday after that you would have had a habit, and in about the middle of May you would have had a column. **You have been careful not to write anything down for six months and you nearly ruined it in one evening by being right about something.**"

"I did not nearly ruin it."

"You have told about nine people in a yard that the district is going to stand in the weather on the fifteenth and that it is going to be the only thing in this city that happens that day, and if you had been *in* it, then somebody in about four years would ask you which day you did it on, and you would have to say a day, and then there is a day in April that a surveyor stood in, and that is the whole of how this district got its street name taken off it six years ago and you know it and I know it and I am not going to be the one to say it to you twice."

He did not say it to her twice. He has written down that she did not say it to him twice, and that it was the correct decision, and that she made it in about four seconds without appearing to think about it, and that he has been in this district six months and has never once watched her think about anything.

**And on the second side of the sheet of who does what and on which day, the week of Monday the thirteenth of April has a blank on the fifteenth, and a blank on that side is a day when nobody stood in the weather, and the sheet is right, and it has been right about Wednesdays since the second of February, and it is the last day of a season in this city that had no calendar and it does not know and it is not going to be able to.**

---

**And on Thursday the sixteenth of April a man of thirty-one went down thirty-four steps into a room beneath Crown Heights that he has been into four times since the seventeenth of December, and there are nine desks on the left of it and a plan chest and a window that has not opened since about 1931 with a chair under it, and he has never sat in the chair, and the first time he came down those steps he told nine people a true sentence out loud and lost an afternoon, and this is the fourth time and he has still not asked that room for a thing.**

Bettina Sallow is fifty-four and has been a reader on a hand index at the ninth desk on the left for four years, which is how long he has known that she was in the room at all, and before that she was on a different index in a different building for about nineteen years, and she has never once said so in this room, and he has it from the back of a form and has not used it.

"You have been down here four times since December," she said. "You have not once asked me for a document."

"I have asked you for three documents."

"You have asked me three times whether a document exists. That is not the same and you know it is not, because the second time you did not write the answer down and the third time you did not ask, and I noticed both and I have not said anything about either and I am not going to now."

---

**And here is the ordinary thing she does, four times a day, and does better than anybody in this city does it, and has never been asked about and is not going to be thanked for.**

"Ask me for something," she said, "and I will go and look. And if it is not in here I will tell you it is not in here, and I will tell you in a specific way: I will tell you what I have looked at, and how far I went, and what I did not open, and how long it took. And if I have not read the whole card I will not tell you it is not there, and I will tell you I have not read the whole card."

"That is a slow way to say no."

"That is the only honest way to say no and about nine people in this city have never worked that out." She moved a tray about four inches without looking at it. "**A card in an index is a claim that a document exists. It is not the document and it is not the absence of the document. And a person who comes down thirty-four steps and says *is it in the index* and gets *no* has been told something about a filing system and not about a city.** And I have watched that go wrong twice in four years and both times it was a man in a hurry, and one of them is why I still have this desk."

"Then what is the card for."

"**Every card in this room is a place.**" She said it the way she says everything, which is as though she has said it before in a corridor and this is not the first time. "A hand index is built out of one card for one named thing, and the named things in it are rateable things with a street on them, because that is what the printing order of this building has said since about 1830, and it has never been printed from a list of anything else. **I have been at the ninth desk on the left for four years and there is no card in it for a drain, a cover, a length, a row of shops, a fence, a woman, a market, or a hole. There is a card for a rateable thing with a street. Everything in this room is built out of that one sentence, and the building is two hundred years old and the street list under the counter is a hundred and forty.**"

"And the district we are in."

"There is a card for the district and the card has a street on it and the district has got about nine streets and about half of them have got people in them who are on the card and the other half are not, and **the card does not know that and could not hold it if it did, because a card is a line and a line is a rateable thing with a street on it, and the twenty-six names in the file that everybody keeps talking about are not on any card in this room and could not be.**" She put a finger on the middle drawer. "I am not being unkind. I am being a reader. **The index is not a record of what happened. It is a record of what is where, and the where is the whole of it, and everything that is not a where is not in this building and not in any building.**"

Milo Rook wrote that down and then did not write anything else for about a minute, and she let him.

"You have just said the same sentence that the man at the index desk said to me in December," he said, "in about nine words, and the word *claim* is in both of them, and he said it standing up in this room, to nine people, on the seventeenth of December, and she was one of the nine, and neither of them has said one word to the other one about it, and when she wrote on a finding aid and turned it round to him he read it and said nothing, and I was in the room."

"Then it is not my sentence."

"It is not anybody's," he said. "**It is the sentence the paperwork says, out loud, in one room, to two people who are not talking to each other, and I am the only man who has heard both halves and I have not joined them and I am not going to, because joining them is me deciding they are the same, and a man who decides two things are the same in a book is a man who has started making the case instead of the record.**"

"You said the whole of the seventh column in a corridor on a Tuesday and then stopped."

"Eleven seconds, and you were not in the corridor."

---

**And at about twenty past eleven on the morning of Thursday the sixteenth, standing at the plan chest with her back to him, Bettina Sallow asked him the only question anybody in that room had asked in four years, and it was not a good one, and she answered it herself in about four seconds.**

"There is a shape in the dust under that chest."

"There is."

"**Nobody sweeps this room.**" She said it without turning round. "Not since the index came down off the third floor in nineteen seventy-one, and not once in the fifty-four years before that that anybody in this building has ever told me about. There is a plan chest on a floor and the floor has been swept round the chest twice since I came and not once under it, and there is dust in there in a shape, and it is not my room and there is no card in this index for a room or a floor or a woman. I have had a broom leaning in that corner since the twelfth of March and I have not used it, because **the day I sweep under that chest I am the person who sweeps this room, and then in about four years somebody can ask me what I did in here, and I will have to say why, and there is no why in it.**"

"I have got a man in this district who sweeps a hundred and ten yards of frontage that is not a street at about ten past six every morning, and he has done it since he was twenty-one, and I am not going to tell you that, and I am not going to put it anywhere near this room."

"**Good,**" said Bettina Sallow, "and I am not going to ask, and you have just made the only correct decision anybody has made about me in four years and it cost you nothing and you did not do it in a yard, and I have noticed the difference and I am not going to say anything about it.**"

---

**And there is a rule he made himself in a yard on the fifteenth of March and has now written down four times, and he wrote it out again that afternoon in a front room without being asked by anybody, and it is here because it is the only instrument in six months that has not gone wrong.**

*I have got a man of about forty in this district who sweeps a hundred and ten yards of frontage at ten past six every morning and has swept it since he was twenty-one, and I have got a woman of fifty-four in a room four floors down who has had a broom in a corner since the twelfth of March and has not used it, and I am not going to put those two things next to each other in a book. **That is the rule I made in a yard in March and I said it out loud in that room once this morning and this is the fifth time I have put it on paper and I am not going to do it a sixth.** And I did not tell her, and she did not ask, and neither of us has been thanked, and there is no card in a hand index anywhere in this city for either of them.*

---

**And on the Thursday the ninth of April, five days before the hearing and six days before that round went out, a form came back down to the quay section office for the second time, and it took a fortnight to come back and it came back with a sentence where it has always had a dash, and the sentence was about the box and not about the line, and nobody has asked anybody to fill it in and nobody is going to.**

> **FORM 4 — QUERY ON ANNUAL RETURN.** *Return received. **Item 4 not completed. Section to complete.***

"Item four," said Cyril Sale, at about a quarter past eleven, holding it at arm's length. "Not *line six*. Not *what did you mean*. **Item four. The box.** The box that wants to know whether the section stands behind the entry. **And it has taken a fortnight and four minutes, and the second time round it has stopped asking about the sentence and started asking about the box, and a man with a pencil in a corridor has worked out that a box with a line through it is not a box with an answer in it, and he is right, and there is no way on the earth to give that department an answer that is not a lie.**"

"So it goes up empty again."

"It goes up empty again, with the line through it and the date, and there is a second line through it with a second date on it, and in about four years somebody in another building is going to read a return with two ruled lines on it and a signature and **they are not going to be able to tell whether the clerk refused twice or whether the section ran out of answers.**" He put it on the desk. "And that is the thing I have got and I am not going to improve it: **the answer to that box is yes, and the answer that keeps eleven men and a foreman out of a room in April is no, and the department has now asked twice and has not been told which, and a machine is a much worse thing than a man about being difficult, because a man stops and a machine does not.**"

"Have they ever asked about line five."

"**Nobody has ever asked about line five.**" He said it without any particular weight, which is how he has said it every time. "Seven weeks. A nil on a box headed *persons observed on the length during the year*, and it has now survived being read by a man with a pencil, a query about the line above it, a second query about the box, and a report of a defect that could not be made because there is no box for a length. **And I could have been that pencil three times and I have said so twice and I am not going to say it a third, and I have got four returns in January of next year and the fifth box gets filled nil and it will get filled nil every January of my life, and the only true answer to it is nil, and I have said that out loud in a yard and it is still true and I am not going to be thanked for it.**"

"And the foreman."

"Has not been told that the line was his and has not been told that a second query has come down, and he will be told about the box in about four minutes because the box is a box and the box is his." Cyril Sale squared the form. "And he will say something about eleven men and I will not answer it and I will do the job, and that is the fourth Tuesday in six weeks and it is going to be about four hundred Tuesdays."

**The Field Book, Thursday the sixteenth of April, 22.40, a front room, and it is two days off the fourteenth, and I am writing one entry on a Tuesday and one on a Thursday this week and there is a Wednesday in between that I have written down nothing about and that is the point of this entry.**

*Sixteenth, 22.40. Two days.*

*One. **I did not go out on the Wednesday, and I have written down that I did not go, and the reason is that I was the only one of the nine who would have had a reason.** About nine people in eleven streets stood in the weather for nothing on Wednesday the fifteenth of April, and it was the day after a hearing in which nothing was determined and the day after a season ended with no calendar and no second outlook and nothing whatever printed for that day, and they were the only people in this city who did that, and nobody has written it down, because the second side of the map of this district has a blank on the fifteenth and a blank is a day when nobody stood in the weather and the sheet is right and it is not going to be able to be otherwise. **That is the second time in four months that this district's own instrument has been right, in its own terms, on a day it could not see, and I have got a man of about forty who put twenty-eight words under a rule in March and I have not asked him whether the fifteenth needs anything and I am not going to, because the answer is no and the asking is the damage.**

*Two. A reader of fifty-four at the ninth desk on the left in a room four floors down, and she told me in about eleven seconds that every card in that room is a place, and that there is no card in it for a drain, a cover, a length, a row of shops, a fence, a woman, a market or a hole, and that the building is two hundred years old and the street list is a hundred and forty, and that the index is not a record of what happened, it is a record of what is where. **And the man at the index desk of that room said the same sentence to me in December in other words, standing up, to nine people, and the word claim is in both of them, and she was one of the nine, and neither of them has said one word to the other one, and I have heard both halves and I have not joined them and I am not going to.** And she will not tell me a document does not exist unless she has read the whole card, and she has not been thanked, and I have not thanked her, and I have been into that room four times since December and I have never once asked her for a document.*

*Three. And I did not tell her. **She wrote on a finding aid on the eighteenth of December and turned it round to him and he read it and said nothing, and I have been in that room four times since and I have not told her that the man said the same thing about a claim in front of her, and she has not asked, and that was the only correct thing either of us did this morning.** A woman of fifty-four at the ninth desk on the left and a man of sixty-three at the index desk, in one room, for four years, with two hundred and forty years of field books in eleven presses behind the pair of them, and not one of them has ever said to the other one what that room is for, and I could put it in a book in about eleven seconds and it would be true, and it would be a document, and in about four years there would be two documents and the second one would be mine.*

*Four. And a broom. She has had one in a corner since the twelfth of March and has not used it, because the day she sweeps under the plan chest she is the person who sweeps that room, and then somebody in about four years can ask her what she did in there and she will have to say why, and there is no why in it. **It is the only room in this city in six months where I have been on my own with a book and nothing has come out of it, and I have not decided what that is and I am not going to put it in a column, and there is no card in a hand index anywhere in this city for a room, or a floor, or a woman.** And I am not going to put that next to a man of forty and a row either, and the rule I made in a yard in March says so, and I have not written the rule down again.*

*Five. And a form. It came back down on the ninth for the second time and it has stopped asking about a sentence and started asking about a box, and the box is a line through with a date on it and there is no way to answer it that is not a lie, and a man of forty-six has now got two ruled lines and a signature on a return in another body's building, **and in about four years a person will read that and will not be able to tell whether the clerk refused twice or whether the section ran out of answers, and that is the finest thing I have heard anybody say in six months and he does not know it is fine and I am not going to tell him.** And nobody has ever been asked about line five. Seven weeks.*

*Six. And the counter. **I did not tell her about the season on the fourteenth, and the hearing sat, and nobody asked what season meant, and I have written that down for the fourth time, and it is not a rule any more and I have stopped giving myself until the fourteenth as a place to stop.** A thing I do quietly is a thing that is going to get done, and a woman of about fifty-four said that to me in a yard in this city in the middle of the winter and I have written it out four times and improved it none of the four times.*

*The page home the hundred and forty-fourth day, blank, and it is there. On the eighth and the twelfth and the fourteenth and the fifteenth and today, and I have written five of those down in advance before the day came round, and I have not asked anybody to fill it in, and nobody has. Ten routes, ten. The token cold, a hundred and thirtieth day, and on Tuesday I stood in a room for about two minutes and it was in the coat on my own front door the whole time and I have not written down the other thing and I am not going to.*
