# Chapter 103: Word For Word, Or Not At All

Lena Marr went up one flight of stairs on Wednesday the eighth of April at about four in the afternoon, on her own, and she has said since that she went because the hearing was on the Tuesday and she had four days and she was not going to spend them on a form.

She came back down at about half past four and she did not tell anybody what had happened for two days, and when she told Milo Rook she told him in a doorway at the back of a shop and it took about eleven seconds and she did not improve it, and he has written that down and has not improved it either.

"She asked me the three questions again," said Lena Marr. "Exactly the same three, in the same order, and she has asked me the same three every time for four months now and I have got so quick at them that I had the first one out of my mouth before she was off the landing.

"And I could not do it word for word."

"You could not."

"**The condition I set was word for word or not at all. I did that so that I could not put my own words on a woman of sixty's mouth. And I got there on the eighth of April and I could not do it word for word, and so I did not do it, and I came down the stairs and stood in a doorway about four minutes and then I came out here.**"

"Do you know what changed."

"I know that I could say those twenty-three words in March because I had heard them once, in a yard, and they were in my head whole, and two months later there is one more thing in my head and it is in the way, and I am not going to stand in a kitchen and try to remember where she put a word. **She has got about ten words of that conversation and I have got about ninety and that is how it is going to stay, and neither of us can put the other one's share back together, and I have not told her that and I am not going to.**"

Somebody said, "You could write them down."

"**No,**" said Lena Marr, and it was the only time in about four minutes that she raised her voice.

---

**And at about half past one on the afternoon of Tuesday the fourteenth of April, in a room in Crown Heights, with about thirty people in it and a reporting officer's book open on a table, Lena Marr paid for something in about four seconds, and it is the fourth of them and it is the only one that has been paid in a room.**

Ivo Sarratt had read out the line and said that it stands while the answer of the fourth of December stood, and then he had said, correctly, that the condition was not his, and then he had sat down, which about four people in that room noticed he had done in a way that was not a formality.

"Advocate Quill, I have to ask the party one question and I would rather ask it now than at the end."

"Go on."

"The answer of the fourth of December is the district's, and the line stands while it does, and the line is the department's protection and I am the department, and so I am not going to decide whether the district wants to keep it. **I am going to ask the party, and I am going to ask it plainly, because there is no column on this proceeding in which to ask it nicely.**"

Mara Quill did not answer.

"Ms Marr."

"I am the named rateable head of a temporary accommodation household for two hundred and fifty-two people in a building that has no number," said Lena Marr. "I have been that since the fifteenth of December past and I pay for it out of my own name at two and four a head a week, weekly, in advance, with no term, and it is not the district rate and it does not come out of the district. **The thirteenth of October is what keeps me whole.**"

"And the answer of the fourth of December, Ms Marr. Does the party stand it."

"Yes."

"Would you say that again, please, into the record, for my own sake rather than yours."

"**Yes,**" said Lena Marr. "The district stands the answer of the fourth of December. There will be no schedule of persons from this district in this department in any proceeding while this line stands, and the line stands while we do, and we are not letting it go this morning."

"May I ask why, since the department's interest is that it holds."

"You may ask and I will answer, and it is not a department's interest, Mr Sarratt, it is a woman's." She did not look at anybody. "**If we let that line go this morning then two hundred and fifty-two people go into one schedule that somebody fills in in about four minutes, and a man with a pencil and nine returns writes down whatever the pencil stops on, and the whole of this winter's work is that we have decided we are not going to be a document. And if we keep it, then there is one woman of sixty on a second landing in a building with no number who is the ninth of twenty-six names in a file in Crown Heights, and she cannot see it and she cannot come off it and she cannot ask anybody whether her name is spelled right, and she asked me on the eighth of April and I could not answer her, and I have not been able to answer her since the fourth of December and I am not going to be able to answer her this afternoon either.**"

Nobody said anything.

"So you have chosen the two hundred and fifty-two."

"**I have chosen two hundred and fifty-two because the alternative is zero.**" Lena Marr put her hands flat on the table. "And I drafted the ask, and I want that in the record, and I have said it in a yard in February and I am saying it in a room now because a yard is not a room. I drafted the prohibition that is stopping her. It is forbidden by us. We asked for it. I wrote the paragraph that keeps her from her own name, and I would like that minuted, and I know exactly what the minute is going to do with it."

"Which is what."

"**Which is that it is going to print that I asked. And that is all it is going to do, and I am asking anyway, because there is no other instrument in this city and I have had a plastic wallet for two years and I have got very good at putting things in rooms and I have never once been in a room that wrote a thing down for me.**"

---

**And Milo Rook was in that room and said nothing, and has written down that he said nothing, and the reason he gave afterwards, in about nine words, to a woman of sixty-one at a counter three days later, is the whole of what he thinks is left of him.**

"She said yes in about four seconds and it cost her the only thing she has got and she said yes to it in a room with thirty people in it, and if I had stood up at that point and said anything at all I would have been the man who made a minute out of it, and there is a woman of sixty on a second landing who is the reason that line exists and she is sixty and she is not in this room and she was not asked, and there is a woman of sixty-eight in this district who is on nothing at all and she was not asked either, and nobody went up three flights of stairs about either of them today or on any day this month, and that is not a kindness, it is the same refusal as the rest of it."

"**Then say the number,**" said Hettie Brann, "and go and sit down, and do not tell me about it again for about a fortnight."

---

**And a clerk of a list wrote the minute, and the minute is correct, and about four people have read it since, and this is the whole of it, and none of it is a victory and none of it is a defeat and it is a page.**

> *CO/6/BELL. Fourteenth day of April. **Continuation of an adjourned matter**, adjourned from the twelfth of January past. Present: reporting officer, Sarratt, I., Survey Enforcement. Second-in-command: Ardery, C. For the district: Advocate Quill, M.; Marr, L., named rateable head, temporary accommodation, two hundred and fifty-two persons.*
> *1. Grounds of the making of the order: not determined; not in dispute as to validity; not determined on the papers.*
> *2. Classification of the site dated the eleventh of the current year: **withdrawn; not to be relied on by any party; the withdrawal stands.***
> *3. Removal of the sentence, no evidence was found of any hazard to persons at the service court: **not determined. The department cannot account for the removal and has not been able to. The sentence cannot be restored by this department.** The reference is not a district reference and is not held by this department.*
> *4. The line of the twelfth of January stands. **The reporting officer has recorded that the line is conditional and that the condition is not the department's.** The party was asked whether the district stands the answer of the fourth of December and answered that it does, and asked the department to minute that the party drafted the ask, and the department has minuted it.*
> *5. **The matter stands over. It is not adjourned. The department has no date to give and has not the standing to give one, and records that it is not adjourning the matter and that the matter remains before it undetermined and without a date.***
> *6. The order CO/6/BELL **is suspended and is not discharged, is not varied, is not withdrawn and is not restored to the public name index.** The public name is not restored by this minute and was not argued.*

Six paragraphs, and it is about four hundred words, and everything in it is correct, and **the whole of what twenty-six people and a woman of thirty-one and a foreman of fifty-four and a works clerk of forty-six and a man of about fifty-one with a machine in it have between them over four months is in paragraph four and it is one sentence long and it is a question and an answer.**

---

**And what came out of the room, and it is the only thing that came out of it, and it is not a document and cannot be made into one, is that a woman of about thirty-four said eleven words to a reporting officer at about ten past four in a corridor and a foreman who had asked for a date got nine words back, and that about nine people in eleven streets stood in the weather on the Wednesday for no reason at all, out of a guess, and were the only people in this city who did that, and nobody wrote it down because the second side of the map of this district cannot see a Wednesday and there is no column on it for a man who is somewhere else.**

---

**The Field Book has one entry for Tuesday the fourteenth of April and it is at 23.05 and it is four paragraphs, and I am not writing a second one tonight, and the reason is in the entry and it is this: the second half of the fourteenth is in this book now as four seconds and a paragraph and a woman who could not say twenty-three words word for word, and I have read all three of them back twice and I could not improve any of them and I am not going to improve them, and a man of about thirty-one in a book in a coat is the worst instrument in this city and the only one available at eleven o'clock at night in a front room and I have not got a better one and I have stopped looking for one.**

*And I have written down one thing tonight that I have not written down in six months and I am putting it at the top and not the bottom, because a thing you put at the bottom of a page is a thing you have finished with.*

*It is not a rule. He said it in a room and I said it in a book and between the two of them I have now written it down three times since the thirty-first of March and the third time is the one that counts, because on the thirty-first of March it was a warning and on the fourteenth of April it is a fact: **I did not tell her about the season, the hearing sat, nobody asked what season meant, and I am going to carry it forward again today and the day after, and the day after that, and there is going to be a day when carrying it forward stops being a procedure and starts being a habit, and a habit is a thing you can be asked about, and on the fifteenth of March a man of about forty with a broom taught this district that a habit is not a calendar and I have written that down myself on a wall at ten to six in the evening and I did not learn it.***
