# Chapter 117: One Man Short

On Monday the fourth of May at about half past three in the afternoon a man of seventeen stood at a gate in a high wall with a satchel and a handcart and was told, by a man of thirty-one, that there was a letter for a tram authority's district office in Crown Heights, and he said no, and he was right, and it took him about nine seconds.

Pell Harrow's round is seven stops in eleven streets and it goes out at about ten past eight and in at about four, and since the second of January it has also gone through the quay section gate on Mondays and Thursdays, and it does that because the district has no address and this is the only gate on a working day that a cart can get out of.

"I will not take it."

"It is a letter. It is a four-page letter, it is not heavy, and you go through there twice a day."

"I know how many times a day I go through there." He put the handcart down. "**I am not doing it because the moment a courier is on a thing, the courier is the record. That is the whole of it and it takes nine seconds and I have been a courier for two years.** If a man of seventeen goes into an office with a letter about the sixteenth of December, then in about four years there is a piece of paper that says a boy carried it, and the boy is on the piece of paper, and the piece of paper is about a morning, and the boy is seventeen now and he will be forty in that year, and the boy cannot get out from under a piece of paper about a morning by being forty."

"You could say you were asked to."

"**I would say it, and then it would be true, and it would be the first true thing anybody has said about that morning since the Friday in December and it would be about me.**"

Nobody wrote it down.

---

**And here is what the letter actually did on the Monday, and it did it in about eleven seconds at a gate and nobody in this district is going to enjoy this.**

"It has to be carried by somebody or it does not go," said Milo Rook.

"Then it does not go." Pell Harrow picked the handcart up. "**And I will tell you why that is right and I will tell you once and I have told nobody else and you can write it down if you want: there is a rule about a timetable division and it says ask nobody, and do not come here, and do not send the man from the round, and if you do any of those three things then a clerk will say that the timetable division does not discuss working timetables outside the department, and it will be true, and it will be in a book, and it will keep. And the third of those three is about me. It is not about you and it is not about a man of forty-one on no list at all. Somebody wrote that rule for a reason and the reason is a boy with a satchel, and I have been the boy with a satchel for two years, and I am not going to be the reason a rule holds in a different direction in the year I am nineteen.**

"Nobody asked you to say that."

"Nobody asked me to say anything and I have not said anything and I am going up the Row now, and the round is one man short, and it has been one man short since the twenty-eighth of December, and you can put that on your form or you can not."

---

**And the round is one man short, and there are two reasons for it, and they cannot be joined, and Milo Rook has written them out separately in his own book and has not joined them and is not going to.**

*One.* The round is one man short because a boy of seventeen went up the south for twenty-two days on his own money at the end of December and came back on the nineteenth of January and nobody asked him where he had been and he said nothing and nobody has asked him since and nobody is going to. **That is a refusal in this district and it is his and it was made by a man of forty-one on no list at all in about four seconds in the middle of January and it has held for four months, and the holding of it is the standing procedure of this place, and a thing that is held by nine people and not written down is not kept by nobody, whatever a woman of forty-five says in a doorway, and I am not going to put those two sentences next to each other in this book because I have made that rule in a yard in March and I have now written it four times.**

*Two.* **The round is one man short because the district cannot have a second man on it.** A round is carried by a postman and a postman is on a form, and the form wants a depot, and a depot is an address, and a district with no rateable unit and no address cannot have a depot, and has not had one since the eleventh of October six years ago when a street name came off an index. And so there is a boy of seventeen doing seven stops in eleven streets on a Monday and a Thursday, and on a Monday and a Thursday he takes a handcart through a gate in a high wall, and there is nobody to send with him, and there has not been since January, and he is not going to be asked why there is nobody. **And that is not an injustice, and it is not a scandal, and nobody has done anything wrong, and it is a design. A district that cannot be a rateable thing cannot employ a courier, and the district cannot be a rateable thing because of a piece of paper in an index in another building, and the order is suspended and is not discharged and is not varied and is not withdrawn and the public name is not on the street name index, and every one of those words is a reason a boy of seventeen is doing two rounds on a cart.** And it is going to be one man short in June and in July and in August and it will be one man short in the fourth year, and it cannot be cured from inside the district, and nobody in this district is going to be able to say that on the twenty-first of May to a tram authority's office, and the letter is asking a question about a car and the answer to the letter is a boy on a handcart and there is no way to write that on a return.

"And nobody in this city has ever asked a district that question in those words," said Cyril Sale, from a doorway, at about ten past four. "**That is the thing. It is not that the department is cruel to a district. It is that this is the first time a form has asked a district a question that a person has to be in to answer, and about eleven people in this district are in to answer every day and not one of them can be printed, and the form has got a box and the box is four lines deep and it is going to sit there open until the quarter is closed and nobody is going to be angry about it and nobody is going to be blamed for it, and in about four years the item will be a line in a book in another building and the line will be the whole of what this district is on the record as having done in December.**"

---

**And at about ten past five, at the gate, a man of forty-one with a wet cap looked at a job that was as nearly his as any job has ever been in this district, and decided in about four seconds that it was not his, and said so out loud, to nobody, in nine words, and it was the fifth time.**

"A card under a brick is a thing and not a job." He shifted the handcart out of the way for nobody. "And about four times a day somebody in this district looks at a job and decides it is not theirs, and this week it was me, four times, and one of them was this gate, and I have not written any of them down and I am not going to and it is the only thing I am good at and I have had four months to get good at it and I have."

Nobody wrote that down either.

---

**The Field Book, Monday the fourth of May, 23.10, a front room, one day off the third, and the gang was on the length at half past seven this morning and the condition column is empty and it is the first walk in May with an empty condition column and the reason column is empty and that is correct, and the sheet goes into the rack tomorrow.**

*Fourth, 23.10. One day off the third.*

*One. **A boy of seventeen said no to me in about nine seconds at a gate and he was right, and it was the first time in seven months that somebody has refused me and been right and known it before they spoke, and I did not put the question to him in a way that could be refused, which is new, and I noticed that at about half past four and not at half past three.** He would not carry a four-page letter to a tram authority because the moment a courier is on a thing the courier is the record, and in about four years there would be a piece of paper that says a boy carried it, and he is seventeen and he will be forty in that year. And he would not carry it because a man in a timetable division with nineteen years in it said three things in a room in April, and the third of the three was *do not send the man from the round*, and I have not broken it and nobody has broken it and a rule holds by not being tested and that is the whole of it and I have never written that down before.*

*Two. And the cost, and it is not curable, and it is not being paid by anybody in this entry who is in it, and it is the round. **The round is one man short and it has been one man short since the twenty-eighth of December and there are two reasons and I am not joining them.** The first is twenty-two days up the south on his own money and nobody asked and nobody is going to and that is a refusal of mine, made by a man of forty-one in about four seconds in January, and it has held four months, and it is a thing this district is good at and I have not decided yet whether the woman who says nobody keeps the Row and the rule about not asking him are the same sentence and I have written four times that I am not going to put them next to each other and I am not going to put them next to each other in June either. The second is that a round is carried by a postman and a postman is on a form and the form wants a depot and a depot is an address, and a district with no rateable unit and no address cannot have a depot, and has not had one since a street name came off an index six years ago. **So a boy of seventeen does seven stops in eleven streets on a Monday and a Thursday and takes a handcart through a gate in a high wall, and there is nobody to send with him, and there will be nobody to send with him in June, and there will be nobody to send with him in the fourth year, and it cannot be cured from inside this district, and it is not an injustice and nobody has done anything wrong, and that is a design.** And the whole of the district's permanent win is standing in that design, and I worked that out in about four seconds at a gate at half past three this afternoon and I have had to go and sit down twice since.*

*Three. And the letter, and the open item, and the twenty-first of May. **There is no third thing.** Answer it and the answer is the answer of the fourth of December and it is a refusal; do not answer it and the item stands open on a return in another building in about nineteen days, and the return does not know what it is holding open, and neither does the district, and neither do I, and I have written in three days' entries that a man of fifty-eight with a spade says I am going to write something on about the nineteenth and he got that from nine words on a paper he has not read. **Not stated is not the same as no response and there are about four words between them and I have not got them and I am not going to have them by the nineteenth.** I have written down on Sunday that I was going to let a rule about one column stop me answering a different one, and I wrote it before the nineteenth on purpose, and I have not decided what I am going to do and I have not asked anybody, and a man of forty-six in four rooms above a fitter's shop has a rule about it that is a number and not a sentence and this is the fourth time in seven months that a rule of his has been the exact instrument for the job and I have still never asked him for the number and I am not going to.*

*Four. And the one ordinary thing in this entry, which is not mine and which nobody will hear about: **eleven men walked six hundred yards at half past seven this morning and stopped where they always stop, and eleven men walked a hundred and forty feet at eleven o'clock on Saturday and stopped at a fence, and nobody on either of those walks has ever written down a time.** A works clerk of forty-six told me in a doorway at ten past four today that the return of a car is a row and a column and a date and four squares and nothing else, and that there is no column for a person getting off at a stop, and he is right, and it is the sixth thing in seven months I have been told the same true sentence by a different mouth in a different building, and I have not joined any of the six and I am not going to join any of the six, and I want it written down in my own hand that I have started counting them, because a count is a column.*

*The page home the hundred and sixty-second day, blank, and it is there, and it is there tomorrow and on Friday, and I have decided to stop writing the sentence that says it is there, and I decided that on Saturday and it is now Monday and I have written it tonight, and I have therefore written it once more, and I have not got anybody to stop me and I am not going to ask. The token cold, a hundred and forty-eighth day, and it was in the inside pocket of this coat at a gate in a high wall at about half past three this afternoon and a boy of seventeen stood about four feet from it and did not know it was there and neither did I, and it is the sixth time I have written that clause and I am going to leave it alone now.*
