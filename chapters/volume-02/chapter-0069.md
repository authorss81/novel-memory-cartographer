# Chapter 69: The Car Comes Back

The carbon came into the depot office at about ten past five on the afternoon of Monday the twenty-second of December, and it went in the second drawer, and it stayed in the second drawer, and it is the reason a man of about thirty with a pencil could go down thirty-one lines in about four minutes and stop twice.

The *Alighted* column had thirty-one lines in it in a small neat hand, and the line at the bottom was signed **N. Bray** with a time against it, and the time is 16.36, and the car came in at twenty to five.

The yard man with the flag had written 16.41 in his book and had not looked inside the car and has never looked inside one, and his name is Fenwick and he is sixty-three and nobody asked him to, and he went home at ten past five and the book says one time and not the other.

Hester Coombe stood at the counter with the sheet flat in front of her and a clerk's pencil in her hand and went down it. Alder Quay interchange, twelve. The water stairs, nine. Crown Heights, ten. She said the last one twice because a man of about forty stood at the end of the counter and could not stop moving his hands, and she said it again, and put a stroke through it, and the second stroke went through the first one and there are two strokes on the twenty-second line and both of them are Hester Coombe's hand.

"That's the whole of it," she said. "Thirty-one. I've been the clerk in this office twenty-two years and I have never ticked a name off a sheet in my life because a name is not a thing you tick, it is a thing you read out. And I have just done it, and I would do it again tomorrow, and I am telling you that I did it and that I would rather somebody saw me do it than found out in a month that I had."

---

**And in a shed at about ten past six on the Monday evening, with about forty people in it and rain on the roof, a woman of thirty-eight who had been a conductor for sixteen years gave account, and she gave it standing up, and she asked for three things first and got all three.**

She asked for the yard to be shut. She asked for the carbon to stay in the second drawer. And she asked that nobody read the thirty-one names out in a yard in the rain, and Rue said yes to the third one in about two seconds, and Nessa Bray looked at her and said, "Thank you," and Rue said, "Do not thank me, I am not doing it for you."

"Say the three things you asked for," said Lena Marr, from the back.

"Shut shed. Carbon in the drawer. No reading out." Then she waited about four seconds. "Right. I am going to say the other thing first because it is the only thing I have that is worth anything and I want it said while nobody is looking at a piece of paper.

"**I have not been lost and I have not been frightened, and I have been on that car for five days and two hours and I have said the next one out loud every single time for five days and two hours, and I am the reason that car is a car.**"

Somebody made a noise.

"I know how that sounds," said Nessa Bray. "It sounds like a woman who has been somewhere else. I have not. I have been on a tram going round a sequence that I could not get out of, and I could not get out of it because **I could not stop talking.** That is the whole of it. That is the answer and it took me two days to work out and about a day and a half to stop being frightened of it.

"A tram is the list of what comes next and the list is on a piece of paper and the piece of paper is no use at all. What I do is the only thing that does it. Twenty-nine years, sixteen years on this run — I say the next one out loud about four hundred times a day and it is not in a book and nobody has ever asked me what it is for and it is the only reason those thirty-one people are thirty-one people and not thirty-one passengers. **If I stop talking it is not a car any more. It is a room with wheels.**"

"What happened on the first day," said Milo Rook.

"Nothing happened on the first day, that is what I want said. Nothing happened and that is what I could not get through my head. There was no event. There was no door, no wall, no dark, no noise except mine. **There was a corner.** There is a corner on that length where the loop comes round and the car goes past a signal and goes round the loop and comes back, and on Tuesday morning at about a quarter past eight we went round that corner and went round it again, and again, and I said the next one out loud, and at about ten I stopped being able to tell you which lap we were on, and at about eleven a man of about forty sat down and did not get up for eleven hours, and I asked him at about four to move so that two people could get past, and he said no, and I said the word anyway, and he moved, and he has not said anything to me since and I have not asked him anything.

"On the second day they started asking me where we were going. I told them we were going round. On the third day a woman of about seventy asked me if her sister was on the car and I said I did not know, and I do not know, and I have thought about that every day since. On the fourth day a boy of about eleven asked me whether it was Tuesday and I said yes, and he said *it was Tuesday on Friday too*, and that was the worst four seconds of the five days, and he was right, and I did not have an answer and I have not got one now.

"And then this morning at about ten past twelve somebody said the word on that length and it was not me."

Nobody said anything.

"It was a man's voice and it came from the north end and it was not loud and there were about eleven men on that length and I could not see a man of yours anywhere on it and there was nobody on it who was not in a jacket with a shovel. **I have been on a car for five days and I have not been frightened of anything until that**, and I would like somebody to explain to me where a voice came from, because I am the conductor of that car and I do not know where a voice came from, and I have been doing this for sixteen years and I have never once not known where the next one was coming from."

"A man from the survey office went in at eleven oh six this morning and came out at eleven forty-one," said Milo Rook.

"Then somebody in your office has been lying to me for five days," said Nessa Bray, "or somebody has, and I have not got time this week to work out which, and I am going to say the last thing and then I am going to go and sleep in my own bed for the first time since Tuesday, and the last thing is this.

"I filled that column in on the length this morning, one at a time, as they got off. Thirty-one people off a car and me behind them. **I have been on a tram since I was twenty-two and that is the first time in sixteen years that I have written down where thirty-one people got off, and I did it because it was the only thing on that sheet that had not been done yet, and because a woman in a yard told me on Thursday that there is a piece of paper in this city that knows those thirty-one people were alive on Monday, and she was right, and it was that sheet, and I am not having anybody read it out in the rain.**"

She got the second one wrong. The sheet was in the depot office, not in a yard, and she had not been in the yard on Thursday. She did not know that and nobody in the shed corrected her, because it was not the sentence that mattered, and the sentence that mattered was the one about the column, and there is a difference between a thing being wrong and a thing being said by a person who has been awake for five days and two hours, and Milo Rook wrote both of those down afterwards and only one of them is about the case.

---

**And at about half past eight on the evening of the twenty-second, on the quay, a foreman of fifty-four handed over a sheet of accountancy paper, and it is the record of the ninth crossing, and it is a record of work.**

It is one side. It is carbon-backed. It has six columns and the six columns have been the same for eleven years and one hundred and forty years before that.

> *Date. Length. Men. Hours. Work done. Condition. Signature.*

Monday the twenty-second. Back length. Eleven. **07.30, fifty minutes; and 11.06, thirty-five minutes.** Work done: *walked, both turns, boundary at wall checked.* Condition: *serviceable.* Signature: **R. PRYCE.**

And in the hours column, in the second entry, in a foreman's hand, the reason, which is nine words and which he wrote before he did anything else:

> *A car is on the length. The gang is the crew on the length.*

It is in a rack on the Friday. The duplicate goes in a tin at the end of the month. That is the whole of the record of the ninth crossing in the city of Alderquay.

**It has no column for the thirty-one.** It has a column for a length, and a column for eleven men, and a column for hours, and a column for work done, and a column for the condition of a drain, and a signature. There is nowhere on the form for *a woman of thirty-eight said the next one out loud for five days and two hours* and there is nowhere on it for *thirty-one people got off a length at the north end at about ten past twelve in the morning of Monday the twenty-second of December in daylight* and there is nowhere on it for a dog.

It is a record of work and it is a true record of work and Roy Pryce wrote it correctly and did the right thing and has said he will not write a word about a person on it and was right, and he said on Friday morning that he would be asked about it in about four months by a fella in an office and he would have to say yes, and that he is prepared for that, and that he has never been asked to be that before and does not intend to refuse.

---

**And on the morning of Tuesday the twenty-third, at about ten past nine, at the quay section office, in four rooms above a fitter's shop, a works clerk of forty-six told Milo Rook in about nine words that the city cannot record what happened on Monday, and then he said it again in more words, and the second time was for the book.**

"There is no form."

"There has to be a form. You have got a form for a car coming in."

"There is a form for a car coming in and it is called a running sheet and it has got a box for a car number and a box for a time in and a box for a time out and a box for a fault, and the fault box is where you would put it, and you would put *ran through a length not on the diagram*, and the sheet would go in a book, and the book would say on the twenty-second of December that car 214 was held for thirty-five minutes on a reserved length by the quay section gang.

"And then in about three weeks somebody's grandchild will pull that page out and say *grandfather, what was this?*, and the fault box will say *ran through a length not on the diagram*, and **not on the diagram is not a place**, and a length that is not a place is not something a car can be held on, and I have known that since the tenth of October when I sent a schedule down for index and the whole of your trouble came out of it." He shut the drawer. "There is no form because there is no route, and there is no route because a length is not a place, and that is correct and it is not anybody's fault and it cannot be fixed by anybody in this building."

"Then it gets fixed in a Registry," said Milo Rook.

"It gets fixed in a Registry or it does not get fixed at all," said Cyril Sale, "and I will tell you what the Registry will want, because I have been dealing with them for four years. It will want a name. There is a route in the Atlas and a route in the Atlas is a name, and a name in the Atlas is entered against a rateable place, and a rateable place is a building with a street, and a length is not a building and it does not have a street and it cannot be given one." He looked at the window for a moment. "**Which is the fifteenth of December, Mr Rook. That is your reach. That is the box somebody put *no rateable place* in.** You have had a woman sign her own name to a levy every week since the fifteenth of December because this city cannot put a name on a building, and a maintenance length is a worse thing to name than a building because a building is at least somewhere a person can be put."

Nobody in the office said anything for about four seconds.

"So the passage happened," said Milo Rook, "and the record of the passage is a line on a sheet about a drain, and the city cannot show anybody that it happened, and in about four months a department can say that no passage occurred, and the district will not be able to prove it, and there is no appeal from that, and there is no hearing that would help, because the hearing's only evidence would be a piece of paper about a drain."

"Yes," said Cyril Sale. "And I have been a clerk for four years and I have never seen that before and I have thought about it for about four minutes and I have not thought of one way round it, and I am not going to think of one for you, because you are the man who will use it and I would like a week."

---

**And in a yard in the river core at about eleven o'clock on the morning of the twenty-third there were about nineteen people, and two women said the two sentences that the whole week was for, and they were said in that order because one of them has to be said first and everybody in that yard knew it and nobody had to arrange it.**

"Nobody died," said Mara Quill.

She was standing by the trestle table and she had not been in the district since Friday afternoon and she had come down at seven in the morning on purpose.

"I am going to say four things and then I am going to say the other one and the other one is the one I came for. **Nobody died.** Thirty-one passengers, a conductor of thirty-eight and a driver of fifty-one, and every one of them is alive, and the driver has not been able to say a sentence since Friday and is at home and is not hurt, and the conductor has been awake for five days and two hours and went to bed in her own bed at ten past three this morning. Nobody died. I am going to be able to say that in a room in about a year's time and it will be the only sentence in the file that nobody can argue with."

"And the other thing," said Hettie Brann, and she did not have a tally in her hand and she did not have a form.

"The other thing is that **a document is the only thing in this city that bites.**" She has said that in three yards and in two offices and in a queue at a clinic and she says it the same way every time. "I have been on that counter nineteen years. I have watched this city eat four people in six years over a document, and it was not the four people who did it, it was a piece of paper in a drawer, and there is nothing else in this city that can be used against anybody. Not a complaint. Not a petition. Not thirty-one people standing in a shed being grateful. A document. That is all it has ever been.

"And there is not one now. The car came back and the people came back and the one thing that could have been said about the ninth of December — that a gang walked a length a second time and thirty-one people came off a car on it — is on a form about a drain in a rack in an office, and in about four years that rack will be cleared, because racks are cleared, and when it is cleared the thing will not have happened.

"And it will be done again." She put her hands in her pockets. "Not by a villain. By a box. Somebody will want a drain rodded and they will be asked for a reason and they will give a number and the number will be in a drawer, and in about nine weeks a bus will go past a stop that is not there, or a school bus, or a fire engine, and there will be nobody in a shed to say *we said the word at four places* because there will be no shed and no length and no sheet. **The next one will not be a tram. The next one will be something with children in it.** And I would like somebody in this yard to write that down who is not me, and nobody has written it down, and I am not going to be the woman who has it on a form."

---

**And on the Tuesday morning a man came back from the south with a card and a fact, and he put both on the table face up, and he had not been asked and had not raised it.**

"I have not had a word from Tern," said Pell Harrow. "That is the first thing and I am putting it first so that nobody has to look at me to find out. Six weeks. I went up in November on a lead with a village that has no address and one of two people who was telling me the truth was a liar, and I came back thinner, and I am going up again on the twenty-eighth whatever happens to this.

"And the second thing is the man in the works jacket." He put the card down. "He was on the quay on the Monday at about eleven, and he was there for the whole of it, and he was not in a car and he was not hiding, and he was about forty feet off the north end for about an hour and he had a notebook out and he wrote in it twice while your gang was walking that length.

"And I did not follow him and I want that written down as a thing I decided and not a thing I forgot. I did not follow him because I have been six years looking for a man in a list and I am not going to be the man who follows a stranger through a yard in a district where I am the one person everybody knows. I have thought about it since and I would do it again and I have written the card down so that if anybody ever wants to know whether I noticed him, the answer is that I noticed him at about eleven feet and wrote down what he was wearing."

---

**And the last thing on the twenty-third of December is the carbon in the second drawer, and the ninety seconds that made it out of its week.**

Somebody from a newspaper was at the depot gate at about eleven o'clock on the Tuesday morning, and there were two men from the Council's transport scrutiny panel in a corridor in Crown Heights at about two in the afternoon, and by four o'clock on the Tuesday there was a suggestion in three shops on Cardew Street of a notice about a tram, and Hester Coombe read the whole of it out to the two men from the panel from the second drawer of her own desk, in a depot office, in daylight, at a counter, to a person who asked.

"Thirty-one, a conductor and a driver," she said, "and here is the sheet, and it is a carbon and the original went down that length with the car and came back with the car, and the original is signed at the bottom and it is signed N. Bray at four thirty-six and the car was on the shed road at twenty to five, and if anybody in this city wants to know that those thirty-one people were alive on Tuesday the sixteenth of December then that is the only piece of paper there is and I am giving it to you now while I have a witness in the room and I am not going to be able to give it to you again because I have not got a second copy and I am not going to pretend to anybody that I have."

One of the two men asked her whether the sheet showed where the car had been.

"It shows where thirty-one people got off," said Hester Coombe. "On the Monday. It does not show where it was, and it cannot, and that is not a fault in my drawer."

---

**The Field Book. Nothing on the twenty-second. Nothing on the twenty-third. He looked at it on the Monday night, on the Tuesday night, and on the Wednesday morning at half past five when the offices shut at noon, and it did not write, and he has not written down what he thinks that is.**

On the twenty-seventh it wrote, and the entry is in the next day's book and it is the last entry of the year.
