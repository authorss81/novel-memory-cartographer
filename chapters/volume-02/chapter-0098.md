# Chapter 98: The Fifteenth Of April

On Monday the thirtieth of March a woman of sixty-one asked a question in a room in Crown Heights that nobody in the city of Alderquay has ever asked, and she asked it in the ordinary way because it is an ordinary question, and that is why it worked, and she has said since that she has thought about how she asked it for about nine days and that she could not have asked it any other way at all.

She asked a man of about fifty in a room with a machine in it a question about the second week of May.

"I am going to ask you a returns question and it is a stupid one and I have asked nineteen years of them from the other side of a counter and I have never asked this one."

"Go on."

"**What do you print in the second week of May?**"

---

He did not think about it, which is the part she has written down.

"That is a quarterly card," he said. "District returns, current quarter, return required, last received, reason outstanding, officer responsible. Four of those a month out of this machine and about nine hundred a month in the building, and the second week of May is one of them and there will be nine hundred of them in the second week of May in about four years and none of them will be about anything."

"That is not what I asked."

"Then ask the rest of it."

"**What is printed for the day after the fourteenth of April?**"

And the man of about fifty with the machine in it, who is fifty-one next month and has been in that room nineteen years, put his pen down and did not pick it up for about four minutes, and has said since that he did not do that on purpose either.

"There is nothing," he said.

"Nothing for the fifteenth?"

"Nothing for any day after it. **There is a card every quarter and a card every month and about nine hundred cards a month, and there are four pages that come by hand from an office that signed no name, and the four pages are the seasonal outlook and they say the season runs from the second of January to the fourteenth of April, and there is no second outlook.**"

"How many have you seen."

"**One.** I have been in this room nineteen years and I have seen the one that came in December and I have never seen another and I have never asked for one and I have never known anybody who has, and I am telling you that as a fact about a room and not as a finding, because I have never once in nineteen years been in a position to know whether a thing does not exist or whether it exists and has not been sent."

---

**And that is the answer, and it is the answer of a woman at a counter and a man at a machine, and it is not a surveyor's, and Milo Rook wrote it down twice and improved it none of the two times, and nobody thanked anybody.**

Hettie Brann said the rest of it in a yard on the Sunday afternoon and about nineteen people were in it and she had read the four pages twice before she asked, which she has not said and which Diley Farr established afterwards in about nine seconds by asking her a question in the ordinary way.

"They are for a season," she said. "The second of January to the fourteenth of April. Eleven weather-driven seam events, four of them in the river core, one in the Outer Reach, and the rest unallocated and allocated on the day. And there are two of the four in the river core gone and nine to go, and **none of the nine has a day on it anywhere in this city, and there is nothing whatever printed for the day after the fourteenth of April, and the people who will be standing in the weather on the fifteenth of April do not know that, and the machinery does not know that, and the only people in this district who might have written it down are me and a woman with a satchel and neither of us can write.**

"And here is why we cannot write, and I said this in this yard in February about another day and I am going to say it again because it has got a second half now. The district cannot ask, because the district cannot show an effect upon a person. I cannot ask, because I am a counter. He cannot ask—" she did not look at Milo Rook, and about four people noticed, **"—because he would make it into a document, and it would have a reference on it, and in about four years there would be two of them, and then there would be a day in April that somebody had written about, and a day in April that nobody had.**"

---

**And then she said the thing that is the whole of what it is to be one of the last of a season that is going to be weather-driven, and it took her about thirty seconds, and it is about her own job, and nobody in that yard had thought of it.**

"I have been the last of forty-one of something," said Hettie Brann. "**That is what my job is. It is not the clinic and it is not the dispenses and it is not the counter. I read across.** Two departments, two documents, and I am the one who notices when they disagree, and I have done it for nineteen years and nobody has ever asked me how and the answer is that nobody asks because you are not asked about a thing you are not paid for.

"**And I have never once been told that any of it was a season, and I found out this year because there is a piece of paper with a date on it, and I have had forty-one of them and I did not know there was a number until a man with a watch stood in a doorway for four days in January.**"

Somebody said, "What do you want to happen on the fifteenth."

"**Nothing,**" said Hettie Brann. "That is the answer and I have had about nine days to find it and it is *nothing.* I am not asking for a second outlook. **I am saying that a district that has spent the winter being careful not to write anything down is going to be standing in the weather on the fifteenth of April with no reason to think anything is different, and a thing that has no reason to think anything is different is a thing that will change its practice on the fifteenth of April, and about nine people in eleven streets will be the only people in this city who do that, and they will do it for nothing and out of a guess.**"

---

**And Rue said one thing about it, in a yard, and it was not soft, and she has said since that it is the only thing she has said in four months that she would want read back to her in ten years.**

"You have just said a good thing and it is going to be used."

"I have said a true thing."

"You have said a true thing that makes a person of about thirty-one feel as though he is the man who is going to do something about the fifteenth of April," said Rue, "and he will not, and you know he will not, and you have said it in a yard of about nineteen on a Sunday afternoon. **I am not saying you are wrong. I am saying I have watched him get a worse week off a good sentence and I have watched it twice and I am not going to watch it three times and I would like it noted that I said it in this yard and not in a corridor.**"

"And what would you have done instead."

"**Nothing,"" said Rue. "You have been saying nothing in a yard for four months and it has been the best thing anybody has done for this district, and you are extremely good at it, and you have just stopped.**"

---

**And at about ten past two on the afternoon of Monday the thirtieth of March, a man of about twenty-six in a high-visibility jacket came off the round, took the second side out of a satchel and put a line on it in his own hand with the date and his initials against it, and wrote nothing else, and would not say why, and had not been asked.**

**The Field Book, Monday the thirtieth of March, 23.30, a front room, and it is four days off the twenty-sixth, and I have got four fours in a row now and I am going to write them out and I am not going to say anything about them, and that is the whole of what I am going to say about them, and I have decided that and I am writing down that I have decided it.**

*Thirtieth, 23.30. Four days off the twenty-sixth, and that is the fourth of the fours in this month, and I am going to write them out and I am not going to say anything about them, and that is the whole of what I am going to say about them, and I have decided that and I am writing down that I have decided it.*

*Where the pairs are, so far this month. **The eleventh to the fifteenth is a four. The fifteenth to the eighteenth is a three and it is the seventh three in this book and none of the four that were here in December is one of the other three.** The eighteenth to the twenty-second is a four. The twenty-second to the twenty-sixth is a four. The twenty-sixth to the thirtieth is a four. One six. One nine. One two. Two fives. One eight. One twenty-five. Two sevens. **Four fours.** That is twenty, and there are fifteen days to the fourteenth of April, and I have not got a use for the number and I am not going to write down what twenty of a thing means, and I have noticed that the second of those is a sentence I have already written in a different book in February, in nine words, and the nine words are better than mine and they are not mine.*

*One. **There is nothing printed in this city for the day after the fourteenth of April.** A man of fifty-one next month in a room with a machine in it has been in that room nineteen years and has seen one seasonal outlook and has never seen a second and has never known anybody who has and has never been in a position to know whether it does not exist or whether it exists and has not been sent. And a woman of sixty-one at a counter asked him the question and got the answer in about four minutes and the reason it worked is that she asked it in the ordinary way, because **there is a card every quarter and about nine hundred cards a month and none of them is about anything, and the only question in that room is the ordinary one, and she asked the ordinary one and the extraordinary one came out of it.**

*Two. And the answer she gave when somebody asked her what she wanted to happen on the fifteenth was *nothing*, and she had nine days to find that and it is the correct answer and it is the worst one, and I am not going to write her sentence out a second time in a book, because I wrote it out in a yard on the Sunday and it is hers and not mine, and if anybody in four years wants to know what she said then they want her and not this. I have read the yard version back to her on the Monday evening and she has said *do not put that in a book* and I have not put it in a book. **It is in this book. I have put it in a book. She said do not and I have done it, and I am going to write that I have done it, and I am not going to take it out, and she is going to find out in April the way everybody finds out in April in this district, which is by reading it somewhere else.**

*Three. And then Rue said the true thing about it in a yard of about nineteen on the Sunday, which is that I have said a good sentence and it is going to be used, and that she has watched me get a worse week off a good sentence twice and is not going to watch it three times. **And she is right, and I have been unusually good at saying nothing in a yard for four months, and I have stopped, and I did not notice that I had stopped, and the reason is a woman of sixty-one who was frightened of being listened to and wanted to be listened to for about thirty seconds.** That is the whole of the risk of this in one sentence and I am the one writing it.

*Four. The second side has got three lines on it in March and about nine blanks expected by the fourteenth of April, and I have got a day-count in this book with four fours in a row in it, and there is no way on the earth to tell which of the four was a day nobody stood out in and which was a day there was nothing to see, and that was said in a yard in February by a man with a shovel in nine words and I have now watched it come true in a book in my own hand and I have not written one word about it, and I have written four entries since.*

*The page home the hundred and twenty-seventh day, blank, three marks in three hands. Ten routes, ten. The token cold, hundred and thirteenth day. The tally has nine lines. I did not cross and I did not hold anything and I did not go in anywhere and I have not had a single day this month that I could have described as a season, and there is going to be one on the thirty-first and one on the first of April and they are not events, they are Mondays, and the season ends on the fourteenth, and the day after is not on anything, and I have known that for about nine days and I have told about four people and I have not asked anybody to do anything about it, and that is the first correct thing I have done in three months and I have written down that I have not done anything about it, and Rue was right, and I am going to go on not doing anything about it for as long as I can.*
