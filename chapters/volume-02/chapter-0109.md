# Chapter 109: Eleven

The gang was on the back length at half past seven on the morning of Thursday the twenty-third of April and eleven men went down six hundred yards with a shovel each and the trolley, and the sheet for that week has two dates in it, the twentieth and the twenty-third, and the reason column is empty on both, and the condition column is empty on both, and it has not had anything in it since the twelfth of February.

**That is six walks in April with an empty condition column, and a foreman of fifty-four has written two columns and two dates and eleven men's names on each of them, and there is no column on that sheet that wants to know why a column is empty, and about nine hundred blanks in six weeks in a building in Crown Heights are the only other record of it and the nine hundred blanks are the machine's dash and not a reason.**

The sheet went into the rack at about ten past ten on the Friday morning and the duplicate went into a tin, and the duplicate went into a second tin on the bottom shelf of the plan room of the Civic Atlas Office under a repair schedule, and **that tin now holds nineteen weeks of gang carbon and one page that is not a gang sheet, and the nineteen is a figure a man of thirty-one has written out in a book in a coat and is not going to write out again, and the page has not been read and is not going to be read and will be produced in about eleven years by somebody with a box.**

---

**And here is the arithmetic, and it is the whole of it, and it is four lines long and it took six months and eleven streets and it is not going to be improved on by anybody.**

Four pages, dated, from an office that signed no name, for a season from the second of January to the fourteenth of April: eleven weather-driven seam events. Four in the river core. One in the Outer Reach. The rest unallocated and allocated on the day.

**The season ended on Tuesday the fourteenth of April. There is nothing printed in Alderquay for the fifteenth. There has never been a second outlook and a man of fifty-one in a room with a machine in it has been in that room nineteen years and has seen one and has never known anybody who has seen a second, and he cannot tell whether the second one does not exist or whether it exists and has not been sent, and he has said that in these terms and I have written it out four times and improved it none of the four times.**

So: eleven were forecast for a season that has ended.

**Either eleven happened, or eleven did not, and this city cannot tell you which, and neither can the four pages, and the reason it cannot tell you is that there is no calendar anywhere in Alderquay with a day on it, and there never was one, and the four pages do not have a column in which to put a day, and eleven events with no days is not a forecast and it is not a record and it is a number on the first of January that somebody was going to have to reduce on the day, and the day was never going to arrive for anybody because nobody knows which day it is.**

Two of the four in the river core have happened, in the only sense this city can put a number to. One of them is a line in a man's own hand in a printed sheet with a district in the row and a time in it and two initials. The other one happened on a hundred and ten yards of frontage in the rain in front about nine people and there is no row anywhere in this city for it.

And a third thing happened on Monday the sixth of April, in wind, and it was the smallest of the three by a long way: a settlement plate with a name and a date on it came off two nails on a fence at the low end of Bridge Street and is in the grass on the far side, and nobody was hurt, and nothing fell down, and it is not a seam, and it is not a length and it is not a remnant and it is not a place of any kind that anybody in this city has a word for, and **nobody has said out loud that it was one of the eleven, and there is no form on which to say it, and I have not said it, and a man of fifty with a pen has written the second column out with his own hand for the fourteenth Monday since the twenty-sixth of January and the four is still four.**

"That is three," said Cyril Sale, at about a quarter past one in the afternoon, in four rooms above a fitter's shop, to nobody who had asked. "I have counted them three times today and I am not going to say the number out loud in this office, and the reason I am not going to is that **if I say three out loud in a room then in about four years there is a form with three lines on it, and a form with three lines on it wants a fourth.**"

"And the eleven."

"**The eleven is a number on a page in December and a man with a pen has not reduced it and is not going to, and there is nobody in this city who could reduce it, and if the eleventh happened it happened on a day and the day is inside a fortnight that ended ten days ago and there is nothing in this city that says a day in that fortnight.**"

---

**And what the end of the season did to the people who were standing in it, in four items, all of which are about the fifteenth of April, and none of which anybody is going to be told about.**

*One.* A round of seven stops in eleven streets went out at about ten past eight in the morning on Wednesday the fifteenth and came in at about seven in the evening, four hours longer than it needed to be, unpaid, and a water clinic counter was open from eight until six as it is every day.

*Two.* About nine people stood in the weather between about five and about seven in the evening, for no reason at all, out of a guess, and they were the only people in this city who did that.

*Three.* The second side of the sheet of who does what and on which day has a blank on the fifteenth, and a blank on that side is a day when nobody stood in the weather, **and the sheet has been right, in its own terms, on every Wednesday since the second of February, and on the fifteenth of April it was right on a day when about nine people did, and it is not wrong, and it is not going to be able to be otherwise, and there is no column on that piece of paper for a man who is somewhere else and there never was and there is not going to be.**

*Four.* Nobody wrote it down.

---

**And that is the fourth thing in five months that has happened in this district and cannot be shown to anybody, and a man of thirty-one has now got four of them, and he has said so out loud in a yard on the Friday evening, and about nineteen people were in it, and he said it in about forty seconds and improved it none of the times, and the four are: a rectangle of standing water on the twenty-ninth of January; a hundred and ten yards of frontage in the rain on the nineteenth of February; a plate in a field on the sixth of April; and about nine people standing about in eleven streets on the fifteenth of April for nothing at all.**

"A plate," said Rue.

"A plate."

"You are not going to say anything else about the plate."

"**I am not going to say anything else about the plate,**" said Milo Rook, "because in about four years somebody is going to ask what this district did in April, and there is no form, and I have got a plate in a field and about nine people in the weather and a season that ended without a day, and if I put a why next to any of them then in four years there will be a document with four lines on it, and a document with four lines on it wants a fifth, and a fifth is a schedule." He put his hand flat on the trestle table. "**I have spent six months learning that a reason is a thing you can be asked about and that a thing you can be asked about is a thing somebody else has to hold, and I am going to keep the four where they are and I am not going to be the man who holds them.**"

---

**And what is still standing at the end of the season, and it is the same as it was in January, and nothing in it has moved, and nothing in it has been paid, and one of the nils has not become four.**

Eleven square miles of fen carry a typed zero on a printed sheet in a room in Crown Heights, and there have been three nils about that place in twenty weeks and there is not going to be a fourth. A man of fifty-eight goes out into it on the first dry week of every summer with a rod and a chain and writes four lines about about nine feet of drain into a pad that is in a drawer in Crown Heights from this year back to about 1969, and nobody has ever asked him for one of them, and **nobody has gone there this winter, and nobody is going to, and the going is the part that has been going wrong for forty-one years, and there is a settlement plate in a field nine hundred yards from here and I have not been to that either and it is on the far side of a fence and I have no business on the far side of a fence.**

And a question, and it is not this district's question and it is not going to be answered in April, and it is sharper than it was in January, and it is four lines long:

**Why does a region the Registry enters as stable appear on a forecast of weather-driven seam events at all?**

Eleven events, one a season, and four pages, and a district stability column, and a row that says MIREWARD and a zero in it, and the ground the zero is entered on is *no maintained seam, no recorded loss, no resident witness and no schedule* — **and all four of those are true, and a place is not stable because nothing has happened in it, and a place is entered as nil because there is nobody writing down what happens in it, and those two facts look identical on a card out of a machine and they are not the same thing, and the second one is worse.** And a man of fifty in a room with a machine in it cannot tell whether the next outlook does not exist or whether it exists and has not been sent, and there is no person in this city in a position to know, and a district that cannot show an effect upon a person cannot ask, and a counter cannot write to a company secretary, and a surveyor would make it into a document with a reference on it and in four years there would be two. **Nobody has gone into Mireward this winter and nobody is going to, and there is a settlement plate in the grass behind a fence at the low end of Bridge Street that has been there since the sixth of April and I have not gone on the far side of that fence either, and it is on the far side of a fence and I have no business on the far side of a fence, and those two facts have the same shape and I have put them next to each other once this afternoon and I am not going to do it again.**

---

**The Field Book has no entry for Friday the twenty-fourth of April and the reason is that it is one day off the twenty-third and I am not pairing it, and the reason I have written that sentence at all is that a man of forty-one on no list at all has told this district four times in six months that a list is a thing you read and a minute is a thing you are in, and a woman of sixty-one has told it that a number which cannot get better is the only kind of number worth having, and both of those sentences are the same sentence, and the same sentence is about a week, and a week is not a calendar.**

*And a season of eleven things is over, and I have got a number, and I am not going to write down what eleven means, because in January a man of about twenty-six on a length of tram route told me in nine words that if I only turn up for the ones that happen then I have not been keeping anything, I have been collecting, and I turned up on the fifteenth of April for nothing at all and did not go, and those two facts are the whole of six months and they are the same fact, and about nine people in eleven streets have been keeping something since the second of February and four of them cannot see that they have, and I have not told them, and I am not going to, and a rule at the foot of a sheet is for the person whose hand it is going to be asked about and there is not one person in eleven streets whose hand is going to be in the answer, and the man who found that out is about sixty, he puts his hand on a corner for four seconds, he has been doing it since before the war, he is eleven minutes away on a Wednesday, and he does not know that a piece of paper exists and he is not going to be told, and on the fifteenth of April there was no coffee on that row and there were about nine people on it anyway, and I was the only one of the nine who would have had a reason and I did not go, and that is five days of nothing and it is not a pair.*
