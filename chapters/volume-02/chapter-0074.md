# Chapter 74: Somebody Else's Work

Pell Harrow went up the south on Sunday the twenty-eighth of December at about half past six in the morning, on a coach from the Terrace with a card and a bag and a coat that was not his, and he did not tell anybody he was going and he did not tell anybody he would be back, and by the Saturday about nine people had found out and about four of them found out from Rue, who had known since Sunday night and who has said since that she has been carrying one piece of information for eleven days and would rather not be asked about it and will not be.

On Thursday the eighth of January the round went out at eight and in at four and it was one man short, and it is a round of seven stops and eleven streets and four of the stops are at a door, and one man short on a round does not mean somebody else does more, it means the round takes longer, and the round took about twenty-two minutes longer, and nobody in this district is paid for the round and nobody has ever been.

---

**The gang was on the back length at half past seven on the Thursday morning and it was the second walk of that week and it was not an event.**

That is the sentence. It is the sentence the whole of the last three weeks of this district has been standing in the rain waiting to hear, and it came at half past seven on the morning of the eighth of January in nineteen degrees below freezing, and eleven men went down six hundred yards of double track with a shovel each and a trolley between them and a clipboard, and a foreman of fifty-four wrote something at the wall and came back up, and **the man of about twenty-six stopped in the down loop.**

The sheet for that week has two dates in it, the fifth and the eighth, and two entries in the hours column, and the reason column is empty on both, and it goes into the rack on the Friday the ninth at about ten in the morning and the duplicate goes into a tin and the duplicate goes into a second tin in a plan room under a repair schedule, and there is nobody in this city who will ever look at any of that again except a foreman checking his own March, and that is the correct state of it.

---

**And on the Thursday morning at about ten past seven, at the north end of the length, a woman who keeps a round asked a man of about twenty-six a question, and he answered it, and neither of them made anything of it afterwards, and it is on the page because it happened and because a drain still talks.**

Rue had come out on her own round. She does not go to the quay. She has not gone to the quay in eleven weeks except once, on the twenty-second of December, when the district was waiting on a car. She came out at about ten past seven because a foreman of fifty-four had said to her on the Monday evening, in a hut, with a kettle on, in about eleven words, that the round could come through the quay section gate on Mondays and Thursdays and on no other day, because there is a lock on the hut and the key is on a nail and eleven men need it, and she had said that she would come on the Thursday because it is the second walk and the second walk is the one where they are not in a hurry.

She stood at the north end for about four minutes.

"You stop in the loop," she said.

"Two years."

"I have watched you from the Ferry Road side about four times and I thought you were looking at the drain."

"I am looking at the drain."

"What for."

He did not answer straight away, and Rue did not press him, and the two of them have both said since that the pause was the most useful thing that happened that morning.

"Whether it still talks," said the man of about twenty-six.

And that is all he said for about three minutes, and then he said the rest of it, and he said it standing at the north end of a length of tram route in the cold to a woman with a satchel, and there were eleven men about four hundred yards behind them with shovels and nobody heard any of it.

"There is a mark on the cover of it. Two of them, a cross and a bar, and that is not a survey mark, that is a fella called Ferrier putting a mark on a drain cover in about 1971 to say it is his, and Ferrier is dead and I never met him. And you can put your ear on a cover and you can hear whether there is air in it or not, and there is, and there has been every Monday and Thursday for two years, and I know it is there because I know what it sounds like when it is not, and I have not heard it not be there once, and that is the whole of what I am doing." He shifted his feet. "There is a man of about forty on this gang who put me on to it and he is not in the second gang any more and he has not been for years, and neither of us has ever said a word about it, and I have not said it to you before today and I am saying it to you because you are not going to write it down."

"I am not going to write it down," said Rue.

"No," he agreed, "and that is why I have said it."

Then the foreman called them in and both of them walked off and neither of them looked back, and about four people on the Ferry Road side of that quay saw a woman with a satchel talking to a man in a high-visibility jacket for four minutes on a Thursday morning and about none of them thought anything of it at the time, and two of them have said since that it was the only useful thing anybody in the district did in January and they said it about a drain.

---

**And on the same Thursday morning at about eleven o'clock, in the depot office at Alder Quay, a woman of thirty-eight came on her own time and asked for a copy, and a clerk of fifty-one made her one, and neither of them told anybody.**

Nessa Bray had not been on a car since the twenty-second of December and had slept in her own bed on the twenty-third at ten past three in the morning and had not been asked a question by anybody in the district since. She came into the depot office at about eleven on the Thursday morning, which is a shift change, and she stood at the counter, and Hester Coombe said, "You have not got a warrant and you have not got a form and if you want the sheet I cannot give you the sheet."

"I know you cannot give me the sheet," said Nessa Bray. "I want a copy of the carbon."

"The carbon is the department's and I have not got a second copy and I have said so to two men from a council panel and I am not going to pretend to you that I have."

"I am not asking for a document." Nessa Bray put her hands on the counter, which is what Lena Marr does and where she learned it. "My mother is sixty-nine and she was not told anything for six days because I did not telephone, and on the Monday she asked me one question, and the question was *did you write it down*, and I said yes, and she said *where*, and I said *at work*, and she has been asking me where for sixteen days."

There is a machine in the depot office for copying a sheet, and it is about as old as the office and it works about two times in three, and Hester Coombe ran the carbon through it at about ten past eleven on the Thursday morning with the door open and a witness in the room, which is what she does, and it came out faint on the left-hand side and she ran it twice and kept the better one.

"That is a copy of a copy of a carbon," she said, "and if anybody ever takes it into a room it is worth nothing at all, and I am giving it to you because you are standing at my counter at eleven o'clock on a Thursday and not because of anything else."

"Thank you."

"Do not thank me, I have got a witness." She put it in a flat envelope and wrote the date on it. "And I want to say one thing, and it is not a favour and it is not for a hearing, and if you repeat it I will deny it. There are about nine people in this city who have written your name on a piece of paper in a kitchen. You have a copy. That makes it ten, and it is the worst one, and I have said so in my own office, and that is the entire opinion I have got and I am not going to have it written down anywhere."

Nessa Bray took the envelope and put it inside her coat and said, "It is not the worst one."

"It is the worst one," said Hester Coombe, "and I have been a clerk in this office for twenty-two years and I have watched about four people ruin a good piece of paper by keeping it, and I am not going to be the fifth thing that happens to a person in this district."

---

**And the round changed on the Thursday, and it was Rue's decision, and nobody asked her to make it.**

She had it in her own book by the Friday in about eleven words, and she read it out in a yard on the Saturday to about nineteen people without being asked, and it is on the page because she read it out.

"The round goes through the quay section gate on Mondays and Thursdays. Not on any other day, because there is a lock on the hut and the key is on a nail and eleven men need it. It is four minutes longer going in and four minutes longer coming out and it is not on anybody's sheet and it is not paid for and I have not asked for it to be paid for, and I would like that last part minuted by somebody who is not me."

Somebody said, "Mrs Quill could get it onto a schedule."

"No she could not," said Rue, "and that is the whole of what happened on the seventh of January and I have been thinking about it for a day and a half and I am going to say it now instead of in a fortnight when I have said it better and worse. **A tram authority's amendment committee cannot hear anything about a stop on a round, and Mrs Quill has spent a fortnight trying to get a length of tram route into a room and the room says there is no effect on anybody, and I am not going to stand in a canteen and ask a man to put my round in a book so that it looks like the same fight.** It is not the same fight. It is a gate that is open twice a week."

---

**And on the Monday the fifth of January a man of about fifty in a works jacket was on that quay, and on the Thursday the eighth he was not, and nobody followed him on either day, and here is the whole of what this district knows about him in January.**

Pell Harrow wrote it on a card before he went. *Monday 5th, about 8.20, forty yards off the north end, in a doorway out of the rain, notebook out, wrote twice, did not go near the gang. Thursday 8th, not there. Badge with a number and not a name. Same coat.*

Rue read the card out in a yard on the Saturday morning, once, and then she put it face up on the trestle table under a brick, which is where things go in this district when they are not going into a file, and she said the sentence that has been said in this yard about eleven times in eleven weeks and has never once been said in a nicer voice than the first time.

"He is not ours and we are not following him and I have said that twice in three months and I am going to keep saying it until one of us is wrong, and I would rather be wrong on a Tuesday than right on a Sunday."

And nobody at the table put a name to him, and about four people have said since that it was the hardest thing anybody did that week, and one of them has said since that it was harder than the walking.

---

**The Field Book, Thursday the eighth of January, 20.20, a front room, and it is four lines and he says so at the top of it.**

*Eighth, 20.20. Four lines and no more, because I have had nothing of my own to write down for four days and I am not going to manufacture a page out of somebody else's work.*

*First: a drain in a down loop that still talks, and a man of twenty-six who has been listening to it twice a week for two years because a man of about forty on that gang put him on to it, and neither of them has ever mentioned it, and neither of them has ever mentioned the mark on the cover either, which is in about 1971 and is older than both of them by a long way, and a woman with a satchel asked him one question on a Thursday morning and he answered her and not me, and he was right to, because she is the person and I am not.*

*Second: a woman of thirty-eight has a copy of a copy of a carbon in her coat and it is the worst of about ten pieces of paper in kitchens in this district and a clerk of fifty-one said so in her own office and then gave it to her anyway.*

*Third: the round goes through a gate on Mondays and Thursdays. Four minutes each way. Not on a schedule. Not asked for. Not paid for.*

*Fourth: I have written down three things that other people did and I did not do one of them and I was not asked to and I would have liked to have been, and that is the fourth thing and it is the whole of this entry and I am going to leave the page there.*

*Nineteen days. The book has not written. Ten routes, ten, and it is a fortnight now since I have stood in a corridor and let anybody read them to me, which I am not going to write anything about yet.*
