# Chapter 123: A Column About A Boy

He drew it on Wednesday the third of June between about seven in the evening and half past nine, at a table in a front room, on a piece of accountancy paper, and the cup beside it is in this because the cup is in everything.

It is headed in his own hand.

> **ROUND — MONTHLY RETURN (COURIER)**
> *Week. Route. Stops. Articles. Carried. Signature.*

Six columns, and he did not invent a single one of them, because he has never in his working life drawn a column that was not somebody else's. He copied the six off a return the boy of seventeen fills in on a Monday and a Thursday, and the six are correct, and the order is correct, and the heading is the heading.

And then the seventh, written first, in about four seconds, the way the other six of them were written first.

> ***Why this round is carried by one man.***

He filled in one line and he filled it in well.

> *June. Route: quay section gate, seven stops, eleven streets. **A round is carried by a postman and a postman is entered on a form, and the form wants a depot, and a depot is an address, and this district has no rateable unit and no address and has not had one since the eleventh of October six years ago when a street name came off an index. The return cannot show the shortage, because a shortage is not a thing the return is about: a round carried by one man and a round carried by two return the same six lines, and both of them are correct, and the second man is not in any of them.***

About eighty words in the seventh column, and every word of it is true, and it has no boy's name in it and no woman's name and no department's, and it is the best thing he has written in two months, and **he read it back and did not feel the thing he felt in March and in April and at the end of April, and he has felt nothing four times now and has not decided what that is and is not going to decide it tonight.**

---

**And here is the part that is the whole of it, and it happened at a gate in a high wall at about ten past seven the next morning, and it took nine seconds.**

Milo Rook had been up since six and had carried the sheet down in his hand, face down, because he had decided in the night that he was going to do the last of it in daylight at the gate, and the reason he decided that is a piece of cowardice and he has written it down as one.

"You have written a seventh column," said the man of forty-one on no list at all, "and it is about a boy."

He had not got the pencil out. He had not unfolded it. He had it face down in his left hand with his thumb on the fold.

"Seven," said the man of forty-one. He shifted his cap. "**That is seven of them and I have watched six and I have never once seen you learn it, and nobody in this district has, and I have been in this gate four times a day since the twenty-eighth of December and I have got very good at it and I have told nobody and there is nobody to tell.**"

He went up the Row. Nobody wrote it down.

---

**And the finding, and it belongs to a man of forty-six in four rooms above a fitter's shop, and Milo Rook went and got it on the Thursday morning at about eleven, and it took about eleven seconds and the works clerk did not look up from four returns.**

"You have got a form about a round."

"I have got a form about a round."

"Then I am going to tell you what is wrong with it and I am going to tell you the same way I would tell a man about a length, and it is not about the seventh column, and I want that in front of you." He squared the returns. "**The other six columns are a return. All six of them say what a round did. Week, route, stops, articles, carried, signature. A round carried by one man and a round carried by two hand me the same six lines and I could not tell you which one I was holding, and that is not a defect in the form, that is the form being correct.**"

"Then what is it."

"**The return is not asking whether the round was enough. The return is asking whether the work was done.** And a shortage is not work undone. Seven stops and every article carried and a signature at the bottom of it, and if you put two men on that round in June you would hand me back the identical six lines and it would be the identical six lines in August, and in about four years somebody in a depot is going to read these twelve returns and see twelve perfect months and see no shortage anywhere in them, and he will be right, and there will not be a column in the whole of that authority that will let him say the other thing."

"So what is on the return."

"**A boy of seventeen's name, once a week, twice a month, in your district, and a route through a gate, and that is the whole of what is on it about him.**" He put a pen down. "And the seventh column wants a why, and a why about that is a sentence, and a sentence in your handwriting on a form about a round is a document about a person, and in about four years it is a piece of paper with a boy's round on it and a sentence on it about why there was only one of him, and he is seventeen and he is not going to be asked about it and he does not know it is on paper, and if it ever came to him he would not be able to get out from under it by being forty."

"He knows I have made a form like this."

"He knows you have been in his gate about four times a day since the second of January, which is a different thing and is his business and not yours." Cyril Sale did not look up. "**And I have had a box of mine asked about twice by a machine, and a box on the annual return asked about twice, and a line six on that return filled once in fifty-four words on the fourteenth of January in a clerk's own hand with a signature against it, and I have not been asked about that line and I am not going to be, and I am telling you that it is eleven weeks now and it is not protected, it is just not yet asked about, and I have said that sentence in this corridor before and I have not got a better one.**"

---

**And on the Thursday morning he did not add one word to the seventh column, and he did not fold it, and he did not put it in the coat, and he did not put it under a counter, and he did not say any of it in a yard, and he did not copy it into his own book. He did the eighth thing, and the eighth thing is that he did not decide.**

It is on the top of his own table in his own office, face up, where it has been since about eleven o'clock on the Thursday morning, with nothing written on the outside of it and nothing written on the back of it, and it has a heading in the seventh column and eighty words in the seventh column, and it is not in a drawer and it is not in a rack and it is not under a brick and it is not in a book in a coat, and **he has not told anybody where it is, including the man of forty-one, and the man of forty-one did not ask, and the not-asking was in about four seconds and it was the fastest four seconds of anybody's week.**

"A table is not custody," he wrote that night, "and a table is not hidden, and I have spent seven months learning six places that are not custody either and I have not once asked whether one of them was better than a table. **A thing left in the open in a room other people walk into is the first of the seven that can be produced against me. Every one of the other six is a place somebody can look in, and a place somebody can look in is a place somebody can take from, and I have wanted that for seven months and it has cost three people their names and one of them hers and one of them a woman of sixty who was not in the room, and this one is on a table and it can be produced and I have chosen it and nobody asked me to and I have written down that nobody asked me to.**"

**And he has not stopped.** That is in the same entry, in the imperative, and it is the clause he wrote before the other one: *I am going to draw another one in about four months and I am not going to be able to help it, and the reason this one is on a table is not that I have stopped, and I am not going to let anybody in this district, including me, turn a custody decision into a reason for not writing.*

---

**And eleven men walked six hundred yards of reserved tram route at half past seven on Thursday the fourth of June and the condition column was empty and the reason column was empty, and that is the eighteenth walk since the first of April with an empty condition column and there is no column on that sheet that wants to know why a column is empty.**

**And on the Wednesday the third of June the second side of the sheet of who does what and on which day had a blank on it, and that was the ninth blank Wednesday since the eighth of April, and the sheet is right about it, and on every one of the nine the only person who stood in the weather in this district was a man of about sixty at a shed at Cardew Docks, and he does not know a piece of paper exists.** A plate in a field at the low end is fifty-nine days in the grass, face down, and the grass has closed over the place where it went down, and nobody has reported it and nobody has told the works and nobody has said out loud that it was one of the eleven and nobody is going to.

---

**The Field Book, Thursday the fourth of June, 23.20, a front room, one day off the third, and there is an entry above this one for the third at about half past nine and I have left it where it is and I am not going to copy it out, and that is the third time since the seventh of May that I have done it and I have noticed it and I have not written down what noticing it is.**

*Fourth, 23.20. One day off the third.*

*One. **The seventh, the same one, and the district knew the shape of it before I had got the pencil out, at a gate, in nine seconds, from a man of forty-one on no list at all, and it was the sixth time he has said that sentence out loud and the fourth time in four months and I have not written down what he is for.** He said *seven*, and he said that nobody in this district has ever watched me learn it, and I have not either. That is the finding and it is the whole of the character I have got: **the seventh column is not a mistake. The seventh column is the only thing I am good at, and I get to be good at it about four times a year, and every time I get to be good at it somebody else has to hold the paper, and in seven months I have not once found another way to be good at it, and the diagnosis has not moved since the fifteenth of March and it is still true and it still does not reach this.** The eight resolutions in order: a tin, a coat, a yard, a table in the rain, a counter, a book, a sentence with a why in it, and a table in my own office with nothing on the outside of it. **Six of the eight are places and two are sentences, and I have not improved any of the eight and I am not going to, and the seventh of the eight was the first one I made on purpose about a thing that was going to happen, and the eighth is the first one I have made that anybody else can walk into.** And nobody has thanked me for any of the eight and nobody is going to.

*Two. And the return, and the finding, which is a works clerk's and not mine. **A round carried by one man and a round carried by two return the identical six lines, and the return is not asking whether the round was enough, it is asking whether the work was done, and a shortage is not work undone.** In about four years somebody in a depot reads twelve perfect months and sees no shortage anywhere in them, and he is right, and there is no column in that authority that will let him say the other thing. And what is on the return about a boy of seventeen is his name once a week and a route through a gate. **That is the seventh time in seven months that a thing somebody built for one purpose has turned out to be the wrong shape for another, and the other six were about a why and this one is about a shortage, and I had it in my hands for a fortnight before I worked out that it was not the seventh column at all, and that is the part I have not written down anywhere but here.** I went and got it on the Thursday morning at about eleven in four rooms above a fitter's shop and it took about eleven seconds and he did not look up.

*Three. And the resolution, and the clause I wrote first. **The form is on the top of my own table, face up, and I have not told anybody and the man of forty-one did not ask and the not-asking took four seconds.** A table is not custody and a table is not hidden, and a thing left in the open can be produced against me, and I have chosen that, and it is the first of the seven that anybody can pick up, and I have written down that nobody asked me to. **And I have not stopped, and I have written that down before I wrote the rest of it, and I am not going to let the district turn a custody decision into a reason for not writing, and if somebody in this district in about four months tries to tell me that I have got better I am going to read this entry out and it will be me that is wrong.** The seventh of eight was a sentence with a why in it and the eighth is a table, and the seventh and the eighth are the same shape, and the reason the seventh was the worst of the six before it was that it was not a place, and this one is a place, and a place is worse, and I have worked that out in about four seconds and I have not put it in a yard.

*Four. **A form about a round is a form about a person and everybody in this district now knows that except me, and I knew it on the sixth of March, and I drew it anyway, and the reason I drew it is that nobody had written it down and there was nobody to ask, and that is the fourth time in seven months I have written that sentence and it is true every time and it has never once been a reason that would survive anybody else.** The boy of seventeen has not been asked about this and does not know there is a piece of paper and is not going to be told. **Twenty-two days up the south in December and twenty-two days is a number I know and he does not know I know, and Tern is not found, and the list in the tin is not a finding, and I have not asked him and I am not going to, and this form is about his round and not about his twenty-two days, and I have checked that four times tonight because I am the only person in this city who would.** The card of his under the brick is blank and has been since the nineteenth of January and there are still three things under that brick and the run number has not moved.

*Five. And the rest of the fortnight, carried without a mark. Eleven men and a foreman and a clipboard. **The second tin held twenty-three on Friday the twenty-second of May and held twenty-four on Friday the twenty-ninth of May, and I have written two figures in ten days and I said on the twenty-first of May that the twenty-two was the last one and it was not the last one, and a number that only goes up is a number I have started keeping, and I said that in this book on the thirtieth of April about the number nineteen and I have now said it twice about two numbers and I am aware of the shape of that and I am not going to write a third.** A plate in a field is fifty-nine days in the grass and nobody has said out loud that it was one of the eleven and I have moved that from a refusal into a decision and I wrote that down on the first of June and I have not told anybody and nobody is going to be told. A woman of sixty-eight on nothing is not named and I have added nothing this fortnight and the count stands at eight, and her name is not written in this entry.

*The page home the hundred and ninety-third day, blank, four marks in four hands and none of them mine. Ten routes, ten, none removed, and I have not crossed and I am not going to in June, and the count is nine lines and the paper is Hettie Brann's and the pocket is Dr Sen's. **The token cold, a hundred and seventy-ninth day, and that is the same figure as the page home carried on the twenty-first of May, and it is a different register and it is a different number about a different thing, and I have written both of them out tonight side by side so that there is a place in this book where the two of them are together and nobody including me is to take them together.***

*And the standing position, which does not improve: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.**
