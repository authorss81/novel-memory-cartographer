# Chapter 130: Both Of Them Took What They Were Given

It is Tuesday the sixteenth of June. The sixteenth of December was a Tuesday. **That is a fact about a calendar and it is the only thing about the two dates that is the same, and I did not plan it and I did not choose it and I noticed it at about a quarter past eight this morning and I have not written it down anywhere except in this entry, and I want it in this entry and nowhere else, because a date that lines up is a thing a person can be shown.**

On Monday the fifteenth I wrote in this book that I was not going to look at that page, and I did not write down that I was going to, because a plan is a reason and I have never in seven months been able to tell a plan from a reason and I was not going to try in a hurry. **I did not write it down and I went, and both of those are true, and the not-writing was not a plan and the going was not a decision, and I have been up since five and it is a Tuesday and there is a bus.**

**Alder Quay, the depot office, at about half past ten. The round does not go on a Tuesday and has not since the second of January and is not going to. The second side of the sheet of who does what and on which day is correct. There is a blank on it for Wednesday and the blank is right.**

She had the carbons out on the desk. That is where a depot clerk's carbons are at half past ten on a Tuesday; they are in a tray, face up, in a wooden box, and she goes down them with a clerk's pencil and puts them in the second drawer when she is through, and the second drawer is where they go, and there are about nine hundred of them in a year.

**She did not stop him and she did not help him and she did not say one word about any of it, and he did not ask her for anything, and he did not touch anything.**

The book was on the shelf. It is four feet from the desk and it takes about four seconds to cross a room. December is a third of the way back. Tuesday the sixteenth is a page with columns on it and a wide ruled margin down the left-hand side, and the margin has a heading printed at the top of it, and the heading is *actual*.

**Actual is where a clerk writes what happened, against what was booked. It is ruled into lines. It is the only part of that book where a person writes, and a person writes in it about four times a day for nineteen years, and it is the right shape for a time and not for anything else.**

The 07:52 off Crown Heights. One car. Four characters in the run column and four in the crew column, in type. A light engine and a two-car set to a gate at a quarter to eight in the move column, which takes nobody.

And the terminus column says **HOLLOWGATE**, in type, in the column that only ever holds a place that is on the diagram, and there is no Hollowgate, and there has never been a Hollowgate, and a depot clerk of fifty-one with twenty-two years in that room has booked about eleven crews a week for twenty-two years and knows every terminus in the book.

And in the margin, in ink, in a small upright hand, down about eleven inches of ruled lines, there are thirty-one names.

---

**He did not count them twice and he counted them once and the once was enough.**

They are in order. That is the whole of it. **They are in the order the people got on.**

The carbon for that working was face up in the tray in front of her at half past ten on a Tuesday, about two feet of it, and the depot keeps the carbon and the conductor keeps the sheet, and the six columns on it are *time on, stop boarded, fares, alighted, run no, conductor*, and the stop-boarded column is the one that was filled in by hand on the length on the Monday morning, one at a time, and it has thirty-one names in a hand, and it is not the same hand.

**Thirty-one names in a conductor's hand on a carbon about two feet long in a tray in a room, and thirty-one names in a different hand in the margin of a bound book on a shelf four feet away, and the two of them are in the same order. And the order is the order a woman of thirty-eight said out loud to a car about nine hundred times a day for sixteen years, and a hole went through nine inches of paper for every one of them, and the roll is right, and the roll does not know who one of them is and that is not a defect and she said so before anybody could.**

So the order in the margin is the order of the holes. And there is no instrument in this city that could have made that order up. **And nobody has ever been asked for it, and nobody is going to be, and there is no column anywhere for it and no reference and no date against it, and the line at the foot of the carbon is signed and the margin has no line at the foot of it at all.**

---

**And here is the whole of what six months was, and it took him about four minutes standing in a depot office, and it is this.**

**The recovered timetable contains a destination that does not exist, and the passengers' names are real, and both of those are in the same book on the same page, and they got there the same way, which is that a column takes what it is given.**

The terminus column is built for a place that is on the diagram and a place called Hollowgate is not on the diagram and it is in the column anyway, in the type, and nobody has ever queried it, and there is a hundred and forty years of list behind the column and a column does not check.

The actual column is built for a time and thirty-one names went into it, in ink, on a Tuesday in December by somebody who had about eleven minutes and nowhere else to put them, and a column does not check either.

**That is the instrument. It is a bound book of columns, printed from a diagram, on a shelf, four feet from a desk, in a room, with a person at the desk who was not sent and was not asked and has twenty-two years in the room. **And the things that are not true about it, said out loud in his own head, standing up, at about a quarter to eleven on a Tuesday in June, and written down afterwards in a book in a coat, and the writing down is the only part of it that is going to be anybody's.**

A destination that does not exist is **a fact about a place and not about a person.** Hollowgate is a word in a column. **There is nobody in it. Nobody came back.** Thirty-one people went home out of the river core on the afternoon of the twenty-second of December and they are at home now, in June, in eleven streets and about four thousand others, and they do not know that a page in a book at Alder Quay has their names down it in the order they got on, and nobody in this district is going to tell them, and there is no form on which anybody could.

The names are real and that is the whole of what is real about it. **They are real because thirty-one people got on a car and the roll punched thirty-one holes and a woman said the stops out loud and a second hand wrote them down in the only place open, and the two of those match, and a match like that cannot be arrived at by anybody who is making something up.**

And nobody inherits that page. There is no column in that book for who maintains it and there never was. It is not a remnant. It is not a place and it is not a fourth kind of anything. A length is a length and a row is a row and a boundary is a place where two people agreed to have stopped and this is none of the three, and **Hollowgate is not a place that anybody has to be responsible for and the thirty-one are not a class and the four characters are a crew and not a car, and if I write one more sentence tonight that puts a person in either of those two things somebody is going to be able to point at this page in about four years and I have written the sentences above down on purpose so that they can.**

And: **the printed part is the false part and the handwritten part is the true part, and they are in the same book, and the printed part is going to be in a drawer in about eleven years and the handwritten part is going to be in the same drawer in the same book on the same page, and there is nobody coming to either of them, and that is the only thing I have got after seven months and it is not a rescue and it is not a comfort and it is not a haunting, it is a column that took what it was given.**

---

**And at about a quarter to twelve she put the last of the carbons in the second drawer and shut it and said the only thing she said to him all morning, and it was four words, and he had not asked her for any of it.**

"You did not ask me anything."

"No."

"**Then you can go and get on with it.**" She did not look up. "There is nothing in this room that anybody is entitled to and there has not been since before you were born, and if you ever come in here and ask me one thing about December I will put the book in the drawer and I will not do it twice."

He went out at about ten to twelve into a yard that is not this district's yard, on ground that has a rateable unit on it and an address and a name, and he stood there for about four minutes and then he went and did a Tuesday.
