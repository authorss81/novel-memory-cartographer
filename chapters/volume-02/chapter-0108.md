# Chapter 108: The Fifth Of May

On Thursday the twenty-third of April, at about half past ten in the morning, a printed side came to the district by hand, in an envelope, because the district is a party and parties are given things.

It is the second time in six months that a piece of paper has come out of that building to this district with this district's name on it, and it is headed the same way the first one was, and it has six items, and the fourth line of it is the same fourth line.

> **HALL OF WARDS — DEPARTMENTAL LIST DAY**
> **BUSINESS OF TUESDAY THE FIFTH OF MAY**
> *Presented to parties. This sheet is a list of the business of the day. **It is not a notice of hearing and it confers no right and no obligation and nothing in it may be produced in any proceeding except the proceeding on the item to which it relates.*** Issued under the standing direction of the clerk of the list.*

> *1. **Continuations.***
> *2. Applications, orders, variations, consents.*
> *3. Ground and seam returns, current quarter: outstanding from sections.*
> *4. Reports of departments and officers.*
> *5. Maintenance returns, current year, presented.*
> *6. Such other business as the chair may take.*

**The matter is not on it.**

Not item one, which is continuations, and not anywhere else, and the order number is not on it, and the string *CO/6* is not on it, and the date of the original order is not on it, and the reporting officer's name is not on it, and neither is a hearing, and neither is a sentence.

The district's name is in the first line, as it was on the thirty-first of March. It is the same district. It is the same envelope. It came by the same hand to the same door.

---

**And nobody in this district can put a form on it, and about four people said so within an hour and a half, in three different rooms, and every one of them was right.**

"You cannot minute it," said Mara Quill, on the Thursday afternoon, in about eleven seconds, in a corridor. "A matter is adjourned by a chair, in a room, on a minute, and that is how a thing gets a date. **A thing does not stop having a date by being left off a list. A thing stops having a date by being discharged or withdrawn or varied, and none of those three words is in this district's name anywhere.** So the matter is before the department, undetermined, and it has no date, and the department has said in a room that it has no standing to give one, and that is all true and it is exactly the sentence I asked for and I have got it."

"So what is on this sheet."

"**A list of what somebody is going to be in a room about on the fifth of May, and the somebody is a chair, and the district is not one of the somethings.** And I want to say the rest of it now and I have not said it to anybody and I am not going to put it in a file. That list is the only instrument in this city that would have put this district in front of anybody again. It goes on a board in a corridor in that building and about four hundred people walk past it every day. **And on Tuesday the fourteenth of April nobody in that building could have found us, and they did not need to, because we were on the list.** And on the twenty-third of April we are not on the list, and there is no procedure for a party to be told that it is not on a list, and the envelope is the only notice anybody gets, and the envelope came three days after the board changed."

"Three days after what."

"**After the sheet for the fourteenth came down and the sheet for the fifth went up.** On a board, in a corridor, in Crown Heights, where a man of about thirty-one stood for about twenty minutes this morning at half past eleven and read two dates and went away without speaking to anybody, because there is nothing in this city to stand in a corridor about and be told anything."

---

**And a man of forty-one with a wet cap said the only sentence anybody said about it in a yard, on the Friday, at about half past four, and it was the standing one and he said it as though he had not said it before.**

"A list is a thing you read and a minute is a thing you are in."

"You have said that three times."

"I have said it four times and it has got a second half now and the second half is the finding. **In March that sentence was the best thing anybody had said about a piece of paper in five months and it was about a list giving us a chair. In April the same sentence is about a list taking the chair away, and nobody has sent for the chair, and nobody has taken it, and there is no form on which a chair goes back.**"

"So it was never ours."

"**It was never ours. It was a number in a column in a hearing, and I told you that on the thirty-first of March, and you wrote it down, and the thing I did not know on the thirty-first of March is that a number in a column can go down as well as up, and nobody has to do anything for it to go down, and the department has not done anything and has not had to.**"

"Somebody should write to somebody."

"**Somebody should write to somebody, and the somebody is a chair, and the chair is going to sit on the fifth of May and there will be a room and there will be a minute and the minute will not have our name in it, and the reason I know the minute will not have our name in it is that ours is not on the sheet.** And I want to say the last half of it because I am forty-one and I am on no list at all and this is the only advantage I have ever had. The first time a list gave us a chair we got about nine words out of it. **We did not use the nine words. We used them to work out what the chair was for, and it took us four months, and on the fourteenth of April a man of about fifty-one said in a room that he had no further date to give, and we have spent the nine days since doing nothing whatever about that, because the nine days have been a fortnight with a plate in a field in it and a hearing in it and a woman of sixty who could not say twenty-three words.**"

"What do you want done."

"Nothing," said Doreen Sankey. "**I want it recorded, in about four years, by somebody who was not here, that a list gave this district a chair on the thirty-first of March and took it away on the twenty-third of April, and that nobody did anything, and that the reason nobody did anything is that there is no form for it.** I have no power, no mark, and no name on any list, and I have been telling this district the standing condition of its own method four times now for nothing, and I would like the four times to have added up to a sentence that somebody else has to read."

---

**And in a corridor of the same building at about half past eleven on Thursday the twenty-third of April, with a book in a coat, on a wall to the left of a board that carries two dates, the Field Book wrote, and it is seven words.**

> *It is dated before it is read.*

*It does not say who dates it. It does not say who reads it, or when, or whether the reading is the point. **And I have stood in this corridor for about twenty minutes and nobody has spoken to me and I have not spoken to anybody, and there is nothing in this city to stand in a corridor about, and the board has two dates on it and one of them is four days old and one of them is in nine days, and I came in here at half past eleven because I have never once in six months been in this building about anything other than a hearing.** That is the finding and it is not a discovery and it has taken me six months. **A hall is a place where things are done in a room with a minute. A corridor is a place where things are done to a piece of paper, and the two are in the same building and about forty feet apart and they do not know about each other and they do not have to.** And the board is the instrument and a board is not a party and it is not a notice and it does not confer anything, and about four hundred people walk past it every day, and on the fourteenth of April every one of those four hundred people could have read a date on it and on the twenty-third of April the district was not on the sheet, and the paper copy came three days late, by hand, to a door.*

*Twenty-third, 22.20. Two days.*

*One. **A list of the business of the fifth of May came to this district by hand and the district's name is in the first line and the matter is not on it.** Not item one, which is continuations. Not a hearing, not an order number, not the reporting officer, not the date of the order. The same envelope, the same door, the same hand. And a list of the business of the fourteenth of April came to this district by hand on the thirty-first of March and had the matter on it in item one with a reporting officer's name, and I have both sheets, and they are both correct, and **every line on the second one is true and there is not one line on it that is wrong, and the whole of what has happened to this district in the last nine days is contained in the difference between two correct pieces of paper.** That is the fourth time in five months that the answer has turned out to be a difference between two correct pieces of paper, and I did not find this one, and a woman of thirty-one found it in about eleven seconds in a corridor and I walked in on her and did not have it.*

*Two. And nobody can put a form on it. **A matter is adjourned by a chair in a room on a minute. A thing does not lose its date by being left off a list. It loses its date by being discharged, or withdrawn, or varied, and none of those three words is in this district's name anywhere, and the order is suspended and not discharged, and it is going to be suspended and not discharged in every year anybody keeps it.** So the matter is before the department, undetermined, without a date, and the department has said out loud in a room that it has no standing to give one, and I asked for a list day in January in about nine words and the reason I asked for it was so that it would be printed, and it was printed, and it gave us a chair, and the chair was worth about nine of eleven days I said out loud in a yard on the fourth of April, and on Thursday the twenty-third of April a corridor in Crown Heights took the chair off a board and nobody rang.*

*Three. And the arithmetic of it, and it is the whole of what I have got, and it is not a complaint. **The third matter of the fourteenth of April is a sentence nobody in this city can produce, and the withdrawal reference is not a district reference and is held in an office that signed no name, and it was requested twice and acknowledged twice and not answered twice. On the twenty-third of April there is a list of the business of the fifth of May and there is no hearing on it.** The order is not discharged. The public name is not on the street name index. The classification of the eleventh is withdrawn and not relied on. A line in a minute with a reporting officer's name on it stands while an answer of the fourth of December stands, and it is conditional, and the condition is not the department's. **None of that has changed and all of it is now in a place where nobody is going to look at it until somebody wants to, and nobody is going to want to, and the only thing that ever put this district in front of anybody in five months was a piece of paper that nobody asks anything in.** And it has gone off the list and it is not going to be missed, and nobody has done anything wrong, and that is the finding and I have got it on the twenty-third of April at about half past eleven in the morning and it took me five months and a plate in a field.*

*Four. And a man of about fifty with a machine in it, on the Monday, with the sheet for the week commencing the twentieth of April that came off the machine on the Friday the seventeenth: five rows, four columns, the figures transferred by hand with a pen for that Monday and for every Monday back to the twenty-sixth of January, **and the four in the river core is still four and the zero against Mireward is still zero and there is not one line in the event column in any of the five rows, and he has said that he is not going to reduce either number and I have not asked him to and I am not going to, and the second column is a forecast and a forecast does not come down because a thing happened, and the only thing that has happened in this fortnight is a plate coming off a fence and nobody has said out loud that it was one of the eleven and I am not going to.** And there is a line under the words *allocated to* that a man of about fifty filled in three times in March off a wall board and has not filled in since, and a line under the words *officer responsible* that has been empty in nineteen years, and Mireward carries a typed zero and that is the third nil about Mireward and it is not going to get a fourth from me. And on the third item of the new sheet, the line about ground and seam returns outstanding from sections: **this district still cannot tell which sections are on it, that has not changed, and nobody has asked, and I am not going to.**

*Five. And seven words on a wall about forty feet from a hearing room, and I am not going to improve them and I am not going to put them in a yard, and I am not going to tell the woman of sixty-one what they are, because she has said twice in six months that the moment a thing can be used it is in use and I have believed her both times and I am not going to be the third person to hand her something she has to hold.*

*The page home the hundred and fifty-first day, blank, and it is there. Ten routes, ten. The token cold, a hundred and thirty-seventh day, and it has not been out of the coat in six months and the coat went to a corridor in Crown Heights on Thursday morning at about eleven o'clock and the token went with it and it was cold and it did nothing and it has never done anything and I have written that sentence down six times and it is still the truest thing I have got about it.*
