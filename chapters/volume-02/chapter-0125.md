# Chapter 125: A Pin In A Hole In A Road

There is a boundary at the low end that is a hundred and forty feet long and one of its ends is a corner in a street and the other end is a fence, and on Saturday the sixth of June, at about ten in the morning, Milo Rook put a tape against the corner and walked it on his own for the third time since the twenty-eighth of April.

**A boundary is a place where two people agreed to have stopped.** It is not a line on a sheet. It is two men starting at two ends and walking towards each other, and the boundary is where they meet, and if they do not meet there is no boundary and there is a piece of ground that two departments will each believe they have. He has written that sentence down four times since the second of May and he is not going to write it a fifth.

The corner end was where it was.

The far end was eleven inches further away than it was five weeks ago.

---

**A gang of four had been on the carriageway since about half past eight, patching, and they had a roller and a shovel and a kettle, and the tar was down to about nine inches square and it was black and it was new and it was the only thing on that length of road that anybody had done in five weeks.**

The road peg was in it. He had driven it himself on the second of May, about nine inches in from the gutter peg, to mark the road peg's true place, because the road peg had gone back in on the twenty-eighth of April and one of the two pegs was standing in the road surface and the other was about eleven inches out in a gutter and neither of them was wrong.

It was in the new tar and it was straighter than the one he had put in.

"Somebody took it out," said the man of about twenty-six in a high-visibility jacket, at about eleven o'clock, not asked.

"I did not ask you anything."

"You did not ask me anything," he agreed. "It was in the way of the shovel. **We take them out and put them back and we do not write down which ones, because there are about nine of them on this side of the river and every one of them is somebody's idea of where a thing was, and in about nine years there will be a hole in this road with nothing in it and a man will put one in and not know what for.**"

"That is what I wrote in my own book on the second of May."

"Then you are about five weeks early and you are not going to get the date right either." He put the shovel down and picked the kettle up. "It is not ours. It is not anybody's. The road is not ours, it is the council's, and the council does not know it is there, and it is not going to know, and the hole is going to be there after the tar and after me."

Milo Rook did not ask him anything else, and it was the second time in two months he had not asked a man of about twenty-six a question, and he wrote the two distances in his own book against the date and nothing else, and the two distances are a number, and a number in his own book against a date is a fact about a boundary and not a fact about a person.

---

**And the thing that had happened, and it was the finding of the morning, and it took him about four minutes at the far end with the tape hanging.**

The hundred and forty feet is a hundred and forty feet and eleven inches.

He had put the two pegs in on the twenty-eighth of April, and the road peg had been in the road surface and the gutter peg about eleven inches out in a gutter, and the distance between the two of them had been whatever it was, and he had measured it and written both numbers in his own book on the second of May. The gutter peg had not moved and could not have, because a man of fifty-four said in a yard in May that the low end floods about twice in four years and the gutter is the only thing on that length that moves and everything that moves gets set again by whatever moved it and there is nobody out here to set it and there has not been since the eleventh of October.

**The gutter peg is in the gutter, exactly where it was. It has not moved a tenth of an inch in ten weeks, and nobody has touched it, and the man of fifty-four's whole account of that boundary was that the water takes it out twice in four years and puts it somewhere else.**

The water has not been here. The water has not been here since February. **What has been here is a gang with a kettle, and it has moved the boundary by eleven inches, and it has moved it in the direction of the fence, and it is correct, and there is nobody to enter it, and there is no form in this city that has a box for a hundred and forty feet and eleven inches.**

"A form that wants a chainage is going to be wrong twice in four years," he said, to nobody, at about a quarter past eleven, with his back to the tape, "and it will be wrong in the direction of the water, and a form cannot be wrong in a direction, and the foreman who said that to me in a yard in May has been made wrong by a kettle and he is not going to be told and I am not going to tell him and he would say it was a kettle and not a boundary and he would be right about the kettle and wrong about the boundary and both of those are correct and there is no column for either of them."

---

**And the plate is sixty-one days in the grass, face down, on the far side, about eleven minutes that way, and the grass has closed over the place where it went down and the ground under it has gone from soft to hard and it is not going to be lifted by anybody.**

The fence is a fence. A works gang comes on the fortnightly inspection and stops at the fence, because a gang stops at a fence, and there is no column on a gang sheet for a fence.

**Nobody has gone over it since the sixth of April and nobody is going to, and the standing condition on that fence is nine words a man of fifty-four gave in a yard and did not improve: do not go over the fence, and not because I have told you not to.** There is nobody on the far side to stop you and there has not been for six years, and that is exactly why you should not go, and he has not got a better reason than that and he is not going to look for one.

There is one new thing about the fence and it is not a fence thing. **The far side is being cut.** A field does that in June; the grass on the far side of that fence is at the height grass gets to in the first week of June and after that somebody cuts it, and the somebody is not the works and is not the council and is not anybody this district has a name for, and it is being cut at the weekend, and nobody in this district is on that field and nobody is going to be.

**A thing that is not wrong cannot be struck out, and there is no column on any paper in this city for a man who is somewhere else, and a field is a field, and a fence is a fence, and a boundary is a place where two people agreed to have stopped, and there have been no two people and there have not been for six years.** The two ends of a hundred and forty feet and eleven inches are a corner in a street and a fence, and the corner end is a corner in a street because somebody built a street there, and the fence end is a fence because somebody put one up, and neither of those is an agreement and both of them are a boundary and a boundary does not have to have anybody in it to keep working.

Nobody wrote any of it down. He wrote the two distances in his own book against the date and nothing else, and he did not put a why next to them, and a why next to two numbers is a column, and there is a form about a round on the top of a table in his own office with eighty words in the seventh column and he is not going to put a second one on a page about a boundary inside a fortnight.

---

**The Field Book, Saturday the sixth of June, 23.00, a front room, one day off the fifth, and the whole of this entry is two numbers and I am going to put them at the top and the finding underneath, which is the reverse of how I have written about four hundred of these and I do not know why tonight.**

*Sixth, 23.00. One day off the fifth.*

*One. **A hundred and forty feet and eleven inches.** That is the distance from the corner in the street to the fence at the low end, measured on the sixth of June at about ten in the morning, and on the second of May it was a hundred and forty feet and the two pegs had been in the road surface and eleven inches out in a gutter, and the gutter one is in the gutter still, exactly, in ten weeks, untouched. **A boundary is a place where two people agreed to have stopped, and a boundary is two ends, and there have been no two people, and there have not been for six years, and neither end of this one is an agreement: one is a corner in a street because somebody built a street there and the other is a fence because somebody put one up.** A peg is a point and a boundary is not a point and a peg only says where somebody started, and I have now three numbers about this boundary in this book and no form anywhere that any of them can be put into, and I wrote the first two against the date and nothing else and I have written the third the same way and I am not going to put a why next to them.

*Two. And what moved it, and it is the finding. **The man of fifty-four said to me in a yard in May that the low end floods about twice in four years and the gutter is the only thing on that length that moves and that the boundary is now wherever the water puts it, and a form that wants a chainage is going to be wrong twice in four years and it will be wrong in the direction of the water.** The water has not been here. It has not been here since February. **And a form that can only be wrong in one direction is the wrong shape for a boundary, and that is the ninth time in seven months that a thing somebody built for one purpose has turned out to be the wrong shape for another, and it is the first of the nine that is wrong in a direction rather than short of a column.** A gang of four with a kettle took the road peg out because it was in the way of a shovel and put it back in the new tar, straighter than I put it in, and did not write down which ones they took out, because there are about nine of them on this side of the river and every one of them is somebody's idea of where a thing was. So the boundary is eleven inches longer than it was five weeks ago and it grew in the direction of the fence, and it is correct, and there is nobody to enter it, and there is no box in this city for a hundred and forty feet and eleven inches. And the standing sentence about a form that cannot be wrong in a direction has been made wrong by a kettle and the foreman who said it is not going to be told and I am not going to tell him and he would be right about the kettle.

*Three. **The road peg is in a nine-inch square of new tar and it is the most durable thing in this district and it is not a record, and a man of about twenty-six told me in about nine seconds that it is not theirs and not anybody's and the road is the council's and the council does not know it is there and is not going to know, and the hole will be there after the tar and after him.** I did not ask him a second question and it is the second time in two months I have not asked a man of about twenty-six anything, and I am not going to make a rule of two. And what is on the other side of the fence is not a fence thing: **the grass is being cut in the first week of June, on a Wednesday, by somebody who is not the works and not the council and is not anybody this district has a name for, and a plate that has been face down in that grass since the sixth of April is sixty-one days old and the ground under it has gone from soft to hard.** It is not going to be lifted. Nobody has reported it and nobody has told the works and nobody has said out loud that it was one of the eleven and nobody is going to, and I have moved that out of a refusal and into a decision and I wrote that down on the first of June and there is no form for a decision and there is no form for a refusal either and the difference between the two of them is that a decision can be produced and a refusal cannot, and I have had six months to work out which one I was doing and I have written down tonight that I now know.

*Four. And what has not moved. **Ten remembered routes, ten, none removed, and the ninth crossing's failure to cost one is still unexplained and nothing in this book has touched it and I have not crossed.** The tally has nine lines, the paper is Hettie Brann's and the pocket is Dr Sen's, and I have not looked at it since the thirteenth of January and I am not going to look at it in June. The token cold, a hundred and eighty-first day, and it is in the inside pocket of this coat and I stood about eleven feet from a fence for an hour with a tape and did not think about it, and that is the fourth time in five weeks and I have stopped writing that clause down and I said I would stop and I have. The page home the hundred and ninety-fifth day, blank, four marks in four hands and none of them mine. The second side had its ninth blank on Wednesday and the sheet is right about it. **The run number has not moved since the twenty-fifth of April. Three things under the brick and there have been three since the twenty-first of January, and the third of them is a boy's blank card and it has been blank for four months and nobody has taken it and nobody is going to. Tern is not found and the twenty-two days are unasked and unsaid and no batch is going to ask.**

*Five. **A practice is not a place and is not a day**, and a boundary is not a fourth kind of place, and a pin is not a seventh column, and there is a form on the top of my own table that anybody can walk in and pick up, and I am not going to put a second document on a page about a fence inside a fortnight, and I have not improved a sentence of anybody's in this book in seven months and I have copied four of them out verbatim and I have told nobody that I have done it, and the not-telling is the seventh of my own habits and it is not going to be a column.

*And the standing position, which does not improve: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.**
