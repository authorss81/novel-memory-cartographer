# Chapter 121: Things That Are Still True

On Friday the twenty-second of May, at about ten past ten in the morning, the weekly sheet for the week of the eighteenth went into the rack in the quay section office, the duplicate went into a tin, and the duplicate went into a second tin on the bottom shelf of the plan room of the Civic Atlas Office under a repair schedule, and **that tin now holds twenty-three weeks of gang carbon and one page that is not a gang sheet.**

The gang had been on the back length on Monday the eighteenth and on Thursday the twenty-first, eleven men each morning and a foreman with a clipboard, and the reason column was empty on both and the condition column was empty on both, and there is no column on that sheet that wants to know why a column is empty, and a works clerk of forty-six worked that out in about four seconds on the eighth of May and said it once and is not going to say it again.

**The machinery is still printing.** At nine o'clock that morning the allocation sheet came off the machine for the week, five rows, four columns, and a man of about fifty transferred five figures out of it with a pen, the way he has done for nineteen years, and the fourth column is still empty and has been filled in three times in nineteen years, and the line under the words *officer responsible* has never been filled in at all, and none of that has moved since the twenty-sixth of January and none of it is going to move before the thirtieth of June.

**And this district has no instruction.** It has had none since the fourteenth of April. A hearing sat as a continuation of an adjourned matter and determined nothing and adjourned nothing and set no date; a second printed list of the business of a Tuesday in May came to the district by hand with six items on it and the district's matter was not one of them, and item one is *Continuations*; a tram authority's district office asked a district to state in writing a number of persons carried on a morning in December and the district put the letter back into an envelope a day early with the flap down and nothing written on the outside of it. **Four machines in this city are still running toward documents that are not going to be printed, and none of the four has been told to stop, and being told to stop is a document, and this is the district that cannot produce documents.** So on the twenty-second of May, in eleven streets, about nine people were in the street in the morning, because that is the only thing they have got, and it is not nothing, and it is every working morning since the second of January and nobody is going to be told to stop it.

---

**And the round went out at about ten past eight and came in at about four, seven stops in eleven streets, on a Monday and a Thursday, and it is one man short and has been one man short since the twenty-eighth of December.**

The second side of the sheet of who does what and on which day runs Monday to Sunday and travels in the satchel. **On the twenty-seventh of May it had a blank on it, and that blank was the eighth one on that side since the eighth of April, and the sheet is right about it, and it is going to be right about the third of June and the tenth of June as well, and it is not going to be able to say why.**

Because on the twenty-seventh of May, and on the third of June, and on the tenth of June, the only person who stood in the weather in this district was a man of about sixty at a shed at Cardew Docks, eleven minutes away, and he has been there since before the war and does not know that a piece of paper exists and is not going to be told. A man of about forty sweeps the Row badly at ten past six every morning and has swept it badly since he was twenty-one, and he put twenty-eight words under a rule about it in March and did not date them and did not initial them, and nobody has ever asked him for them, and the coffee man of about sixty is not on that Row on a Wednesday and has not been since before the war.

**Nobody in eleven streets stood in the weather on the twenty-seventh of May. That is a fact about a day and not a fault, and a blank on a day is not a fault, and a thing that is not wrong cannot be struck out, and there is no column on that paper for a man who is somewhere else.** It is the eighth blank on that side since the eighth of April and the sheet is right about it, and **it is not one of the things in five months that cannot be shown to anybody, and it is the eighth blank and not the fourth thing, and the fourth thing is the fifteenth of April, when about nine people in eleven streets stood in the weather for nothing at all and the sheet was right about that day as well.** The other three are a rectangle of standing water on the twenty-ninth of January, a hundred and ten yards of frontage in the rain on the nineteenth of February, and a plate in a field on the sixth of April. **There is no fifth, and a blank that is right about a day it cannot see is not a fifth thing, and none of these eight blanks is a series and none of them is going to be made into one.**

---

**And here is the shape of it, and it is not a sentence anybody prepared, and it was said out loud in a yard on Monday the first of June at about ten past ten in the morning with about nine people in it, and it was a question, and nobody answered it.**

"Is any of it still true."

Nobody answered it.

"It is the first of June," said Milo Rook. "**Everything I am going to say was true in January and is true now and nobody has asked whether it is. That is the whole of what has happened in six months. Not one of us has gone back and checked one, and not one of us has asked another one, and the two facts are the same fact and they are why this district is where it is.**"

Somebody said, "Which two."

"**That it was true, and that nobody has asked.** Everything else in six months has been a consequence of that and not one of the consequences has been a document." He put his hand flat on the trestle table, which is a thing he has watched a man of thirty-one do, and which he learned off a woman who is not a clerk and has never been one, and he has never once said he learned it off her. "The order is suspended and it is not discharged. The public name is not on the street name index. A sentence cannot be restored and a desk has been asked twice and has not answered twice. The matter stands over and it is not adjourned and it has no date and it was not on the business of the fifth of May. Two and four a head a week, in advance, week by week, with no term, for two hundred and fifty-two people in a building with no number, and a woman of about thirty-four drafted the paragraph that is stopping a woman of sixty on a second landing out of the only paper in this city with her name on it, and she said yes in about four seconds on the fourteenth of April in front of thirty people and asked for it to be minuted, and the minute prints that she asked. A boy of seventeen is one man short on a round that cannot be cured from inside this district. A cast-iron cover has been flush since the sixth of April and there is no form in this city that will take a sentence about a fault that stopped. **All of it was true in January. Not one item of it has been cured, and not one item of it has been got worse, and I have been in this district seven months and I have watched about four people check and every one of them checked a machine.**"

"Then check one," said Nell Crale, from a doorway, at about ten past one that afternoon, with a hand barrow in the doorway behind her.

"**Which one.**"

"That is the answer," she said. "You have got six things you could go and look at and you have not got one, and I have been in this street since before you were and I have watched four men stand about where you are standing and not one of them could have named a thing to go and look at. **A thing you will not name is a thing you will not go and look at, and that is the whole of what is wrong with you and it is not your handwriting.**"

She went. Nobody wrote it down, and the number of times that has happened stopped being written down in this book on the twentieth of May, and there has been no figure on it since, and there is not going to be one.

---

**And at about half past four on the same Monday, at a gate in a high wall, a man of forty-one on no list at all looked at a job that was as nearly his as any job has ever been in this district and decided in about four seconds that it was not his, and said so out loud, to nobody, in nine words, and it was the sixth time.**

"A card under a brick is a thing and not a job." He shifted the handcart out of the way for nobody. "**And there is a rule in this district that a rule holds by nobody testing it, and I have been the one not testing it since April, and I have got good at it in about two months and I have not told anybody and there is nobody to tell.**"

Nobody wrote that down either. **And the three things under the brick are three, and they have been three since the twenty-first of January, and the run-number card has three lines on the back of it and the line on the front has not been taken off, and the third of them is a boy's blank card that has been blank since the nineteenth of January, and nobody has taken it and nobody is going to.**

---

**The Field Book, Friday the twenty-second of May, 23.15, and Monday the first of June, 22.50, a front room, and there is an entry in this book on every night of this month and every night of last month, and I am going to keep that up and I am going to say here once, in the imperative, that the reason is not discipline.**

*Twenty-second, 23.15. One day off the twenty-first.*

*First, 22.50. One day off the thirty-first. Eleven nights, ten of them in a row across a month, and not one interval. **An interval of one day is not a pair whether or not either of the two days has anything on it, and I have now proved that eleven times in eleven days, and the list on the facing page still reads twenty-seven, and it is going to read twenty-seven on the first of June and it read twenty-seven on the twenty-fifth of April.** That is thirty-seven nights in this book without a gap and I have noticed the number tonight and I have not written down why I noticed it. I put the twenty-seven on the twenty-first of May and I have not put it since, and that is the only thing in this fortnight I have managed.*

*One. **A season that ended on Tuesday the fourteenth of April is a column that stops being printed, and eleven weather-driven seam events were forecast between the second of January and that Tuesday, and either eleven happened or eleven did not and this city cannot tell you which, and there is nothing whatever printed in Alderquay for the fifteenth of April and there has never been a second outlook, and a man of fifty-one in a room with a machine in it has been in that room nineteen years and has never known anybody who has seen a second one and cannot tell whether the next one does not exist or whether it exists and has not been sent. Nobody has asked for one in writing in six months. Nobody is going to ask this month and I have written that down tonight so that I cannot pretend in a year that I did not know that I was not going to.*** And the standing question, in three parts, and none of the three is about this district, and none of them has moved since the twenty-first of May and one of them is half answered: **why does a region the Registry enters as stable appear on a forecast of weather-driven seam events at all, when the only fact about it in this city is that nobody has ever been there to write down what happens in it; and why is there no second outlook, and who would print one, and is the absence of one a decision or an oversight; and what is it about an instrument that a person cannot produce which makes the thing it cannot produce the most important thing in a district.** I have a punch roll about nine inches long and a man of sixty-three with a flag, and that is half of the third of those, and half of an answer is not an answer, and I am not going to go round the district asking anybody the other half.

*Two. And the yard, and the question, and the not-answering. **Is any of it still true.** Nine things, and I read them out, and nobody answered, and then a woman of sixty-three with a hand barrow told me in one sentence at ten past one that a thing you will not name is a thing you will not go and look at, and that is the answer, and I have not put it in a yard and I am not going to and I have not improved it. And I have written down tonight, in my own hand, that I did not ask anybody to check one, and that it is the first time in seven months I have named a thing I could have done and did not do, and that naming it has not made it a column yet and I do not know what makes it a column and I have four people in this district who have told me and I have taken advice from none of them.*

*Three. And the blanks. **Eight now on the second side, and the twenty-seventh of May was the eighth, and the third of June and the tenth of June are the ninth and the tenth, and the sheet is right about all ten, and on every one of them the only person standing in the weather was a man of about sixty at a shed eleven minutes away who does not know a piece of paper exists.** On the fifteenth of April the sheet was right as well and about nine people in eleven streets stood in the weather for nothing at all, and that is the fourth thing in five months that cannot be shown to anybody, and a blank that is right about a day it cannot see is not a fifth thing and I wrote that down in May and I am writing it down again because it is still true and not because I need it to be. The twenty-eight words under the rule are undated and uninitialled and the man of about forty has read them and has not asked the man they are about and I have not asked either. **The coffee man has not been told and is not going to be. I have not gone near the north end of that row this month and I have not gone since the nineteenth of February. The plate in the grass is fifty-six days old and nobody has reported it and nobody has told the works and I have not said out loud that it was one of the eleven and I am not going to, and that is a standing position now and I have moved it out of a refusal and into a decision and I have written that down tonight and told nobody.***

*Four. And the third of the things I have not asked about. **A woman of sixty-eight on nothing is not named this month and I am adding zero references and the count stands at eight across eight chapters and I have said the count in a book in a coat and the book is the only place it exists, and I have not gone up three flights of stairs about her and she is not to be asked and her name is not written in this entry.** The woman on the fourth floor of Cedar Court is not in this month's account either, so that the two sixty-eights cannot be folded, and the woman of sixty on the second landing is a different and unnamed resident of the same building and is the ninth of twenty-six names and is not named. Old Mabb is asleep and is not woken. Ivy Marsden is not told anything and a page with her name struck through on it is on a bottom shelf and has not been read and is not going to be. The two school places lost in October are lost. A woman of fifty-four has a plot number and no bill in six years. **A conductor of thirty is in a printed rule book and his own hearing still has no date and nobody has sent him anything, and the man of fifty-four who is going to be asked in about four months what he saw has not been asked and it is two months and he has not been asked and I am not going to ask him, and that silence is ours now and it is not his and he has not given it to us, and I have not put any of that in a form and there is no form for it and the not-putting is the whole of the price.***

*Five. And what has not moved. **Ten remembered routes, ten, the original eight intact, none removed, all ten marked by somebody who is not me, and the ninth crossing's failure to cost one is still unexplained and nothing in these five weeks has touched it. I have not crossed since the twenty-second of December. The tally has nine lines in it, the paper is Hettie Brann's and the pocket is Dr Sen's, and I have not looked at it since the thirteenth of January and I am not going to look at it in June and I have now written that sentence down three times.** The two sensory losses did not come back in this fortnight and that is a fact and not a withdrawal, and it is a sound and a taste and no face and no place and no person and no name, and I have still decided nothing about whether it is one thing or two. The line of the twelfth of January stands and its condition is not the department's and the answer of the fourth of December stands behind it and it was used once in May as a shield and a woman of thirty-one said in a corridor that she does not know yet whether it is the shield or the gag, and she has not softened it and neither have I.*

*The page home the hundred and ninetieth day at the end of the first of June, blank, four marks in four hands and none of them mine, and I am not writing that it is there, and I have not written it since the eighteenth of May, and nobody was told that I had stopped and there is nobody to tell and that is the right way round. The token cold, a hundred and seventy-sixth day at the end of the first of June, and it is in the inside pocket of this coat and it has not been out of it since the seventh of December, and a street that opens because of something in a man’s coat is a place kept by that man, and I wrote that on the facing page of the sixth of December in my own hand and I have not improved on it and I am not going to.*

*And the standing position, which is the last thing in this book and is not going to improve: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.**
