# Chapter 94: Why There Is No Line

He wrote it on Saturday the fourteenth of March between about two and four in the afternoon at a kitchen table with a cold cup of tea beside him, and the cup is in the Field Book because the cold cup is in the Field Book every time, and because on the thirty-first of January a cup of tea was the whole of a sensory cost and it is not going to be the instrument twice.

It is on a piece of accountancy paper and it is headed in his own hand.

> **SECOND SIDE — DAYS WITH NO LINE**
> *Between the second of January and the fourteenth of April. This sheet does not say that a day is blank. It says that a day has no line on it and asks why.*

And it has the columns of the sheet of who does what and on which day, in the same order, because that is the sheet it is about and because he has never in his working life drawn a column that was not somebody else's.

> *Day. Weather. Whether the round went. Whether the gang went. Who stood in it. Who wrote it down. **Why there is no line on this day.***

That last one is the seventh and it is the whole of it, and it is not a question about the weather and it is not a question about a person. **It is a question about a practice, and there is no such question in this district that has ever been answered by anybody, and there is one now, in his own hand, on accountancy paper, in a kitchen, on a Saturday.**

---

He filled in two lines and he filled them in well.

> *Sat 21 Feb. Wind NE, dry. No. No. — . — . **The round does not go out at a weekend and the sheet goes with the round, and the sheet has a week on it that runs Monday to Sunday, and the sheet is not carried on the Saturday.*
> *Sun 22 Feb. Wind NE, dry. No. No. — . — . **Same as the Saturday and I am not writing the same words twice because a person who writes the same words twice has made a rule, and a rule about Saturdays is a schedule, and I said the price of that out loud in a yard nine weeks ago.***

He has said since that it took him about nine minutes for the frame and about two hours for the two lines, and that the two hours were the two hours in which he found out that the reasons were true, and that the two lines are the best two lines he has ever written about anybody's work, and that neither of them is about a day.

"I did not ask for them," he said, in a yard, on the Sunday afternoon, to about nine people, standing up, with the sheet in his hand. "I want to say that first because in this district that is now a thing that gets said, and it is true. **I did not ask Rue for the round's week. I did not ask the fishmonger what she did on a Saturday. I read the rule at the foot of her side, and the rule says a side with nothing on it is a day when nobody stood in the weather, and that rule is not true on five days in seven, and a rule that is not true on five days in seven is a rule that wants a footnote, and a footnote is a column.**"

**And a man of forty-one with a wet cap was in the yard and said the four words before he had got to the bottom of the page.**

"There is a why in it."

He stopped.

"Say that again," said Milo Rook, "and then I am going to do what I have done three times and I am going to ask you what you are talking about."

"I am not talking about anything. **There is a why in it and you are still holding it,**" said Doreen Sankey, "and you have not got to the bottom of the page and you have already said the word, and I have watched three men in this district go to the bottom of a page after they had already said the word, and the word is always the first thing anybody hears and never the second."

He put the sheet down on the trestle table face up and did not let go of it, which he has been doing since the first of February in a different yard, and about nine people stood and looked at it, and the second side of the map of this district was in a satchel in the yard and a man of about twenty-six came out of the satchel and looked at the form and did not say anything at all.

---

**And then he asked the only question he was entitled to ask, and it is the question, and about four people in that yard have said since that it is the first time a man of thirty-one has stood in a yard and asked a room whether anybody wants a thing.**

"**Who wants this?**"

Nobody said anything for about eleven seconds, and then the fishmonger said, from her own doorway at the top of the Row, where she had been listening for about four minutes:

"**I do not want it and I want to say why I do not want it, and I have been wanting to say why I do not want it since the second of February and I have not said it because I did not think anybody was asking.**"

"Go on."

"A rule at the foot of a sheet is for the people the sheet is about," she said. "That is all a rule at the foot of a sheet is. It is for the person whose hand it is going to be asked about. **And that is what I have understood since the second of February and I have not been asked to understand it by anybody, and it is the reason I put my initials on the column headed *wrong* and it is the reason he—"** and she moved her chin at the man of about twenty-six without looking at him, **"—put his initials on my side and not on mine, and I have never once asked either of them about it and I am not going to.** And your seventh column is a rule at the foot of a sheet for nobody, because there is not one person in eleven streets whose hand is going to be in the answer."

"Who is the answer for, then."

"**The answer is for a man in an office in about four years,**" said the fishmonger, "and I have known that since about the tenth of January and I have had a fish stall to run."

---

**And the arithmetic was done by the man with the wet cap, and it took him about forty seconds, and it is not the same arithmetic that was done in the first week of February, and about four people have said since that this is the second time in this district that a man who is on no list at all has been the person who worked out what a piece of paper costs.**

"You have written down, in your own hand, with a heading, that the round does not go out on a Saturday," said Doreen Sankey. "That is true. I have seen it. It is the truest thing anybody has put in a book about that round and I would not have said it in those words."

"Yes."

"**Then nobody has to be asked,**" he said. "That is the whole of it. There are six people in this district who could be asked to say that the round does not go out on a Saturday, and not one of them is going to be asked, and you have not asked one of them, and it does not matter, because you have written the sentence down. **The asking has already happened. You did it in advance, in a kitchen, in your own hand, under a heading, and it is sitting on a table in a yard in the rain, and in about four years the six people are going to remember that a man of thirty-one stood in a yard in March and held a piece of paper about their Saturdays.**"

"And if I take it off the table."

"**Then it is a man who had a form about other people's week and put it away,**" said Doreen Sankey, "and that is a different sentence and it is not a better one, and both of them are things a person can be asked about in about four years, and about four times a day somebody in this district looks at a job and decides it is not theirs, and you have taken this one and made it everybody's, and I do not know what the answer to that is and neither do you."

---

**And then Milo Rook said the thing that he had written down on the sixth of February in his own book in a coat that he is carrying, and he said it in a yard, in about eleven seconds, in front of about nine people, and it is the same sentence as the first of February and it is the third time he has said it and he had written down that he was not going to say it in a room again for about a year.**

"That is the seventh column," he said. "**A box that wants to know why is the amendment. That is what the amendment is, and I have now made one three times in ten weeks and a man of thirty-one in my own office said it to me in a corridor in January and I have written it down twice since and I have still done it three more times, and this time I have written down other people's week and not their names, which I thought was better, and it is not better, it is the same thing with the person taken out of it, and taking the person out is what I did in January and a foreman of thirty-one years found the page in a tin in a drawer nine hundred yards from this yard.**"

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds.

"I said on the sixth of February in this yard that I was not going to be the man who said that in a room again for about a year," he said. "It is the eighth of March. **I have broken that in this yard about four minutes ago, in front of the same people, and I have not got a reason and I am not going to make one, and I would like somebody to write down that I said I would not and then did.**

Nobody wrote it down. That was the eighth time in nine weeks and about four people noticed that nobody had, and one of them was the man of about twenty-six.

---

**And he did not tear it up and he did not put it in his coat, and the resolution of it took eleven seconds and it is the first one of his that has left a district without him.**

He put the sheet face up on the trestle table in the yard, in the rain, the way a man of about sixty took a clipboard off a wall in December and put it back exactly, and he squared it, which is a thing a man of thirty-one does and a woman with a satchel does, and he took his hand off it.

"Nobody wants it," he said. "**It is going to stay there. It is the wrong shape and about nine people have read it and it is going to be wet in about an hour and it is going to be off this table by Wednesday in a wind, and I am not going to put it in a pocket and I am not going to burn it and I am not going to give it to anybody, because giving it to somebody is a favour and I have had two of those this winter and one of them was a page in a tin.**"

"And if it stays," said Rue.

"Then it is in the district and not in me, and I have had three of those and every one of them has cost somebody else, and I have written down that I am frightened of this one, and I am not going to say the reason out loud in a yard and I am not going to put the reason in this book, and **that is the first decision I have made in ten weeks that is mine and it is a decision about a piece of paper and it is the first one of those I have ever been able to keep.**"

---

**And at about ten past four on the afternoon of Sunday the fifteenth of March, a man of about twenty-six in a high-visibility jacket came off the round, took the second side of the sheet of who does what and on which day out of a satchel, and put a line on it, in his own hand, with the date, and his initials against it, and wrote nothing else on it at all.**

> *15.iii. — standing in it. M.H.*

"**Nothing else,**" he said, to about nine people, when somebody asked. "It is a day and I was on it. That is the whole of what this side is for and there is no room on it for the reason and I am glad there is no room on it for the reason, and I am not going to say anything else about today."

The page home is the hundred and twelfth day of it and it is blank and it is going to be blank on the fourteenth of April, and nobody has asked anybody to fill it in since the twenty-seventh of December and this district is not going to ask, and that is a refusal and it is on the page.

---

**The Field Book, Sunday the fifteenth of March, 22.20, a front room, and the cup on the table is the second instrument and it worked and it is the fourth of these in six weeks.**

*Fifteenth, 22.20. Four days off the eleventh, and I have not written on the twelfth, the thirteenth or the fourteenth and those three days are the longest run of nothing in this book since February and I am not pairing them.*

*I have failed three times in ten weeks and this one was the fourth and it is the same one, and I am going to write the shape of it down one more time and then I am going to stop writing the shape of it down, because a thing you write down every time you do it is a thing you are proud of and I am not proud of this one and I have been very pleased with myself about it three times and that is the actual finding of the last ten weeks and it took me until about ten past four this afternoon to notice it. **The seventh column is not a mistake. The seventh column is the only thing I am good at, and I get to be good at it about four times a year, and every time I get to be good at it somebody else has to hold the paper.** That is the sentence. I am not going to improve on it and I am not going to write it in a yard.

*Three things happened this afternoon and all three of them are somebody else's and I was the reason for none of them.*

*One. A man of forty-one said four words before I had got to the bottom of the page. **There is a why in it.** And then he did the arithmetic, and the arithmetic is not the arithmetic that was done in the first week of February and it is worse, because in February the harm was that a form would say a round ran. **In March the harm is that a form says a round does not run, and that is true, and nobody is harmed by it today, and in about four years six people will remember being in a yard in March.** I did not ask. I have said the not-asking out loud in three yards in nine weeks and each time I have got smaller about it, and the shrinking is the thing, and I have not got a word for the shrinking.

*Two. A woman of forty-five, from her own doorway, said that a rule at the foot of a sheet is for the person whose hand is going to be asked about, and that my seventh column is a rule for nobody, and that the answer is for a man in an office in about four years, and that she has known that since the tenth of January and has not said it because nobody was asking. **Nobody was asking. She has been holding a sentence for sixty days waiting to be asked and the asking would have been me, and I have spent five months learning that the only question anybody asks me in a yard is what do you do, and I have answered it four times and it is the wrong question and she is the only one who has told me so.** And she said "I am not for anybody" in the first week of January and she has said it four times since and this afternoon she said the other half of it, which is that she is not for a man in an office either, and she has still not given me her name, and I am not going to ask for it, and I have written that down for the fourth time and I am not going to ask on the fourteenth of April either.

*Three. I broke a thing I wrote down. On the sixth of February, in this yard, in my own book, in a coat I am carrying: **I am not going to be the man who says that in a room again for about a year.** It is the eighth of March. I said it at about ten past four in a yard of about nine people, out loud, for the third time, and then I said out loud that I had promised not to and had done it, in the same yard, to the same people, and about four of them looked at each other, and nobody wrote it down, and that is the eighth time in nine weeks and I have stopped counting and I have started noticing, and noticing is worse.*

*And what I did with the sheet, which is the only thing this month I would write the word method next to, and I am not going to be thanked for it and I have not been: **I left it face up on a table in the rain and I did not take it and I did not give it away.** Three of mine have gone into a tin, into a coat, and into a pocket. This one is going to be off a table by Wednesday in a wind, or it is going to be wet and then dry and then off, and nobody in this district owns it and it is about six people's Saturdays and it came from me, and if anybody comes in in April and asks who wants it, the answer is that nobody did and it is on the record that I asked.*

*The page home the hundred and twelfth day, blank. Ten routes, ten. The token cold, ninety-eighth day. The tally has nine lines. I did not cross and I did not hold anything and I went nowhere near the north end of that row, and there is a poise on a beam scale at the north end that I have not written down four times now and I did not go and look this month and I have written down that I did not go and look, and that is a decision and I have not worked out whether it is a good one, and I am going to leave it exactly where it is until the first of April and I am not going to look.*
