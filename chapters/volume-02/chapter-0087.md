# Chapter 87: Twenty-Six Names, And One Of Them

She went on the Thursday afternoon of the fifth of February, at about half past two, up one flight, and she did not take anybody with her, and Milo Rook was told about it on the Friday morning in a yard by a woman of about thirty-four who put her hands flat on the table the way she does, and who is the named rateable head of a temporary accommodation household for two hundred and fifty-two people in a building that has no number, and who has two children of her own and who is not on the rate roll and never has been.

Lena Marr said three things in the yard, in this order, and about nineteen people were in it and nobody interrupted her, and she has said since that she had them in that order on the Friday morning before she went out of the door.

"One. I told her about the hearing on the twelfth of January and the twenty-six names and that one of the twenty-six is her, and that she is the ninth of the twenty-six, and that the ninth is the one I have told the fewest people about, and that I told her first and I have not told the other seven and I am not going to in a week."

"Two. She asked me three questions and I could answer one of them and I answered it straight and I did not dress it up. The three questions were: *is it in a file, can it be taken out, and can I see it.* And the answer to the first is yes and to the second is no and to the third is also no, and the second and the third are the same no, and I want to say why, because it is the worst part of the whole thing and it is in January's minute and it has a man's name on it.

"**In January this district won the only thing it has won in four months that nobody can take back on a Tuesday. A reporting officer wrote into a hearing that no schedule of persons resident at the withdrawn alignment is to be obtained from the district by that department, in that proceeding or in any proceeding, while my answer of the fourth of December stands. And it is permanent while it holds and he said he can deliver it for about two years.**

"And that is the reason a woman of sixty on a second landing cannot get a copy of the only piece of paper in this city that has her name on it. **Because the way she would get it is a schedule of persons, and schedules of persons are exactly what is now forbidden, and it is forbidden by us, and we asked for it, and I drafted the ask.**"

Nobody said anything.

"Three. She is not on anything else. Not on a rate book, not on a schedule, not on a roll, not on a list of a fire, not on anything. **She is on one piece of paper in a building in Crown Heights and she has been off every list for nine years, and she got off all of them on purpose, and in one afternoon in January a woman with a two-year note said twenty-six names out loud in a room with forty people in it and did not say whose they were, and I am that woman, and it is the same kind of thing and it is not the same size of thing and I have said so and she has said so and I would like it in a book that somebody has said so twice.**"

---

**And Milo Rook, who has a book, said the thing that nobody in that yard had thought of, and it took him about four seconds and it is the fourth time in five weeks that he has been the fastest person in a room and it is the last time he is going to be for a while.**

"That ruling is doing two jobs," he said. "**It is protecting two hundred and fifty-two people from a schedule and it is preventing one woman of sixty from finding out what is written about her. And both of those are true at the same time and neither of them is in the minute, because a minute records what a department agreed and not what a department's victory cost.**"

"Lena—"

"I am not criticising him," said Milo Rook. "He wrote a line with his own name on it and not the department's and he said he could deliver it for two years and he said in a hall that he might not be in the job in two years and that if he was not, somebody could look at him. That is the only kind of undertaking in this city that is worth anything and I have got it in my own record and I will have it on the fourteenth of April. **And I am saying the other half of it out loud in a yard, because in about four years there is going to be a file with twenty-six names in it and one line saying that nobody may ask for the list, and somebody in a room is going to read those two things next to each other and come away feeling very well, and one woman on a second landing is going to be sixty-four.**"

Rue said, "That is the seventh column."

"That is the seventh column," said Milo Rook, "and I have got it now, and I am not going to be the man who says it in a room again for about a year."

---

**What the woman of sixty said, and it is the whole of the room, and it is on the page because Lena Marr repeated it in a yard on the Friday morning word for word and would not improve it and said that was the condition on which she would repeat it.**

Lena said she would repeat it word for word or not at all, and the twenty-three words are these, and there is a version in Lena Marr's own hand in a pocket notebook that nobody has asked to see and that she has offered once and been refused once.

"**I have been off every list for nine years and I did it carefully and I did it on purpose and nobody in this building ever asked me why, and that has been the arrangement for nine years, and I would like it recorded that it has been an arrangement and not a difficulty.** And on the twelfth of January twenty-six names went into a file in Crown Heights and one of them is mine, and I have been on it for twenty-four days and I found out about nine days ago, and I want to say the thing that is the actual thing and it is not the file. The file does not bother me. **A piece of paper saying I was on a reach in December is not worse than me being sixty and living on a second landing. What bothers me is that I cannot get at it. I cannot see it, I cannot correct it, I cannot come off it, and I cannot ask anybody whether my name is spelled right.** That is nine years of being careful undone by one afternoon, and nobody in that room on the twelfth of January did it to me, and that is the part I am going to have to get used to, and I have not got used to it and I am not going to pretend to a woman in a yard that I have."

Then Lena said that there were two more things and asked whether the room would take them, and the room took them.

"The second is that you are going to come to about nine of these rooms," said the woman. "One at a time. And I know that because that is what you are, you are a woman with a plastic wallet and the school list, and I have seen you do it for about two years. **I am the first and I want you to know it is not the same as you coming here about a form, and I am not being kind to you, I am telling you that I noticed the difference and that I am the only one of the twenty-six who is going to notice it and that it is mine.**"

"And the third," said Lena Marr.

"The third is that you are the list," said the woman of sixty. "Twenty-six names, in your head and in that wallet, and you have not written it down, and you are not going to. And I know why, because I have been not written down for nine years and you are doing the version of it where you are the paper instead of me. **And it works, and it is not nothing, and there is a man in a basement in this city who has said in words that a body must not hold a copy of another body's record, and you have just been that body, and he is right, and one day somebody is going to ask you for that list in about four years and you are going to have to decide then whether you are a person or an office.** And I would like it recorded that a woman of sixty who is on nothing said that to you before you said it to yourself, and that she said it kindly, and that you are not to tell anybody she was kind about it."

---

**And Milo Rook wrote it down, and he wrote down that he was not in the room, and he wrote down the two sentences he is not going to improve on, and then he did the thing he has done three times in five weeks and he checked it.**

*Fifth, 21.00. I was not in that room. I have now been told about a room I was not in, twice, by two people, in two yards, and both of them told me different halves of it and both of them told it word for word, and neither of them asked me for anything, and I have written down that I would have liked to have been in it and that wanting to be in it is the seventh column and I am not going to.*

*A woman of sixty on a second landing, nine years off every list, on purpose, in an arrangement, and she cannot see the one piece of paper she is on and she asked whether her name is spelled right. And I got there in about four seconds, in a yard, on the Friday, out loud, in front of nineteen people, and what I got to was: **that a permanent line in a minute can be a protection and a gag at the same time and a minute will only ever print one of those two words.** And that is on the page and it is true and I have said it once and I am not going to say it in a room for about a year and I have written down here that I am not going to so that the not-going is a fact and not a mood.*

*The page home the seventy-fourth day, blank, three marks in three hands, and nobody has been asked to fill it in since the twenty-seventh of December and it is the seventy-fourth and it is going to be blank on the fourteenth of April. Ten routes, ten. The token cold, sixtieth day, and I have not taken it out, and the number sixty is the number of a woman on a second landing and I noticed that on the Friday afternoon and I have decided to leave it alone.*
