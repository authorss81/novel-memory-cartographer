# Chapter 105: A Column That Wants To Know

He wrote it on Saturday the eighteenth of April between about two and four in the afternoon at a kitchen table with a cold cup of tea beside him, and the cup is in the book because the cup is in the book every time, and because on the thirty-first of January a cup of tea was the whole of a sensory cost and it is not going to be the instrument twice.

It is on a piece of accountancy paper and it is headed in his own hand.

> **CO/6/BELL — MATTERS NOT DETERMINED**
> *Matters listed for determination on the continuation of the fourteenth of April and not determined. This sheet does not say a matter was refused. It says a matter was not determined and asks why.*

And then the six columns, in the order of the minute, because he has never in his working life drawn a column that was not somebody else's.

> *Matter. Determined. By whom. On what record. Put to the department. **Why this matter was not determined.***

That last one is the seventh and it is the whole of it, and **it is a question about a reason, and there is no such question in this city that has ever been answered by anybody, and there is one now, in his own hand, on accountancy paper, in a kitchen, on a Saturday, nine days after the sixth of March and about twenty-nine days after the fifteenth of March.**

---

He filled in two lines and he filled them in well.

> *1. Grounds of the making of the order. No. Reporting officer. Minute, para. 1. Yes. **A department that cannot say the grounds were wrong cannot say they were right, and this one has said in a room that it does not contend either way, and a form that wants to know why is asking a department to be wrong about one of the two halves it has already said out loud to thirty people.***
> *3. Removal of the sentence, no evidence was found of any hazard to persons at the service court. No. Reporting officer. Cannot account. Twice requested, twice acknowledged, not answered. **The withdrawal reference is not a district reference and is held in an office that has signed no name and produced no person in six months and has been asked twice, and the asking is the whole of the record and there is no box on this form for an asking.***

He has said since that it took him about nine minutes for the frame and about two hours for the two lines, and that the two hours were the two hours in which he found out that the reasons were true, and that the two lines are the best two lines he has ever written about anybody's work, and that **neither of them is a reason anybody can do anything with**, and that on the fifteenth of March he had written down the same discovery in different words and had been pleased with himself about the difference.

**And a works clerk of forty-six was in the quay section office at about half past eight on Monday the twentieth of April with a form in his hand, and he said the shape of it before Milo Rook had got to the bottom of the page.**

"You have got a column and it is the same column."

"I have got two columns about a matter and a sixth one and a seventh one."

"You have got a seventh one and I know what it says because I have had one a fortnight and a fortnight and a fortnight and I have watched four men in this city fill one in, and every single one of them did it in a kitchen, and you are the fifth." Cyril Sale did not put the form down. "**And you have got a name in it.**"

"There is not a name in it."

"There is a line under *put to the department* and you have put something in it about four inches from the edge of a page, and I have been in this office four years and I have watched about nine hundred forms come through and I can tell what a line under words is for, because there is one on a form of mine in a drawer and it is the only dangerous square inch in this building." He held the second sheet edge on. "Who did you put."

Milo Rook did not answer for about nine seconds.

"Nobody. **There is no name in it. I wrote *reporting officer*, which is an office, and an office is not a person.**"

"**An office is what you write when you have not decided to write a person.** And I am telling you the rest of it because I have got a query on line six that I have not answered twice and a box on the annual return that a machine has now asked me about twice, and I have not got the patience to watch a fifth one get made. A column that wants to know why wants a person to put their name against the why. **That is what it is for. There is no name on your page, Mr Rook, and you are about to write one, because you always write one, and in about four years the name on that page is the name of a woman of about thirty-one who drafted the paragraph that caused the third matter and has a two-year note, and you will have made her the officer responsible for a sentence nobody can produce.**"

Nobody wrote it down. That was the tenth time in thirteen weeks and about four people in that office noticed that nobody had, and one of them was a man of about twenty-six who was in the doorway about a sentence and went out again.

---

**And he did not tear it up, and he did not put it in his coat, and he did not leave it on a trestle table in the rain, and the reason he did not do the third of those is about nine words and he said them out loud to a man of forty-six at a desk at about ten past nine in the morning on Monday the twentieth of April.**

"The fifteenth of March was a yard and about nine people and a wind," he said. "That was the right resolution for that one and it took eleven seconds and it was the first decision I have made in about ten weeks that was mine. **I am not doing it again because I have now done the same thing five times and a man of thirty-four in a corridor in January told me in nine words that a box that wants to know why is the amendment, and I have written that sentence down twice and I have made five, and doing it a sixth time in a yard in the rain would not be a method. It would be a habit.**"

"So what are you going to do with it."

"**I am going to stop about a third of the way down.**"

He picked the pencil up, put the head of it in the seventh column of the first line, and then took it out again, and drew nothing, and put the pencil down on the desk edge.

"Two lines out of two, and the seventh column is headed and the first of them is headed and the second of them is not, and that is the whole of what is on this piece of paper, and it is a half-filled box."

"That is a document with a hole in it."

"That is **a half-completed box, and I have had that sentence in a file since January and I have never once been on the receiving end of it.**" He squared the sheet. "A half-filled box in this authority is not an exception and it does not generate anything. It is the exact opposite of an exception, because an exception has to be cleared and a half-filled box does not. **I have been making exceptions for six months and I have just made a hole instead, and a hole cannot be chased, and I want it noticed that the reason I did it is not modesty. The reason is that there is no row on this piece of paper for me, and I have been putting myself in a row five times, and there is not one, and I have finally noticed that.**

"And I am not going to say the rest of it out loud in this office and I am not going to put the rest of it in the book, and I would like somebody to read this paragraph back to me in July."

Nobody read it back. That was the eleventh time in thirteen weeks and about four people noticed.

---

**And the form went out of that office in a man's hand at about ten past ten in the morning on Monday the twentieth of April, and it did not go to a tray and it did not go to a drawer, and it did not go into anybody's file, and the reason it did not is a woman of sixty-one who has a rule in her own hand and a counter that shuts between one and half past one and about nine people in a room who had been told in advance how many pieces of paper are under it.**

"You are not taking that to the works," said Hettie Brann, at about ten past one in the afternoon, reading nine words of it upside down across a counter.

"No."

"**It is a document about a hearing that nobody can produce and it is half filled and it has got a seventh column on it with a heading and nothing in it, and if that goes into a tray in this building then in about four years a person with a box takes it out and reads the seventh column and finds it empty and has to decide what an empty seventh column means, and there is no right answer, and the wrong answer is a name.**"

"I know."

"You do not know, you have said the true thing out loud, which is a different thing." She put her hand flat on the counter. "I have told nine people in a room on the thirty-first of March that I would not make a third without saying so out loud, and I am going to say it out loud to about nine people in a yard on Wednesday morning at half past six, because it is a Wednesday and the coffee is not coming and there will be about nine of us there anyway. **It is the third. And it is not mine, and it is not about nine people's week, and it is a thing a man of thirty-one made in a kitchen in about two hours and could not finish.**"

---

**The Field Book, Monday the twentieth of April, 23.20, a front room, and it is four days off the sixteenth, and that is the sixth of the fours in this book, and five of them are since the eleventh of March, and I am not going to write that twice.**

*Twentieth, 23.20. Four days.*

*One. **The fifth time, and the same one, and I have written the shape of it down three times since the fifteenth of March and I have said I would stop and I have stopped.** The seventh column is not a mistake. The seventh column is the only thing I am good at, and I get to be good at it about four times a year, and every time I get to be good at it somebody else has to hold the paper. That was the sentence and I said I would not improve on it, and I have not, and I have now been wrong five times in thirteen weeks and about a man of forty-six told me the shape of it before I had got to the bottom of the page, and that is four times, and the district knows the shape of it before I finish the sentence, and I wrote that down myself in the last week of March as though it were a finding about a week sheet, and it was about me.*

*Two. And what I did about it, and it is new, and it is not a resolution: **I did not finish it.** A heading in a column and a line under words and two of the six columns filled and the seventh column headed and the first of the two headed and the second of the two not, and a man of forty-six in four rooms' time told me that a half-filled box is the exact opposite of an exception because an exception has to be cleared and a half-filled box does not. **I have been making exceptions for six months and I have made a hole, and a hole is not a resolution, it is the withdrawal of a hand, and I have not got a word for the difference and I have been using the word method about things since the fifteenth of March and this one is not method, it is stopping.** And the reason I stopped is not modesty and I said so out loud, and the reason is that there is no row on that piece of paper for me and there has not been one since the first of February and I have been putting myself in a row five times.*

*Three. And the man of forty-six, who has had a box of his own asked about twice by a machine and a line of his own asked about once by a person, and who refused to fill a risk box with a nil in March because a nil there is a lie about the twenty-second of December: **he told me in about nine seconds that a column that wants to know why wants a person to put their name against the why, and that there is no name on my page, and that I am about to write one, and that in about four years the name on that page is the name of a woman of thirty-one who drafted the paragraph that caused the third matter, and I would have made her the officer responsible for a sentence nobody in this city can produce.** I have written it down word for word because I cannot do it justice and I am not going to try. He is right. I have never once in six months considered that the person who ends up under a heading is not me and is not a department and is a woman who has been in two rooms in six months.*

*Four. And the condition. I wrote on the second of February, in a yard, in my own hand, in a coat I am carrying: *I am not going to be the man who says that in a room again for about a year.* It is the twentieth of April, which is seventy-seven days after the second of February, and I said it out loud in a yard on the fifteenth of March and asked for it to be written down and nobody wrote it down, and I have not said it in a room since. **I have written down that the not-saying is now a practice and not an undertaking, and a practice is a thing a man of about twenty-six in a high-visibility jacket put next to a collection in February, and I am not going to improve on his nine words with my seventy-seven days.** Nobody wrote it down again today either. That is the eleventh time in thirteen weeks and I have stopped counting the weeks and started counting the times, and counting the times is a column and I know what columns are.*

*The page home the hundred and forty-eighth day, blank, and it is there. Ten routes, ten. The token cold, a hundred and thirty-fourth day, and it has been out of the coat for six months. The tally has nine lines and I have not crossed and I have not held anything and I have not gone in anywhere since the twenty-second of December, and on Tuesday I stood up in a room for about two minutes and on Monday I filled in two lines of a form in a kitchen and I have not held anything either time and I have written down that I have started to notice the difference between those two sentences and I have not decided what the difference is.*
