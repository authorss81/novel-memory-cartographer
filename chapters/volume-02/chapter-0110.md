# Chapter 110: What Is On The Schedule Now

On Friday the twenty-fourth of April a man of about forty-one with a wet cap walked into a room above a canteen on Parcel Street at about four in the afternoon and asked a question, and nobody sent him, and about four times a day somebody in this district looks at a job and decides it is not theirs, and on this occasion the somebody was a man who is on no list at all and who has been in that district for nine years and has not been thanked once.

The man behind the counter is a clerk in the timetable division of a tram authority, nineteen years in the job, with a list of nine items in front of him, and there is no first name for him anywhere and there is not going to be one.

"Mr Rook has asked me three times in six months and I have said no twice and yes once," he said. "**What I want to know is whether you have been sent.**"

"Nobody has sent me."

"Then sit down and I will say one thing and then I am going back to my nine items, and I am not going to be asked anything else, and if you are asked anything else by anybody in this building you are to say that the timetable division does not discuss working timetables outside the department."

"There is a column for run number and there is a column for depot move," said Barrow, "and they are not the same column and they have never been the same column, and a number in the run column is a crew booked to a run, and a number in the move column is a crew going to a gate. **A booked run goes out and comes back and it takes people. A depot move goes out and comes back and it takes nobody, and it is a light engine and a two-car set, and it is on the sheet because the cars have to be accounted for and not because anybody is in them.**"

"So the number on the card."

"**Is in both columns on that page, because it is the same crew, and the occurrence in the run column is the 07:52 booked working off Crown Heights on the morning of Tuesday the sixteenth of December, and that is the car that went round, and you have known that since January.**" He turned a page over and did not read it. "And the other occurrence is a light engine and a two-car set going to a gate at a quarter to eight, and it has no run in it and no passenger in it and it is not the thing anybody is looking for, and **the man who wrote that card down in a yard in January wrote down two things and chose neither, and one of the two is not it, and I am telling you which one so that you can stop.**

"And I am telling you one more thing and then I am going back to my nine items. In nineteen years I have never once seen a crew entered in the run column for a morning when the car did not go out. Not once. If a car does not go out the column is empty and the row is not there, because **a crew that does not work does not happen.**"

"You have never asked me what I am looking for."

"I have not, and I am not going to, and if you tell me I will decline to identify a document in my custody and I have got quite good at that in the last three days because somebody else in this building has been saying it in a room and it is going round." He put his pen down. "**You are asking me about a morning in December and I am a clerk and the sixteenth of December is a hundred and thirty days ago and there is a thing in this city that does that to people and I have watched it happen to about four of them since 1971, and the four of them were not in this building, and I am not going to be a fifth of anything.**"

"Thank you."

"Do not thank me. **If you want to know anything else, ask nobody, and do not come here, and do not send the man from the round, and if you do any of those three things then I will say that the timetable division does not discuss working timetables outside the department and it will be true and it will be in a book and it will keep.**"

**And the card went back under the brick with a third line on the back of it in a hand that is not Milo's and is not Hester Coombe's, and the line on the front of it was not taken off, and there were three things under that brick, and there had been three things under that brick since the twenty-first of January, and there are three things under it now.**

> *Checked by the timetable division. The run column is the 07:52. The move column is a light engine and a two-car set to a gate and it is not it. Not for index. — B.*

**He has not written it down. That is a decision and it is the first decision he has made in seven months that is not a shape, and he has written that down instead, and the two are not the same and he has said so.**

**And the question, and it is a Saturday, and it is the same question as on the first of January with eleven things on it, and it is not answered and nobody in this district is going to answer it.**

Four pages from an office that signed no name say that between the second of January and the fourteenth of April there will be eleven weather-driven seam events in this city, and the season ended on Tuesday the fourteenth, and **there is nothing whatever printed in Alderquay for the fifteenth, and a man of fifty-one in a room with a machine in it has been in that room nineteen years and has seen one seasonal outlook and has never known anybody who has seen a second and cannot tell whether the second one does not exist or whether it exists and has not been sent, and he is not the man who can answer that and nobody in this city is.**

And a region of eleven square miles carries a typed zero in a printed row, on the stated ground that there is no maintained seam, no recorded loss, no resident witness and no schedule, and all four of those are true, and **a place is not stable because nothing has happened in it, and a place is entered as nil because there is nobody writing down what happens in it, and those two facts look identical on a card out of a machine and they are not the same fact, and the second one is worse.**

So the question, and it is sharper than it was in January, and it has three parts and none of them is about this district:

*Why does a region the Registry enters as stable appear on a forecast of weather-driven seam events at all, when the only fact about it in this city is that nobody has ever been there to write down what happens in it?*

*And why is there no second outlook, and who would print one, and is the absence of one a decision or an oversight — and a man in a room has been in that room nineteen years and has never once been in a position to know?*

*And what is it about an instrument that a person cannot produce which makes the thing it cannot produce the most important thing in a district, and why does it take a woman of sixty-one at a counter to notice that a department and a weather season have the same day and a man of thirty-one to notice that a list and a minute are different objects — and neither of those is about a seam, and both of them are about who is allowed to author a place, and it is the same question, and it is not going to be answered in April, and it is going to be answered in about a year and a half, and I do not know how, and I have written that down so that I cannot pretend in a year that I do.*
