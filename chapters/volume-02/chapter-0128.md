# Chapter 128: The Last Ordinary Day

On Friday the twelfth of June, at about ten past ten in the morning, the weekly sheet for the week of the eighth went into the rack in the quay section office, the duplicate went into a tin, and the duplicate went into a second tin on the bottom shelf of the plan room of the Civic Atlas Office under a repair schedule, and **that tin now holds twenty-six weeks of gang carbon and one page that is not a gang sheet.**

He has written the figure on the last day of the week for six weeks running. He is not going to write it again and he knows that the way he knows the other things in this book, which is to say he knows it and does not act on it.

---

**An ordinary Friday, in order, and the whole of it took about nine hours and none of it was a decision.**

At half past seven the gang was on the back length, as it is on Mondays and Thursdays, eleven men, a foreman, a shovel each and the trolley, six hundred yards, and it does not walk on a Friday because a Friday is the day the sheet goes into a rack. The weekly sheet for that week has two dates in it, the eighth and the eleventh, and the reason column is empty on both and the condition column is empty on both, **and the one on Thursday the eleventh of June was the twentieth walk since the first of April with an empty condition column.** The cast-iron cover has been flush since the sixth of April, and there is no form in this city that will take a sentence about a fault that stopped.

The round did not go, and it does not go on a Friday, and it has been a Monday and a Thursday round since the second of January, and the boy of seventeen was in the district on the Thursday and it was thirty-nine minutes late and nobody has written a line about it anywhere except a book in a coat.

At about half past nine the second side went out in the satchel for a week that runs Monday to Sunday, and it is correct.

At about eleven a man of fifty in a room with a machine in it transferred five figures out of the allocation sheet with a pen, the way he has for nineteen years. **The fourth column has been filled in three times in nineteen years and the line under the words *officer responsible* has never been filled in at all, and the forecast column is still not typed, and a forecast does not come down because a thing happened, and there is nothing whatever printed in this city for the fifteenth of April and there has never been a second outlook.**

At about one, a letter went into the Friday post, and it was a letter about a length, and it was correct.

At about half past two a works clerk of forty-six read two of six columns on a weekly sheet and did not read the other four, which is what he does four times a day.

At about four, at a dispensary counter at the water clinic, a woman of sixty-one finished four returns and did not stop.

At about half past four, at a gate in a high wall, a man of forty-one on no list at all looked at a job and decided in about four seconds that it was not his, and said so out loud, in nine words, and it is the eighth of those nine words and I have never written down what the counting is for.

And at about half past five, in a doorway on the low side of eleven streets, a woman of sixty-three with a hand barrow said a thing that had been in a form of his since the sixth of April, and he did not answer it, and it is the last thing he gave up in six months and he gave it up on purpose.

---

"**Somebody should tell the works,**" said Nell Crale, "**that there is a plate off a fence.**"

He did not say anything for about four seconds.

"It is a plate off a fence," he said.

"It has been a plate off a fence since the sixth of April and the sixth of April was a Monday and I found it at about eleven and there are four doors on that street and all four of them shut and all four of them are correct, and I have not told the works and I am not going to, because I have a barrow and I am sixty-three and a plate is not my plate." She shifted the barrow. "**I am not asking you what it was. I have not asked you what it was and I am not going to, and I have asked you exactly one thing in four months and I have got nothing and I have not been after you about it, and this is the one thing I am after you about and it is not what it was.**"

"Nell."

"I am not going to stand in a doorway in the rain for this." She went. "Tell them or do not tell them. It is a plate and it is a fence and it is a field and I have a barrow."

---

**He did not tell the works, and it had been a decision since the first of June and it was a decision before she said it and it was a decision after she said it, and being asked about it in a doorway did not make it into one, and he wrote that down on the following night in his own book in his own hand, which is the only place it exists.**

**A refusal cannot be produced. A decision can.** A refusal is a thing a person did or did not do and there is nothing on any paper in any building in this city that says he declined to tell the works about a plate. A decision is a thing a person intends, and a thing a person intends can be described by whoever asks him what he intends, and what he intends is: **that plate is never reported, that the works is never told, and that nobody in this district ever says out loud that it was one of the eleven, and that is final, and it was carried and not improved from the night of the first of June to this one, and it is not because I have found out that it was not one of them, and it is not because nobody has asked me, and it is because there is nowhere to put the answer and the only place it could go is a form and a form is a document.**

Nobody has been told that he has decided it. He told a man of forty-one at a gate, in nine words, in May, that there was a card under a brick; he has not told anybody that there is a decision about a plate, and there is no form on which the decision could sit, and the deciding of it is the only thing about it that is on paper anywhere and the paper is in a coat in a front room.

**That is the price, and it is not curable, and he chose it, and nobody asked him to choose it.** For seven months he has been looking for a place where a thing cannot be produced against anybody, and he has built six, and every one of them is a place, and a place can be looked in. This one is not a place. This one is a thing he has decided, and a decided thing is a fact about a man, and a fact about a man is the easiest thing in the world to get hold of.

---

**And on Saturday the thirteenth of June the far side of the fence at the low end is going to be cut, and the boundary there is a hundred and forty feet and eleven inches, and there is nobody on the far side to stop anybody going over it and there has not been for six years, and nobody is going to go over it, and the standing condition on that fence has not changed and has not been improved and is not going to be in the fourth year.**

**And on Monday the fifteenth of June the gang walks, and on Friday the nineteenth of June the sheet goes into a rack in a building where a man of fifty-one has been in a room with a machine in it for nineteen years, and on Wednesday the twenty-fourth of June a reply falls due on a tram authority's return for the quarter ending the thirtieth, and it is going to want a number of persons carried on the working of the ninth, and the district is not going to supply it, and the item will be returned as not stated, and the quarter will close with the item standing open, and there is no form in this city on which any of that can be said to anybody.**

**And there has never been a second outlook, and a man of fifty-one in a room with a machine in it cannot tell whether the next one does not exist or whether it exists and has not been sent, and nobody has asked for one in writing in six months, and nobody is going to ask in June, and there is a district stability column in this city with a place entered as nil on the stated ground that there is no maintained seam, no recorded loss, no resident witness and no schedule, and that is the third nil about it, and it is not going to be a fourth, and nobody has gone there.**

---

**The Field Book, Friday the twelfth of June, 23.30, a front room, one day off the eleventh, and this is the last ordinary working day of this district's year as far as I am concerned and I have not decided that, the week has, and I am not going to improve the sentence.**

*Twelfth, 23.30. One day off the eleventh.*

*One. **An ordinary Friday, in order, and none of it was a decision: the gang at half past seven on the Monday and the Thursday with two dates on a sheet and the condition column empty on both, the second side out at about half past nine and correct, five figures transferred with a pen at about eleven in a room with a machine in it, a letter about a length into the Friday post at about one, two columns read out of six at about half past two, four returns finished at about four at a dispensary counter, a gate at about half past four, and a doorway at about half past five.** That is the whole of what this district produces in a day and it has been producing it since the second of January and it is not nothing and it is not going to improve, and a district that cannot produce a document is a district that cannot be instructed, and a district that cannot be instructed has to be in the street in the morning, and in the street in the morning is the only thing it has got.

*Two. **And the thing I gave up, which was given up on purpose, and it is the last thing, and it is a plate.** A woman of sixty-three with a hand barrow asked me in a doorway at about half past five on Friday to tell the works that there is a plate off a fence, and she said she was not asking me what it was and had not asked me and was not going to, and that she was asking for one thing in four months and had got nothing and was not after me about the other thing. **And I did not tell the works, and the deciding of that was done in my own hand on the first of June and it was in a book in a coat before she opened her mouth, and a refusal cannot be produced and a decision can, and I have spent seven months building places where a thing cannot be produced against anybody and I have built six and every one of them is a place, and this is not a place, this is a fact about a man, and a fact about a man is the easiest thing in the world for anybody to get hold of.** I chose it. Nobody asked me to. It is not because I have found out it was not one of them, and it is not because nobody has asked me, and it is because there is nowhere to put the answer and the only place it could go is a form. **Nobody in this district has been told that I have decided it, and there is no form on which the decision could sit, and I am not going to describe it in a yard, and it is the only one of the four things in five months that has been made into a decision and the other three have not and they are not going to be.**

*Three. **The run number, and where it has got to, and it has not got anywhere.** It is a card under a brick in eleven streets with three lines on the back of it, the third in a clerk's hand, and the line on the front has not been taken off, and there are three things under that brick and there have been three since the twenty-first of January, and the third of them is a boy's blank card that has been blank for four months. The number belongs to a crew and not to a car, and the occurrence of it in the run column on the morning of Tuesday the sixteenth of December is the 07:52 booked working off Crown Heights, and the occurrence in the depot-move column is a light engine and a two-car set going to a gate, and it takes nobody and it is not it. **A clerk with nineteen years in the timetable division said the standing thing: he has never once seen a crew entered in the run column for a morning when the car did not go out; if a car does not go out the column is empty and the row is not there, because a crew that does not work does not happen. And I have now worked out where that sentence stops and it stops at a hand, because that sentence is about an entry and not one hand has been near any of it, and a car did go out on the sixteenth of December and it came back, and what did not happen is that it got to the end of the working, and the end of the working is a place that is not on the diagram.** So the row is printed, because the book is printed from a list and a printed row is a plan and a plan is not a record, and four characters are against the working because four characters are what the list says, and a list of a hundred and forty years ago has never been queried by anybody in this city. **And the standing thing is still the standing thing and the row is still in the book, and both of those are true, and a column does not check, and the second of those three is the whole of what I have got this month.** That has not moved in seven weeks and it is not going to move this week, and nobody has been sent, and the rule holds by not being tested, and I have not tested it, and the man of forty-one has not tested it, and there is one thing left in this district that nobody has looked at and I have not looked at it and I am not going to write in this book that I am going to look at it, because that would be a plan and a plan is a reason.

*Four. And the days and the numbers, carried and unmoved and not improved. **The walks in these ten days are the twenty-fifth and the twenty-eighth of May and the first, fourth, eighth and eleventh of June, and there is one more on Monday morning and none on this Friday, and the condition column is empty on every one of them, and there is no column on that sheet that wants to know why a column is empty.** The racks are the twenty-second and the twenty-ninth of May and the fifth and the twelfth of June, four Fridays, and the second tin has gone twenty-three, twenty-four, twenty-five, twenty-six, and I have written every one of those figures and I said on the fourth of June that I was not going to write any more of them and I have written two. **The blank Wednesdays are the twenty-seventh of May and the third and tenth of June, the eighth, the ninth and the tenth, and the sheet is right about all ten, and there are four things in five months in this district that cannot be shown to anybody, the fourth of them being the fifteenth of April, and there is no fifth and none of the ten blanks is one of them.** Eleven men and a foreman and a clipboard. A round one man short and thirty-nine minutes late on a Thursday. A boundary of a hundred and forty feet and eleven inches, one end a corner and the other a fence, and a pin in a hole in a road that nobody owns. A plate sixty-seven days in the grass.

*Five. **The page home the two hundred and first day, blank, with four marks in four hands and none of them mine, and the fourth of the four was written on the first of April at a counter in about four seconds by a woman who was not asked and refused to put it in an envelope, and it is a line somebody else can walk me home by, and that is all it is, and I have not written that down tonight in case the writing of it is the thing that has kept it alive.** Ten remembered routes, ten, the original eight intact, none removed, all ten marked by somebody who is not me, and the ninth crossing's failure to cost one is still unexplained and I have not crossed since the twenty-second of December and I am not going to in June. The tally has nine lines and the paper is Hettie Brann's and the pocket is Dr Sen's and I have not looked at it since the thirteenth of January. **The token cold, a hundred and eighty-seventh day, and it is in the inside pocket of this coat, and a street that opens because of something in a man's coat is a place kept by that man, and I wrote that on the facing page of the sixth of December in my own hand and I have not improved on it and I am not going to.** The two things I have lost that have no name on them are a sound and a taste, and the sound came and went this month in a clinic for about four seconds, and it did not come back, and no face and no place and no person and no name has come with it, and I have still not decided whether it is one thing or two and I decided that in the first week of January and I have not improved on it in five months. I have added nothing in these ten days about a woman of sixty-eight on nothing and the count stands at eight.

*And the standing position, which does not improve and which is the last line I am going to write in this book for a while: **the writer of the map is not the maintainer of anything, and that is a list of about fourteen other people and not one of them has been asked to be on it.**
