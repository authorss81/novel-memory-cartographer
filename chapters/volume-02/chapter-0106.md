# Chapter 106: Nobody Else

The rule at the foot of the envelope is in Hettie Brann's own hand and it is one line and it is a conjunction, and it has been on the paper since the nineteenth of January, and Milo Rook had not understood it until the afternoon of Tuesday the twenty-first of April.

> *First working day of the month — the four copies are taken out of the four places and looked at, the version and the date are compared and nothing else is read. If one of them is not the same, **ask the person whose hand it is and nobody else.** The holder is not M.Q. and is not M.R. and will not be.*

He read it in a corridor in Crown Heights in the third week of March and took the first half of it and left the second half, and he has written down that he left it, and that he left it because the second half is about four people he does not know the names of.

"**It is not about four people,**" said Hettie Brann, on the Tuesday, at the counter, at about ten past one in the afternoon, with a piece of accountancy paper face up in front of her and a pencil in her hand. "It is about a hand."

---

"Start again," she said. "You have got a thing on the table and I have read about nine words of it upside down and there is a heading on it and there is a seventh column and there is nothing in the seventh column, and I am not going to guess at the rest, and I want you to tell me what is on it in about nine seconds and then I am going to do something I have told nine people I would not do."

"It is a form about a hearing that was not determined on the fourteenth of April. Six columns. The last one is headed *why this matter was not determined.* Two lines, and the second of the two does not have a heading in the seventh column because I did not get to it."

"And who is it for."

"It is not for anybody. It is a—"

"**I asked you who it is for.**"

"Reporting officer," said Milo Rook. "Which is an office."

"**It is not for him, and you know it is not for him, and that is the whole of why it is on my counter and not in a tray, and I am going to do the thing now and I am going to do it in front of about nine people on Wednesday morning and you are not going to be thanked for it and I am not either and I would like that said in the same breath.**"

She put it face down under the counter, on top of two others.

"That is three," she said. "One from the thirty-first of January, which is a piece of paper about a man with no rateable unit. One from the twenty-second of March, which is nine doors and belongs to a nurse of twenty-three and is not mine. And this one, which is a man's, and which is not a document, and which I have read about nine words of. **I said in a room on the thirty-first of March that I would not make a third without saying so out loud, and I am saying so out loud at a counter, and the room is going to be a yard at half past six on Wednesday morning because it is a Wednesday and the coffee is not coming and there will be about nine of us there anyway, and you are not going to be there and I am not going to explain why.**"

"I will be there."

"You will not, and if you are, I will hand it back to you in front of everybody and you will have to take it, and that is not a threat, it is a procedure, and the procedure is that **the moment a thing can be used it is in use**, and if a surveyor of about thirty-one is standing in a yard when a woman of sixty-one puts a third piece of paper under a counter then that piece of paper is now about a surveyor, and it will be in a yard in about four years, and somebody will ask a question about it that is a question about you.**"

---

**And he did not go, and the reason is that he understood the rule for the first time at about four minutes past two that afternoon, standing at a counter in a front room, and it is a thing he has had in his own book since the seventh of January in the imperative and has never read as an instruction.**

*Ask the person whose hand it is and nobody else.*

The hand is his. **The rule has been in a drawer in four buildings since the nineteenth of January and it has been about four people he does not know the names of, and it is a rule about custody, and custody is a thing a person holds and not a thing a person puts under a counter or in a tin or in a coat.**

*Not a tin. Not a coat. Not a table in the rain. Not an office. Not a drawer in his own office that a man of thirty-one signed for in December and has never opened.*

He stood there and the counter was shut and the room was empty and Hettie Brann was washing a pen.

"You have gone the colour you go," she said, without looking up. "Say it or do not, and I am not going to help you and I have got four returns and a bag."

"**I have had a page in a tin on a bottom shelf in a plan room in my own office since the thirteenth of January,**" said Milo Rook, "and a form with about nine days of another woman's week in it in the inside pocket of this coat, and a form with my own week in it in a shed drawer in Crown Heights, and a card under a brick in a yard, and a piece of accountancy paper with a fourth copy of a route home on it in your satchel, and I have not got one of them in my hand, and about four of those are in a building I walk into every morning."

"**And what is in this coat right now.**"

"A brass token with the name of a street this city does not have on it, and it has been in this coat since the seventh of December and it was cold when I put it in and it is cold now and I have not taken it out once in six months, and the coat is going to be the reason for one or two things that are not in my book and I have written that down three times without writing the other thing down."

"Then it is in your hand," said Hettie Brann, "and I am not going to say another word about it, and you are not going to tell anybody what I said, and that is the arrangement and I have made arrangements before."

---

**And the changed method is one line long and it is not his and it is not new, and it has been in a drawer in four buildings since the nineteenth of January, and it is the second half of a conjunction.**

"Ask the person whose hand it is and nobody else."

"That is the whole of the changed method and it has cost me seven months and it is on a piece of accountancy paper in four buildings and nobody has ever read it out loud to me," said Milo Rook, in a yard, on the Wednesday morning of the twenty-second of April, at about ten past six, to about nine people, having not been there on the Tuesday and having been asked nothing and having sent nothing and having told nobody he was coming, and about four of the nine looked at him when he said the number of months out loud.

**And the recovery is a counter, and it is not his, and the thing he gets back is not a fact and not a document and not an instrument, it is a place to put a thing in that nobody can use, and the whole of what he has learned in six months arrives in his own hand on Tuesday the twenty-first at about nine at night in a front room, and it is the first line of this entry and it is the only sentence in it that he is not going to improve on.**

*Twenty-first, 22.50. One day. **I am not pairing it, and I am writing the entry anyway, and the reason I have never been able to work out why is that I have been confusing an entry and a pair since December and this afternoon I have written the difference down and it took a woman of sixty-one about nine words to do it and four months to notice the rule and seven months to read it as an instruction.** An entry is a day. A pair is an interval. A day is not a chronology and an interval is not a calendar, and a rule that says ask the person whose hand it is and nobody else is not about four people, it is about custody, and I have had five things in five places in six months and not one of them in my own hand.*

*One. The method, and it is the volume's and I did not find it. **Custody is a thing a person holds. Not a tin, not a coat, not a table in the rain, not an office.** And a half-filled box under a counter cannot be chased and cannot be produced and cannot be given a reference, because a counter is not a repository and this counter has a rule on it that is a sentence and a holder who is not the person whose hand it is, which is the most wrong-shaped arrangement in this district and she made it on purpose and she made it in about four seconds and she has now made three of them and she has said so out loud and she is not going to be thanked.*

*Two. And the other half of it, and I am not going to put this in a yard and I have not put it in a yard. **I have a brass token in the inside pocket of this coat that has the name of a street this city does not have on it, and it has been there since the seventh of December, and a street that opens because of something in a man's coat is a place kept by that man, and I wrote that sentence on the facing page of the sixth of December in my own hand and I have not improved it and I am not going to.** And this afternoon a woman at a counter told me that a thing in my hand is in my hand, and I have not been able to think about anything else since about ten past four, and I am not going to write down what I did about that, and I want it in this book as a fact and not as a resolution, because a resolution is a thing I do when I have worked it out and I have only worked out that I am not going to say it out loud on a Wednesday morning to about nine people.*

*Three. And a form that is not a document. Six columns and a seventh with a heading in it and two lines, and the second line has no heading because I did not get to it, and a man of forty-six told me in about nine seconds that a half-filled box is the exact opposite of an exception, and a woman of sixty-one has now put it under a counter with two other things and has counted them out loud. **On Wednesday morning at half past six I am going to say out loud that it is the third, and about nine people are going to hear me say it, and that is a cost and it is hers and she named it in advance and she paid it in advance like a woman who knows exactly what she is doing.** Nobody has thanked her and nobody is going to.*

*Four. And the tin, and the coat, and the shed, and the brick, and the satchel, and I have now written them out in a list in a book in a coat and a list of my mistakes is a form with a column about me, and I am going to do it anyway because the alternative is that I do not know where my own papers are, and **being right about where they are is the same as being able to produce them and I have just been told by a woman at a counter that those are two different sentences and I have believed her on the strength of a rule about envelopes.**

*Five. And what I have not done. I have not asked her about the seventh of January. I have not told her about the season. I have not turned round in a yard and put a column on anything. I have not gone near the north end of that row. I have not asked a woman of sixty-three about her mother's boy. I have not asked a reader of fifty-four for a document. **I have written all six of those down and the list is a list of things I have not done and it is the only list in this book that is about me and it is the worst object in the whole of six months, and a man of about twenty-six in a high-visibility jacket signed one line on the second side of a sheet in the middle of February and wrote nothing else on it at all, and he is right and I have been trying to do it with six lines for six months.** The page home the hundred and forty-ninth day, blank. Ten routes, ten. The token cold, a hundred and thirty-fifth day, and it is in my hand and not in a tin and not in a coat drawer and not in a shed, and that is the sentence for tonight and I am going to let it be the last one.*
