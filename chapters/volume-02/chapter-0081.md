# Chapter 81: Nobody Can Name The Day

The clinic counter was open from eight until six for seven days and the round went out at eight and came in at seven for seven days, and both of those were decided by one person each, and neither of them was decided for money, and by the Wednesday of that week the two of them had been decided for five days and were about to be tested for two more and then they would stop, because a thing that stops is not a thing that failed.

What the seven days were for could not be written down anywhere, and the reason was already on a table in a room in Crown Heights and had been since the twenty-eighth of December, and there was no register in this city that the four pages could be entered in and there had never been a proposal to make one.

The four pages said: the first of the eleven is expected in the last week of January. They said it is the week of Monday the twenty-sixth. They said the remainder are unallocated and allocated on the day. And they did not say the day, and there was nowhere in them to say the day, and nobody in Alderquay has ever been able to make a four-page document say a day it was not going to.

**So the week went on without a day in it, and the two women who had put seven days into it were the only two people in the district who knew that, and neither of them was told which day to be ready for, and neither of them has said since that she would have preferred being told.**

The weather did nothing for four days. It was cold and it was bright and the wind was out of the north-east and stayed there, and the Cardew Street frontage had standing water on it that was ice by the Tuesday morning, and about nine people commented on the cold and nobody commented on anything else, and the round went out at eight and in at seven and the counter was open and both of those were the correct behaviour of a thing that could not be dated.

---

**And then it came, and there is nobody in this district who can say what time it started raining, because every one of the eleven men who walked that length at seven in the morning had been asleep at two, and the whole of the finding is in that.**

The gang was on the back length at half past seven on the morning of Thursday the twenty-ninth of January and eleven men went down six hundred yards with a shovel each and a trolley between them and a clipboard, and it was nineteen degrees below freezing at the water stairs and the trolley wheels had a noise on them that morning that nobody had heard before and about four people have said since that they would have heard the trolley in a wind and there was no wind.

The sheet for that week has two dates in it, the twenty-sixth and the twenty-ninth, and two entries in the hours column, and the reason column is empty on both of them, and it went into the rack at about ten in the morning on Friday the thirtieth with the week before it, and the duplicate went into a tin at the end of a corridor, and the duplicate went into a second tin on the bottom shelf of a plan room in another building, under a repair schedule, and nobody in this city will ever look at any of it again except a foreman checking his own March, and that is the correct state of it.

At about twenty-five minutes past seven the man of about twenty-six stopped in the down loop, which is what he does, and this time he did not go down to the drain.

That is the thing four people noticed and nobody wrote down for nine days. He has stopped in that loop every Monday and Thursday for two years and he has always gone down to the cover and put his ear on it, and on the morning of the twenty-ninth of January he stopped about nine feet short of it and stood still, and then he came back up, and he did not say anything, and he went and got a shovel.

The cover was up out of its frame by about two inches. The ballast had washed out from under about a foot of it and there was a hole about the size of a dinner plate at the north side of the frame, and the hole was clean, and the iron was rusted brown underneath the flange and clean on the top face, and the two marks on it — a cross and a bar, put there by a man called Ferrier in about 1971 to say the cover was his — were under the water in the hole and not on the iron any more, and a man of about fifty-four stood over it for about two minutes and then went back up the length and wrote four words.

> *Cover up. Ballast washed. Hole.*

**In the condition column. And the reason column is empty on that line as it is empty on every other line in every sheet in the rack, and that is correct, and a sheet about a drain has no column that wants to know why a drain cover came up at twenty past two in the morning in a wind off the south-west, and there was no wind off the south-west when the gang was on the length, and there had not been one since the Thursday morning.**

"What did it do," said the man of about twenty-six.

"It filled," said Roy Pryce. "There was rain in the night. There has been rain in the night since about two o'clock and I heard it and I got up and I came out and I stood in my own doorway and I did not come down here, because it is a drain and it is not a road and nobody was on a car."

Nobody was on a car. That is on the page and it is the first thing anybody said about it and it is the truest thing anybody said about it that morning, and it was said by a foreman of fifty-four standing in six hundred yards of reserved tram route at twenty-five past seven in the morning in nineteen degrees, and it has been said in a yard since and it has not been improved on.

---

**The clinic shut at six on the Wednesday and the thing came at about twenty past two on the Thursday morning, and a woman of about fifty wrote both of those facts in the same line of a counter book on the Friday and did not put a mark between them.**

Dr Iona Sen had opened the water clinic counter every day of that week from eight until six and had told about four hundred people why, at volume, at the counter, on five separate days, and the answer was the same each time and about nine people have said since that it was the best sentence anybody said in January about anything.

"I am not going to be told by a four-page document from an office that does not sign its name what day somebody in this district is ill."

And on the Friday morning at about half past eight she wrote the following on the counter's own day book, in her own hand, and it is a clinic counter day book and it goes in a drawer in a room above a shop and nobody has ever asked for a copy of it.

> *Fri 30.i. Counter open 08.00 to 18.00. Wed night 02.20 — rain, SW, about forty minutes. Eleven seen 08.00–10.00, of whom nine are not unusual and two are not about the water. Cover up in the down loop on the quay. Nobody hurt. **The clinic was shut four hours before it and I am writing both of those down together on purpose and I am not putting a line between them.***

"It shut at six," she said, to nobody in particular, at the counter, with a queue of eleven in it. "I want to be exact, because in about four months somebody is going to read that a forecast came true and a clinic was open and it was neither of those things in the way it sounds. The clinic was open ten hours a day for seven days and it shut at six, and the thing came at twenty past two in the morning, and a water clinic is not a thing that is open at twenty past two in the morning, and if anybody in this city wants a counter open at twenty past two in the morning then somebody is going to have to pay for it and put it on something, and I am not doing it on a four-page document with no name on it."

Nobody argued with her, which is unusual, and the reason is that about four of the eleven in the queue had been to that counter before and had heard her say a similar thing about a different claim in the same week.

---

**And the water went where water goes, which is the part nobody in this district wanted to hear about on the Friday morning, and it is this.**

The loop's cover came up because the loop's cover had about nine feet of silt and four years of iron under it, and the rain in the night put water down a hole that had not had water down it since the alignment was built, and the water did what water does on eleven-year-old ground and four feet of brick and a lead lateral laid in 1904 the size of a man's wrist.

There was two inches standing in the low streets by about ten in the morning on the Friday, in a band about forty feet wide along the Cardew Street side and across onto the Ferry Road side, and **the district flood line is nine inches, and four inches in a month with nine-tenths of an inch of rain is on the page from the twelfth of October, and two inches is a Tuesday and not a flood and about four people in eleven streets have been wetter in their own kitchens than that in four years.**

The back of the cold store had water under the door and about nine inches of it inside, standing on a floor that slopes, and it went across the boards at the boatyard on the Ferry Road side, which are three new boards nailed in the second week of last year over a rotten channel crossing, and the crossing is rotten and has always been rotten and the boatyard owner has never once had it looked at.

Doreen Sankey went out at half past seven that Friday morning and came back at about ten past eight and said one sentence about it, and the sentence is on the page because he said it to a clerk of fifty-one at a counter and not to the district, and it is: **"I could not get the boards under at the low end and I went round the wall, and the wall is the only way round and it is not a road."**

He then did what he has done between eleven and nineteen times a day for about nine years, which is to turn round in the Ferry Road side and come back, and he told nobody he was doing it and nobody asked, and he is a man of forty-one who is on no list at all and never has been, and the water at the boatyard is now a fact about a man who does not exist in this city's records, and there is no form in any office in Alderquay for a man who cannot get out of his own yard.

---

**And on the Friday morning at about eleven, a surveyor went round all seven of the seven doors with a sheet of accountancy paper and came back with the same answer seven times, and he had known the answer for ten days and had gone anyway.**

The tram authority's running sheet for the twenty-ninth of January has a car number on it and a time in and a time out and an empty fault box, and the fault box cannot be completed because there is no column for where the car was, and the shed return for the night has no box in it at all because the shed is a loop with a flag. The Civic Atlas Office's incident and casualty book has no page for the twenty-ninth of January, because the book is for people who are hurt, and nobody was hurt. The Registry's continuity file is opened into three things and a weather-driven seam event in the river core is not one of them and the file says so in the department's own hand. The gang's weekly sheet has four words in a condition column. The route custody register says nil and its continuation sheet says one. The district index counter cannot enter a length. And the seventh door is nobody.

**And there was an eighth place, and it is the only one that had anything in it at all, and what it had in it was nothing that had happened on the twenty-ninth of January: a heading.**

> *district stability, entered by the office and not amended by any district*
> *river core — recorded, not stable*

Four weather-driven seam events were expected in the river core between the second of January and the fourteenth of April. One of them had happened, in the sense that a drain cover on a length that is not on the running diagram came up out of its frame with the ballast washed from under it, and the district had no instrument for the other sense, and the four were not reduced to three by anybody, and no allocation of anything was made, and the machine in the drainage section that prints the district returns had not been asked.

Milo Rook wrote the seven doors out again on the same sheet in the same order and turned the sheet over and wrote on the back of it in his own hand, which he has done every time since the first of January, and the back of it now has forty-one lines on it and is the only record in this city of the state of the case and is in a coat.

*Fri 30.i. The first of the eleven has happened, or a thing has happened that four pages said would happen, and I cannot show anybody which of those two it is, because to show them I would have to say what the eleven are. I went to all seven doors and not one of them had a box for it and the seventh of them is a heading with a number under it.*

---

**The Field Book, Thursday the twenty-ninth of January, 20.40, a front room, standing at a table, and it is the first entry in this book since the fourteenth and he notes that and does not make anything of it.**

*Twenty-ninth, 20.40. I have not written since the fourteenth and the silence is fifteen days and I am not going to write the two ends of it down, because I have got a habit now of pairing silences and pairing a silence is how a book becomes a set of numbers.*

*The first of the eleven. Or the first of something.*

*A cover came up. That is the whole of what the foreman has and the whole of what I have got. Eleven men walked the length at half past seven and the man of about twenty-six stopped nine feet short of a drain he has put his ear on every Monday and Thursday for two years, and did not go down, and got a shovel instead, and did not say why, and the foreman wrote four words in a condition column and left the reason column empty, and the reason column has been empty on every walk of this length since the year before the war and it is empty again today and **I have stopped finding that a comfort and I have not found it a problem either and I do not know what to do with a thing that is neither.***

*It is not a seam. I have been on the length and I have put a chain over my shoulder and I did not go in and I did not hold anything, because a man of fifty-four stood over a hole in nine inches of rust and told me it was a drain and it is a drain, and **I am not going to be a man who turns a length into a wound because I have learned a new thing about wounds.***

*The clinic was shut at six and it came at twenty past two. I have written that down twice in two books today because it is the finding of the week and I do not know what the finding is yet. Rue's round was out at eight and in at seven and came in at seven, and it does not run at night, and a round is a person with a satchel and a person with a satchel is asleep at about eleven.*

*Ten routes, ten. The page home the sixty-seventh day of it and blank with three marks on it in three hands, and nobody has asked anybody to fill it in since the twenty-seventh of December, and I have not read the sentence back this month and I am not going to.*

*The token was cold this morning and it is cold now. The fifty-third day since the seventh of December and I have not taken it out and I am aware that I have started counting it the way I count the other thing, and I have written that down here so that I have to look at it in six months, because a man who counts his coat is a man who is about to put it somewhere.*
