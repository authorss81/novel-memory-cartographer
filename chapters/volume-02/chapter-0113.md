# Chapter 113: A Column Wants A Person

He wrote it on Wednesday the twenty-ninth of April between about two and four in the afternoon at a kitchen table with a cold cup of tea beside him, and the cup is in the book because the cup is in the book every time.

It is on a piece of accountancy paper and it is headed in his own hand.

> **OCCUPANCY — RESIDENTS, TEMPORARY ACCOMMODATION**
> *Persons resident in a building with no number. This sheet does not say a person is not a resident. It says a person is a resident and is not on any schedule the district holds, and asks why.*

And then the columns, in the order of the minute, because he has never in his working life drawn a column that was not somebody else's.

> *No. Floor. Description. Named rateable head. On the district's schedule. **Why this person is not on the district's schedule.***

That last one is the seventh, and it is the whole of it, and **it is a question about a reason, and it is the sixth time he has drawn one and he has said out loud, in a kitchen, to a cold cup of tea, that he is not going to draw a seventh.**

He filled in one line and he filled it in well.

> *9. Second landing. One resident, woman, sixty, present since the fifteenth of December past. Named rateable head: Marr, L. On the district's schedule: no. **A department made her rateable by a schedule of persons that this district has refused to supply since the fourth of December, and the refusal is the district's and it is minuted, and the paragraph that stops her is the one the named rateable head drafted herself, and the answer to why she is not on a schedule is a minute in another building that says a department shall not obtain one from this district while that answer stands.***

Nine words of column, about a hundred and ten, and every one of them true, and he read it back and it is the best thing he has written in seven months, and **it has no name in it, and there is no name in it because he does not know her name, and neither does the file, and there is exactly one person in this city who knows it and it is not him and it is not the department.**

He put the pencil down and looked at the seventh column for about four minutes.

---

**And at about half past eight on the morning of Thursday the thirtieth of April, in a doorway on the low side of eleven streets, a woman of about thirty-four said nine words to him before he had got to the bottom of the page and he had not shown it to anybody.**

"You have written a seventh column."

"I have written a seventh column."

"I know what it is about, and I am not going to ask, and you are not going to tell me, and I want you to know that those two things are the same decision and you made it before I got here." She did not put her bag down. "**A column that wants to know why wants a person to put their name against the why. That is what it is for. And you have written one about my household, and I have twenty-six names and I have not written them down, on purpose, for about four years, and you have spent seven months finding that out about a plate in a field and you have never once applied it to a piece of paper in your own coat.**"

Nobody in that doorway said anything for about eleven seconds and about four people on the other side of a wall heard none of it.

"The five before this one went somewhere," she said. "I know where four of them went, because this district has nine people in it and I am one of them and I count things the way you count things. One went into a tin on a bottom shelf. One went into the inside pocket of a coat. One went onto a trestle table in the rain and off the table in a wind. One went face down under a counter and a woman of sixty-one said the number out loud in a yard on a Wednesday. **And this one is in your hand, and that is the sixth resolution and it is a new one and it is the worst of the six, and I am telling you that as a person who is not a clerk and has never been one.**"

"It is in a book."

"Then it is in a book, and in about four years somebody is going to want a book, and you are the only person in this district who keeps one, and I have not got one." She shifted her bag. "**And that is the arrangement, and I have worked it out about four years ago and I have not improved on it since and I am not going to start now: a head is the only repository in this city that cannot be produced against anybody.** You can send for a tin. You can send for a counter. You can send for a coat and a man in it. You cannot send for what I am thinking about at eleven o'clock at night, and that is not because it is hidden, it is because it was never written, and a thing that was never written is the only kind of thing in this city that nobody can take off me, including the twenty-six, including the woman on the second landing, including the one I drafted the paragraph for on the fourth of December past."

"And if the twenty-six are in my head and not on paper, then that woman of sixty is on paper and not in anybody's head."

"**Yes,"** said Lena Marr, "and she has been on paper since the fifteenth of December and not in anybody's head since the day before that, and that is the whole of the price and I have paid it in four seconds in a room in front of thirty people and asked for it to be minuted, and the minute prints that I asked, and it does not print her.**"

She looked at him for about four seconds longer than she needed to.

"You are not going to finish it."

"I am not going to finish it."

"Then finish not finishing it properly. **Do not put it in the coat, because a coat is a place somebody can look in. Do not put it under a counter, because that arrangement is made and I know about it and it is not mine and I am not going to be in it. Fold it in at the back of your own book and put the date on the fold in your own hand and do not take it out again,** and that is the last time anybody in this district is going to be able to say a thing to you about a piece of paper and be right, because I am not going to do it again either."

Nobody wrote it down. That was the fourteenth time in fourteen weeks.

---

**And he did that, at about nine o'clock that night, in a front room, and it took about ninety seconds, and the sixth resolution of six is a folded sheet at the back of a book in his own coat with the thirtieth of April written on the fold in his own hand, and the book is the only book in eleven streets and it is a book about him.**

"One page at the back, and a date on the fold, and a heading in the seventh column and one line under it, and a second heading not written," he wrote. "**And the whole of the six, in order, and it is the finding of seven months and it is not a resolution: a tin, a coat, a yard, a table in the rain, a counter, and a book. Five of the six are places and one of them is a sentence said out loud in a yard in front of about nineteen people, and a place can be looked in, and a thing that can be looked in is a thing that can be produced, and a thing that can be produced can be produced against a person, and a woman of about thirty-four has just told me that the only custody in this city that cannot be produced is a head, and she has been carrying twenty-six names in one for four years on purpose, and she told me that in a doorway at half past eight in the morning and she is not a clerk and she was not asked.**"

"And I have drawn the column six times since the fifteenth of March and I have got one line in the sixth of them and it is the best line and it is about a woman I cannot name in a building with no number, and the name is the whole of what the column is for."

---

**The Field Book, Thursday the thirtieth of April, 23.20, a front room, one day off the twenty-ninth, and the sheet for that week goes into the rack tomorrow, and the second tin holds nineteen weeks of gang carbon and the one page that is not a gang sheet, and the nineteen is a figure I have been writing in this book since the third of April and I have just resolved, in my own hand, in the imperative, that I am going to stop, and a number that only goes up is a number I have started keeping, and I have written four sentences in two days about not writing a number down and a sentence about not writing a number down is a seventh column in everything but the paper.**

*Twenty-ninth, 23.40. One day off the twenty-eighth.*
*Thirtieth, 23.20. One day off the twenty-ninth.*

*One. **The sixth time, and the same one, and this time I did not get two lines, I got one, and the one is the best thing I have written in seven months, and it is about a person whose name I do not know.** About a hundred and ten words in a seventh column headed *why this person is not on the district's schedule*, and the answer is a minute in another building and a refusal of the fourth of December and a paragraph written by the woman who is standing in the room, and every word of it is true, and a column that wants to know why has been answered by a department's standing and not by a person, and I sat here for about four minutes waiting to feel the same thing I felt in March and about the nineteenth of April and I did not feel it and I have not decided why and I am not going to decide it tonight.*

*Two. And the naming of the shape, and it was not a clerk, and it was nine words, and it was in a doorway at half past eight in the morning, and I had said one sentence: **I have written a seventh column.** And she said: *I know what it is about, and I am not going to ask, and you are not going to tell me, and I want you to know that those two things are the same decision and you made it before I got here.* **Four years, on purpose, twenty-six names, not written down. She has been carrying the answer to the thing I have been drawing a column for since the fifteenth of March in her head since about 1970, and nobody ever asked her, and I am not going to, and she is not going to be thanked, and she is not a clerk and she has never been one.**

*Three. And the sentence, and I am putting it at the top of the second entry and not the bottom, because a thing you put at the bottom of a page is a thing you have finished with: **a head is the only repository in this city that cannot be produced against anybody.** Not a tin, which can be sent for. Not a counter, which is wrong-shaped on purpose. Not a coat. Not a book, which is a place. A head, because it was never written. And she has got the twenty-six in one and has had them in one for about four years, and the reason she cannot write them down is not fear and it is not a rule and it is not modesty, it is that a written name can be produced against a person and an unwritten one cannot, and the whole of what I have learned in seven months is a longer way round to the same sentence from a plate in a field, and I have been in this district seven months and she worked it out in about an afternoon.*

*Four. And the six resolutions, in order, and I am writing them as a list because a list is the only shape they have ever had: **a tin, a coat, a yard, a table in the rain, a counter, a book.** Five places and one sentence said out loud, six ways of not being responsible, and a thing that can be looked in is a thing that can be produced, and the fifth of them was the best decision I have made and it is somebody else's counter and I was not thanked and I was not thanked on purpose, and the sixth of them is the worst of the six and it is in a book in my own coat and it is about a person and I cannot get it out again without making a date on a page in a book, and a woman of about thirty-four has told me to leave it there in a doorway and has not been asked and I am not going to ask her about the twenty-six or about the woman on the second landing or about the twenty-three words, and I have written that down because I am not going to be able to say it to myself again in June.*

*Five. And what I have not done. I have not asked a conductor about a printed sheet. I have not asked a timetable clerk anything and I have not sent anybody and the rule about the round and the three things it says stands exactly where it stood on the twenty-fourth of April. I have not gone near the north end of that row. I have not asked for the fourth comparison and it is on Monday and it is the fourth and last. **I have not asked her about the seventh of January. That is six times and I have stopped counting and started dating, which is worse, because a date is a column and I have been told about columns by about nine people in seven months and I have not taken one of their advice.**

*The page home the hundred and fifty-eighth day, blank, and it is there, and I have written in this entry that it is there, and I have written that in seven of the last eight entries, and I am now aware that writing that it is there is the same shape as a reason and I am going to stop it tonight and I do not know what a book looks like afterwards. Ten routes, ten. The token cold, a hundred and forty-fourth day, and it was in a doorway at half past eight this morning in the inside pocket of this coat and a woman of about thirty-four stood about two feet from it and did not know it was there and neither did I, and I have not written down which of us was closer to it, and I have now written down that I have not, and that is two sentences about a thing I am not going to do.*
