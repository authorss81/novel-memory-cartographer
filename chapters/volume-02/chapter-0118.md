# Chapter 118: One Of Four

At about ten past four on Monday the fourth of May, in the half hour after four when the counter is shut to the public, a woman of sixty-one who is not M.Q. and is not M.R. and will not be took four pieces of paper out of four places and compared the version and the date on each of them and read nothing else, and that is the fourth and last of the comparisons and it is the rule at the foot of an envelope and it has been the rule since the nineteenth of January.

**The third of May is a Sunday.** The first working day of the month is the fourth, which is a Monday, and a man of thirty-one has had a note in a book in a coat since January that said the third, and the third is a Sunday, and he has had it wrong on paper for four months and has now got it right in a book in a coat, which is the only place in this district where anything gets corrected, and he has not told anybody, and nobody asked.

Three of the four say version one.

One says version two.

The fourth has been read and will not be described and was not described then and has not been described since.

Milo Rook was not in the room. He had written at the top of the fourth's entry on Monday morning that he would not be in the room, and the reason is in the entry and it is four lines long and the four lines are: **a comparison of four people is not his, and the holder is not M.Q. and is not M.R. and will not be, and the four are four people, and he is one of the four and there is no version of this that he is in a room for.**

At about ten past four he was in the front room four streets away and at about a quarter past four she came in and shut the counter behind her and said nine words.

"It is the same. Three and one, one of them is version two, and the number has not moved, and I am not telling you which one, and you were not going to ask me, and I am not going to be thanked for the nine words."

"What is on the outside of the envelope."

"**One of four. — 1.iv.**" She wrote it on the back of her hand without looking down, which she has done four times. "In pencil, in my hand, and it is the same number it has been since the nineteenth of January, and it has meant a different piece of paper every single time, and on the first of April it meant one thing and today it meant another, and it is still the same number.

"**And I did not change it today. I have changed it every month since the nineteenth of January and today I did not change it, and the corner of that envelope is four weeks old and it says the first of April on a piece of paper that was last handled on Monday, and I am not going to tell you why and I am not going to write anything next to it, and I said in January that I wanted to write something else next to it and I have now said that three times and I am not going to say it a fourth.**"

"You said in January you wanted to write something else next to it."

"I did say that and I am not going to and I have now said that twice, in January and in April, and this is the third time I have said it and it is the last time and I would like it noticed that the sentence has got shorter each time and I have not improved it and I have not explained it and a number is the one thing about four people I am allowed to have and I have just told you in nine words that the number is the only part of this that is the same and the rest of it is a different piece of paper every month, and I am not going to say the rest of it, and I am going to do this in June or I am not going to do it in June, and I have not decided which and I have not got anybody to decide it with, and there is a rule about the round if I am ill and the round is a woman with a satchel and she is not going to stand in a canteen and ask a man to put her round in a book."

"That is a rule about a round and not about four pieces of paper."

"It is a rule about custody and I wrote it in January and you have had four months of it and you have used it about a tin, and about a coat, and about a counter, and about a book in your own hands, and you have not once used it about this." She put the returns down in a pile. "**Ask the person whose hand it is and nobody else. That is my sentence, from January, and I have watched you take the first half of it four times and I have watched you leave the second half four times, and the second half is the whole of it, and the second half is the only part of it that is about this envelope.**"

He did not say anything, and about four seconds went past, and she went back to the four returns, and the conversation was over, and nobody wrote it down.

---

**And on Tuesday the fifth of May, at about ten past four in the afternoon, a hall in Crown Heights emptied into a corridor, and about four hundred people walked past a board in that corridor every day of the working week, and one of them stood about forty feet from a door and was spoken to by nobody for about twenty minutes, for the second time in three weeks.**

Six items had been printed on it. The business of the day had been: *Continuations; applications, orders, variations, consents; ground and seam returns, current quarter, outstanding from sections; reports of departments and officers; maintenance returns, current year, presented; such other business as the chair may take.*

**And the district's matter is not on it, and was not on it, and could not have been, and the order is suspended and is not discharged and is not varied and is not withdrawn, and the classification of the eleventh is withdrawn and is not relied on, and a sentence cannot be restored by anybody in this city, and the matter stands over, is not adjourned, and has no date, and has had no date since the fourteenth of April. The fifth of May did not touch any of that. The fifth of May could not have touched any of that. A matter is discharged or withdrawn or varied or it is not, and none of those three words is in this district's name anywhere, and nothing happened in that room on Tuesday to this district and nothing could have.**

"So what is the finding," said Rue, in a yard, on the Wednesday.

"That the fifth of May did nothing to us," said Milo Rook, "in about six hours, correctly, and I stood about forty feet from the door while it did it, and about four hundred people walked past a board that had two dates on it and not one of the four hundred stopped, and I have been in that corridor twice in three weeks and been spoken to by nobody twice, and I have worked out the whole of it and it took about twenty minutes and a plate in a field in April and two months."

"Tell me the whole of it."

"**A list is read by the people who are on it and by the person who made it, and that is the entire readership of a list, and it is about eleven people and four hundred passers-by who do not stop.** A minute is read by nobody. Not by the parties and not by the department and not by the chair; a minute is read by whoever produces it, in about four years, in a room, on behalf of somebody who is not in it. **Those are two different readerships of two different objects and they are in the same building about forty feet apart and they do not know about each other and they do not have to, and I have been trying since the twenty-third of April to work out how a district gets from one of them to the other, and it does not, and there is no procedure, and there is no form, and the answer is that you do not get from one to the other, you are in one or the other, and this district is in neither, and it got nothing on Tuesday and it is going to get nothing on the twelfth, and it is going to get nothing for about a year.**"

"And the chair."

"The chair is not ours and never was, and I knew that on the thirty-first of March and I have written it down four times, and a number in a column can go down as well as up and nobody has to do anything for it to go down, and that is a sentence a man of forty-one said in a yard in April and I have used it twice this week and I am not going to use it a third time and he would tell me not to." He put his hand flat on the trestle table. "**And the thing I have got that I did not have on Friday: the room is not the list and the list is not the room, and about four hundred people a day walk past the difference and nobody has ever made a form of it, and that is not an oversight, that is the design, and the design is about eleven pages long and it works, and it is the reason this district is where it is and not anywhere else.**"

**The Field Book, Tuesday the fifth of May, 23.05, a front room, one day off the fourth, and there is no entry in this book for anything that happened in that hall today because I was not in it.**

*Fifth, 23.05. One day.*

*One. **A comparison of four pieces of paper on the Monday, and it is the fourth, and there will not be a fifth, and I have known that since the twenty-third of April.** Three of the four say version one and one says version two and the fourth has been read and will not be described, and the number on the outside of the envelope in pencil in her hand is **1 of 4. — 1.iv.** and it has not moved since the first of February and it has meant a different piece of paper every time. **And I was not in the room and I wrote at the top of Monday's entry that I would not be, and the reason is that a comparison of four people is not mine and the holder is not M.Q. and is not M.R. and will not be, and I am one of the four, and there is no version of that in which a man is in the room.** I got the number and I did not get the fact and that is the arrangement and it has been the arrangement since the nineteenth of January and it is the arrangement that the whole of this book is about, and I have spent seven months trying to get the fact out of a woman of sixty-one and I have got the number four times and I am not going to ask her a fifth time and I have written that down and I am not going to write it again.*

*Two. **She said the sentence about wanting to write something else next to the number for the third time and it has got shorter each time and she has not improved it and she has not explained it, and a number is the one thing about four people she is allowed to have, and she told me in nine words that the number is the only part of this that is the same.** And then she said the second half of her own rule out loud, in a front room, at a quarter past four on Monday, and she has been saying it in this book since the twenty-first of April and she said it to me today, and **I have had it since the nineteenth of January and I took the first half of it four times and left the second half four times, and the second half is about a hand and not about four people, and the second half is the only part of it that is about an envelope, and I have been using a rule about custody on a tin, a coat, a counter and a book for four months and not once on this.** And I did not ask her who the fourth holder is. And I did not ask her what she was going to write next to the number. And she told me neither and she was not asked and I have now written down that she was not asked, which is the fifth time, and the fifth time is the one I am going to stop at, and a woman of forty-five has told me in a doorway that a rule with a second paragraph in it is an instruction, and this has five paragraphs, and nobody can say no to a line and I have written a great many lines.*

*Three. And the hall, and the corridor, and the two readerships. **A list is read by the people on it and by the person who made it, and that is the whole of it. A minute is read by nobody until somebody produces it in about four years on behalf of a person who is not in the room.** They are about forty feet apart in the same building and about four hundred people walk past the difference every working day and not one of them stops, and I have been in that corridor twice in three weeks and been spoken to by nobody twice, and the whole of the finding is that a district does not get from one to the other, it is in one or the other, and this district is in neither. **The fifth of May did nothing to us in about six hours and it did it correctly and it did it on a printed side served by hand to a door, and the only instrument in this city that would have put this district in front of anybody is a board about forty feet from a hearing room, and a board is not a notice and confers nothing, and the twenty-third of April was the first time in six months that it was the other way round and it took three days for a piece of paper to say so.** Nobody has sent for the chair and nobody has taken it and there is no form on which a chair goes back and there never was.*

*Four. And the letter, and the twentieth day of May being four days away, and the item standing open. **I have not written anything on the back of that letter and I have not rung anybody and I have not sent it and it is going to sit in my own office until the twentieth and on the twentieth I am going to do one of three things and I do not know which.** And a man of fifty-eight with a spade has told me I am going to write something and he has not read the paper and he is right, and a woman of thirty-one has told me that the first time anybody uses the line of the twelfth of January as a shield is the day you find out what it actually is, and I have not asked her about the season and it is the tenth week and I told her on Monday that I had not and she said she was not going to ask, and there is a page in a drawer with a coat in it that has not been produced for four months and we are both counting and neither of us has said the count out loud and she says that is the most intimate thing that has happened between us and it is a document neither of us has mentioned.*

*The page home the hundred and sixty-third day, blank, and I am not writing that it is there tonight, and I have stopped, and I did not tell anybody that I had stopped and there is nobody to tell and that is the right way round. The token cold, a hundred and forty-ninth day, and it was in a corridor in Crown Heights at about ten past four last night about forty feet from a door, and nothing was printed in this city for the fifth of May about anything that happened in that building today, and a token in a coat has still never opened a door.*
