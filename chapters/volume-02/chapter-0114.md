# Chapter 114: The Route Is The Doors

He got on at the bottom of the ramp at about ten past seven on Friday the first of May and he got off at the top of it at about twenty past six that evening, and in between he said eleven words, all of them to a woman of thirty-eight who was doing a different figure of three, and Corin Mabey said none.

He is fifty-one. He has driven line three for twenty-nine years. He was on the car on the morning of the sixteenth of December and he came back with it, and **he has not been able to say a sentence since the Friday and he is at home when he is not driving and he is not hurt**, and nobody has said that sentence in a yard and nobody has needed to.

"You are going to be on that car all day," said Nessa Bray, from the step of a different car.

"Until about six."

"Then I am going to say one thing and it is the only thing I am going to say and it is not about December. **Do not try to make him talk. Not today, not tomorrow, not because you have been in a car with him all day and it would be a kindness. He is working. He has been working since the Friday and he is going to work today and he will have done nineteen calls out and nineteen back and he will have said the next one in his own head, the way I say it out loud, and that is the whole of what is in him at the moment and it is not a small thing and it is not a sad thing and if you go in there looking for a thing to write down you will find it and it will not be his.**"

"I am not going to ask him anything."

"Then do not get on, because getting on and saying nothing for eleven hours is a thing a man does when he is about to ask somebody something, and I have been sixteen years on that car and I know the shape of a man who is about to ask a question and it is not a man who is quiet."

He got on.

---

**And he did not ask him anything, and that was the whole of it, and it is the first decision he has made in seven months that cost him nothing at all and he has written that down as the finding and he does not trust it.**

He sat on the off side of the second seat from the front, which is where a person sits who is not going to be asked to move, because in twenty-nine years a driver learns that the seat is taken by a man who is not going anywhere and there is no point in clearing it, and Corin Mabey looked at him once at the bottom of the ramp and once at the top of it and on neither occasion appeared to see anything remarkable.

The car went out. Nineteen calls out. Nobody announced anything, because announcing is a conductor's job and the conductor was on the other figure of three, and **the car went out into a morning with nobody in it saying where it was going, and it went to the right places, and Milo Rook was in it and had no idea how, and by the fourth call he had stopped being a passenger and become something much worse, which was a man with a book in a coat going out to find out where a car went.**

The seventh call is before the eighth on a Tuesday and after it on a Friday. He learned that by sitting in it. He has a sheet in the office with the times on it and he has had one since the middle of February and he has checked the car against it four times and the car has been right four times, and he has been assuming for six weeks that the car was reading the sheet.

**The car was not reading the sheet. The car was being driven, and the driver had it, and the sheet is a print of a man of fifty-one who has had it for twenty-nine years, and a print cannot go out and come back.**

He wrote nothing down about it. He has written that sentence down three times now and he is not going to write it a fourth because on Tuesday a woman of thirty-eight said in about nine words that she could tell him the doors on that line in the order they come, in the dark, backwards, and could not tell him why the seventh is before the eighth on a Tuesday and after it on a Friday and has never needed to. That was on Tuesday. It is now Friday. **The two of them have worked it out separately, on the same line, a day apart, and neither of them has been told about the other, and I am not going to be the man who tells them, and that is a rule and I have made it myself, and it is the first rule I have made in seven months that is not a shape.**

---

**And at about ten past five in the afternoon the car came into the yard at Crown Heights and a man of about sixty-three was on the ramp with a flag and did not look inside it, and has not looked inside a car in twenty-two years and was not asked to and is not going to be, and his book says the time a car came in and nothing else at all.**

The book says 16.41.

The car came in at 16.41.

"Seventeen minutes early on the second," said the man with the flag, to nobody, writing nothing, because there is nothing to write; the time has already gone in.

"Your book is right," said Milo Rook.

"It is right because the times are the times." The flag came down. "**I have never looked inside one of them and I am not going to start, and I will tell you why in nine words, because you have got a pen out: if I look inside a car then I know something about a car, and what I know about a car is that it came in, and the day I know that about one then somebody will want me to know that about the other kind, and there is no box anywhere in this yard for what I know and I would rather be the man with the flag.**"

"And if the car had come in at 16.36."

"Then my book would say 16.36 and I would have written 16.36 and the times are the times." He hung the flag on its hook. "There was a day in December that my book has an hour in that has not got anything behind it, and I have not asked about it and I am not going to, and my book is a book about times and not about cars, and the hour is in it and nothing is, and I have had five months to be asked and nobody has."

Nobody asked him. That was the fourteenth time in fifteen weeks and the man with the flag had a reason for it, which was the first time in five months that anybody in this district had.

---

**And at about ten past one that afternoon, at a counter across four streets, a woman of sixty-one asked a man of thirty-one what he had done all day and he told her in about nine seconds and she said the second half of what he said.**

"You got on a car at ten past seven."

"I got on a car at ten past seven and I got off at twenty past six and I did not ask a man of fifty-one one question in eleven hours."

"That is a man who cannot talk and you have spent a day not asking him." She put four returns down in a pile. "**You have spent a day not asking him, and you have spent six months not asking about four people whose names you do not know, and you have spent four months not asking a question about a plate in a field, and you have spent three weeks not asking me what is in an envelope, and you have spent five months not asking a man of sixty whether he is at a shed on a Wednesday.** And I am going to say the half of it that you are not going to like, and then I am going to go and do the four returns, and you are not going to be thanked and I am not going to hear about it again.

"That is not a method. That is **a man who has worked out that not asking is the cheapest thing he can do**, and it is the cheapest thing he can do because every one of those four would cost him something he has not got, and you have got very good at it in six months and you have not once written down what it is, and I have watched four people in this city get very good at it and not one of them could tell you afterwards what it was for."

"It is not a method."

"It is not a method and it is not nothing either, and I am not going to give it a name, because **the moment I give it a name it is a thing in a book and I have got four returns and one counter.**"

---

**The Field Book, Friday the first of May, 22.30, a front room, one day off the thirtieth, and the sheet for this week went into the rack at about ten past ten this morning and the second tin now holds twenty weeks of gang carbon and the one page that is not a gang sheet, and I wrote on Thursday that the nineteen was the last figure I was going to write in this book about it and I have now written the twenty, and I am not going to write a sentence about that, and the not-writing is the whole of the discipline and I have got it from about nine people in seven months and I have not asked any of them for it.**

*First, 22.30. One day.*

*One. **The changed method, and it is not mine, and it is not new, and it belongs to two people who have never spoken to each other and whom I am not going to introduce.** It is one sentence and I have got it from a cabin on line three and from a book in a shed: **the route is the doors, and the sheet is a print of the man who has the route, and a print cannot go out and come back.** The stops are in him. The times are the department's and they are on a card in an apron and the card is for the manager and not for the doors. A woman of thirty-eight could give me the doors in the order they come, in the dark, backwards, and could not tell me why the seventh is before the eighth on a Tuesday and after it on a Friday. A man of fifty-one has had the run for twenty-nine years and did not consult a printed sheet once today and was not once wrong about anything I could see, and he said nothing for eleven hours, and both of those people have worked this out on the same line a day apart and I have not told either of them about the other and I am not going to and that is the first rule I have made in seven months that is not a shape.*

*Two. And the recovery, and it is not a fact and it is not a document and it is not an instrument, and it cost me nothing, and I do not trust it. **A man who cannot talk is the only person in this district I can sit next to for a whole working day without putting anybody in a column.** That is what I got back today and it is not a thing anybody in this district can use, and I have been looking for a thing to put in a book since the second of January and today I have been on a car for eleven hours with nothing to put in one and I have written at the top of this entry that I did not find anything, and I want it recorded that a day of that is a different kind of day from any I have had since the twenty-second of December, and I am not going to call it a method, because a woman of sixty-one told me at ten past one this afternoon that not asking is the cheapest thing I can do and she would not name it and went to do four returns.*

*Three. And a yard book. **A man of sixty-three with a flag has never looked inside a car in twenty-two years and his book says the time a car came in and nothing else, and the book was right, and it was right at 16.41, and it would have been right at 16.36.** And he told me the reason in nine words, unasked, and it is the same reason a woman of thirty-eight gave me on Tuesday about a woman of seventy-four's counterpart on a different line, and it is: if I know a thing, somebody will want me to know it, and there is no box for what I know, and I would rather be the man with the flag. **He has an hour in that book from December with nothing behind it and he has not asked about it and he is not going to, and nobody has asked him, and I am not going to, and it is the fifth thing in this district in five months that cannot be shown to anybody and it is the first one I have not been told about by a person who told me on purpose.** I have four and the fifth one arrived by accident and I have not said a word about it and I am not going to.*

*Four. And what I did not do. I did not ask a conductor about a printed sheet and I did not ask a driver about a printed sheet and I did not ask a man with a flag about an hour in a book, and the rule about the timetable division says ask nobody and do not come here and do not send the man from the round, and it stands, and I have not added a fourth thing to it, and a works clerk of forty-six wrote a sentence on the back of a form on the ninth of April and a reader of fifty-four had a broom in a corner from the twelfth of March and neither of them has ever been asked by me about any of it, and the count of people in this district I have not asked is larger than the count of people I have and I have written that down and I am not going to improve it.*

*Fifth. The walk. There is one on this morning and there is one on Thursday and the condition column is empty on both, and the reason column is empty on both, and it is the seventh and eighth of April's walks and this one is May and I am going to keep them apart in my head and I have, and there is no column anywhere for a walk that went normally, and that is not a fault, and I am not going to put a why next to it.*

*The page home the hundred and fifty-ninth day, blank, and it is there. Ten routes, ten. The token cold, a hundred and forty-fifth day, and it was in the inside pocket of this coat at the bottom of a ramp at ten past seven and at the top of one at about twenty past six and I have been in a car for eleven hours and I have not thought about it once and I have written that down and I want it written down that I have written it down, because the last time I went eleven hours without thinking about it I was on the far side of a gate in October and I did not know it at the time and I have got a great deal of use for knowing it now.*

*And I have written it down, and that is the fourth time I have written a thing down in this book in seven months about what I have not done, and a woman of forty-five told a yard on the twenty-second of April that a rule with a second paragraph in it is an instruction, and a person can say no to an instruction and nobody can say no to a line, and I have got a list of things I have not done in my own book in my own hand, and it is a list, and I said the word out loud in a doorway a week ago and a woman of about thirty-four looked at me as though I had handed her a knife.*
