# Chapter 73: The Proceeding The District Cannot Attend

The authority's amendment committee is a standing committee of three and it is convened under rule 14 of the amendment procedure, and rule 14 says that any three members may call it, and it says what a party is, and it took about nineteen minutes for Milo Rook to understand that the district could not be one.

The two men from the Council's transport scrutiny panel had been in a corridor in Crown Heights at about two in the afternoon of the twenty-third of December and had been handed a carbon manifest by a depot clerk of fifty-one while she had a witness in the room. They wrote to the authority on the twenty-eighth, which was a Sunday, and the authority's board clerked it on the twenty-ninth, and the committee was called for **Wednesday the seventh of January at ten in the morning** in a room above the Parcel Street canteen that has a table in it and eleven chairs and a barometer and no ventilation anybody has ever praised.

The two questions before it were, in the notice, in the authority's own printing:

> *Whether service amendment 12/44 (Line 3, Alder Quay; length between Alder Quay interchange and the water stairs, approximately 600 yd, comprising the down loop and the quay service access; reserved, no booked service; effective Monday the 13th of October) was validly made under rule 11.*
> *Whether the authority's record of a car held on that length on Monday the 22nd of December is a record the authority is able to produce.*

---

**Rule 14, paragraph three, and it is on the notice, and Milo Rook read it standing up in a corridor at about a quarter to ten on the Wednesday morning and then read it again.**

> *A party to a review under this rule must show an effect upon a person. An effect upon a person is a change in that person's access to a route, a service, a rate, a residence, a burial place, or a school place, and is not an effect upon a document, a schedule, a map, a diagram, a practice, or a place that has no rateable unit and no address.*

"Custody after withdrawal is nil," said Milo Rook. "The quay section is not a party because nobody is responsible for it, and the Registry is not a party because it does not hold it, and the district is not a party because—"

"Because there is no street," said Advocate Quill, from behind him. She had come down for the notice and not for the room, and she had read rule 14 in a carriage. "Milo, nobody in the river core can be a party to this and you are going to spend the morning working that out in a corridor, so I have done it for you on a train. There is one person in eleven streets who could stand in that room and be asked the question in paragraph three and she is the only one, and she is the one who is a rateable head of a household with no address, and she has to be asked to come, and if she says no then this district is not in that room at all except as eleven people who came to watch."

---

**Lena Marr came, and she was asked to be a party, and she said yes, and it took eleven minutes, and it is the whole of the chapter.**

The clerk of the committee read the paragraph and then read it again in the words it is written in, which nobody had asked him to do and which about four people in the room have since said was the only decent thing that happened to them that morning.

"I want to say it plainly for the record," said the clerk. "You are asked whether there was an effect upon you."

"Yes."

"Tell the committee what it was."

Lena Marr put her hands flat on the table. She does that. She is about thirty-four and she has two children of her own and she is not on the rate roll and never has been, and since the fifteenth of December she has been the named rateable head of a temporary accommodation household for two hundred and fifty-two people in a building with no number, at two and four a head a week, charged to her and not to the district rate, in advance, week by week, with no term, ended only by the household ceasing to exist or by the district producing a rateable basis for a block that has no street.

"There is no effect on me," she said. "That is the answer. I have come here to say that to three men above a canteen and I have practised it in a kitchen and I am saying it."

Somebody said, "Then you are not affected."

"I am not affected," said Lena Marr, "and I am the only person in eleven streets who can stand here and say it, and both of those are the same fact and it is not a coincidence and it is not anybody's fault. **Two and four a head a week, in advance, week by week, with no term, charged to my name and not to the district, for every person entered in a building that has no street.** And that is what being unaffected is. If the district had produced a rateable basis for that block in October then the compensation would have stopped, and the seven children would have lost the place the intake is going to give them in the spring, and I would have lost the only piece of paper in this city that says two hundred and fifty-two people are in those rooms. Nobody withdrew anything from me on the thirteenth of October. **The thirteenth of October is what keeps me whole.**"

The chairman of the committee asked her one more question and it was the right one.

"Then you are before us as a person who is content, and paragraph three asks for an effect, and I am obliged to ask you: is your attendance evidence of a decision you have taken, or is it a thing you have come to say?"

"It is a thing I have come to say," said Lena Marr. "And I would like it recorded that I have been asked whether I am content by a member of a tram authority's committee at about eleven o'clock in the morning on the seventh of January, and that the answer I have given is the answer I have been giving a department since the fourth of December, and I would like it recorded that I am not being bitter, because I have been told twice this month that I sound bitter, and I am here on a Wednesday morning and I have not come to argue with anybody."

---

**And the one person the committee would hear, because she was on their own staff.**

Hester Coombe, depot clerk, Alder Quay, fifty-one, twenty-two years, came of her own time and was not asked and had not told the district she was going. She was allowed to speak because rule 14 does not stop a member of the public service giving evidence, and the authority's own legal position is that a person who works for the authority and is not a party may still be heard, and the chairman said so in a sentence and she was in the room for about eleven minutes.

"Twelve o'clock on the twenty-second of December," said Hester Coombe. "A car on a reserved length is not a fault. I want to say that first because I have read the file and the file has a fault box and somebody in this building is going to want the fault box filled in and it cannot be, and it cannot be for a reason that has nothing to do with anybody's carelessness."

"Go on."

"The fault box is completed from the car's own return." She had the return's carbon on the table and she did not open it, because she had it by heart and has said so since. "The conductor's return has six columns. Three of them are times and one of them is fares and one of them is who was on the car. The last one is a line at the bottom where the conductor signs when the car is back. On the twenty-second of December that return said *thirty-one fares, a conductor's pass, a driver's pass*, and it said in the alighted column that twelve people got off at the Alder Quay interchange, nine at the water stairs, and ten at Crown Heights, and it was signed at four thirty-six by a conductor of thirty-eight who had been on that car since twenty to eight on the sixteenth of December, which is five days and about eight hours."

She stopped for about four seconds.

"**There is no column on that return for where the car was.** There has never been one, in my twenty-two years, because a car has always been somewhere that somebody was responsible for. And on the thirteenth of October it stopped being somewhere anybody was responsible for — I am not saying that as a complaint, I am saying it as the reason a box is empty — and the box in the shed that is not a box went in the hole with it, and the fault box cannot be completed from a record that has nowhere to say the thing the fault box wants."

Somebody asked her whether the fault box was therefore blank.

"It is half filled," said Hester Coombe. "It is half filled on a sheet in a book in my office, with a car number on it and a time in and a time out, and a fault box with a line in it where the fault goes, and it has been half filled since the twenty-second of December, and I have not queried it, and I would like the committee to understand that a half-filled fault box in this authority does not generate anything. It is not an exception. It does not appear on a report. **It is the exact opposite of an exception, which is the most dangerous thing it can be, because an exception has to be cleared and a half-filled box does not.**"

---

**And they asked her one more thing, and then they asked Advocate Quill, and the room did not get over the second one all morning.**

The question to the depot clerk was whether the authority held a record of the car being on that length, and she said no, and the chair accepted it, and it went in the minute.

Then the chair turned to the woman at the end of the table who had introduced herself as a Continuity Records advocate of the Registry of Continuance with a two-year note on her conduct and no preferred access, and asked her a question, and the question is on the notice of the meeting and had been printed since the twenty-eighth of December, which meant that she had known for ten days that it was going to be asked and had said nothing to anybody.

"Advocate Quill. The amendment was made under a standing instruction of the office of the Prefect, reference 11/D/6. A document with that reference is also cited on a district works notice of the sixteenth of November, and a district continuity order of six years ago cites a standing instruction of the same office without giving the number. Does the Registry hold, or does any person in this room hold, any document that connects that reference to that order?"

The room did not get over it all morning, and the reason is not that it was a difficult question. The reason is that Advocate Quill took about eleven seconds to answer it, and in those eleven seconds she did three things in the wrong order, and Milo Rook, who was standing at the back with a Field Book he had not opened, watched all three and did not know for about six weeks what any of them were.

She looked at the chairman. She looked at the table. And then she said:

"I hold a document that I am not permitted to produce, I am not going to say what it is, and I would like it recorded that I have declined to identify a document in my custody."

Nobody said anything for about four seconds.

"Advocate Quill, that is a statement about your state of mind."

"That is a statement about my custody, and it is true, and I would like it minuted in the words I have used and not in yours." She did not raise her voice. "I am going to say the other half of it, because I have thought about it on a train and I would rather it was in the minute than in a corridor in a fortnight. **I am not refusing this committee because I think it is the wrong committee. I am refusing it because I am an officer of a different body, with a note on my conduct, and because the reason I hold the thing I hold is a reason I am not going to give in a room, and I would rather that be a thing this committee can write down than a thing it can imagine.** If this committee wants a document about a standing instruction, it wants one from a body with a standing to produce it, and there is not one in this city that will produce it, and every one of them would be right, and I have been looking for about four weeks and I have not found the door and the door is not locked, the door is *not there.*"

The chair said, "That will be minuted."

"Thank you."

"And I am obliged to say that a statement that an officer holds a document she will not identify is itself a matter the committee can act on, and I am not exercising that power today, and I am exercising it because the statement is a refusal and not an assertion, and there is a difference and I have thought about it in the four minutes since you sat down."

---

**And afterwards, in a corridor, in about ninety seconds, and Cass Ardery was in it and did not ask.**

He was there because he is second-in-command on an investigation of a thing he classified and there is nothing in the world that connects that to a tram authority committee except that he had come to look at a car number in a minute, and he said so, and it was true, and about four people noticed.

"Advocate Quill."

"Mr Ardery."

"I am not going to ask you what it is." He said it at volume, in a corridor, with a clerk of the committee within earshot, because he had thought about where to say it and had chosen the worst place on purpose. "I want you to hear me say that I am not going to ask you, and I want a clerk to hear me say it, and I have decided that on purpose and I have been deciding it since about the sixth of December. On the sixth I noticed it in a bag and I did not ask and I asked for that to be minuted, and that was a small thing and I did it because I was embarrassed. **I am not asking now and I will not ask in February, and there is no version of this that ends with me asking you in a room in front of a chair.** You will want a reason. The reason is that I wrote a classification of a site on the eleventh of the current year, and it was correct on the day I made it, and I have had four months to find out what it is like to hold a piece of paper that somebody else wants, and it is like this, and I am not going to be the man who reaches for it."

Rue was nearest. She said, later, that she had watched Advocate Quill's face while that was being said and that it had not moved once, and that she had been in that district for eleven weeks and had seen about nine people be given something they had not asked for, and that this was the first time she had watched a person be given a thing they had **decided** they were never going to be given, and that it looked like a person sitting very still in a bus shelter.

Milo Rook did not know then. He wrote in his own book that evening that Advocate Quill had refused a question correctly and that the refusal was worth having, and he wrote the reason she gave and he believed it, and **what he did not write down, and did not for six weeks, is that she had looked at the table for about four seconds before she answered, and that he had been in a room with nine witnesses on the seventeenth of December and had learned what four seconds of somebody's hesitation looks like.**

---

**What the committee found, and it is four paragraphs and it was read out at about twenty past twelve, and about eleven people were in the room and eleven of them wrote it down.**

> *That amendment 12/44 was validly made under rule 11 and stands.*
> *That the justification recorded on the amendment is a filing number and not a reason, and that rule 11 does not require a reason, and that the committee notes the practice of the authority in recording a filing number in a justification box.*
> *That the requesting body is Permanent Way, quay section. That the lines headed **requested by** and **instructing officer** on the amendment are blank. **That the committee has been unable to identify the person who requested the amendment and records that it has been unable to identify the person who requested the amendment.***
> *That the authority is unable to produce a record of a car held on the length on the twenty-second of December, the reason being that the length was not on the running diagram and that there is therefore no box in it, and that the fault box on the shed return is not capable of completion, and that a half-completed fault box in this authority is not an exception and generates nothing.*

"There is not a person in that minute," said Rue, in the corridor, at about ten past one, with about nine people around her. "There is a reference number and a blank line and a filing number in a box, and in about four years somebody is going to read *the committee has been unable to identify the person who requested the amendment* and put it next to a form and say *well, at least they tried*, and it will be the best sentence in the file and it will not have cost anybody anything."

"And it is the best thing anybody in this city has written down about that amendment," said Milo Rook, "and it is eleven days old and it is on a piece of paper in a room above a canteen and there is no copy of it in this district, and I am not going to ask for one, and I want that written down as a decision and not as modesty."

---

**The Field Book, Wednesday the seventh of January, 22.50.**

*Seventh, 22.50. The authority's amendment committee. Rule 14, paragraph three. A party must show an effect upon a person, and an effect is a change in a person's access to a route, a service, a rate, a residence, a burial place, or a school place, and is not an effect upon a document, a schedule, a map, a diagram, a practice, or a place that has no rateable unit and no address.*

*I have read that about nine times today and I keep stopping at the same four words and the four words are **place that has no rateable unit**.*

*There were nine people from this district in that room and not one of them could be asked a question, and the one woman who could stand up and answer it did, and she is the one the department made into a rateable person on the fifteenth of December so that seven children could go to school in the spring, and she stood up in a tram authority's committee and said: there is no effect on me, I am the only person who can be here, and both of those are the same fact.*

*That is the third time the same thing has come round. The fourth of December, the fourth box. The fifteenth of December, a levy in a hall. The seventh of January, a rateable head in a room above a canteen saying she is unaffected. **A person in this city becomes a person when somebody makes them one, and the making of them is always a cost, and the cost is charged to the person and not to the department that charges it.***

*And they wrote down that they could not find the man who asked. They wrote it themselves, in the authority's own minute, in four paragraphs, and nobody made them, and it is the truest sentence anybody has written about the thirteenth of October, and it cost nothing and it will stop nothing, and I have written down that I am glad it exists and that I am not going to be able to use it, and I want both of those in the book in the same line.*

*Eight doors now. There were seven on the first of January and there are eight tonight, and the eighth is a room above a canteen with a barometer in it, and the eighth door has a rule in front of it that says a party must show an effect upon a person, and a person is a person because somebody made her one, and I have now been to all eight.*

*Eighteen days. The book is silent and I have stopped looking, which is a change and I have not written down what it is, because I do not know and I have written enough things down this week that I did not know either.*

*The token cold. Ten routes, ten. The page home the forty-fifth day, and I have asked nobody to fill it in since the twenty-seventh of December, and the reason is a sentence in the imperative that I wrote about a week ago and I am not going to read it back.*
