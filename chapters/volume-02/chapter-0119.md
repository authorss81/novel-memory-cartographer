# Chapter 119: The Same Sentence Twice

On Wednesday the sixth of May, at about half past four in the afternoon, Milo Rook counted the fortnight out loud in a yard and got it wrong by one day, and corrected it, and about four people heard him do it and nobody said anything and that has been the standing procedure of this yard for seventeen weeks.

He had started on the twenty-sixth of April because that was the Sunday after a Saturday on which a man of forty-one on no list at all had come out of a room above a canteen with something in his head that he had not written down.

"There are thirteen days in it if you count the twenty-sixth and there are fourteen if you count the twenty-seventh, and the first of them is the first night I have put anything in this book since the fourteenth of April, and I know why I did it and I am not going to write down why."

"You did it because you were frightened," said Rue.

"I did it because a man of fifty-eight with a spade said something in a yard on Sunday about the nineteenth of May, and it was nine words, and I have got four days to think about it and I have been thinking about it since Sunday and I have got a great deal further than a man with a spade would like." He put his hand flat on the table. "**And I did it because there is a second side of a sheet in this district with five blank Wednesdays in it, the eighth, the fifteenth and the twenty-second of April, the twenty-ninth, and today, and a blank on that side is a day when nobody stood in the weather, and about nine people stood in the weather on all five of them, and there is not one line on that sheet against any of the five, and the sheet is right, and the sheet is going to be right about the twenty-second of May as well, and it is not going to be able to be otherwise, and nobody is going to be able to say why.**"

"You have been in that book every night for a fortnight."

"I have, and I have not written one of those five days in it, and I have not written the plate in it, and I have not written the standing water in it, and I have not written the frontage in the rain in it, and I have not written about nine people in it, and there are five things in this district in five months that cannot be shown to anybody and I have been in a book every night for a fortnight and not one of the five is in it, and that is correct and I am not going to put a why next to any of them and I have written that sentence down four times and I am not going to write it a fifth."

"And two instruments in one district that cannot see each other."

"**A book with a night in it and a sheet with a day in it, and one of them is mine and one of them is the district's, and they are both about this district and they cannot see each other at all, and I noticed that on Wednesday morning and I have not done anything about it, and I want it written down that I noticed, because I have written down four times that I was not going to notice things and noticing is not the same as joining and I have not joined them and I am not going to.**"

---

**And at about ten past six that evening, at a counter across four streets, a woman of sixty-one said nine words about a number on the outside of an envelope, and they were not the nine words she had said on Monday, and Milo Rook has written both sets down and has not joined them.**

"Ask me what the number is for."

"No."

"Then do not ask me what the number is for, because I have never told anybody and I am not going to start with you on a Wednesday evening, and you did not ask, and that is the fourth time you have not asked and I have noticed the four, and I am not going to say anything about the four."

"Twenty-six days."

"Eleven days with an entry in it and not one interval. **I have written down that I have not paired one of them, eleven times, and a woman of forty-five told me in a doorway in April that a rule with a second paragraph in it is an instruction and a person can say no to an instruction and nobody can say no to a line, and I have written the same thing eleven times in eleven different hands of my own and I have not got anybody to hold any of it, and there are twenty-six of them by the end of this month and I have decided to stop saying so out loud, and it has taken me about ten minutes to work out and four days to stop.**"

"The number has not moved."

"No."

"It has meant a different piece of paper every month and it has not moved once, and you have told me it means four things, and I have written in a book tonight that it certifies nothing, and I have worked that out from nine words you said on Monday, and it is mine and not yours, and I am not going to put it in a yard."

"You are not going to put it in a yard because there are about nine people in that yard and a woman of forty-five in it has said five times that a thing is not for anybody, and she has not been asked by you and you are not going to ask, and if you put it in that yard it stops being yours." She squared a pile of returns. "**A number is the one thing about four people that I am allowed to have and you have worked out why and I have not told you and you have not asked and I want it noted that you did not ask and that you were right.** Go away, and do the four returns, and come back on Friday and tell me what the gang's sheet says, and do not tell me what you think about it."

---

**And on the morning of Thursday the seventh of May, at about half past seven, eleven men went down six hundred yards with a shovel each and the trolley, and the condition column on the weekly sheet for that week is empty, and it has been empty since the twelfth of February, and there is no column on that sheet that wants to know why a column is empty.**

And at about ten past ten the next morning the sheet went into the rack, and the duplicate went into a tin, and the duplicate went into a second tin on the bottom shelf of the plan room of the Civic Atlas Office under a repair schedule, and that tin now holds **twenty-one weeks of gang carbon and one page that is not a gang sheet**, and the page has not been read and is not going to be read and will be produced in about eleven years by somebody with a box.

**And a works clerk of forty-six stood at the end of a corridor in four rooms above a fitter's shop on the Friday morning with a sheet in his hand and read two of the six columns on it and did not read the other four, which is what he does four times a day, and then he said the sixth true sentence anybody in this district has said to him in seven months, and he did not know it was the sixth.**

"**That column is the only one in this building that is allowed to be empty.**" He held the sheet at arm's length. "I have got five columns and four of them are supposed to have something in them, and one of them is supposed to have something in it twice a month and it has had nothing in it since the twelfth of February, and that is the only empty column in this office that nobody has ever queried, and I have worked out why this week and it took me about four seconds and I have not told anybody and I am telling you, so pay attention, because I am only saying it once and I am not saying it again.

"**It is the condition column. The other five columns are about what we did. That one is about what it was like.** And nobody in this city has ever asked a gang what a length was like, and the day one of them does there is going to be a seventh column on that sheet with a heading in it and a man's name against it, and it will be my name or Roy's, and in about four years somebody will read it and be entitled to say we knew and did not say."

"Then write something in it."

"No. **A half-filled box is the exact opposite of an exception and I said that to a man of thirty-one in an office in April and I have not said it twice until now and this is the twice.**" He put the sheet on the desk. "Eleven men walked that length on Monday and on Thursday and the cover is flush and it has been flush since the sixth of April and nobody knows when it stopped and nobody is going to know, and the honest answer to that box is a man of fifty-four saying *I did not do it*, and he said it in a yard in April and it was not written down then and it is not going to be written down now, and if I put his four sentences in that column then in about four years that column is a reason and a reason is a thing you can be asked about."

"Nobody is going to ask about it."

"**Nobody has ever asked about line five in nine weeks and I have stopped saying that out loud, because a thing that has never been asked about for nine weeks is not protected, it is just not yet asked about, and the only reason it has not been asked about is that a man in another building has been asking about a box on a different form, and that is all that is holding it, and it is not holding it, it is just in the way.**" He squared it. "And you are going to write something in the seventh column on Monday the nineteenth, and it is going to be the best thing you have ever written, and it is not going to be a number of persons and it is not going to be a why, and I have no idea what it is going to be, and I am going to read it in about four years and I am not going to be able to tell whether you refused or ran out of answers."

**The Field Book, Thursday the seventh of May, 23.00, a front room, one day off the sixth, and there is a second entry for the sixth further up and I have left it where it was and I am not going to copy it out.**

*Sixth, 22.20. One day off the fifth. Five blank Wednesdays now, the eighth, the fifteenth and the twenty-second of April, the twenty-ninth, and today, and about nine people stood in the weather on all five of them for nothing at all, and there is not one line on that sheet against any of the five, and I counted the fortnight out loud in a yard at about half past four and got it wrong by a day and corrected it, and about four people heard me do it and nobody said anything, and that has been the standing procedure of this yard for seventeen weeks and I have not counted it and I am not going to.*

*Seventh, 23.00. One day off the sixth, and there is an entry above this one and I have left it where it is and I am not going to copy it out.*

*One. **Two instruments, one district, and no line between them, and I have not drawn one.** A book with a night in it, which is mine, and eleven of them so far this month and twenty-six of them by the end of it, and not one interval. A sheet with a day in it, which is the district's, five blank Wednesdays, all five correct, none of them able to see about nine people standing in the weather for nothing. **Both are about this district and neither can see the other and I noticed on Wednesday morning and I have written down that I noticed, and noticing is not joining, and I have joined them four times in my head today and written it down once.** And a woman of forty-five has said five times that a thing is not for anybody, and a woman of fifty-four in a room four floors down has had a broom in a corner since the twelfth of March and has not used it, and a man of about forty sweeps a hundred and ten yards at ten past six every morning and has swept it badly since he was twenty-one, and I made a rule in a yard in March that I was not going to put those two next to each other in a book, and I have put it on paper five times and the fifth time was the Thursday the sixteenth of April and I am not going to do it a sixth. **A book with a night in it is one night closer to a record and a sheet with a day in it is the only instrument in this district that has never needed a reason, and I have got both of them and I am not going to put them next to each other and I do not know why and I have not written down why and I am not going to.**

*Two. And the real thing, and it is the only thing in this entry. **A head is the only repository in this city that cannot be produced against anybody.** Said by a woman of about thirty-four in a doorway at half past eight on the thirtieth of April, and not written down, and not by me, and I have had it for eight days and I have not improved it and I have not used it about anything. **A number is the one thing about four people I am allowed to have. On the outside of an envelope, in pencil, in her own hand: 1 of 4. — 1.iv.** Said by a woman of sixty-one at a counter at about ten past four on Monday, and not written down, and not by me, and I have had that for eight months and I have been using it about four people when it is about a number. **And they are the same sentence. They are the same sentence twice, said by two people in eleven streets who have never met and have no reason to and are not going to, and I worked that out at about ten past six last night at a counter and I have not put them next to each other, and I have written down that I have not, and the reason is that the moment two of them are the same sentence there is a third one, and a third one is a pattern, and a pattern is me deciding that two things are the same, and I have written that down four times in this book and I am not going to be the man who decides it a fifth time in a fortnight on a Wednesday.** And I want it written down, in my own hand, tonight, that I did not join them. That is the first time in seven months I have had the whole of a thing in my hands and not put it down, and I did not put it down on purpose and I was not brave about it and nobody helped me, and a man of fifty-eight with a spade helped me and he does not know he helped me and he has never heard of either of them.*

*Three. And the fourth of May, and the fact behind a number. **The number on the outside of the envelope says a version and a date. A version is a fact about a person, because only people make versions. The date is the first of April. She has not written anything on the first of April. So the number on the outside of that envelope is a record of a person deciding not to write something, and it is the only mark in that arrangement which is about a person and cannot be produced against one, because the thing it is about has not been written, and it has not moved in thirty-five days and it has meant a different piece of paper four times and it is the only thing in this district I have found in seven months that is about a person and safe, and I have worked that out in about nine seconds at a counter and I have not put it in a yard and I am not going to, and she did not tell me and she was not asked and I have written down for the fifth time that she was not asked and the fifth is the one I stop at.** She wants to write something else next to it and is not going to. She has said that three times, in January, in April and on Monday, and the sentence has got shorter each time and she has not explained it and she has told me that a number is the only part of it that is the same and the rest of it is a different piece of paper every month, and I have believed her on the strength of a rule about envelopes for eight months and I am not going to ask her the ninth question and the ninth question is the only one I actually want.*

*Four. **The condition column is the only empty column in that office that has never been queried, and the reason is that it is the only one of the six that is about what a length was like, and nobody in this city has ever asked a gang what a length was like.** A works clerk of forty-six worked that out in four seconds on Friday morning and said it once and said he was not going to say it again, and he also said — and I am writing this down because he said it and nobody else has — **nobody has ever asked about line five in nine weeks and a thing that has never been asked about for nine weeks is not protected, it is just not yet asked about, and the only reason it has not been asked about is that a man in another building has been asking about a box on a different form.** And he says I am going to write something on Monday the nineteenth and that it is going to be the best thing I have ever written and that it is not going to be a number of persons and it is not going to be a why. **A man of forty-six who has had a box of his own asked about twice by a machine, and a box on the annual return that has been asked about twice and has gone up empty with two ruled lines on it, and a line six of fifty-four words on the fourteenth of January in a clerk's own hand with a signature against it that I still do not know the contents of and that nobody in this district has told me and that I am not going to be told and that the box is the only thing anybody in another building is asking about.** And that is the whole of what a machine does and it is the whole of what a form does and it is the whole of what a list does: it asks the same box twice, and the twice is what gets written on it, and in about four years two ruled lines and a signature and nobody will be able to tell whether the clerk refused twice or whether the section ran out of answers. **I have not got anything for the nineteenth and I have two days to get it and I am not going to get it by asking anybody about a box, and I have written that down in advance, on the seventh, in my own hand, and that is the first reason I have written in advance about a thing I am going to do since the twenty-first of April, and the difference is that one was about a person and this one is about a form, and I am hoping that is the difference, and hoping is not a method.*

*The page home the hundred and sixty-fifth day, blank, and I am not writing that it is there. The token cold, a hundred and fifty-first day, and eleven men walked six hundred yards at half past seven this morning and it was in the inside pocket of this coat on a length of reserved tram route that came off the running diagram in October and is correct to be off it and has eleven men on it twice a week, and it did nothing, and I have not said what it ought to have done, and a street that opens because of something in a man's coat is a place kept by that man, and I wrote that on the facing page of the sixth of December and I have not improved it and I am not going to.*
