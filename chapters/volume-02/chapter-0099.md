# Chapter 99: A List Is Not A Hearing

A sheet of paper came to the district on Tuesday the thirty-first of March at about half past ten in the morning, in an envelope, by hand, and it is the first document in five months that has come out of the Registry to this district with this district's name on it, and it is not a notice of anything.

It is headed:

> **HALL OF WARDS — DEPARTMENTAL LIST DAY**
> **BUSINESS OF TUESDAY THE FOURTEENTH OF APRIL**
> *Presented to parties. This sheet is a list of the business of the day. **It is not a notice of hearing and it confers no right and no obligation and nothing in it may be produced in any proceeding except the proceeding on the item to which it relates.*** Issued under the standing direction of the clerk of the list.

**Nobody in this district had ever seen one.** A man of about thirty-one in a route room had said in January that there is no such thing as a copy of a list day, and that was about a copy, and this is not a copy — this is the list, given to a party, because a party is given things. And the fourth line of it, in the authority's own printing, is the reason the whole of the last two months has been pointing at this morning.

> *1. Hearing, district continuity order CO/6/BELL (suspended; adjourned from the twelfth of January past). To sit from eleven. **Reporting officer: I. Sarratt, Survey Enforcement.**
> *2. Matters listed for determination: the grounds of the making of the order; the classification of the site dated the eleventh of the current year, withdrawn; the removal from the original safety report of the sentence, no evidence was found of any hazard to persons at the service court.*
> *3. Continuance: applications, orders, variations, consents.
> *4. Ground and seam returns, current quarter: **outstanding from sections.**
> *5. Reports of departments and officers.
> *6. Maintenance returns, current year, presented. **Permanent Way (quay section) — presented.***

---

**And the finding is in item six and it is four words long and it took a surveyor about nine seconds and then eleven streets about a year to be in a position to see it.**

Six hundred yards of reserved tram route that has not been on the running diagram since the thirteenth of October is on a printed sheet of the business of the fourteenth of April, and the whole of what the sheet says about it is the word **presented**, in a line about forms, on an item about forms, between a line about a district nobody has been to and a line about reports of officers.

"There is a length of tram route on the departmental list for the fourteenth of April," said Milo Rook, in a corridor in Crown Heights at about ten past twelve, with the sheet in his hand.

"There is," said Mara Quill.

"**It is item six. It is a form. It says *presented*.** It does not say where it is, it does not say what is in it, it does not say that there is anybody responsible for it, and it is on the same sheet as a hearing about a sentence somebody took out of a report six years ago, and **the sheet does not know that those two things are in the same city, and neither of them is going to find out on the fourteenth.**"

"Nor am I," said Mara Quill, "and I want to be exact about that, because you have said the word *exact* in a corridor to me about three times in five months and you are about to say it a fourth time and I am going to answer the thing you have not asked."

"Go on."

"**Item four is a line about outstanding returns and I cannot tell you which sections are on it,**" she said, "and I am not going to find out, and I would like it noted that I have not even asked, in a corridor, with a clerk about thirty feet away, on purpose, for the same reason I have not asked you something in a corridor three times in a month."

He did not ask. He has not asked since the seventh of January. He wrote in his own book in February that not asking was the correct procedure and not a courtesy, and he wrote it again on the twentieth, and he wrote it a third time in a book in a coat on the sixth of March, and **this is the third time he has not asked in a fortnight and it is the first time he has written down that the not-asking has stopped being procedure and become something else, and he has not decided what, and that sentence is in his own hand in a book in a coat and it is not about her and she does not know it is there.**

"Item four is not for us to be told," he said instead.

"No."

"It is for us to be told nothing, and it is the only line on that sheet that is doing to this district what it is doing and it is doing it in about four words."

---

**And then she said the thing that is the whole of what a list is, in about eleven seconds, and she was right and about four people in that corridor have said since that it is the single most useful sentence anybody in this district has heard in five months.**

"**Mr Rook, this sheet is the only piece of paper in five months that has this district's name on it, and it has our name on it in the first line, and it has asked us for nothing, and it has given us a chair.** And a list is not a hearing and a hearing is not a list and I have been doing this for eight years and I can tell you exactly why that matters.

"**A hearing is a thing somebody asks questions in. A list is a thing nobody asks anything in, and a list goes on a wall and gets read by about nine people and then goes in a file for eleven years.** And the district has spent five months being unable to show an effect upon a person, and it turns out that there was never a room in this building that needed showing one."

---

**And at about four o'clock on the afternoon of the thirty-first of March, in a yard, a man of about forty-one with a wet cap and a boy of seventeen with a blank card said the other two halves of it and neither of them was a surprise and both of them had been said before in this district, and the boy's name is Pell Harrow and his card is under a brick and it has been blank since the nineteenth of January.**

"**A document in the hand of a party is a thing that can be produced against the party,**" said Doreen Sankey, "and it is the first one you have ever had, and I have been waiting since December for the first one, and I want to say the two things about it and then I am going to go and do something else. The first is that everything on that sheet is correct. Every line. I have read it about four times and I have looked for the one that is wrong because there has got to be one, and there is not one, and that is item four, and I am not going to touch it."

"And the second."

"**The second is that a list is a thing you read and a minute is a thing you are in,**" he said. "There is a minute in a room in this city with a reporting officer's name on it under a line that says the district is not to be given a schedule of persons, and it is permanent while it holds, and it is the only line in this district's name in anything. **And on that list it is a number in a column in a hearing, and the list does not print what is being heard, and so the one thing this district has won in five months is on that sheet as a number.**"

"He wants the list minuted," said somebody.

"**He asked for the date to be minuted in January and it was minuted,**" said Doreen Sankey, "and I have read about that more times in the last fortnight than I have read anything else in this district's paper, and the list is not a minute and the minute is not a list and if a man of thirty-one in an office reads that sheet in April and says *and what is item one about*, then somebody has to be in a room on the fourteenth and say it out loud, and that is not a document and it cannot be made into one and I would like that said in this yard today."

---

**And a second side of a sheet of accountancy paper got a line on it on Monday the thirtieth and nothing on it on Tuesday the thirty-first, and the Tuesday is the last blank of that week that anybody has looked at, and the Thursday of that week was a walk day and there are two men in a gang on six hundred yards of reserved tram route at half past seven on Thursday morning.**

And the second tin on the bottom shelf of a plan room in another building held **fifteen weeks of gang carbon and one page that is not a gang sheet**, the last duplicate having gone in on Friday the twenty-seventh of March, and the fifteen is a figure a man of thirty-one has written out nine times, and the page has not been read.

---

**The Field Book, Tuesday the thirty-first of March, 21.50, a front room, and this is one day after the last one and I am not writing the two ends of it, because a silence is not a pair, and I have been told that by this book four times now and this is the fifth and I am not going to take it on trust.**

*Thirty-first, 21.50. One day.*

*One. **A list came.** Single side, six items, the authority's own printing, and the fourth line of it says that it is not a notice of hearing and confers nothing and may be produced only in the proceeding on the item to which it relates. And item one is the hearing, and the reporting officer's name is on it in a form, and I have now seen that name on a piece of paper in front of me for the first time since the twelfth of January and I have not written down what I felt about it and I am not going to.

*Two. And the thing that is on that sheet, which is **presented**, in a line about forms, on an item about forms, for a length of tram route that is not a street and has no address and cannot be given one, and which nobody in this city can find with a barrow because the diagram took it off in October and the diagram was right to take it off.** Six hundred yards, a gang of eleven, a foreman of fifty-four, a weekly sheet with a reason column that is empty every Thursday, a cover rocking since the second of February with no box in this city that will take a sentence about it, and the whole of what the city has for it is one word in a list. And item two of the same sheet is a hearing about a sentence that was removed from a safety report six years ago by somebody whose reference is not a district reference and has never been answered. **Those two things are four lines apart on the same piece of paper and neither of them is going to find out about the other on the fourteenth, and I am not going to write to either of them about the other, and I have not, and I have written that down.*

*Three. And the one thing I got wrong today, and it is about eleven seconds long and it is the fourth time. **I did not tell her about the season.** There is a hearing in a hall on the fourteenth of April and a season that ends in a hall on the fourteenth of April and a woman at a counter found it out from a card on a wall in February, and I have not told the woman who acts for the district in that hearing, and I did not tell her today in a corridor with a clerk about thirty feet away, and I have written down in this book three times since the seventh of January that not asking her about something is the correct procedure and not a courtesy, and this is not that and I want it recorded that this is not that. **She acts for the district in that hearing. The season is not in that hearing. The season is in a four-page document from an office that signed no name, and a four-page document from that office is not evidence in a hearing about a sentence in a report, and if I say the word weather in a corridor to an advocate it goes into a file by Friday, and on the fourteenth of April there will be a document in a room that says a surveyor of the district had a season, and a man will read it and ask what season.** So I did not tell her, and I have written down that I did not tell her, and I have not decided whether that is a rule or a fear, and I have decided to go on not telling her until the fourteenth, and if I go on not telling her after the fourteenth then it is not a rule and I will write that down in April in the same hand as everything else in here.

*Four. The tin. Fifteen weeks and the one page, and the fifteen I have written out nine times and it is a count of a thing that happens twice a week and a half a century, and it will be sixteen on Friday the third of April, and I have written that down in advance, and **a man of thirty-one in a route room signed for that tin in December and has never asked what is in it and is not going to and I have not asked him whether it is still there, because I know that it is, and because a rule in grease pencil says you do not take a line off, and because I wrote the rule's author a card in December and he is the one man in this district I would not put in a position of choosing.**

*The page home the hundred and twenty-eighth day, blank, three marks in three hands. Ten routes, ten. The token cold, hundred and fourteenth day. The tally has nine lines and I have not crossed. **Tomorrow is the first of April and it is the last time four copies of a card come out of four places and get looked at before the fourteenth, and there is no fifth, and a woman of sixty-one has written on the outside of an envelope in pencil that it is going to be *1 of 4*, and she has written it in advance, and nobody has asked her to, and I have asked her about a version number on the seventh of January and I have never asked her about a number she put there herself.***
