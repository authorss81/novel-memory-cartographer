## Hard constraints — these are not suggestions

1. **Chapters 31–40 are *The Flood-Order*.** `outline/volume-01.md` fixes the shape: **Orrin begins the flood-control work and presents the seam as a reason to accelerate the closure; Registry field reports flatten Bellwether into a generic thin hazard even as residents demonstrate that the route has a living witness and a usable service; Tarin Kest arrives to audit the order and initially treats Milo's evidence as an unauthorised private claim; the first full seamquake of the volume hits the tram interchange and shows that sealing the old route pushes pressure into the present; and Mara wins a temporary hearing but cannot yet protect the neighbourhood from a technically valid order.** All five beats happen. The order is not lifted.

2. **The answer to the bells is fixed and is not re-litigated.** Chapter 30 established on the page that Bellwether was a coordinated evacuation broken by the order: the first two calls moved people toward the old service court, the third is a check — a person saying a space is clear before the next turn — and the works' shift interval misfiled the whole question as a flood matter. **The chapter may be tested, probed, disbelieved, quoted at an audit, and used as evidence. It may not be reopened, hedged, or re-decided.** Tarin Kest arriving and disbelieving it is a *character* beat, not a reversal; a later chapter may show him coming to it, and if it does, it must cost something and it must not be a speech.

3. **The triad accounting is CR-010 and is now prose.** Two supports withdrawn — the public name and the ordinary routes — and the witness support broken by scattering, with **one routine left standing and overloaded** because Ada Fenn never left the river core. **Never write it as all three withdrawn and never as a thin remnant that later grew.** The thin remnant at the western culvert is a separate, smaller thing and did not create Bellwether. Chapter 30 is the chapter that stated this; a later batch may not restate it as if it were news.

4. **Tarin Kest arrives in this batch and behaves like the character in `bible/characters.md`.** Twenty-eight, a Registry field inspector, understands enforcement, evidence, and the practical limits of legal permission. He wants to prove the Registry can correct itself before somebody decides to dismantle it. **His flaw is that he believes a well-placed signature can solve a political problem and he underestimates the cost of delay.** He initially reads Milo's evidence as a private claim. **He is not a hidden powerful mage, does not become a magical administrator, and does not become a friend inside ten chapters.** He is an adversary who is competent and whose competence is on display. He also arrives into a division where Mara Quill's review is open on her own hand, which is a fact he may notice.

5. **Orrin's argument is still proved and unanswered, and this batch must not resolve it.** It is right about the mechanics: a believed route is a used route, and the map made the seam legible. The 22nd proved it — the water went down the alignment and the court settled nine inches over forty feet — and the thing that gave was the service door, which still cannot be plated because a variation takes six days. **If it moves, let it move by costing somebody something.** Not by reassurance, not by a speech, not by making Orrin wrong, and not by Milo producing a better map. He is a pressured local official with a real safety basis, a Prefect's standing instruction behind him, a regrade running, and a district that will flood in November behind a 1904 lead lateral the size of a man's wrist. **He is not a final antagonist and he is not suspended in this batch** — that is Chapters 58–60.

6. **The first full seamquake of the volume happens here, at the tram interchange, and it is not the climax.** It must show that sealing the old route pushes pressure into the present, and it must cost something real and specific. **It must not be named or foreshadowed as a pinned seam — the phrase does not appear in Chapters 31–40 — and the pinned-seam condition is not due until Chapter 51.** It is not the floodgate climax, which is Chapters 54–57. Nobody drowns and the hero does not defeat it. It is the volume's evidence, not its settlement.

7. **Mara wins a temporary hearing and it is not enough.** The order is technically valid and stands. Whatever she wins must be **small, specific, dated, and expiring**, in the same shape as the stay of Chapter 10 — which expired at 10.25 on the 28th of Sazur in one line and had never done anything else. **Do not let the new instrument be extended, renewed, or quietly converted into something larger, and do not let it restore the name.**

8. **The address/money trap is still live and still unsolved.** The relocation payment is assessed on displacement *from a named place*, so the name must stay on the compensation schedule and off the street index; a current address for Cedar Court — which the clinic, the school and the post all require — ends the payment. **Chapters 58–60 pay this off, and they pay it by costing somebody money. No clerk may solve it in this batch.** The nine released school places and the spring intake are on the page and unpaid.

9. **Who may speak for a household is still unresolved and must stay so.** The last registered head of household for the Cedar Court block is **old Mabb, asleep on the third floor since the night of the closure six years ago**, and his one waking in the volume is the sixth of Sazur, when he asked Owen for a light. **Do not wake him up, do not write him down, and do not let a Registry clerk be the villain who notices him.** No character has said his name out loud since Chapter 12. **Tarin Kest is not to become the person who notices him** — that would turn a refusal into an obstacle.

10. **Owen Fitch is a hole in the plan and an addition to the record at once, and must not be scrubbed.** His **initials** are on the Registry instrument of the 5th-of-Sazur one-shift inspection, put there by Mara herself, and the hearing of the 14th read the refusal line into the record against them. He wrote **not asked to** beside his own name on the third-floor line of the accountancy-paper rota. He keeps the third-floor check once a night, going up, unwritten, and on the third of the month he said in a yard: *I'm not going to say anything about the stairwell, and I am not going to do it twice as often.* **He is not to be thanked for a hole. His refusal is the standing counterweight to Lena's, and the batch still has to hold both.**

11. **Ada Fenn's withdrawal is legitimate, load-bearing, and is not a problem to be solved.** She said in a yard of about forty that she is stopping, and refused to be put on the rota *including by way of signing herself off it*, and her own name for herself is **a woman with a habit, and those are not the same.** She has refused to interpret the third bell **thirteen times**. **The batch may show what the district does without her. A fourth-floor name and a fifth-floor name have appeared on the rota; the fifth-floor person has not come down and Milo has not written the name down.

## Beat map for Chapters 31–40

Use this as a spine and vary the pressure. **Batch 0003 leaned civic, and the standing instruction in `state/current.md` is that this batch should lead with action and the working site and let the procedure arrive late.**

- **31 — Tarin arrives.** Political and character. He reads the file, he reads the four-account public record, and he reads Milo's measurements as a private claim by a man with a private motive. He is competent and he is about to be wrong about a document, and the reader should be able to tell the difference between the two.
- **32 — the works.** The regrade, the plate, the new dip, the machine on a fill that has already moved once. Orrin presents the seam as a reason to accelerate the closure. Physical pressure, a real site, and a foreman who is tired and honest.
- **33 — the rota and the fifth floor.** Character and investigation. A name has been on the fifth-floor line for a week and nobody has come down, and one of the four sworn witnesses changes their mind. **A witness who withdraws is the batch's cost, and it is not Ada.**
- **34 — practice and failure.** Milo attempts a durable multi-witness route for the first time and it fails for a reason that is not his fault, or attempts something he is told he may not do, and is refused by a person who is right.
- **35 — the low streets.** Consequence and political pressure. The drainage consequence arrives early or in a way nobody planned, and the two hundred and thirty-one people are the ones who pay.
- **36 — the cost.** Something moves. It moves by costing somebody something, and it is not Milo's map that pays.
- **37 — Tarin looks again.** Investigation and relationship, in that order. He finds something in the file he did not expect, and it is a document, not a feeling.
- **38 — escalation.** The works and the Registry disagree in public about a number, and the number is one somebody will be dismissed over.
- **39 — the seamquake.** The volume's first full seamquake, at the tram interchange. It shows that sealing the old route pushes pressure into the present. It costs something real and specific. **It is not the climax and it is not a pinned seam.**
- **40 — the hearing and a new problem.** Mara wins something small, dated and expiring, and it does not protect the neighbourhood from a technically valid order. **End on a new problem, not a resolution** — and the new problem must be the one Chapters 41–50 is built on: a durable provisional return route with several witnesses and a deliberate blank, revisable by people who were not there at the first crossing.
