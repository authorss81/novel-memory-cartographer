## Hard constraints — these are not suggestions

1. **Chapters 21–30 are *The Broken Sequence*.** The batch outline is explicit: repeated crossings wear Milo down until a return mark he set himself fails and strands him on the wrong side of a shift change; Tomas and Jo reconstruct the old use of the bells **without pretending their memories agree**; Milo has to work with a history that three honest witnesses describe three ways. But read `outline/volume-01.md` carefully on one point: **Chapter 30 establishes the midpoint reversal, and this batch must not complete it before Chapter 30.** Chapters 21–29 build the three incompatible accounts and the failure of Milo's own return mark; Chapter 30 states the answer — Bellwether is a partially witnessed evacuation broken by the order, not a massacre echo. Do not let a Chapter 21 scene settle what Chapter 30 is for.
2. **The triad accounting is fixed and is Chapter 30's job to state.** Two supports withdrawn, one left standing and overloaded: the public name withdrawn, the ordinary routes closed, the witness community scattered, and Ada Fenn's evening third strike and door-check left standing because she never left the river core. Never write it as all three withdrawn; never write it as a thin remnant that grew. The thin remnant at the western culvert is a separate, smaller thing and did not create Bellwether.
3. **The bells' original purpose stays unresolved until Chapter 30, and then resolves in one direction only.** On the page after Chapter 20 there are **five** accounts and **three families**, and Milo organised them on the 19th and was told to his face by Lena Marr that a tidy box diagram is a surveyor making shapes. The three families are an **evacuation**, a **count of the dead**, and a **later reuse of an older route**. The five accounts are: **Ada Fenn** — a pause to check the lane is clear, which is what her door-check actually is, and she has explicitly refused to interpret the third bell; **Jo Lask** — a handover, door to front, the count passed at the third call, the tram not moving until it has been handed; **Lena Marr** — a count of who is still on the street, her mother's list of forty-one; the **closure notice** — a count of the dead; and **Tomas Rusk** — a works shift interval at ten to two, now paid for and on the page. **Verrick Assal** is a sixth voice and he confirms nothing: he was off shift, came out for a coat at ten past one, said *clear* at the door, and the door was shut at the other end, and he does not know what the third bell was for. **The five accounts do not map one-to-one onto the three families, and that is the point.** Do not let a chapter quietly favour one. Do not tidy the boxes again. Do not let Ada interpret the bell.
4. **Chapter 30 must be able to overturn all of it.** The answer is that the bells were a coordinated evacuation: the first two calls moved people toward the old service court and the third called for a witness to confirm a space had been cleared before the next turn, and Orrin's order interrupted the sequence while residents were still being moved. **Three honest witnesses describing it three ways is the price of the answer, not an obstacle to it.** Work out in the prose how the batch arrives at that without anybody being made wrong: Tomas's works interval and Jo's works bell are the reason the whole question was misfiled as a flood matter, not the reason the bells were meaningless.
5. **Tomas Rusk's condition is unpaid and is this batch's hinge: *why did the road shut in the middle of a run?*** He gave the book on the 21st and said he would not be thanked for handing it over and would not be forgiven for not handing it over, and both are correct. The batch owes him the answer. **His account, already on the page and to be built on, not retracted:** the third bell was answered by *the people*, and the people had already been sent down to Cedar Court on the strength of the first two, and the foreman walked the reach alone with a lamp and no bell.
6. **The 28th of Sazur is four days out at the end of Chapter 20 and is the nearest hard deadline in the volume.** The temporary stay expires at the timetable review and the route will be re-cut around the service court. **Do not let the batch run past the 28th without addressing it**, and do not let the stay be extended, renewed, or quietly converted into something larger. It is small and expiring and it prevents the re-cut and nothing else.
7. **The address/money trap is the batch's civic engine and it is not solved.** The relocation payment is assessed on displacement *from a named place*, so the name must stay on the compensation schedule and off the street index; a current address for Cedar Court — which the clinic, the school and the post all require — ends the payment. Lena Marr has the forms in her coat and has said she can get a street back in the index inside a month. **Chapters 58–60 pay this off, and they pay it by costing somebody money. This batch must keep the trap live and must not let a clerk solve it.**
8. **Who may speak for a household is unresolved and must stay so.** A household is a named consenting adult, a dwelling, and a signature. The last registered head of household for the Cedar Court block is **old Mabb, asleep on the third floor**; he was awake on the sixth only to ask Owen for a light. No character in Chapters 11–20 says the name out loud. Do not wake him up, do not write him down, and do not let a Registry clerk be the villain who notices him.
9. **Owen Fitch is a hole in the plan and an addition to the record at once, and he must not be quietly scrubbed.** His initials are on the Registry instrument of the 5th-of-Sazur one-shift inspection, put there by Mara herself, and on the 14th the refusal line was read into the record as *One household has declined to be entered. Initials already held on the instrument of the fifth. Said no with knowledge of them.* He also wrote his own name on the third-floor line of the accountancy-paper rota with four words beside it in his own hand: **not asked to.** He keeps the third-floor stairwell check once a night, going up, not the ground floor, stopping when he likes, unwritten, and he is not to be thanked for a hole. **The fourth and fifth floors were kept out of the drill and six people on the fifth woke at four in the morning to a test they were not told about.** His refusal is the volume's standing counterweight to Lena's, and Chapter 30 still has to hold both.
10. **Ada's cooperation is revocable and structurally load-bearing, and the third source of witnessing exists and is not enough.** The rota in Rue's satchel has two names on it, one struck through by the woman it belongs to, and three floors of a five-storey building with nothing against them. **Do not treat the rota as a replacement for Ada and do not let Ada's withdrawal be written as a problem to be solved.** She has refused a wall, a rota, a note, a chalk mark and a thank-you, and she has said out loud that a thing which cannot say who is in it is a thing that gets decided about you.
11. **The interval is called by hand, and the hands change, and nobody inside a seam may call the count from outside it.** The batch outline expects **a return mark that Milo set himself to fail.** The 22nd proved the point: Rue held the count and for nine seconds she was on her own, and Jo Lask is the only person in the yard qualified to say that was nobody's fault. If you want the return mark to fail, make the failure a *handover* — a person who stops, a person who has withdrawn, a person whose mark is somebody else's labour — and not a magical lapse. A route with no count in it goes round, and Milo's Chapter 15 sheet is the precedent on the page.
12. **Milo is across four times and Iona Sen has the tally.** The 4th, the 6th, the 18th, the 21st. Chapters 1–10's two are not inflated and may not be. This batch is permitted to add crossings, and the volume outline expects wear; **but every crossing must be named, dated, and recorded in his own hand, and the count must be said out loud.** He has signed four conditions with Iona: a named person outside holding a count, stated minutes, walked in and walked out by someone who is not his chalk and who can stop the work with no argument on the way back, and the clinic the morning after. He has also set himself a condition in a yard — no further passage before the 28th, and none after it unless somebody who is not him says in public that they want one. **A man who has promised himself a limit is a man who will one day treat a promise made in public as a debt owed by other people. Put that under pressure; do not let him keep it easily; and do not let him keep it by simply not going.**
13. **The two-stage orientation cost stays two stages.** Stage one costs the remembered route and has cost five named routes so far. The **written line home is intact, complete, correct and legible to anyone else, and its loss is a Chapters 51–53 event.** Do not book it, hint at it, or let a character find a missing page. Every loss in this batch is another remembered route that returns when somebody marks it. **No sensory-detail failure, personal blank, seam-scar, record blank, or authorial blank may appear; those belong to Stages 2, 3, 4, 5, and 6.**
14. **The clue chain is carried, not resolved, and not Soren.** The bracket-and-dot is confirmed on two documents — the Chapter 1 timetable margin, whose ink carries a damp halo and bridges the printer's fold, so the ink predates the paper and the press is not the clue — and the Chapter 6 works-perimeter annex. The Chapter 10 carbon attachment's upper mark is *consistent with* it and is recorded as a possible third instance, not sworn to; its lower mark is in different lead by a different author. **The 21st's Registry certification is demonstrably *drawn on* its sheet where those two are demonstrably *pressed onto* shut sheets: a difference in method, not in age, and it identifies nobody.** The Chapter 20 copy register carries a **third pair** of unsigned margin marks in two different leads, and on all three documents the lower mark asks *who gave the order*. **Soren Rook is not named anywhere in Chapters 1–20 and must not be named as the hand here.** Keep the chain separate from the withdrawn replacement page, from the certified copy, from the ledger number, and from Attachment 9. Do not turn the fold into a chronology, and do not assert that a mark transferred this week is older than the sheet it landed on.
15. **Attachment 9 is a dated, unplaceable real name**, not a phantom: correct current neighbouring streets, a bus route that stopped serving it in a datable year, gone out of use by people getting older. **The batch's new purchase on it is the copy register**: the maintenance volume it was copied from went out on an ordinary district works office reference and came back **eleven days after the date of the original safety report on a Crown Heights repository reference**, and Milo has served a public request for the register and the two consultation references. This is a clue about **maintenance records being used to move or hide people.** It does not touch the Quieting, the First Atlas, or the One Survey.
16. **The withdrawn page, the ledger number, and the roll are three separate threads.** The Registry produced the file on the 21st as directed and it contains a **certified copy** of the replacement page — proof the file was extended, and no statement of what was removed, and a nameable absence where the sentence about no hazard at the service court used to be. The **maintenance ledger number** on that certified copy is the number on the first page of Tomas's mechanic's book, which means whoever wrote the page had a private book in front of them. The **second-shift roll** carries Jonas Venn's next-of-kin line, and Lena Marr has told the widow, who is alive, unnamed on the page, and cannot be told where he is buried because the disposal authority names a cemetery whose register for that district was not kept. **The death record is still closed and reopening it is a Chapters 58–60 job. Do not reopen it here.**
17. **Orrin's argument is proved and unanswered, and the batch must not resolve it.** On the 22nd it was read out twice, Danner reached it independently, the drill demonstrated it, and the thing that gave was the **service door** — a personnel door eleven inches above the low point of the old alignment, with a steel plate in a store, a variation that takes six days, and a gauge reading that needs the door open at ten. **Both men are correct. Do not resolve it by reassurance, by a speech, or by making Orrin wrong. If it must move, let it move by costing somebody something.** He remains a pressured local official with a real safety basis, and he is not a final antagonist.
18. **The service court is now materially changed** — a foot and a half of silt, sets standing in ridges, the handrail four feet out of true, three storage cages emptied into the river, one of them a filing box of a dead woman's letters. **Nothing collapsed and nobody drowned.** The culvert was throttled to eighteen inches on the 6th and driven out to full flow on the 22nd and is **never sealed in Volume 1.** Do not call the 22nd a first opening. Do not stage the floodgate climax — that is Chapters 51–60.
19. **Pell Harrow has not appeared since Chapter 10 and his round is running at two of four.** His brother's name is on a list at a village with no official address, one of two sources is a liar, and he has said he is not Milo's chalk. **Give him a beat.** The batch outline's own debts include him and his search; the Chapter 20 page names him. Rue clears his post-slot, Idar does his Sundays, and the school pack that sat nine days in the slot is a direct consequence of his absence.
20. **No new grammar, faction, antagonist, or final enemy.** **Tarin Kest's audit of the order is reserved for Chapters 31–40 and must not become this batch's second half.** Sel Iri and Halden Vey are not for this batch. Milo does not stabilise Bellwether alone; every workable passage is held by named people doing named labour. **Atlas residue should stay at or below Batch 0002's frequency, which was one appearance in ten chapters, and one is not required.**
21. **Milo must not have a clean victory and must not be cured.** His flaw — compulsive authorship under fear — is intact and is worse, because he has changed method *and* still refused the disclosure that would protect his work. He has disclosed the Lark token to Mara and refused her offer to file it; she recorded the refusal, signed and dated, in her own hand. Do not write a kiss, a declaration, a jealousy scene, or a rescue. Work, disclosure, disagreement in front of danger, and repair are the vocabulary.

## Beat map for Chapters 21–30

Use this as a spine and vary the pressure types. `outline/volume-01.md` says the batch's escalation is *repeated crossings wear Milo down until a return mark he set himself fails and strands him on the wrong side of a shift change*.

- **21–22 — the 28th and the re-cut.** The timetable review arrives and the stay expires. The route will be re-cut around the service court, and the question of who may speak for a household becomes a question about who may speak for a route. Political and practical pressure, with a real loss.
- **23 — the return mark fails.** Milo's own mark, or the mark he set on somebody's labour, strands him on the wrong side of a shift change. Action and consequence. He is across again and Iona reads the count.
- **24 — reconstruction without agreement.** Tomas and Jo put the old use of the bells together and their memories do not match, and neither of them pretends they do. Investigation and character.
- **25 — the cost of the work.** Recovery that is not a rest. Iona's conditions, the clinic, and Milo's dependence on being maintained. Do not let this be a chapter where nothing happens.
- **26 — the missing hour or the missing page, whichever the material supports.** The replacement page's certified copy has a hole where a sentence was; the copy register has a Crown Heights reference nobody has explained. Political pressure on Mara, who is holding a disciplinary disclosure on bad arguments.
- **27 — the 28th's consequence, or the notice of it.** A decision with a price, taken in public, by a named person who is not Milo.
- **28–29 — the two halves of the reversal.** Assemble the three incompatible accounts and put them in front of people. Do not state the answer before Chapter 30.
- **30 — the midpoint reversal.** Bellwether is a partially witnessed evacuation broken by the order, not a massacre echo. The worker's death is a concealed civic crime, not the source of a haunting. **This chapter fixes the triad accounting for the volume.** It should change what the team is trying to do, not merely what they know.
- **31–40 are the next batch.** Do not pre-empt Orrin beginning the flood-control work, the seamquake at the tram interchange, or Tarin Kest arriving. **Chapter 30 must end with a new problem, not a resolution.**

Milo's motive is now known to Mara and is in no file; Mara's second set of initials is known to Milo and is in no file. If there is warmth, it is in the form of a document somebody signs, a route somebody marks, a morning somebody is walked.
