## Hard constraints from the plan — these are not suggestions

1. **Chapters 11–20 are the *Housing File* batch.** Their job is to force the seam into ordinary civic stakes: addresses, school routes, clinic access, relocation payments, burial rights, and **who is allowed to speak for a household.** The magic must keep being administered through paperwork, labour, and shifts.
2. **This batch must supply the character, training, and recovery share that Chapters 1–10 could not.** The Batch 0001 outline recorded that four of its first ten chapters were civic or institutional in pressure, against the guideline in `OUTLINE_GUIDE.md`. Chapters 7 and 8 were action and recovery; almost nothing since has been practice. **Put real practice in this batch** — Milo learning to draw a route another person can follow without him beside them, learning to ask for consent before a mark, learning to be examined. Also put the Iona clinic and the cost of his crossings on the page as something that stops being theoretical. If this batch drifts back toward procedure, give the training and recovery beats more room, not less.
3. **The bells’ original purpose stays unresolved.** The plan for Chapters 11–20 requires at least these to remain live: the evacuation reading, the death-ritual reading, and the later-reuse-of-an-older-route reading. On the page after Chapter 10 there are **five** accounts of the third turn, and the Chapter 30 reversal has to be able to overturn all of them:
   - **Ada Fenn** — a pause to check the lane is clear. Her current version of it is a substitute and she says so: the doors are four storeys up now, so she counts floors. It is the same work and not the same work.
   - **Jo Lask** — a handover. On a run the count is passed door-to-front at the third call and the tram does not move until it has been handed, so the third turn belongs to whoever is at the door. Ada calls it a tram; Jo says she would be right to.
   - **Lena Marr** — a count of who is still on the street (her mother’s list of forty-one names).
   - **The closure notice** — a count of the dead.
   - **Tomas Rusk** — a works shift interval, proved by the pump-house bell schedule he has withheld.
   Note that **Ada has explicitly refused to interpret the third bell**, so the working rule in Chapter 8 is Milo’s own inference from inside the circle, not something a witness handed him. Do not resolve any of them. Do not let a chapter quietly favour one. Do not let Milo reach Stage 2 and name a grammar with certainty; he is Stage 1 and cannot stabilise, cannot command, and cannot hold a place open by drawing.
4. **Ada’s cooperation is revocable and structurally load-bearing.** Volume 1’s winnability rests on one person who is allowed to say no at any time. Her refusal is never a problem to solve. This batch should show the cost of that and start building a **second and third source of witnessing** — a rota, a written schedule, a door-check another resident can perform — without resolving the dependency. Owen Fitch agreed on the 10th, on the fourth-floor landing and in his own words, to take the third-floor stairwell check off her: once a night, going up, not the ground floor, stopping whenever he likes, and **not written down anywhere**. He made Milo tell Ada herself and not the yard, because a rota that goes round the yard would stop her doing it altogether. Let that be difficult, temporary, and insufficient.
4a. **Owen Fitch is a hole in the plan and an addition to the record at once.** Since CR-017 his initials have been on the Registry's 5th-of-Sazur inspection instrument, because Mara named him herself under a protective finding, and she told him so in the Chapter 9 yard in front of eighty people. He required the refusal line to carry those initials so a reader in twenty years can see he knew he was on the sheet and said no anyway, and Milo has said out loud that the paper is not clean. **Do not treat his entry in the plan as clean paper, and do not let him be quietly scrubbed.** His refusal is the volume's standing counterweight to Lena's, and Chapter 30 still has to hold both.
5. **The two-stage orientation cost stays two stages.** Chapter 4 spent the first stage: the *remembered* routes. **Milo is across exactly twice in Chapters 1–10** — Chapter 4’s lane and Chapter 8’s loop — and does not cross in Chapter 9. Iona Sen corrects him on the number in Chapter 8 and he records *on the fourth of Sazur and again on the sixth* in his own signed report. Add crossings if this batch needs them; never inflate the batch retroactively. **The Field Book is intact and the written route home is complete, correct, and legible to anyone else.** Do not book the written-line loss — that is Chapters 51–53. Every loss in this batch is another remembered route that only returns when somebody marks it. No sensory-detail failure, personal blank, seam-scar, record blank, or authorial blank may appear; those belong to Stages 2, 3, 4, 5, and 6.
6. **The temporary stay is small and expiring.** It runs to the timetable review on the **28th of Sazur** and prevents the route being re-cut around the service court. It does not suspend Orrin’s order, does not restore a name, and does not lift anything. The hearing is on the **14th**; the floodgate test is on the **22nd**. **Three procedural dates must stay distinct and correctly named:** the **5th** was a *Directions Bench variation*, not a hearing; the **9th** was the one-shift inspection; the **14th** is the hearing. Keep that calendar. **Chapters 1–10 run on a tight 48-hour clock before the gap**: the 4th into the 5th (Chapters 1–5), the dawn of the 5th (Chapter 6), sunrise and late morning of the 6th (Chapters 7–8), then the 9th, 10th, 11th–13th and 14th. Chapters 11–20 therefore open *after* a hearing whose outcome Chapter 10 deliberately never stages — decide and show what happened to it, and do not leave the reader assuming it went well.
7. **The culvert is throttled to eighteen inches and is never sealed in Volume 1** (CR-011). The seal crew has *no window* because Chapter 9’s passage occupied the site. The 22nd test drives the gate back out to full flow. Do not seal it and do not call that a first opening.
8. **The annotating hand is not resolved and is not Soren.** The bracket-and-dot mark is confirmed on two documents: the Chapter 1 timetable margin and the Chapter 6 works-perimeter annex. On the Chapter 1 mark the **ink carries a damp halo and bridges the printer’s fold**, so it was pressed wet onto a shut, damped sheet rather than drawn on it: the halo means the ink arrived already wet, the fold means the sheet was shut when it landed, and the fold itself is the office’s own tray handling and dates nothing. **The press is Tuesday or later and is not the clue. The ink is.** The only sound conclusion is that the mark was not drawn for this stock and came off something older, so **the ink predates the paper** — which is the direction that matters, because it dates the hand and not the sheet. Do not turn the fold into a chronology, do not assert that a crease predates the paper it creases, and do not assert that a mark transferred this week is older than the sheet it landed on. The Chapter 10 carbon attachment’s upper mark is *consistent with* it and is recorded as a possible third instance, not sworn to; its lower mark is in different lead by a different author. The two questions are read aloud in **Chapter 10**, in the records alcove on the **13th**, the day before the hearing; Chapter 9 ends on Mara stopping at *there’s writing on this*. **Soren Rook is not named anywhere in Chapters 1–10** — Ada interrupts before Milo can say it in Chapter 5 and refuses in advance to be handed a name — **and must not be named as the hand here either.** Keep carrying the chain. Keep it separate from the swapped replacement page and from Attachment 9.
9. **Attachment 9 is a dated, unplaceable real name**, not a phantom (CR-014). It has correct current neighbouring streets and a bus route that stopped serving it in a datable year. It came out of use by people getting older, not by a decision. It is a clue about **maintenance records being used to move or hide people.** It does not touch the Quieting, the First Atlas, or the One Survey.
10. **No new grammar, faction, antagonist, or final enemy.** Tarin Kest’s audit of the order is reserved for Chapters 31–40 and should not become this batch’s second half. Sel Iri and Halden Vey are not for this batch.
11. **Milo does not stabilise Bellwether alone and cannot protect a group by drawing a line.** Every passage that works is worked by named people doing named labour.
12. **Milo must not have a clean victory and must not be cured.** His flaw — compulsive authorship under fear — is intact. The plan expects his first private attempt to draw a single continuous route to produce a false path and to risk treating a witness as a tool. He must change method and agree that a map has to be legible to someone other than himself.
13. **Orrin’s best argument is now on the page and it is unanswered.** In the Chapter 10 corridor he told Milo that the map did not make the seam coherent, it made it **legible**; that a believed route is a used route; and that on the 22nd the thing Milo spent a week teaching eighty people to believe in will be standing in the way of a river. He demanded Milo say so to the yard *before* the test and not after, and Milo wrote the argument down in Orrin’s own words without editing it. **This is the sharpest live problem in the volume.** Do not resolve it by reassurance, by a speech, or by making Orrin wrong — he is right about the mechanics. The honest shape of it is that both men are correct and something has to give before the 22nd, and it should cost.
14. **The interval is called by hand, and the hands change.** In Chapter 7 Milo called it himself for nine minutes before handing it to Pell at seven; Pell keeps it up from seven until the moment Jo comes out at ten past eight, and then goes back to his round and takes no credit for it; Jo Lask calls it from his tram in Chapter 9 while the marshal keeps the tally. **Nobody inside a seam may call the count from outside it.** Every workable passage in this batch must again be held by named people doing named labour, and the handover of the count should be a recurring, imperfect, human problem.

## Relationship milestone

Volume 1’s milestone is **mutual competence**, and Chapters 1–10 reached it and stopped. This batch should push it one careful step further by **disclosure, not affection.** Two specific openings are already on the table and neither has been used:

- **Mara has not been told the brass Lark token exists**, and Milo has not told her that this district is his mother’s erased neighbourhood. She knows he has a private motive. She does not know what it is. Chapter 6 put him in a corridor reading his book aloud to her; that is the precedent for how he discloses things, and it cost him.
- **Mara has not been told the whole of what she did six years ago**, or how much of it she has carried alone since Chapter 6.

Do not write a kiss, a declaration, a jealousy scene, or a rescue. Work, disclosure, disagreement in front of danger, and repair are the vocabulary.

## Beat map for Chapters 11–20

Use this as a spine and vary the pressure types; do not write ten procedural scenes.

- **11–12 — the appeal and the household.** Mara’s review of the Cedar Court housing appeal becomes the batch’s engine. School routes, clinic access, relocation payments, and the question of who may speak for a household. **Owen Fitch and Lena Marr argue the resident question in public and refuse to resolve it for Milo.** Give one of these chapters a strong non-institutional pressure type — a training or a character scene — rather than a hearing.
- **13 — practice and the clinic.** Milo learns a way of drawing that another person can follow; Iona’s clinic makes the accumulated cost practical; recovery is part of the field work and not an excuse to hide the injury.
- **14 — the courier network.** Pell is gone south, so this is what a Streetkeeper route looks like without him. People who live near the seam have practical knowledge the office has ignored.
- **15 — the false route.** Milo’s private attempt at a single continuous route produces a false path and risks using a witness as a tool. He changes method. This is the batch’s turn and it should hurt.
- **16–17 — pressure, escalation.** Political or practical consequence of the hearing date, or the amended timetable moving pressure toward the guarded side threshold. The team cannot say whether the bells were an evacuation, a death ritual, or a reuse of an older route.
- **18–19 — the batch climax.** A smaller, harder version of Chapter 9: a supervised civic action that works because current people could revise it, and that costs something specific and unrecoverable-ish. Do **not** stage the floodgate test — that is Chapters 51–60 — and do not unseal, suspend, or defeat Orrin’s order.
- **20 — aftermath and a new question.** Short, clean, no manufactured interruption. Advance the hearing, the 22nd test, Tomas’s withheld page, the *pending* ledger line for Jonas Venn, or Attachment 9. End on a decision, a cost, a discovery, or an emotional shift.
