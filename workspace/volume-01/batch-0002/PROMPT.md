## Hard constraints from the plan — these are not suggestions

1. **Chapters 11–20 are the *Housing File* batch.** Their job is to force the seam into ordinary civic stakes: addresses, school routes, clinic access, relocation payments, burial rights, and **who is allowed to speak for a household.** The magic must keep being administered through paperwork, labour, and shifts.
2. **This batch must supply the character, training, and recovery share that Chapters 1–10 could not.** The Batch 0001 outline recorded that four of its first ten chapters were civic or institutional in pressure, against the guideline in `OUTLINE_GUIDE.md`. Chapters 7 and 8 were action and recovery; almost nothing since has been practice. **Put real practice in this batch** — Milo learning to draw a route another person can follow without him beside them, learning to ask for consent before a mark, learning to be examined. Also put the Iona clinic and the cost of his crossings on the page as something that stops being theoretical. If this batch drifts back toward procedure, give the training and recovery beats more room, not less.
3. **The bells’ original purpose stays unresolved.** The plan for Chapters 11–20 requires at least these to remain live: the evacuation reading, the death-ritual reading, and the later-reuse-of-an-older-route reading. On the page after Chapter 10 there are **five** accounts of the third turn, and the Chapter 30 reversal has to be able to overturn all of them:
   - **Ada Fenn** — a pause to check the lane is clear (which is what her door-check is).
   - **Jo Lask** — a handover to whoever is holding the door, the way a run changes hands.
   - **Lena Marr** — a count of who is still on the street (her mother’s list of forty-one names).
   - **The closure notice** — a count of the dead.
   - **Tomas Rusk** — a works shift interval, proved by the pump-house bell schedule he has withheld.
   Do not resolve any of them. Do not let a chapter quietly favour one. Do not let Milo reach Stage 2 and name a grammar with certainty; he is Stage 1 and cannot stabilise, cannot command, and cannot hold a place open by drawing.
4. **Ada’s cooperation is revocable and structurally load-bearing.** Volume 1’s winnability rests on one person who is allowed to say no at any time. Her refusal is never a problem to solve. This batch should show the cost of that and start building a **second and third source of witnessing** — a rota, a written schedule, a door-check another resident can perform — without resolving the dependency. Owen Fitch is currently covering the third-floor check once a night while Ada is ill with the flu, on his own terms. Let that be difficult, temporary, and insufficient.
5. **The two-stage orientation cost stays two stages.** Chapter 4 spent the first stage: the *remembered* routes. **The Field Book is intact and the written route home is complete, correct, and legible to anyone else.** Do not book the written-line loss — that is Chapters 51–53. Every loss in this batch is another remembered route that only returns when somebody marks it. No sensory-detail failure, personal blank, seam-scar, record blank, or authorial blank may appear; those belong to Stages 2, 3, 4, 5, and 6.
6. **The temporary stay is small and expiring.** It runs to the timetable review on the **28th of Sazur** and prevents the route being re-cut around the service court. It does not suspend Orrin’s order, does not restore a name, and does not lift anything. The hearing is on the **14th**; the floodgate test is on the **22nd**. Keep that calendar.
7. **The culvert is throttled to eighteen inches and is never sealed in Volume 1** (CR-011). The seal crew has *no window* because Chapter 9’s passage occupied the site. The 22nd test drives the gate back out to full flow. Do not seal it and do not call that a first opening.
8. **The annotating hand is not resolved and is not Soren.** The bracket-and-dot mark is confirmed on two documents: the Chapter 1 timetable margin (ink older than the stock, a printer’s fold through the ink) and the Chapter 6 works-perimeter annex. The Chapter 10 carbon attachment’s upper mark is *consistent with* it and is recorded as a possible third instance, not sworn to; its lower mark is in different lead by a different author. **Soren Rook is not named in the prose so far and must not be named as the hand.** Keep carrying the chain. Keep it separate from the swapped replacement page and from Attachment 9.
9. **Attachment 9 is a dated, unplaceable real name**, not a phantom (CR-014). It has correct current neighbouring streets and a bus route that stopped serving it in a datable year. It came out of use by people getting older, not by a decision. It is a clue about **maintenance records being used to move or hide people.** It does not touch the Quieting, the First Atlas, or the One Survey.
10. **No new grammar, faction, antagonist, or final enemy.** Tarin Kest’s audit of the order is reserved for Chapters 31–40 and should not become this batch’s second half. Sel Iri and Halden Vey are not for this batch.
11. **Milo does not stabilise Bellwether alone and cannot protect a group by drawing a line.** Every passage that works is worked by named people doing named labour.
12. **Milo must not have a clean victory and must not be cured.** His flaw — compulsive authorship under fear — is intact. The plan expects his first private attempt to draw a single continuous route to produce a false path and to risk treating a witness as a tool. He must change method and agree that a map has to be legible to someone other than himself.

## Relationship milestone

Volume 1’s milestone is **mutual competence**, and Chapters 1–10 reached it and stopped. This batch should push it one careful step further by **disclosure, not affection.** Two specific openings are already on the table and neither has been used:

- **Mara has not been told the brass Lark token exists**, and Milo has not told her that this district is his mother’s erased neighbourhood. She knows he has a private motive. She does not know what it is. Chapter 6 put him in a corridor reading his book aloud to her; that is the precedent for how he discloses things, and it cost him.
- **Mara has not been told the whole of what she did six years ago**, or how much of it she has carried alone since Chapter 6.

Do not write a kiss, a declaration, a jealousy scene, or a rescue. Work, disclosure, disagreement in front of danger, and repair are the vocabulary.

## Beat map for Chapters 11–20

Use this as a spine and vary the pressure types; do not write ten procedural scenes.

- **11–12 — the appeal and the household.** Mara’s review of the Cedar Court housing appeal becomes the batch’s engine. School routes, clinic access, relocation payments, and the question of who may speak for a household. **Owen Fitch and Lena Marr argue the resident question in public and refuse to resolve it for Milo.** Give one of these chapters a strong non-institutional pressure type — a training or a character scene — rather than a hearing.
- **13 — practice and the clinic.** Milo learns a way of drawing that another person can follow; Iona’s clinic makes the accumulated cost practical; recovery is part of the field work and not an excuse to hide the injury.
- **14 — the courier network.** Pell is gone south, so this is what a Streetkeeper route looks like without him. People who live near the seam have practical knowledge the office has ignored.
- **15 — the false route.** Milo’s private attempt at a single continuous route produces a false path and risks using a witness as a tool. He changes method. This is the batch’s turn and it should hurt.
- **16–17 — pressure, escalation.** Political or practical consequence of the hearing date, or the amended timetable moving pressure toward the guarded side threshold. The team cannot say whether the bells were an evacuation, a death ritual, or a reuse of an older route.
- **18–19 — the batch climax.** A smaller, harder version of Chapter 9: a supervised civic action that works because current people could revise it, and that costs something specific and unrecoverable-ish. Do **not** stage the floodgate test — that is Chapters 51–60 — and do not unseal, suspend, or defeat Orrin’s order.
- **20 — aftermath and a new question.** Short, clean, no manufactured interruption. Advance the hearing, the 22nd test, Tomas’s withheld page, the *pending* ledger line for Jonas Venn, or Attachment 9. End on a decision, a cost, a discovery, or an emotional shift.
