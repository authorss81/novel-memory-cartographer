# Continuity State

No chapter prose exists yet. The bootstrap canon below is authoritative for the first volume and later batches unless a future outline explicitly records a change.

## World and timeline

- Alderquay is a river-delta city built on reclaimed marsh and governed through the Continuance Charter.
- The Long Rain struck the Alder Fault forty-one years before the opening. During the emergency response, crews built the incomplete First Atlas, the original design of the Common Atlas.
- The Quieting occurred when the Atlas forced incompatible accounts of the disaster into one route. Nine districts became inaccessible and tens of thousands were displaced or lost.
- The Registry of Continuance was formed afterward. It can issue necessary safety closures and also uses continuity orders for land, identity, and political control.
- Lark Street was erased eighteen years before the opening. Soren Rook approved a necessary route severing to stop an immediate seamquake, then used the emergency to erase claims and suppress inquiry. Neris Rook died during the evacuation. Her route trace remains distributed among survivors and is not a recoverable person.

## Magic rules

- A remnant street forms when a place is forcibly cut off from its shared public name, usable routes, and original witness community.
- Remnants occupy seams and follow a dominant memory grammar: Cadence, Keepsake, Kinship, Mercy, Ledger, Weather, or Threshold.
- Habit memory is repeated human use, not a perfect recording of the past. A rumor without a lived route or shared practice is not magical.
- A viable map uses a threshold, a meaningful turn, a witness, and a return route. Durable restoration also needs a blank for current life.
- A map can trace, guide, stabilize, restore, or hold a route. It cannot read minds, resurrect the dead, force consent, or prove one history is the only truth.
- Fixed restoration without a blank creates a claimed place and binds the custodian to inherited roles.
- Milo’s costs progress from orientation blanks to sensory and personal blanks, a physical seam-scar, record blanks, and finally the relinquishment of sole Atlas authorship. The sound of Neris’s voice is the final protected sensory memory.
- Atlas residue may offer one short Field Book prompt. It is incomplete and never solves the moral or political choice.

## People and relationships

- Milo Rook is thirty, a Civic Atlas Office field surveyor, with a controlling-authority flaw and a private Lark Street goal.
- Mara Quill is thirty-one, a Registry Continuity Records advocate, professional foil, and the only planned romantic partner.
- Pell Harrow is a seventeen-year-old courier; his brother Tern is found alive in a moving community in Volume 8 and does not return to the old neighborhood.
- Iona Sen is a seam physician and former Thread Warden. Tarin Kest is a reform-minded Registry inspector. Sel Iri leads the Open-Door Choir.
- Orrin Sable is the local Volume 1 antagonist; Halden Vey is the institutional antagonist; Soren Rook is the final antagonist and Milo’s father.

## Ending constraints

- Soren initiates the One Survey. It briefly suppresses seamquakes by forcing singular agreement, then draws excluded pressure through the Common Index and causes the terminal Greywake.
- Milo refuses both sole authorship and a fixed, claimed restoration of Lark Street.
- The coalition maintains a distributed Atlas. Selected places return as living, revisable districts; unstable places remain marked hazards or memorials.
- Soren releases the Index and dies. Neris is not resurrected. Milo loses a fixed autobiographical coordinate, reliable official location, and the remembered sound of Neris’s voice.
- The larger extent of the Margin remains intentionally open, but no new cosmic antagonist may be introduced.
