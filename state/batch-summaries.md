## Volume 01, Batch 0001 — Chapters 1–10

**Files:** `chapters/volume-01/chapter-0001.md` … `chapter-0010.md`. All ten planned chapters exist as finished prose. POV is Milo throughout except Chapter 6, which is Mara. Pressure rotation as planned: ordinary work, investigation, controlled experiment, first crossing, witness dispute, institutional pressure (Mara's POV), action, rescue and recovery, public accountability, aftermath.

**Three procedural dates the reader must be able to hold apart:** the **5th** is a dawn *variation* before the Directions Bench, which grants a one-shift inspection and no stay; the **9th** is that inspection; the **14th** is the hearing. Chapter 6 is built on keeping these distinct, and Chapter 7 refers back to the 5th, never to the 9th or the 14th.

**Fixed future dates, all now on the page:** Orrin's contractor's bond lapses **11th**. The hearing sits **14th**. The floodgate test drives the throttled gate out to full flow on the **22nd**. The next timetable review, and the expiry of the temporary stay, is the **28th**. The amended Line 7 timetable removed the Bellwether entry on the morning after the rescue.

**What the batch establishes.** Bellwether is dangerous in a way that is being administered. The order withdrew the name, closed the routes, and scattered the people who kept the routines, and one routine kept going — Ada's evening third strike and her door-check — which is why the seam is coherent enough to negotiate with and why the residents can argue with it at all. The magic is administered through gates, timetables, records, and maintenance labour. Milo's working rule has two halves, both earned: keep the interval, and yield at the third turn rather than fill it. A witness may refuse and the route does not punish the refusal.

**What the batch must not be read as.** It is not a restoration. The lane does not return, the name is not restored, the order is not lifted, and the bells' original purpose is **not** established — four readings of the third turn are alive (Ada's clear-the-lane, Jo's handover, Lena's count, and the notice's count of the dead), plus Tomas's account of the third call as a works shift interval that he will not release until the road's closing is explained. Chapter 30 still has everything to overturn.

**Power ledger.** Stage 0 competence demonstrated before any power appears (Chapters 1–2, and the arithmetic in Chapter 1 is the proof). Stage 1 trace-sight begins in Chapter 3 with no crossing and no cost; the first Field Book residue appears there. Chapter 4 is the first cost-bearing crossing and the orientation blank begins there, in its first stage only: the remembered routes. **Milo is across twice in the whole batch** — the lane at half past twelve, and the loop at ten past eight on the 6th. The embankment and the arch stone are sightings, not crossings, and Chapter 9 he does not cross at all. Any later batch that inflates this number breaks Iona's correction and the signed report, which now records the two dates as *the fourth of Sazur and again on the sixth*. The Field Book is complete and the written route home is intact through Chapter 10. No sensory failure, personal blank, seam-scar, record blank, or authorial blank appears. The Lark token resonates once, with a memory, and opens nothing.

**Atlas residue used twice, both in Chapters 3 and 4, and both on the one night of the 4th:** "A return mark is missing" and "The lane counts." Both incomplete, both immediately limited by Milo in his own notes, neither used to decide anything. Chapter 9 deliberately has *no* residue, and Milo notes and distrusts the silence — the book has been quiet for five days and is quiet again on the afternoon it is most wanted.

**Clue chain, as on the page.** The bracket-and-dot hand is confirmed on two documents — the Chapter 1 timetable margin, where the ink carries a damp halo and bridges the printer's fold, so it was pressed wet onto a shut, damped sheet — the press is this week's, the ink is not, because the mark was not drawn for this stock and came off something older, and the fold itself is the office's own tray handling and dates nothing — and the Chapter 6 works-perimeter annex. The Chapter 10 carbon copy's upper margin mark is *consistent with* that hand and is written down as a possible third instance and no more; its lower mark is in different lead and is explicitly a different author. **Soren Rook is not named anywhere in Chapters 1–10.** In Chapter 5 Ada comes down the stairs and interrupts before Milo can say the name, and refuses in advance to be handed one. His presence is the hand, and the method of a polite young man from Crown Heights who asked Ada what the third bell was for nine years ago and collected the answer.

**The copied route.** Attachment 9 to the order is a carbon maintenance route copied from an older survey book. Its diversion line names a destination that can be dated out of use and cannot be placed on any current map — not an invented name, but a name people got older out of. Milo was offered it as the demonstration's route in Chapter 9 and refused it in front of the yard because a name on a public sheet is a claim, not a discovery.

**Batch-level win, honestly stated.** A one-shift supervised passage worked on the ninth, which is a witnessed incident, which is what Mara needed to file anything at all. The seal crew had no window, so the gate is still at eighteen inches. Bellwether residents have a temporary stay until the 28th and one provisional eleven-inch plan of a handrail with a deliberately empty third for people who are not here yet. The order is still valid. The route is still closed to the public. Orrin's order is still enforceable pending the hearing on the 14th. Nothing has been won that cannot be taken back.

**Batch-level cost, honestly stated.** Milo's orientation has gone from rooms he can get to by himself (two) to a page of correct, legible, useless handwriting. He has asked, out loud, in his own office, to be walked to a window. He has refused to hand over his Field Book and offered an extract instead. He has signed a report that admits its own subject is incomplete. He has not told Mara about the Lark token, and the batch's central emotional beat is mutual competence between two people who are both keeping something back.

**New supporting cast, now registered:** Dilley Farr (route-room supervisor), Cass Ardery (Survey Enforcement officer), Danner (contractor's foreman, first name unused on the page), Rue and Idar (Streetkeepers in Pell's round). **Minor proper nouns now registered in `bible/terminology.md`:** Verrick (the pump man on Lena's mother's list, *came back at the third*, alive in the works yard in Chapter 9 and silent), Ardin Ossick (the old surveyor who taught Milo to use his eye), old Mabb (asleep on Cedar Court's third floor, and the reason Ada goes up), and Wragg Street (the sluice from Milo's second year). Neris and Soren Rook are registered there too, with the standing prohibition on resurrecting the first and naming the second as the annotating hand.

**Owen Fitch's two entries.** He is a hole in the plan and an addition to the record at the same time. Mara put his initials on the Registry's 5th-of-Sazur inspection instrument and told him so in the Chapter 9 yard in front of eighty people, and he required the refusal line to carry those initials so that a reader in twenty years can see he was on the sheet and said no anyway. Milo says out loud on the fourth-floor landing that the paper is not clean. His second agreement — the third-floor stairwell check, taken off a sick Ada Fenn once a night, in his own words, unwritten, and on condition that Milo tell Ada and not the yard — is the second source of witnessing the volume needs, and the next batch must build a third rather than lean on it.

**Debt carried into Chapters 11–20:** character, training, and recovery share, as flagged in the batch outline's distribution note. Specifically — Iona's clinic as a place where the cost stops being theoretical; the Cedar Court housing appeal and who may speak for a household; Pell's absence and Tern; Tomas's withheld page; the *pending* ledger line; the missing hearing page; Owen's third-floor check, taken on his own terms on the tenth and unwritten, and the third source of witnessing that will have to replace it; Mara's procedural review, still live; and Milo's first private attempt at a single continuous route, which the batch plan expects to fail.

## Volume 01, Batch 0002 — Chapters 11–20

**Files:** `chapters/volume-01/chapter-0011.md` … `chapter-0020.md`. All ten planned chapters exist as finished prose, ~34,100 words. POV is Milo throughout. Pressure rotation as written: political (hearing, and again the argument), character walk through three civic doors, training and recovery (the route card and the clinic), investigation through the courier round, the turn (the false route), relationship and disclosure, political consequence, action, consequence, aftermath. **The training and recovery debt recorded by Batch 0001 is paid in Chapters 13, 19 and 20.**

**The hearing did not go well, and the batch opens on the fact of that.** The continuity order is upheld in full and the 22nd test proceeds. The only things won are the temporary stay to the 28th, a direction to produce the complete file, and a nameable absence in it.

**The batch's engine is civic, not magical, and it is a trap Lena Marr finds before anyone explains it to her:** the relocation payment is assessed on displacement *from a named place*, so the name has to stay on the compensation schedule and off the street index. A current address for Cedar Court ends the payment. A school place needs a tenancy the closure converted into a claim. A clinic letter needs a registered location. **The block can have an address or the money and cannot have both, and the only person the register recognises as able to speak for it is asleep on the third floor.** Nobody solves this in the batch and it is not meant to be solved until Chapters 58–60.

**Third and fourth source of witnessing, built and left insufficient.** Ada's unwritten third-floor check with Owen is the second source. The **third is the accountancy-paper rota in Rue's satchel** — a door-check schedule belonging to the courier round, with the rule *anyone can cross their own name off, nobody else crosses it for them*. It is written in Rue's yard, on Rue's paper, with Milo made to stand in the chandler's store for the twenty-two minutes it takes, and its third-floor line is Owen's own name with four words in his own hand: *not asked to*. It is five lines long, three of its five lines carry a name by the twenty-first, the round is two of four, and Ilse Oller strikes her own name off it. Ada's refusal is still the live question, and Ada says so to Milo in Chapter 13 and refuses him a wall.

**Milo's method changes and it costs him.** Chapter 15 is the turn: he draws nineteen inches by eleven of one continuous route, anchors it on **Owen's third-floor labour and Jo Lask's live interval** without asking either, and knows at one o'clock on the second night that a route with no count in it goes round — and draws for two hours more. The sheet is false, and it puts Ilse Oller through a wall of a stairwell, and Ada Fenn has to spend an afternoon getting her out. He never destroys the sheet; he files it *as false*, with the sentence *I knew that, and there was no way to put one in that did not require me to admit what I was doing.* Owen's prediction from Chapter 10 — that he would do the correct thing and then the other thing — is paid exactly. He discloses the **brass Lark token** to Mara on the eighteenth and **refuses her offer to file the disclosure**; she records the refusal, signed, dated, in her own hand. His flaw is intact.

**Interval, hand, and handover.** The count is called by hand and the hands change: Rue counts the drill and comes in four seconds slow on the second call; Jo Lask counts the nine seconds in which Rue was on her own and is the only person in the yard qualified to say that was nobody's fault; the marshal writes both the success and the nine seconds into one minute because if he writes one he writes the other. Nobody inside a seam calls the count from outside it, and Chapter 15 is the proof of why: a route with no count in it goes round, and no amount of a good line substitutes for a person holding a number.

**Orrin's argument is proved and unanswered.** Milo reads Orrin Sable's own words out of his own notebook **twice** — once in the works office on the twentieth, once in a yard of thirty-one people — and Danner independently arrives at Orrin's mechanics from a stop log in a nineteen-something drawing. The drill then *proves* the argument: the water went down the alignment and settled the ground nine inches over forty feet. What gives is the **service door**, which would take a steel plate in four hours and cannot be plated, because a variation takes six days and Orrin refuses on the morning of the test — and his reason is good, that the day he plates a door on a surveyor's rod without a variation is the day a man with a lever plates the evidence locker. Both men are right. Neither is answered. Milo's own record ends on the condition he sets himself: no further passage before the 28th, and none after it until somebody who is not him says in public that they want one.

**The 22nd test, stated briefly and not as a climax.** The structure comes out to full flow, the clay holds, the wall holds, the water goes where a foreman said in a fortnight it would go, and the service court comes out of it materially changed: a foot and a half of silt, sets standing in ridges, the handrail four feet out of true, three storage cages' contents gone into the river. **The culvert was throttled to eighteen inches on the sixth, is driven out to full flow on the twenty-second, and is never sealed.** Nobody drowns. Nobody is cured.

**Crossings and the power ledger.** **Milo is across four times in the volume: the fourth, the sixth, the eighteenth and the twenty-first of Sazur**, and he says the number out loud in a yard on the twenty-second because Iona Sen made him. Chapters 1–10's two are untouched and are not inflated. The Chapter 20 record lists the four with dates and places and the four rooms and four hundred metres he has lost, and states that the **Field Book is sound and the written line home is complete, correct and legible to anybody asked to walk it.** The **written-line loss remains booked for Chapters 51–53.** No sensory failure, personal blank, seam-scar, record blank, or authorial blank appears. The Lark token is **cold through the whole batch** and produces nothing; Milo's note on that is *I do not know what I expected and I am not going to write down what I expected.*

**Iona Sen's conditions are signed and binding.** Milo has agreed in writing (Field Book, 16th of Sazur) to four things: a named person outside holding a count, a stated number of minutes, being walked in and walked out by a person who is not his chalk and who can stop the work, and reporting to the clinic the morning after every time. He asks, in writing, for who walks him. The 22nd's last line is that he was walked home.

**Atlas residue used once in the batch, in Chapter 18**, at the ninth minute of the ninth of the drill: *By the fourth, a count is not a number. It is a person saying a number, out loud, so that somebody else can hear that a person said it.* Incomplete, limited in his own notes before he acts on it, and it settles nothing.

**The clue chain, carried and slightly advanced, not resolved.** The recurring bracket-and-dot is confirmed on two documents as before, with Chapter 10's carbon pair recorded as a possible third. The Registry's **certification mark of the 21st is drawn on its sheet** — nib down, no halo, sits in the paper, bridges nothing — and Milo writes that this is a difference in *method* and not in age, which is the first forensic statement the batch makes about the hand and it does not identify anybody. The **copy register is the batch's discovery**: the maintenance volume Attachment 9 was copied from went out on an ordinary district works office reference and came back eleven days after the date of the original safety report on a **Crown Heights repository reference**, which means somebody in the central archive had the book in the week after a pump-house failure. And the register entry carries **a second pair of unsigned margin marks in two different leads**, the upper a bracket-and-dot and the lower not. So the *pair* stands on two documents — the Chapter 10 carbon and this register entry — with the upper and lower in different hands on both, and the lower one asking *who gave the order* on both. The Chapter 1 timetable margin and the Chapter 6 annex carry the mark alone. **Soren Rook is not named anywhere in Chapters 11–20.**

**Tomas Rusk's withheld page is paid, and it is not only a bell schedule.** He gives it on the 21st at seven in the morning, in his own front room, with Mara writing verbatim. It proves the works shift interval at ten to two — one of five accounts, and it settles nothing — and it carries the maintenance ledger number cited on the withdrawn replacement page, which means whoever wrote that page had a mechanic's private book in front of them. **It also carries the second-shift roll**, and Jonas Venn's line on it is not empty: there is a next-of-kin contact, a living woman of fifty-four in four rooms behind a boatyard on the Ferry Road side, who has not been told and who has not been paid. Lena Marr finds her and tells her on the 21st and cannot answer her third question, which is where he is buried. The death record is **not** reopened in this batch; what changes is that there is now a name to ask about.

**Verrick is asked.** For the first time in six years, in the works compound, on the 19th. He was off shift, on the relief, and came out at ten past one for his coat. He said *clear* at the door. When he got to the other end the door was shut, and he cannot produce one thing that happened between the door and the frontage, and he has never been asked, and he does not know what the third bell was for. **He confirms no reading** and is not a device.

**Three readings, set out and left unranked.** Milo writes three families on a table leg — an evacuation, a count of the dead, a later reuse of an older route — and puts the five Cedar Court accounts under them, and Lena Marr tells him in front of twenty-two people that the boxes are his, made on Thursday night with a pencil, because a surveyor makes shapes, and that the day somebody comes with a warrant he is going to hold the table leg up and say *that*. **All five accounts and all three families survive the batch intact.** Ada's fifth floor of not saying is not broken.

**New supporting cast, now registered:** Hettie Brann (the water clinic's counter, a former laundry hand), Ola Petrossian (a clinic nurse who walks the route card and finds the fault), Ilse Oller (second floor, sixty, the woman the false route takes through a wall, and the person who strikes her own name off the rota), Verrick Assal (the man on Lena's mother's list, *came back at the third*, spoken to for the first time). Cedar Court's structure is now fixed at **five storeys**, with Mabb on three, Owen on four, and six rooms on the fifth that nobody has been inside.

**Nothing was won that cannot be taken back.** The order stands. The name is not restored. The route is protected to the 28th and no further. The service court is silted and its handrail is out of true. Jo Lask has lost his vehicle for four weeks and half pay. Tomas Rusk has said out loud, in a yard, that he sat on a dead man's next-of-kin address for six years. The compensation ledger still says *pending*. The bell's purpose is still unknown, and the seam is quiet rather than healed, and the quiet is administrative.
