### What the batch is, and what it is not

**It is the midpoint and its cost, not a victory.** Three honest witnesses describing one night three ways is *the price* of the answer, not an obstacle to it: Tomas Rusk is right that the three calls are a works bell sounded from the gantry and wrong only about the word *change*; Jo Lask is right that a count is passed from a person to a person and wrong only about the word *only*; Lena Marr is wrong only about the word *counting*; Verrick Assal still does not know and is asked a third time and confirms nothing again, at his own request, in writing. **Paragraph six of the original report is the load-bearing corruption, not any of them**: an engineering classification made in the first week — *the three calls are not a signal to persons and indicate no entry and no refusal* — that governed the case for six years, and that survived the replacement page while the sentence about the hazard did not. **Nobody is made wrong and the file is.**

**The order's act is now on the page, exactly once, and it is this:** the name was taken off Bellwether, the routes were shut, and the people who kept the routines were scattered — and one routine was not scattered, because the woman who kept it never left the river core. **Two withdrawn, one left standing and overloaded.** Ada Fenn states it in a yard, refuses the words *third support* and *broken*, and will not be put on the rota.

**Jonas Venn's death is separated from the lane for the first time on the page.** A fault in a second-stage main in flood season is a real accident; the concealment is a real civic crime; and the sentence that put *a hazard at the frontage* and *nobody at the frontage* into one document is the sentence that is the closure. **The death did not create the grammar and is not in it.** The death record is still closed and reopening it remains a Chapters 58–60 job.

### The return mark that failed, and why it is a handover

The failure in Chapter 24 is not a magical lapse and it is not Milo's chalk. He needed a turn and went into Rue's satchel on the Wednesday and read that **the second-floor line of the accountancy paper has a name with a line through it in a woman's own hand**, and decided that a line crossed off is a line occupied by nobody and therefore a turn. He wrote it in at 08.10 and went in at 13.58. **The lane believed his sheet rather than the woman.** Owen Fitch, who walked him in and out, stopped the work on the landing and did not argue with him about it on the way back; Rue said out loud, before Milo did, that the four missing seconds were nobody's fault; and Jo Lask is not the only person qualified to say so this time — the boy holding the counter is.

**The four signed conditions were all satisfied and the work failed anyway**, which is the point Iona Sen makes: *a condition is a floor and you have been treating it as a ceiling.* Her finding, which is not a condition and cannot be signed, is **that Milo is not to be left alone with a piece of paper about a person** — not once, not ever, for as long as he is in this district. The Field Book residue for the batch, used **once**, at the ninth minute: ***A line crossed off is not a line given.*** It describes what he did and not what the road wanted, and he says so in his own notes before acting on it.

### Milo's condition, and the pressure on it

On the 22nd he promised, in a yard, no passage in the district before the timetable review. **The batch does not let him keep it by simply not going.** On the 26th he works out that the sentence was never a restraint on his work: it was an instruction he had given *to a foreman*, and a foreman told that the district's surveyor has declared no passage will not send two of his own into a court with a tape. So Milo breaks it to un-break it, and gets a fifth crossing, a seventh lost route, and a fallen sequence out of it. On the third of the month he says the whole of it out loud in a yard: **a man who has promised himself a limit ends up owing it to other people.** That is the flaw intact, in his own voice, and it is not cured by the reversal.

### Crossings and the power ledger

**Stage 1 held. No advance and no new cost.** **Milo is across five times in Volume 1: the fourth, the sixth, the eighteenth and the twenty-first of Sazur, and the twenty-sixth.** Chapters 1–10's two are untouched and uninflated. Every crossing is named, dated, recorded in his own hand, and **said out loud in a yard on the 26th with Iona Sen's tally present**. The count is a person saying a number aloud: Pell Harrow from the path head with a trade counter, nine minutes stated in advance, Owen Fitch as the walker who can stop the work. The orientation blank has widened to **seven named remembered routes**, all recoverable when marked, and every one of them marked by somebody else: the office; a corridor of six years' use; his own front door; the four hundred metres between the pie shop and the chandler's; the consulting room to the dispensary; the ferry steps up to the chandler's yard; and **the two hundred and fourteen metres of his own route card**. **The Field Book is sound and the written line home is complete, correct and legible; the written-line loss is still a Chapters 51–53 event and is not hinted at.** No sensory failure, personal blank, seam-scar, record blank, or authorial blank appears. **The brass Lark token is cold for the fifth time and produces nothing.**

### What was won, and what it cost, stated plainly

**Won:** a printed and minuted statement of the district's loss of drainage behind the works; a public record of four incompatible accounts with four names on it, which is the first evidence in the file that is not a hole; the answer to Tomas Rusk's condition; the answer to the case's central historical question; and a fifth-floor line on the accountancy paper with a name on it.

**Cost:** **nine school places released until the spring intake.** Two of nine stops gone from the round and the round down to **seven on foot through a works gate for eleven weeks, unpaid**. **A letterbox in a hoarding**, gone, with no version of the district in which it returns. **A stay that ended at 10.25 on the 28th in one line and had never done anything else.** A court a foot and a half down in silt with a new dip in it and a handrail four feet out of true over the old one. **A woman of sixty-eight who has been the only standing support for six years saying, in a yard, that she is stopping.** And the low streets, which will flood in November in a dry month behind a lead lateral laid in 1904 and checked by nobody.

### The clue chain, carried one step and not resolved

The Registry's answer to the public request **confirms the copy-register line is a return and gives the shelfmark and the date**, and states that an internal consultation reference is not producible and that the officer holding one is not an officer of the district. **A new observation, in the same form:** the withdrawal reference on the file index (*p.9 withdrawn*) and the internal consultation reference on the copy register are **both four letters and two numbers entered by a clerk**, and Milo writes the resemblance down once and says nothing for four days. **The withdrawal reference is a Crown Heights repository consultation reference, not a district continuity reference** — so page nine of the order was withdrawn on the instruction of a desk that is not in the Registry's organisation and is not in the district's book. **The twenty-first's Registry certification is still demonstrably drawn where the Chapter 1 timetable and the Chapter 6 annex are demonstrably pressed; the difference remains method and not age, and identifies nobody.** **Two documents carry a pair of marks, not three.** **Soren Rook is not named anywhere in Chapters 21–30.** Milo got as far as two letters of something on the 29th and stopped, and Mara Quill left it where it was and said so.

### Pell Harrow, given his beat

He is absent for the first eleven chapters of the batch's material and then returns **for one day on the 23rd**. His lead has run out: the village with no official address is real, Ostrow's list is real, **and it is a maintenance list** — bridges, culverts, sluices, the low ends, with a column of *state* and one line reading *no access, road closed, residents not notified*, and **his brother's name in the left-hand column of that line**. One of his two sources is a liar and the lie was about a year, the year the bus stopped going there, so he was being handed a place built out of a timetable. He will not say the name, he will not be thanked, he says a count is not a favour, and he holds the count on the 26th from outside. **He is nobody's chalk and he goes south on the 28th.** This is the first purchase on the Volume 2 question from the courier side and it does not touch the Quieting, the First Atlas, or the One Survey.

### The new source of witnessing, and the answer to the case's real question

The reversal changes what the team is *doing*. It is not trying to reopen Bellwether; the order will be upheld in October and the name will not come back this volume. **It is trying to build a witness that changes hands.** The rota is five lines on a sheet of accountancy paper in a satchel, it belongs to the courier round and not to the surveyor, and the case's finding is that it is the whole of the district's answer to a check that has been going out every evening for six years: one line and a half, and one of those is a man who has written four words next to his own name so that nobody can use him, and a woman of sixty-eight who is **not on it and will not be**. **Four names on the sheet when it went out and one of the five lines still carrying nothing on the third of the month — the ground, the second struck through in her own hand, the third, and the fourth since the twenty-fourth — and a fifth-floor line that has a name in it since the third, in a hand nobody recognises, and Rue does not pin the sheet to the wall.** Ada Fenn's withdrawal is written as legitimate, unargued and unrescuable; the batch does not treat it as a problem to be solved and she is not asked a twelfth time.

### Nothing was won that cannot be taken back

The order stands. The name is not restored. The alignment is out of the timetable and the disused platform is going to be recovered. The service door is still unplated and a variation still takes six days. Jo Lask is still suspended for four weeks and half pay and his inquiry is still four weeks off. The flood levy's per-head schedule is still on two hundred and thirty-one people and Mara's rates authority case is still undecided. The widow still cannot be told where her husband is buried. The worker's death record is still closed. The public name is not coming back in this volume, and what the batch has is a page with four honest, incompatible accounts on it and a rota with a fourth name on it.
