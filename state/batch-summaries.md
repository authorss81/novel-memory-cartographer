### What the batch is, and what it is not

### The five beats the volume outline fixed for this block, and where they landed

1. **A durable provisional return route with several witnesses and a deliberate blank on paper, revisable by people who were not present at the first crossing.** Chapters 44, 48, 49 and 50. The **turn sheet**: one page, typed, four columns, a rule at the foot, **one space left empty on purpose and labelled in Rue's hand**, a nail behind a dispensary counter, a woman who holds it and will not read it, and one line in it written and rewritten by a person who was not there on the fourth of Sazur. **Three sources of witnessing now stand: the rota, the schedule, and a door-check performed as a written turn.**
2. **The method succeeds only when people are allowed to revise the route and choose where they go.** It succeeds twice and fails once. The fourth-floor woman fills the blank and changes it twice. The three answers are **not chosen**: a street, a way to walk from a door to the tram steps, and a memorial. **Lena Marr says she is not going to pick, and says it must be decided in a yard and not in a kitchen with a plastic wallet.**
3. **A false witness and a copied map create a second dangerous route, exposing the cost of trying to restore Bellwether too quickly.** Chapters 44 and 45, and the bill arrives in Chapter 50. **A copy of the district's own sheet, made on a works office machine, with a second route nobody authorised in the head of it, six names four of which had not agreed, and a rotten channel crossing that shut a boatyard for five days and cost a man of forty-one with no record the only way out of his own gate.** Not magical, not a conspiracy, not a villain: a man with four rooms to let and a shilling for a ream.
4. **Orrin's project is both an engineering necessity and a political instrument.** Chapter 47, in one scene, both true, unresolved. **He is not made wrong, not softened, not suspended, and not a final antagonist.** The political instrument is a works notice that says *not a public route* in the shape the order used, and a form on a district levy, and a foreman's certificate bought with eleven days of full rate.
5. **The residents must decide whether they want a neighbourhood, a memorial route, or a safer connection between existing homes.** They do not decide. Chapter 46 gives all three, names the three holders, refuses to let the holders be the same people, and leaves it open on the fifteenth and on the last page of the batch.

### What was not touched

**The Chapter 30 reversal is not reopened.** The third call asks for *clear* three times in this batch and is answered three times by a person at a door, which is the answer as fixed, used rather than debated. Nobody re-argues what the bells were for. **The triad accounting is not restated at all** — it is not used once in Chapters 41–50, and the thin remnant at the western culvert is not mentioned. **The answer to the bells may be quoted, used, and doubted by a competent official; it is not hedged and not re-decided.** **The order stands, the four-inch plate stays on the only door in the flood-control wall, the public name is not restored, and the address/money trap is untouched** — nobody applies for an address, nobody solves it, and the *no person available* phrase appears on a fourth instrument in Crown Heights as the correct reason for a refusal.

### Crossings and the power ledger

**Stage 1 held. No advance and no new cost.** **Milo is still across six times in Volume 1: the fourth, sixth, eighteenth, twenty-first and twenty-sixth of Sazur, and the tenth of the month at 14.06.** **This batch adds no crossing, and the reason is on the page in six words: he is under an awning on the tenth of this month and the door is shut.** The orientation blank is **eight named remembered routes and no ninth**, and he has walked four of the eight this month without losing any of them and has written down that he does not know what that means. The **Field Book is intact and the written line home is complete, correct and legible; he walked it twice this week. The written-line loss remains a Chapters 51–53 event and is not hinted at anywhere.** **Atlas residue was used once in the batch**, in Chapter 48, in **five** words: ***An empty line is a door.*** He limits it on the facing page before he acts on it and does not take the book near the porch until the fifteenth. The book is **silent on the 23rd, the 9th, the 10th, the 11th, the 13th, the 14th and the 15th** of the month, and wrote once, on the 12th. **Five dated pairs are now in the book — six days, nine days, three days, three days, two days — and he writes them all down, joins none of them to any other, and looks at a sixth, of one day, and does not write it.** The **Lark token was cold again on the 13th and he did not take it out.**

### The new problem Chapters 51–60 is built on

**Somebody in this district is putting the people who keep a street onto paper, and the people on it cannot take themselves off it.** Five copies of a document with a flood-control contractor's head on it, one of which has **Owen Fitch and *third floor*** in a space that was left empty on purpose, a fourth of the rule cut off in the copying, a copy in a school office's letter box **addressed to *the person keeping the street***, a copy in a flat in Wragh Street, and a man with two prospective tenants. **A boy of seventeen has come back from a fenland village with a fourth column of ticks in six houses in four streets in four months, in a copying hand, from a list a man there has had a long time** — and Milo Rook writes the two facts in different paragraphs and says why, and does not join them. It is a document problem, it is local, it is administrative, and **it does not touch the Quieting, the First Atlas, or the One Survey.**
