# Open Threads

## Central mystery

Why do forcibly erased streets persist as physical remnant streets, and why does the Common Atlas suppress their pressure while making Greywake more likely? The planned answer is the Quieting, the First Atlas’s unreleased Singular Mode, the Registry’s use of continuity orders, and Soren Rook’s One Survey. Partial erasures are deliberately *not* the answer: cutting one or two of the triad — name, route, witness — leaves a ruin, a false address, or a thin unstable seam.

## First-volume promise

Bellwether Lane opens inside an active tram timetable as a Cadence remnant. Milo must protect residents, learn the rule through observation and witness work, expose Orrin Sable’s continuity order, and pay a first orientation-blank cost. The immediate threat must be resolved even as the larger Atlas mystery grows.

## Unresolved questions the first volumes must respect

- Why does the Common Index behave as one authority when the First Atlas was built plural? The planned answer is a setting that was never switched off, not a design flaw.
- What does the Registry gain politically from a continuity order beyond the land? A worker’s death and a flood-control project are the Volume 1 entry point, not the whole motive.
- Where is Soren’s eighteen-year inventory of excluded routes, and who has seen it?

## Personal and relationship threads

- Milo’s route-token and his protected memory of Neris’s tram voice point toward Lark Street without providing a simple answer.
- Mara and Milo begin as professional opponents; the romance must grow through disclosure, changed decisions, and accountability rather than instant attraction.
- Pell’s search for Tern gives the Streetkeeper plot a separate human objective.
- Iona’s past with Soren and Tarin’s loyalty to the Registry should emerge as later pressure, not opening-arc exposition dumps.

## Antagonist ladder

1. Orrin Sable: local institutional convenience in Volume 1.
2. Halden Vey: sincere institutional stabilizer through Volumes 1–10.
3. Sel Iri: ideological foil who opens streets without adequate consent.
4. Common Atlas single-authorship culture: structural antagonist.
5. Soren Rook: missing father and final antagonist, foreshadowed from the beginning. No new final enemy may be added.
