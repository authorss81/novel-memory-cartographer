# Open Threads

## Central mystery

Why do forcibly erased streets persist as physical remnant streets, and why does the Common Atlas suppress their pressure while making Greywake more likely? The planned answer is the Quieting, the First Atlas’s unreleased Singular Mode, the Registry’s use of continuity orders, and Soren Rook’s One Survey. Partial erasures are deliberately *not* the answer: cutting one or two of the triad — name, route, witness — leaves a ruin, a false address, or a thin unstable seam.

## First-volume promise

Bellwether Lane opens inside an active tram timetable as a Cadence remnant. Milo must protect residents, learn the rule through observation and witness work, expose Orrin Sable’s continuity order, and pay a first orientation-blank cost. The immediate threat must be resolved even as the larger Atlas mystery grows.

The fixed plan lives in `outline/volume-01.md`, with Chapters 1–10 detailed in `outline/batches/volume-01-batch-0001.md` and Chapters 11–20 written. **The item-by-item status of Chapters 11–20 is at the end of this file; read that before acting on anything below, because several entries here are now historical.** Threads the volume has deliberately left open, so later batches do not accidentally close them early:

- **STATUS AFTER CHAPTERS 1–10 AND THE CR-015/CR-016 REPAIRS.** All four readings of the third turn are alive and have people attached to them: **Ada Fenn** — a pause to check the lane is clear, which is what her door-check actually is; **Jo Lask** — a handover: on a run the count is passed door-to-front at the third call and the tram does not move until it has been handed, so the third turn belongs to whoever is at the door; **Lena Marr** — a count of who is still on the street, which is her mother's list of forty-one names read aloud at the third call; and the **closure notice** — a count of the dead, *one loss recorded, persons to be withdrawn from the interval affected between the second and the third calls*. A fifth account now exists and carries no turn-reading at all: **Tomas Rusk's** — the third call was a works shift interval at ten to two, proved by the pump-house bell schedule he has withheld. None of the five can be separated with anything Milo has. Chapter 30 still has all five to overturn. Note that **Ada has explicitly refused to interpret the third bell**, so the working rule in Chapter 8 is Milo's own inference from inside the circle, not something a witness gave him.
- Why Bellwether entered the active timetable now, six years after the order, in the same season as the flood detour. **Open and unanswered.** The batch gave a partial physical answer — a live line under the embankment pulsing at sixty-nine, and a diversion corridor that the works keep pointed at the old alignment — but not a cause for the timetable entry itself.

The Chapter 10 carbon attachment carries an upper mark in the same careful hand, written down as *consistent with, not sworn to, a possible third instance and no more*, and a lower mark in different lead by a different author. **Soren Rook is not named in Chapters 1–10** and the hand must not be attributed to him without evidence on the page. The only presence of his method so far is Ada's memory of a polite young man from Crown Heights who asked her what the third bell was for nine years ago and collected her answer. The volume's other clue documents — the Chapter 6 replacement page of mismatched filing date, and the copied maintenance route — remain **separate threads** and may not be collapsed into the hand.
- **Why the copied maintenance route names a place that is not on any map.** Open. It is not an invented name: it has current neighbouring streets and a bus route that stopped serving it in a datable year, so it went out of use by people getting older rather than by a decision. Milo offered it and refused it in Chapter 9. The next volume's question starts here.

Note that Owen takes it on the same afternoon he has insisted his own refusal be recorded, and that Milo's own statement of the terms is not neutral: he wants it said aloud in the yard and refused to write it, which is the second half of the Chapter 30 problem about a route that is kept by whoever is standing nearest. Chapters 21–50 must build a third — a rota, a written schedule, a door-check another resident can perform — so the resolution is not hostage to one person, and must treat Ada's withdrawal as legitimate pressure rather than an obstacle to be solved.

- **Orrin's argument against Milo's map, now on the page and unanswered.** In the Chapter 10 corridor: the map did not make the seam coherent, it made it **legible**; a believed route is a used route; and on the 22nd the thing Milo spent a week teaching eighty people to believe in will be standing in the way of a river. Ada and Jo are the current options. Nobody has yet marked a route for Milo and then withdrawn it.
- Whether the Lark token resonates with Bellwether because of Neris, because of a method, or because Milo's private memory is making the connection. **Open.** In ten chapters the token produced one thing: his mother's remembered voice giving a tram instruction to a conductor who was not him. He has recorded that the resemblance is not evidence. He has not told Mara the token exists.

## Status after Chapters 11–20 — what closed, what moved, what is now more dangerous

Batch 0002 closed the hearing question, paid Tomas Rusk's page, built the third source of witnessing, and turned two live questions into larger ones. The detail is in `state/batch-summaries.md` and `state/continuity.md`; this is the short list.

### CLOSED OR ANSWERED IN THIS BATCH

- **The hearing on the 14th is now staged and it went against Mara.** The order is upheld in full, the temporary stay survives only to the 28th, and the 22nd test proceeds. Nothing in Chapters 15–30 may describe the fourteenth as a win.
- **Tomas Rusk's withheld page is paid** (Chapters 17 and 19), and it contained a second thing nobody had known was in it. It still settles nothing about the bells: it proves the third call was a works shift interval at ten to two, which is one of five accounts.
- **Pell's chalk is no longer the only chalk.** Milo now has a written arrangement with Iona Sen, a written request each morning for who walks him, and a rota in Rue's satchel. The dependency has moved but has not been discharged.
- **The third source of witnessing exists**: the accountancy-paper door-check rota, owned by the courier round, with *anyone can cross their own name off, nobody else crosses it for them*, and Owen's own name and *not asked to* on the fourth line. It is five lines, two names, one strike-through, and three floors of nothing.

### MOVED

- **The third turn: three families instead of five answers.** An evacuation, a count of the dead, a later reuse of an older route. All five Cedar Court accounts are alive and **none of them maps cleanly onto one family**, which is the point. Lena Marr has rejected Milo's tidy box diagram in front of twenty-two people. **Chapter 30 now has to overturn an organising principle, not five memories.** Do not let a later batch tidy the boxes again.
- **Orrin's argument: proved, and unanswered.** Milo said it out loud twice as Orrin demanded; Danner arrived at the same mechanics from a stop log; the drill demonstrated it; and the thing that gave was the service door, for a reason neither man can argue with. **This is now the volume's sharpest problem and it has not softened by being agreed with.** Milo's own standing condition is that no further passage happens before the 28th, or after it unless somebody who is not him asks for one in public.
- **The clue chain: one new structural fact and no new hand.** The Registry's certification of the 21st is demonstrably *drawn on* the sheet where the two confirmed marks are demonstrably pressed onto shut sheets, which is a difference in **method, not age**. And the **copy register** shows a maintenance volume going out on a district works reference and coming back eleven days after the original safety report on a **Crown Heights repository reference**. That is a person in the central archive, in the week after a pump-house failure, with an old maintenance book.
- **The pair of marks now recurs on three documents** — Attachment 9, and now the copy register — with different hands above and below on every one, and the lower mark asking *who gave the order* every time. **This is the Volume 2 question's first real purchase, and it is about maintenance records being used, not about the Quieting, the First Atlas, or the One Survey.**
- **Jonas Venn is now a person to ask.** Tomas's second-shift roll carries a next-of-kin line that is not empty. Lena Marr found the woman on the 21st and told her, and could not answer her third question: **where is he buried.** The disposal authority names a cemetery whose register for that district was not kept. The death record is still closed and reopening it is a Chapters 58–60 job.
- **Verrick Assal has spoken** and confirms nothing. He was off shift, came out for his coat at ten past one, said *clear* at the door, and the door was shut at the other end. He does not know what the third bell was for.
- **The relationship has moved one careful step.** Mara knows about the Lark token and about the district being his mother's; he refused her offer to file the disclosure and she recorded the refusal, signed and dated. She has told him about the second set of initials in the annex box and about six years of off-hours work. **Nothing is in a Registry file. Nothing romantic has happened. The vocabulary is work, disclosure, disagreement and repair.**
- **The orientation blank has widened and is now a list.** Five named routes, in his own hand, on the page, every one of them marked by somebody else. All recoverable. The **written-line loss remains a Chapters 51–53 event** and has not been pre-empted.

### NOW MORE DANGEROUS

- **The 28th.** Four days out at the end of the batch. The stay expires at the timetable review and the route will be re-cut around the service court. That is the only thing the stay was holding, and Chapter 20 leaves it as the nearest hard deadline in the volume.
- **The address/money trap.** The block can have an address or the compensation and not both, and the forms for the address are in Lena Marr's coat. The Volume 1 resolution must cost somebody money.
- **Milo's condition is a condition, not a restraint.** He has promised himself no more passages, and a man who has promised himself a limit is a man who will one day treat a promise made in public as a debt owed by other people. Chapters 21–30 should put that under pressure and should not let him keep it easily.
- **The service door.** It is the only piece of physical protection anybody has identified and it cannot be fitted without a variation that takes six days. A year of test, a bigger test, and a plate in a store. The Ordinary Maintenance problem of Volume 1 is now concrete and dated.
- **Mara's review is still live and is now about her refusal to testify about the second set of initials.** She has held a disciplinary disclosure on bad arguments for five days. Somebody will notice.
- **Jo Lask's inquiry is a Thursday in four weeks**, and Jo has said the interesting thing in public: *the truth is cheaper when it's a person than when it's a street.* Whatever the inquiry decides, that sentence is now in a yard and not only in a file.
- **Ada is better, and the rota is thinner than the building.** Five storeys, two names, one strike-through, and six rooms on the top floor that nobody has been inside. Her refusal is still legitimate pressure and must never be written as a problem to be solved.

## Personal and relationship threads

- Milo’s route-token and his protected memory of Neris’s tram voice point toward Lark Street without providing a simple answer. **Ten chapters in, neither Mara nor any reader has been told the token exists**, and Milo’s private motive is now visibly costing him other people’s routes. Chapters 11–20 are the natural place for Mara to learn that the case is not only a public one.
- Mara and Milo begin as professional opponents; the romance must grow through disclosure, changed decisions, and accountability rather than instant attraction. **The batch has reached mutual competence and stopped there.** Each is still keeping something: Mara has her countersigned routing slip and her fear that procedure is her armour, Milo has the token and the reason he is in the district at all. The next step is disclosure, not affection.
- Pell’s search for Tern gives the Streetkeeper plot a separate human objective. **Pell has left for the south** with a lead that puts Tern’s name on a list at a village with no official address, one of two sources being a liar, and he has told Milo he is not his chalk. His route-keep work and his search now conflict on the page.
- Iona’s past with Soren and Tarin’s loyalty to the Registry should emerge as later pressure, not opening-arc exposition dumps. **Iona has appeared once, has treated Milo, and has said nothing about the Atlas or her own history.** Tarin has not appeared at all and is reserved for Chapters 31–40.
