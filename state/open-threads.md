# Open Threads

## Central mystery

Why do forcibly erased streets persist as physical remnant streets, and why does the Common Atlas suppress their pressure while making Greywake more likely? The planned answer is the Quieting, the First Atlas’s unreleased Singular Mode, the Registry’s use of continuity orders, and Soren Rook’s One Survey. Partial erasures are deliberately *not* the answer: cutting one or two of the triad — name, route, witness — leaves a ruin, a false address, or a thin unstable seam.

## First-volume promise

Bellwether Lane opens inside an active tram timetable as a Cadence remnant. Milo must protect residents, learn the rule through observation and witness work, expose Orrin Sable’s continuity order, and pay a first orientation-blank cost. The immediate threat must be resolved even as the larger Atlas mystery grows.

The fixed plan now lives in `outline/volume-01.md`, with Chapters 1–10 detailed in `outline/batches/volume-01-batch-0001.md`. Threads the volume has deliberately left open, so later batches do not accidentally close them early:

- What the third bell originally was. The opening batch must keep three incompatible readings of the missing turn alive — check the lane, hand over to whoever is nearest, count who is still on the street — plus the fourth theory the closure notice supplies, a count of the dead, so the Chapter 30 reversal has something to overturn rather than confirm.
- Why Bellwether entered the active timetable now, six years after the order, in the same season as the flood detour.
- Whose hand is the old-ink correction in the Chapter 1 timetable margin — ink that predates the paper it is on — and the survey notation in the Chapter 6 annex? The same annotating hand is on both documents. The two unsigned marks on the Chapter 10 carbon copy are in different inks, so at least one of them belongs to a different author, and the volume does not settle which. This must not be resolved in Volume 1 and must not be named as Soren without evidence. The volume’s other clue documents, the replacement page of mismatched filing date and the copied maintenance route, are separate threads and may not be collapsed into it.
- What happens to the route, and to the Volume 1 resolution, if Ada stops ringing or is relocated? Her standing routine is the third support of Bellwether’s triad and therefore the reason the seam can be negotiated with at all, so the volume’s winnability currently rests on one revocable person. Chapters 31–50 and 51–60 must build a second and third source of witnessing — a rota, a written schedule, a door-check another resident can perform — and must treat Ada’s withdrawal as legitimate pressure rather than an obstacle to be solved. Recorded as a guardrail in `outline/volume-01.md`.
- What the western culvert’s pressure was doing before the order, and whether the thin remnant there is still carrying anything once the climax drives the throttled gate back out to full flow.
- What Jonas Venn’s death was, who concealed it, and what Orrin needs the concealment to stay official.
- How many current residents want a return route, a memorial route, or no connection to Bellwether. Lena Marr and Owen Fitch are the two visible answers so far and they disagree.
- Whether the emergency hearing Orrin schedules is ever held, and who controls its terms.
- Whether Milo’s orientation blank can be marked by someone else, and what happens when the person who marked it changes their mind.
- Whether the Lark token resonates with Bellwether because of Neris, because of Soren’s method, or because Milo’s private memory is making the connection.

## Unresolved questions the first volumes must respect

- Why does the Common Index behave as one authority when the First Atlas was built plural? The planned answer is a setting that was never switched off, not a design flaw.
- What does the Registry gain politically from a continuity order beyond the land? A worker’s death and a flood-control project are the Volume 1 entry point, not the whole motive.
- Where is Soren’s eighteen-year inventory of excluded routes, and who has seen it?

## Personal and relationship threads

- Milo’s route-token and his protected memory of Neris’s tram voice point toward Lark Street without providing a simple answer.
- Mara and Milo begin as professional opponents; the romance must grow through disclosure, changed decisions, and accountability rather than instant attraction.
- Pell’s search for Tern gives the Streetkeeper plot a separate human objective.
- Iona’s past with Soren and Tarin’s loyalty to the Registry should emerge as later pressure, not opening-arc exposition dumps.

## Antagonist ladder

1. Orrin Sable: local institutional convenience in Volume 1.
2. Halden Vey: sincere institutional stabilizer through Volumes 1–10.
3. Sel Iri: ideological foil who opens streets without adequate consent.
4. Common Atlas single-authorship culture: structural antagonist.
5. Soren Rook: missing father and final antagonist, foreshadowed from the beginning. No new final enemy may be added.
