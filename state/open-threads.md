# Open Threads

## Central mystery

Why do forcibly erased streets persist as physical remnant streets, and why does the Common Atlas suppress their pressure while making Greywake more likely? The planned answer is the Quieting, the First Atlas’s unreleased Singular Mode, the Registry’s use of continuity orders, and Soren Rook’s One Survey. Partial erasures are deliberately *not* the answer: cutting one or two of the triad — name, route, witness — leaves a ruin, a false address, or a thin unstable seam.

## First-volume promise

Bellwether Lane opens inside an active tram timetable as a Cadence remnant. Milo must protect residents, learn the rule through observation and witness work, expose Orrin Sable’s continuity order, and pay a first orientation-blank cost. The immediate threat must be resolved even as the larger Atlas mystery grows.

The fixed plan lives in `outline/volume-01.md`, with Chapters 1–10 detailed in `outline/batches/volume-01-batch-0001.md` and now written. Threads the volume has deliberately left open, so later batches do not accidentally close them early:

- **STATUS AFTER CHAPTERS 1–10.** All four readings of the third turn are alive and have people attached to them: **Ada Fenn** — a pause to check the lane is clear, which is what her door-check actually is; **Jo Lask** — a handover: on a run the count is passed door-to-front at the third call and the tram does not move until it has been handed, so the third turn belongs to whoever is at the door; **Lena Marr** — a count of who is still on the street, which is her mother's list of forty-one names read aloud at the third call; and the **closure notice** — a count of the dead, *one loss recorded, persons to be withdrawn from the interval affected between the second and the third calls*. A fifth account now exists and carries no turn-reading at all: **Tomas Rusk's** — the third call was a works shift interval, proved by the pump-house bell schedule he has withheld. None of the five can be separated with anything Milo has. Chapter 30 still has all five to overturn.
- Why Bellwether entered the active timetable now, six years after the order, in the same season as the flood detour. **Open and unanswered.** The batch gave a partial physical answer — a live line under the embankment pulsing at sixty-nine, and a diversion corridor that the works keep pointed at the old alignment — but not a cause for the timetable entry itself.
- **The annotating hand.** Confirmed on two documents: the Chapter 1 timetable margin, whose ink lies across the printer's fold — so the crease is older than the mark and the mark is older than the stock — and the Chapter 6 works-perimeter annex. The Chapter 10 carbon attachment carries an upper mark in the same careful hand, written down as *consistent with, not sworn to, a possible third instance and no more*, and a lower mark in different lead by a different author. **Soren Rook is not named in Chapters 1–10** and the hand must not be attributed to him without evidence on the page. The only presence of his method so far is Ada's memory of a polite young man from Crown Heights who asked her what the third bell was for nine years ago and collected her answer. The volume's other clue documents — the Chapter 6 replacement page of mismatched filing date, and the copied maintenance route — remain **separate threads** and may not be collapsed into the hand.
- **Why the copied maintenance route names a place that is not on any map.** Open. It is not an invented name: it has current neighbouring streets and a bus route that stopped serving it in a datable year, so it went out of use by people getting older rather than by a decision. Milo offered it and refused it in Chapter 9. The next volume's question starts here.
- What happens to the route, and to the Volume 1 resolution, if Ada stops ringing or is relocated. **Now on the page as a live problem:** Ada has been ill with the flu during Chapter 10 and Owen Fitch has taken the third-floor stairwell check off her, once a night, on his own terms and for his own reasons. That is the second source of witnessing and it is revocable in exactly the way the guardrail requires. Chapters 21–50 must build a third — a rota, a written schedule, a door-check another resident can perform — so the resolution is not hostage to one person, and must treat Ada's withdrawal as legitimate pressure rather than an obstacle to be solved.
- What the western culvert's pressure was doing before the order, and whether the thin remnant there is still carrying anything once the 22nd floodgate test drives the throttled gate back out to full flow.
- **What Jonas Venn's death was, who concealed it, and what Orrin needs the concealment to stay official.** Now on the page as a single ledger line — *Venn, J. — pump crew, 2nd shift — relocation due, pending* — with no cause of death and no next of kin, and with the sentence *no evidence was found of any hazard to persons at the service court* removed from the original report by a replacement page filed eleven days late. Nobody has asked Tomas Rusk about it on the page yet.
- **Who amended the order, and when.** The Chapter 6 replacement page is an incomplete record, not proof of a later amendment. A person or a desk did it in a hurry, badly, and expected nobody to lay the pages out in order. Open.
- How many current residents want a return route, a memorial route, or no connection to Bellwether. Lena Marr and Owen Fitch are now the two visible answers and they are worse than opposites: Lena is named in full on the provisional plan and Owen's entry is a deliberate hole, and neither will accept the other's answer.
- **Whether the hearing on the 14th is won.** Mara's temporary stay runs to the timetable review on the 28th and dies if the hearing goes against her. Orrin has said aloud, with four witnesses in the room and before a minuted hearing, that he will bring the file to the hearing himself if the culvert turns something up in the next three days. The 22nd floodgate test sits between them.
- Whether Milo's orientation blank can be marked by someone else, and what happens when the person who marked it changes their mind. **Partly answered on the page:** Pell's chalk worked and did not satisfy the Field Book, and Pell has left for the south and said so. Ada and Jo are the current options. Nobody has yet marked a route for Milo and then withdrawn it.
- Whether the Lark token resonates with Bellwether because of Neris, because of a method, or because Milo's private memory is making the connection. **Open.** In ten chapters the token produced one thing: his mother's remembered voice giving a tram instruction to a conductor who was not him. He has recorded that the resemblance is not evidence. He has not told Mara the token exists.

## Unresolved questions the first volumes must respect

- Why does the Common Index behave as one authority when the First Atlas was built plural? The planned answer is a setting that was never switched off, not a design flaw.
- What does the Registry gain politically from a continuity order beyond the land? A worker’s death and a flood-control project are the Volume 1 entry point, not the whole motive.
- Where is Soren’s eighteen-year inventory of excluded routes, and who has seen it?

## Personal and relationship threads

- Milo’s route-token and his protected memory of Neris’s tram voice point toward Lark Street without providing a simple answer. **Ten chapters in, neither Mara nor any reader has been told the token exists**, and Milo’s private motive is now visibly costing him other people’s routes. Chapters 11–20 are the natural place for Mara to learn that the case is not only a public one.
- Mara and Milo begin as professional opponents; the romance must grow through disclosure, changed decisions, and accountability rather than instant attraction. **The batch has reached mutual competence and stopped there.** Each is still keeping something: Mara has her countersigned routing slip and her fear that procedure is her armour, Milo has the token and the reason he is in the district at all. The next step is disclosure, not affection.
- Pell’s search for Tern gives the Streetkeeper plot a separate human objective. **Pell has left for the south** with a lead that puts Tern’s name on a list at a village with no official address, one of two sources being a liar, and he has told Milo he is not his chalk. His route-keep work and his search now conflict on the page.
- Iona’s past with Soren and Tarin’s loyalty to the Registry should emerge as later pressure, not opening-arc exposition dumps. **Iona has appeared once, has treated Milo, and has said nothing about the Atlas or her own history.** Tarin has not appeared at all and is reserved for Chapters 31–40.
