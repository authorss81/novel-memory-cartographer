## Milo Rook

- **Where he is:** Alderquay, out of the yard at Cedar Court on the afternoon of the third of the month, having just watched a name appear on a piece of accountancy paper and walked away from it.
- **Stage:** 1 — trace-sight. Unchanged and not advanced. He can identify a likely seam, draw a rough threshold and return route, and make a short-lived crossing with a witness. **He cannot stabilise, cannot name a grammar, and cannot hold a place open, and after the reversal he says in his book that he has stopped trying to name what the lane is and can only describe what it asks for.**
- **Crossings: five, all in Volume 1, and he says the number in a yard on the 26th because Iona Sen made him.** The fourth of Sazur (the lane, about sixty metres from the disused platform end on the west face of the hoarding), the sixth (the loop, from the arch foot), the eighteenth (from the ground-floor door of Cedar Court, out at the coal hatch side), the twenty-first (the same door, in and out the same night), and **the twenty-sixth (the same door, in at 13.58 and out at 13.58, on a count of nine called from the path head by Pell Harrow and walked in and out by Owen Fitch)**. **Chapters 1–10's two are not inflated and must not be.**
- **Cost paid so far:** the **orientation blank**, first stage only, and it is now **seven named remembered routes**, all recoverable when marked, all marked by other people: the route to the office; a corridor of six years' use; his own front door; the four hundred metres between the pie shop and the chandler's; the way from Iona's consulting room to the dispensary; **the way from the ferry steps up to the chandler's yard (lost on the 23rd)**; and **the two hundred and fourteen metres from the Cedar Court main door to the Alder Quay tram steps, which is the route he wrote on a card on the sixteenth of Sazur for a nurse of twenty-three to walk (lost on the 26th)**. The **Field Book is intact**, the written route home is complete, correct and legible to anyone asked to walk it, and the written-line loss is still booked for Chapters 51–53. No sensory failure, personal blank, seam-scar, record blank, or authorial blank has appeared and none may.
- **The Iona Sen conditions, signed in the Field Book on the sixteenth of Sazur, and binding.** **All four were satisfied on the twenty-sixth and the work failed anyway**, and Iona Sen's judgement is that a condition is a floor and he has been treating it as a ceiling. Her finding, which is **not a condition and cannot be signed**: *you are not to be left alone with a piece of paper about a person — not once, not ever, for as long as you are in this district.* He has not yet told anyone about this, and it is the one entry in the book for the case that is deliberately not written down.
- **The return mark that failed, and the failure is a handover.** He needed a turn, went into Rue's satchel on the Wednesday morning, read that the **second-floor line of the accountancy paper carries a name with a line through it in a woman's own hand**, and decided that a line crossed off is a line occupied by nobody and therefore a turn. He wrote it in at 08.10 and went in at 13.58. **The lane believed his sheet rather than the woman.** Owen Fitch stopped the work on the landing. The cost of the mistake is not a new cost — it is that he has now done to a *withdrawal* exactly what he did to Owen's and Jo's *labour* in the long sheet, and worse, and he knows it.
- **His public condition, and what it turned out to be.** On the 22nd he promised a yard no passage before the timetable review. **He did not keep it by simply not going.** He worked out on the 26th that the sentence was an instruction he had given *to a foreman* — a man told that the district's surveyor has declared no passage will not send two of his own in with a tape — so he broke his own promise to un-break it, got a fifth crossing and a seventh lost route out of it, and **said the whole of it out loud in a yard on the third of the month: a man who has promised himself a limit ends up owing it to other people.** He finished by saying he is not asking that yard for a passage and that if anybody wants one it is not from him.
- **The one change he has made, and it is a method change and not a warmth.** On the 29th he told Mara Quill that from that day anything of his bearing on the file comes to her **in writing, in his hand, with the date on it, whether she can file it or not and whether it helps him or not.** She refused the apology that came with it, told him what the refusal costs her every day, and did not ask him for the Lark disclosure.
- **The Lark token is cold for the fifth time and produces nothing.** He has written, for the fourth time, that he does not know what he expected and is still not going to write down what he expected.
- **What the reversal did to him.** He asked the false question in front of twenty-two people and was taken apart by a physician. He did not build a table for four days, which is the longest he has gone in nineteen years. He is now working on a witness that changes hands rather than on a map, and he has told a yard that he is not the man who asks for one.

## Mara Quill

- **Where she is:** the river core, under a review opened on her own paper, and the advocate of record on a file with a hearing in October.
- **Position and cost.** Continuity Records advocate. **She has filed a written disclosure of a personal interest in the Bellwether file** — that she has worked it off her own hours for six years in a records alcove and has not told her review panel — because the Registry will not answer a question about an internal repository reference unless the applicant states one. It is a filing she would rather not have made, and it is not a bad filing, and the review is now opened on her own hand. **The second set of initials in the intake routing slip's annex box is still unresolved and still hers alone.**
- **What she got, on the 29th, and what she did with it.** The reference on the file index against *p.9 withdrawn* is a **Crown Heights repository consultation reference, not a district continuity reference**, so page nine of the order was withdrawn on the instruction of a desk that is not in the Registry's organisation and is not in the district's book. It is the same form as the copy register's return line — **four letters and two numbers, a clerk's hand** — and she has two of them and has promised herself for a month not to make a structure out of a coincidence.
- **The relationship, one careful step past where Batch 0002 left it.** He gave her the whole of the cost of his refusal on the 18th in a corridor and offered her a written discipline she has not taken up. She refused to be angry about it, told him she is not going to hold it for him, and said she is not going to ask him for the Lark disclosure, **because a disclosure gets filed and Lark Street would then be in a building in Crown Heights for the rest of her working life, put there by four hundred correct jobs.** Nothing has been said about how either of them feels and neither is going to. **Nothing is in a Registry file about him.**
- **What she has not let happen.** On the 29th he got two letters of a name out and stopped, and she left it exactly where it was and said so, and asked that it be recorded that she left it there.
- **A professional position now on the page, in a meeting room, in public:** *a Bench will take four accounts or it will take one, and a Bench can look at a file that says four things and cannot look at one that says a thing nobody said.* The four accounts were read into a public record at the district's own request on the second of the month. **They cannot be minited by a works office, so she is having them sworn, which is a cost to Tomas Rusk, Jo Lask, Lena Marr and Verrick Assal that she has named in advance.**

## Pell Harrow

- **Where he is:** gone south again on the 28th, with Iona Sen's clinic address on a card in his pocket and a bag of sandwiches.
- **What he brought, on the 23rd, after twenty-two days away:** the village with no official address is real, and **Ostrow's list is a maintenance list** — bridges, culverts, sluices, the low ends, with a column of *state* and one line reading *no access, road closed, residents not notified*, and **his brother's name in the left-hand column of that line.** One of his two sources is a liar and the lie was about a year: the year he was given is the year the bus stopped going there, so he was being handed a place built out of a timetable. **He has not seen Tern and will not say the name in a yard.**
- **What he did:** refused to be thanked, refused to say which source lied, said **a count is not a favour**, and **held the count of nine from the path head with a trade counter on the 26th at 13.58 while Milo went in and came out on it.** He was the first named person to make the count out loud after the crossing, and he is not Milo's chalk and has never been.

## Rue

She told Milo, told him not to put it in a book, and asked that it be written down as a promise made to a Streetkeeper rather than a surveyor's note.

## Ada Fenn

- **Where she is:** Cedar Court, third floor. **She has said in a yard, in front of about forty people, that she is stopping.**
- **What she refused this batch, in public and in order:** the certification box on the amendment (a person familiar with the route); a question from the Board school about what the bell was, put so that a woman with a list could have a reason for a note to the education authority; **being put on the accountancy-paper rota, including by way of signing herself out of it** — *I am not going to be on that sheet and struck off it, because then I am a line with a line through it, and a rota can be handed to somebody else in the morning with a piece of chalk*; and the words **third support** and **broken**, which she rejects in favour of two taken and one left standing.
- **What she gave, once, and will not give again:** what a door-check is — *you find out whether the people behind the door are where you think they are, and if they are not, you do not go on* — and the one biographical fact the volume now has from her, that she came down to the river core in 1971 to find out why a man had a van outside her work, and has been at the top of that stairwell at six o'clock every evening since.
- **What she still will not do:** interpret the third bell. **She has now said no thirteen times**, and on the second of the month she told a room of forty that she gave the available morning away on purpose and that in about four years' time one of them will want a sentence out of her.
- **Her own words for what she is:** *I am a woman with a habit, and those are not the same.*
- **Why she is stopping, which is not a plot and is not a problem to be solved:** she is sixty-eight, there is a fever coming, two people on her floor are over seventy, and if she dies on that stairwell the only thing anybody will say about it is that the closure got her — and she has been a woman in a blue coat at a door since 1971 and will not be a woman who died in a street that was closed. **Nobody has ever walked up three flights of stairs to ask her whether she would mind stopping, and she said that out loud, and she is not going to be asked again.**

## Lena Marr

- **Where she is:** Cedar Court, the public record, the school counter, and a washerwoman's back door behind a boatyard on the Ferry Road side.
- **Her decision this batch, in public, with a price, and not with Milo's advice:** to file the Continuity referral as eleven individual households rather than one form with a household name at the top, because Owen Fitch told her to her face that the one form is a roll, and because a name at the top of a form that nobody offered is how a closure gets written. **It cost nine school places until the spring intake.** She told the yard the number twice and asked for no thanks and told Owen she was not going to repeat what he said in the street.
- **Her third-turn reading, corrected on the page and not retracted:** *my mother never once said she was counting dead people; she said she was reading names so nobody could be missed.* **She read all forty-one names out loud in a yard in the rain on the first of the month, in four minutes and eleven seconds.**
- **The closure notice, read properly for the first time in six years, on the second:** it is **one sentence with a semicolon in the middle**, and everybody in the district stops at the first half and looks at *one loss recorded*. *Persons* is a plural, and they are being *withdrawn from* an interval, and the interval between the second and the third call is thirty-one seconds long.
- **The address/money trap is still live and unsolved and she has not moved on it.** The forms for the street-index application are still in her coat. **She has not been to the widow again since the 21st and has said she will find out where Jonas Venn is buried and does not know whether she can.**
- **What she said the answer changes:** *we are not trying to open Bellwether Lane. The answer is not a map. The answer is a witness that changes hands* — and that she has been reading forty-one names out loud at that kitchen table since she was nineteen and did not know that was a rota.

## Jo Lask

- **Where he is:** suspended from driving for four weeks and half pay, pending an inquiry into the ninth and the twenty-first, working the yard and the shed. **The hearing is still not dated on the page.**
- **What he added this batch:** on the 28th, alone, in a yard, unasked, he read out the new working timetable and the fact that **the diversion loses eleven seconds at the Ferry Road turn and eleven seconds is the difference between handing a count over and not**, and that a count of forty-one seconds on a segment with no slack is not a stop but a hold, and that a hold is a car standing at a place waiting for somebody. He said he would like it noticed that nobody asked him to read it out and that he is the only man in Alderquay who can tell you what a count costs in seconds.
- **His third-turn reading, corrected on the page and not retracted:** *a count is a thing you say to a person, and there is nowhere in a bell to hand it to.* He is right about the handover and wrong about *only*.
- **What the reversal cost him in public:** he is named in a four-account public record that is about to be sworn, and he said so himself on the 28th — *it will be in it, and it will be the only thing in that hearing that is actually about a bell.*

## Tomas Rusk

- **His condition is paid and it is paid in his own words:** the road shut in the middle of a run because the crew came off the frontage to the pump house for a fault in a second-stage main, and a frontage with nobody on it is a road that is not being kept, and he was on that reach alone with a lamp and no bell, and he has had six years to decide which of those two things he would rather have been and has not decided. **He also said, for the first time out loud, that he has been talking about a bell and not about a lane for three weeks and that he never claimed a works bell could not be used for something else.**

## Dr Iona Sen

- **Where she is:** the clinic, a dispensary counter open in Cedar Court since the ninth, and a locked drawer.
- **What she did this batch, and it is the most important thing she has done in the volume:** she **named the finding that is not a condition and cannot be signed** — *you are not to be left alone with a piece of paper about a person, not once, not ever, for as long as you are in this district* — and she stated that all four signed conditions were satisfied on the 26th and the work failed anyway, because a condition is a floor and he has been treating it as a ceiling.
- **What she gave him that is not in any file:** **the nine other people.** Three hurt in the drill, four the same woman on the second floor of Cedar Court, a works-office man who has not slept since the 22nd, and a nineteen-year-old who went through the coal hatch side on her own on the 21st and came out and has not come back. Two hundred and thirty-one people in unlicensed rooms in the low streets. She has had a dispensary counter open in that building since the ninth and it is the best thing that has happened in the district in six years and it is also the reason she has not slept. **The number is in a locked drawer and is not going in a report, and Milo is the first person she has told.**
- **What she said in the yard on the third of the month, and it turned the volume's central question:** *for* is a word about a decision, and nothing in that yard decided anything, and a thing nobody decided has a use rather than a purpose. The question is not what the three calls were for. It is what the third one is asking for.
- **Unchanged:** she has still said nothing about the Atlas, or about her own past, or about the man who taught Milo's hand to let the pen do the work.
