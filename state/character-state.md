# Character State

Updated after Volume 01 Batch 0005 (Chapters 41–50). Keep this file to immediate state, wants, and known costs. Long-range arcs live in `bible/characters.md`; canon rules live in `state/continuity.md`.

**Where the district is.** Cedar Court and eleven streets in the river core, on the **fifteenth of next month**, with **twenty-one days to the floodgate test on Saturday the sixth of December** and **the works' closing of the coal hatch path running to the nineteenth**. The four-Thursday instrument expired at noon on Friday the fourteenth. Milo Rook's evening entry for the fifteenth is the last thing on the page.

## Milo Rook

- **Where he is:** the river core, out of a yard on the Saturday afternoon of the fifteenth, with four versions of a sheet of paper tied with twine in a bundle he does not hold.
- **Stage:** 1 — trace-sight. Unchanged and not advanced.
- **Crossings: six, all in Volume 1, and this batch adds none.** The fourth, sixth, eighteenth, twenty-first and twenty-sixth of Sazur, and the tenth of the previous month at 14.06. **He did not go in on the tenth of this month and the reason is on the page in six words: he is under an awning and the door is shut.** Chapters 1–10's two are not inflated.
- **Cost paid: the orientation blank, first stage only, and it is still eight named remembered routes with no ninth.** He has been to four of the eight this month and lost none of them, and has written down that he does not know what that means and is not going to write down what it might mean. **The Field Book is intact and the written line home is complete, correct and legible; he walked it twice in the week ending the fifteenth.** The written-line loss remains a Chapters 51–53 event and is not hinted at anywhere.
- **The Iona Sen finding is on paper for the first time and it has not helped.** On Wednesday the twelfth she made him write it out in his own hand, dated the sixteenth of Sazur, signed M.R., and took the page for a locked drawer: *you are not to be left alone with a piece of paper about a person, not once, not ever, for as long as you are in this district.* She then said, out loud, in a yard, in the rain, that writing it down has made it worse, because there is now a piece of paper about a person and he is the man holding it, and that it is on her and she would do it again and is not going to apologise for it. **He is not cured and he has been broken twice in this batch.**
- **What he gave away, and what it cost.** He refused, in a porch at half past four on the fourth, in front of about thirty people and at Lena Marr's request, to be the author, the holder, the clerk, or the person who puts up the next sheet of the district's turn sheet. His whole remaining part is named on the page: **the columns, the rule at the foot, four dated versions in a bundle held by Rue, the arithmetic, the reading-distance finding, and the refusal.** On the twelfth he was told the design had to be given away as well, and he argued for about nine seconds and accepted it.
- **Where he failed, and it is the thing he is good at.** On the afternoon of the fourth he had a copy of the sheet in his hands for about six minutes. **He read the head, the date, the reduction, the four columns, the rule and the route in the head, and he did not read the column of names** — because he has been told not to be alone with a piece of paper about a person — and Rue read them aloud and one of the six was a man who had never agreed to anything. He put his hand flat over the column, said it was a piece of paper about six people, took his hand off it, and handed it over. He describes it himself as *the same mistake in the same direction* and it cost two people who are not him: a woman of sixty who has kept off every list for nine years, and a man of forty-one who has no record at all.
- **His position in the October file, unchanged:** the level in it under a foreman's signature and not his; his interpretation of it excluded; the joint finding of the twentieth in it; three sworn accounts and one unsworn; the Registry's own classification in it and unexcluded; and the Bench of **Tuesday the twenty-first of October** sat on it, refused the classification as evidence, admitted the level as a document, and entered the count of two hundred and forty-nine as a count.
- **The Lark token was cold again on the thirteenth and he did not take it out.** No count is claimed beyond that and the token is not a key.
- **What is now on his page and is not his:** a document with his arithmetic in it, four columns he wrote, a rule at the foot he typed, and a finding of forty per cent legibility — in **five** houses and buildings, one of which has his name in it in a space left empty on purpose.

## Mara Quill

- **Where she is:** the river core, under a review on her own hand that **sits on the third of next month**, advocate of record on a file with a Bench after the twenty-first of October that he is not going to be in.
- **What she decided on the twelfth, in a yard, with about twelve people.** The promise of the twenty-ninth of Sazur — forty-four days — **stands, is hers, and does not get wider.** She cannot carry thirty people, and the day she promises a woman of sixty that nothing about her reaches her in writing is the day she has become a second Registry. **What is given away instead is the design**: a fifth version typed on Registry paper she is not supposed to use, with her own columns, held on alternate weeks with Hettie Brann, so that **on the week it is on her side of the nail the rule at the foot is in her hand and not his.** Rue wrote that sentence down; it is the only sentence in any version in Advocate Quill's handwriting.
- **What she refused, and it is the biggest refusal of the batch:** she will not apply to extend the four Thursdays, minute them again, convert them, or attach them to a schedule, because **a thing which is only ever four weeks is the only instrument she has ever made that could not be turned into a form**, and she is not going to be the woman who finds out whether that was true. She said it would not help a yard and that she is not going to stand there and be sad at them.
- **What she disclosed in public for the first time:** the intake routing slip's fourth set of initials, six years old, in the box that means *I have seen that an address is affected and passed it on.* She has read it four times since the fourth of Sazur and **still will not read it aloud to anybody**, and she said in a yard that this is what she is carrying into the room on the third.
- **What she asked for and got:** Tomas Rusk's mechanic's sentence, one sentence, true, unsigned, available in a room, in the street or in a book. She asked by telephone at nine in the morning because he is sixty-one and cannot be asked in a doorway. He attaches one condition to the account, has not decided it, and will not be asked about it twice; she has promised not to ask twice either and has told him he may tell her this side of Christmas or the week it is wanted and that either is his to choose.
- **What is now against the district's own sheet:** the Crown Heights refusal of the twenty-ninth of the month notes, among the matters considered, that a Bench minute permits one named person on four dates and that **no similar arrangement is proposed after the fourteenth of next month.** It grants nothing and it can be produced.

## Pell Harrow

- **Where he is:** back on the round at Cedar Court since **two o'clock on the afternoon of the twenty-seventh**, thinner, with a mudguard and no other visible acquisition. Seventeen. He is a courier and a Streetkeeper. **He has no power of any kind and is not Milo's assistant.**
- **Tern: no answer, and he has not come home changed.** He has **not seen Tern.** He was in a hut for nine weeks with the man who has the list in a tin and **asked him one question, which he will not write down, and got a true answer**, and would rather have had a lie. He has told Milo not to ask him what to do with it. The list is not a finding. **Ostrow confirms nothing and is not a source.**
- **What he brought back, and it is the batch's plant for the next volume.** A card in Rue's hand on the back of a delivery docket, put on the table face up, which he has never done. And a second fact he gave once and then stopped: a woman in **Wragh Street** keeps a sheet for three rooms over a chandler's, kept **nineteen years**, with a column for the room, a column for the name, a column for what is owed, **and a fourth column with a tick in it in every third line**; the tick is in a different, copying hand from the rest; **he has seen about six of them in four months in four streets**; the woman cannot tell him what the tick is for and says her husband did it, and her husband has been dead four years. **He will not go back and look**, because *the day I go back and look is the day I am a man with a list instead of a courier with a round, and I know which one I am and I have known since I was fourteen.*
- **What he said in the yard, and it is the design of the deliberate blank.** *A name with nobody behind it, you can go and knock. A space, nobody knows what is supposed to go in it. A space is an invitation to whoever has got a pen and an afternoon.* And then: **then leave it and put a line under it that says leave it. Not left because nobody has come. Left because it says so.** Nobody in the district had thought of the second half of that. The line went up in Rue's hand on the Monday the tenth at about eleven.
- **What he refused to do in this batch:** make the round into a service. On the fourteenth he said it plainly in the yard — *A service is a thing with a timetable and a man who can be fined, and this round is a man on a bicycle going to seven doors because a woman on the ground floor went out at eight this morning and could not get to the low end... that is not an answer, and I know it is not an answer, and I have said it out loud so that it is on the page that I said it.*
- **His mark holds for a working shift and not overnight, and that limit is still not tested.** He has not been asked to hold one overnight and must not be.
- **What he still does not do:** write a name of his own on anything, or answer a question about the route he has not been asked.

## Rue

- **Where she is:** the round, out at eight and in at four, and the trestle table, and the porch.
- **What she did this batch, and each of it is now a rule somebody else has to keep:** she told Milo at nine words that nobody had taken a turn and the sheet had come off the nail; she refused a day in the empty line because she has had a hole with a name in it for six weeks and will not go up; and **she wrote the deliberate blank's line in, in her own hand, in the same pen as the rule at the foot — *This line is left empty. It is not an oversight. It is for a person who was not here* — and read it out twice to make sure the typing clerk had got it right.** She holds the bundle of four versions tied with twine and is the holder of the accountancy-paper rota and is nothing else.
- **What she said that stops Milo, and she said it to about nine people:** he will be in a room with a piece of paper about thirty households inside of ten minutes, and she is not his doctor and is not going to pretend to be. And: *it is a rule about the school and about every room with a door in it, and I have watched a man be useful in a room four times and it is always the fourth time that somebody asks him where he got the list.*
- **What she still will not do:** name the fourth-floor or fifth-floor person, go up, report the round, draw the cuts, or put the dead lamp in a report. She has added nothing to that list. **She has now been asked twice, by Milo and by Mara Quill, and has answered the same way both times: I do not read it.**

## The woman on the fourth floor

- **Not named on the page, and this is now a structural fact rather than a piece of discretion.** Rue has carried her name on the accountancy-paper rota since the twenty-fourth of last month, with four words beside it in her own hand, *asked for herself, Tuesday*, and **has not said it in a yard since and is not going to.** Milo has not asked and has written down twice that he has not asked.
- **What she did on the tenth of the month, on her own, with nobody going up for her and nobody having asked:** she came down at about four in the afternoon because there was nothing on the nail. She said the column called TURN cannot exist without somebody agreeing to a turn and that she would not, and refused to let two unagreed pencil turns be counted as two turns. Then she wrote, in the deliberate blank on the nail, **\*Second.\***, and under it, in her own hand: ***I was not here on the fourth of Sazur and I am not here now by anybody's asking. Take it or leave it.*** **On Wednesday the twelfth she changed it to \*Third\*.** She came down twice in one week, which nobody in this district has managed in six weeks.
- **Why it matters:** she is a person who was **not present at the first crossing** — the fourth of Sazur — and she filled the blank, and she changed it, and the sheet is designed so that she can. **The first written turn in the batch belongs to her and it is the only line on the sheet nobody else has touched.**
- **What she has not agreed to:** anything else. She has not undertaken a door-check, has not been entered on any list, and has said she is not coming down again for anybody.

## Ada Fenn

- **Where she is:** Cedar Court, third floor. **She does not appear in Chapters 41–50 and her name is not written once in them.** She is not named, not thanked, not replaced, not argued back, and not put on anything, and nobody goes up three flights of stairs about her.
- **Unchanged and binding:** she has refused to interpret the third bell thirteen times and will not be asked a fourteenth; she is not on the rota and will not be, including by way of signing herself off it; her own name for what she is remains *a woman with a habit, and those are not the same.*
- **What the batch does to her absence:** it builds three things she is not in. The rota, unchanged. The schedule with a blank, which is the first source of witnessing the district owns rather than borrows. And **a door-check performed as a written turn** — the volume guardrail's second and third sources — held by a woman of sixty on the second landing, a woman on the fourth floor nobody can name, and Lena Marr for four Thursdays. **The batch's whole argument about what the district does without her is carried by Lena Marr's sentence on the fourth Thursday** — that she is doing it because a check goes at six o'clock and nobody has stood at a door since a woman of sixty-eight decided to stop — and by the fact that the third Thursday produced a second voice from a landing when a person was actually at a door. Later batches may not solve it, thank her, name her, or let a neighbour become her door-check.

## Owen Fitch

- **Where he is:** Cedar Court, fourth floor, and the stairwell, and a washing line behind a boatyard in the rain.
- **The refusal this batch, and it is a real refusal and is not a wound to be healed.** On the sixth of next month he came down at about half past five, alone, with a stick, and said no **outside**, at a washing line, because *in there I am the man on the third-floor line with three words next to my name, and out here I am nobody*, and because the yard would make it a thing that happened. A memorial route needs a turn; he is the only person anybody in this district can think of; **a memorial with nobody at the turn is a road with flowers on it and no way of finding out whether the people on it are where you think they are.** He told the yard not to put him on the sheet in any form and said he would take it off himself, in his own hand, with everybody watching and nobody saying a word afterwards. **Nobody thanked him, nobody healed him, and the only person who quoted him approvedly was Milo, in a book, and then only to say he is not going to pretend to anybody in a room that he has been on the right side of this.**
- **And on the fifteenth his name was put on a piece of paper for him anyway**, in a space left empty on purpose, with *third floor* and nothing else, in a clean copy in five places, one of them in a school office's letter box addressed to *the person keeping the street*. **He came down and said the sentence the batch ends on: to take a name off a document somebody has to say the document was never yours, and the rule at the foot is his, and if it was never theirs then it comes off the nail and on the fifteenth there is nothing.** Nobody explained it to him. **This is now the new problem and it is his, and it is not resolved, and it is not going to be resolved by anybody persuading him.**
- **The record, for the fifth time:** one household has declined to be entered; his initials are on the instrument of the fifth of Sazur; a refusal line has been read into the record against them at a hearing; his own capitals and three words are on the third-floor line of the accountancy-paper rota; his third-floor check is unchanged, once a night, going up, unwritten, revocable at will.

## Lena Marr

- **Where she is:** Cedar Court, the Board school, the trestle table, a washerwoman's back door, and her own kitchen at half past nine at night.
- **What she did:** worked the four Thursdays; **recorded the fourth Thursday herself as not worked**, in her own hand, because she was not at the threshold; led the count for the third time and **refused to put the new number on anything**; removed the *household* box from the second version of the sheet with her thumbnail in four seconds; **refused to withdraw the sheet** when a copy had put two people's names on it that had not agreed; said the three things out loud and did not pick one; and stopped the twenty-six people going to the school door from being thirty-one.
- **What she refused:** a fifth Thursday in any form; and being the one who chooses what the district gets. *If the choice is going to get made then it is going to get made in a yard by people who live here, and I am going to be in it, and it is not going to be me on my own in a kitchen with a plastic wallet.* **She is organising a thing and by the fifteenth of the month she is not the centre of it**, because the three answers are held by three other people and the rule at the foot is in Advocate Quill's hand on alternate weeks and the blank is in Rue's.
- **The price, unchanged and unpaid:** two of the nine children are lost for the spring intake because she put her name to a Bench instrument. On the seventh the woman at the school door said the number is nine and two and will stay two.
- **The widow:** not visited again in this batch, and not reopened. Lena Marr has still not been back, still does not know what question to ask, and the woman of fifty-four who mends other people's clothes is alive, unnamed on the page, and cannot be told where he is buried. The death record is still closed.

## Tomas Rusk

- **Where he is:** his front room, sixty-one, a bad knee, four years of a district works pension, and a taxi from the low end at three and six a trip.
- **What he gave this batch, and it is the batch's only instrument:** **the interval inside the pump house is twenty-nine seconds, not thirty-one**, and the reason is that the shaft is a two-inch flat on a six-inch drum and it takes up before it takes out and there is a lad in it with his hand on a lever. So the thirty-one in the street is real and the twenty-nine is real, and a person at a door counting to thirty-one is counting a machine. **The instrument is: do not count, wait until it stops, and then say it.** He gave it because he cannot get to a door, and he said the district had needed six weeks and a bell to be told it.
- **His mechanic's sentence, asked for and given, true and unsigned:** *On the night of the closure the road shut in the middle of a run. Not at the end of it. In the middle. The crew came off the frontage to the pump house and the reach emptied, and a frontage with nobody on it is a road that is not being kept.* Available in a room, in the street, or in a book, and it will not be sworn.
- **The condition, which is Chapters 51–53 and is not written:** he wants one thing attached to the account itself, has not decided what it is, has said so out loud so that nobody can say he sprang it on them, and will not be asked about it twice. Mara Quill has promised the same.
- **What he still will not do:** swear. He has not taken the account back. The reason is a wage and nobody has argued him out of it for six weeks.
- **The three answers:** he is the holder of the memorial position, in ninety words, from a kitchen table, with his stick against the edge of it, and he has said he would rather be in a thing that is wrong than outside of it.

## Doreen Sankey

- **Who he is:** forty-one, two rooms behind a boatyard on the Ferry Road side, nine years, **paid nobody anything for any of them**, and on no rate book, no district index, no rate, no tenancy and no schedule, and never has been. **One of the thirty-one rooms on no list, and the boatyard is not a place anybody in this district has ever put on a piece of paper.**
- **What happened to him, and it is the batch's cost:** a man who lets four rooms used a works office copying machine on the third and put his name in a district document that had six boxes and no agreement in any of them. The route in the head of that copy is his eleven-minute walk, and a man looking at rooms put his foot through eleven feet of rotten board on the old wash-house channel crossing and went in to the knee in a tide. **The crossing was boarded shut for five days and that put him on the wrong side of his own gate**, with the dispensary counter in Cedar Court and the milk and the only cold store within four streets on the other side of it. He wrote **\*I only read it\*** beside his own name, in his own hand, in front of about nine people, and nobody wrote it for him.
- **What the district owes him and has not paid:** a record. The Recovery officer wrote four lines in her own hand, dated them, and kept them — *No record of this person. No rate. No tenancy. No schedule. He is not on any list in this office and never has been. He was on a sheet of paper in a yard and that was not this office* — and would not give them to him, because the moment it is a piece of paper about a person it wants a file number and the moment it has a file number it is a record, and a record is a thing that can be produced.
- **What he wants, in his own words:** not a street. **Connected.** A way to walk from his door to the tram steps in the rain that about forty people in Cedar Court have never heard of, and the only thing he wants is that somebody can tell a stranger how to walk it. **And he would rather be hard to find, which he calls a stupid thing to want.**
- **What he is owed and has not got:** the boatyard man has told people that the surveyor is the reason the crossing shut, and Milo Rook has been told and has not gone to argue, on Nell Crale's advice, because he would win.

## The woman of sixty from the second landing of Cedar Court

- **Not named, by the same rule as the fourth floor and for the same reason.** She came out for the count on the seventeenth of the previous month, would not stop, would not talk, and counted, and Rue did not tell Milo her name and Milo did not ask.
- **What she holds:** **a street.** A name on the index, a number on the door, a shop that sends to it, a post that comes to it, a doctor that can be found, and a school that can be asked whether a child lives here. *I have been on the second landing of a building with no number for nine years and I have got nine years left of doing that and I would like one of them to be in a house that post can find.* And: *I do not care what the bells were, and I went back to sleep the first morning they went after the closure and I have gone back to sleep every working day since, and I would like somebody to say that on the record, because it is the only thing I have ever wanted said about me.*
- **What happened to her, and it is real and it is hers:** the woman at the Board school door read her name off a list to a man who lets rooms, and his copy went into four places. **Nine years of not being on anything, ended in about ninety seconds, by her own answer to a question she should not have been asked.** She has said the fault is hers and has said so in that order and will not have it softened.
- **What she did:** she came down at half past five on the fifth — the first time in nine years she had come down at that hour — said she wanted her name off it and wanted to be the one to take it off and wanted to be asked first and is not going to be asked first because there is nobody to ask her, **and crossed her own name out, wrote nothing beside it, would not be asked why, and would not let anybody put a reason in for her.**
- **What she will not do:** be the door-check. She said it and she is right and nobody argued with her.
- **She holds the second of the three answers and she is not a member of the rota.** Her two pencil turns on the Friday are not turns and she has said so and the record says so.

## The woman at the door of the Board school

- **Who she is:** she keeps a list, the list closes at noon, and there are **nine children on it from the low streets out of the school until the spring intake, two of whom are gone for good and seven of whom come back if the intake opens.**
- **What she said, and it is a refusal and it is right:** she will not be the door-check and will not be talked into it and Lena Marr knows because she has told her twice. *There is a woman in this building who can stand at a door and I am not her and I am not going to be, because a woman who stands at a door in this district is a woman who will be asked whether the people behind it are where she thinks they are, and if the answer is no then the next question is what she did about it, and I have got thirty-one children in three rooms and I cannot answer that question on a Thursday.*
- **What it cost her:** she read two names off her list to a man who lets rooms, one of which was the woman of sixty's, and she said so in a yard as the fault, in the order that puts it on her, and she would not have it softened.
- **What is still unpaid:** the intake. It opens on a tenancy, a temporary registration or a Continuity referral, and **every one of the three asks for a place**, and no third of the nine may be quietly regained.
- **What she brought on the fifteenth:** a letter out of her own letter box addressed to *the person keeping the street*, and a copy in a fourth-floor flat in Wragh Street, and she gave neither name.

## Nell Crale

- **Who she is:** sixty-three, Bridge Street, four rooms over a shop with a cellar under it, twenty-two years keeping the only cold store within four streets, and a milk-and-ice round on a hand barrow.
- **What she did this batch:** she went and told Doreen Sankey about the boarded crossing in about ninety seconds, without being asked; she told Milo Rook what the boatyard man had said about him and told him not to go and argue with him because he would win; and she said in a queue on the fifteenth that it was not the rain.
- **What she still refuses:** to talk about a man who has not asked her. *I do not talk about a man who has not asked me. But I want it said that a sheet of paper with a head on it shut a crossing.*
- **Her cellar is still under and the district's answer is a lead lateral from 1904 and the works' answer is November.** Five pumps are running on three streets, one of which is hers.

## The Recovery officer

- **What she is:** a civil servant of nineteen years, and the person in this case who could be dismissed over a number.
- **What happened to her this batch: she has been put under more pressure and not less.** A rates authority has asked for **a schedule of rooms, by street and by dwelling, within fourteen days**, on the ground that the district's figure of two hundred and thirty-one is disputed by a count of two hundred and forty-nine, and failing such a schedule the per-head schedule stands at two hundred and thirty-one and the charge continues on it. She has fourteen days, thirty-three rooms she has never been inside in streets she could not find on the district's own index, and **no way to make one.** She has said in a yard that she is going to write to a rates authority and tell them she cannot produce a rateable basis, and that this is the first time in nineteen years she has written a letter saying she cannot do her job.
- **What she said, and it is unchanged and is not a solution:** a rate is built on a property and a person in a room is not a property; the count of people and the count of rooms will never be the same number and a rate cannot be built on a door; the moment this city is willing to be told that a person is a rateable thing about ninety forms get simpler and about two hundred and thirty-one people's lives get worse. **The second count is two hundred and fifty-two in fifty-five rooms and twenty-one short of the schedule, and no batch closes this administratively.**
- **What she has now done twice:** turned the schedule face up instead of face down; and written, in her own hand and kept, a statement that a man of forty-one does not exist, so that the next time his name comes through the counter she can say truthfully that she has never heard of him.
- **What is against her from the other side:** the works clerk has asked, in a corridor, in words, whether the count of the seventeenth could be adopted for the schedule. The levy is a schedule and a schedule is a contract and eleven weeks of a regrade is paid out of it.

## Dr Iona Sen

- **Where she is:** the water clinic, the dispensary counter in the ground floor of Cedar Court, and the edge of an awning in the rain.
- **What she did on the twelfth:** she made Milo Rook write the fifth finding down in his own hand, in a yard, in front of about eleven people, dated the sixteenth of Sazur and signed, and took the page for a locked drawer and said she would not give it back. **And then said the thing that is the point of it: writing it down has made it worse, because there is now a piece of paper about a person and he is the man holding it, and that it is on her, and she would do it again and is not going to apologise for it, and it will not help.**
- **What is unchanged:** she has said nothing about the Atlas, or about her own past, or about the man who taught Milo's hand to let the pen do the work. **The fifth finding is not cured, is not signed, cannot be signed, and is now also on paper.**
- **What she is right about and the batch confirms:** the third Thursday produced a second voice from a landing when a person was actually at a door. She has not commented on it and nobody has asked her.

## Orrin Sable

- **Where he is:** the works, a trench, a foreman back on full rate for eleven days, a plate on a door, a four-day window in front of a floodgate test, and five pumps on three streets he is not responsible for.
- **What he did this batch, and it is the batch's double-edged chapter:** he consented, through his site manager, to a person standing at a door four times, in eleven minutes, on a form that reads *site* and not *route*, and gave the reason out loud to a portacabin door — a person at a door in daylight is a person in a photograph, and he would rather have one person on his site who is a person than a return saying nobody is here, which is what he has been certifying every morning since the sixteenth and has not slept properly on account of. He went **into the trench** and ruined a coat doing it. He put the works' notice up at 09.10 on a Saturday, on the same hoarding as the plate notice, and told Milo Rook to write down both the day and the hour, and said he had decided **not** to move the date and would say so out loud, and that Milo may write it down and it does him no good whatever. And he said, in a yard of forty, that he has read the drainage box and let the plate go anyway and would do it again on Monday.
- **The engineering, and it is correct:** the soft lens stops on the first of next month; a level has to be certified on ground that has stopped; **the floodgate test is on Saturday the sixth of next month**; **the window is the second, the third, the fourth and the fifth of next month — four days, and it has not opened** — and the trench for it goes under the only made public path on the reach, and the north side is not a path.
- **The political instrument, in the same scene and also true:** the notice says *the path to the north is not a public route*, in the shape the order used, from a different department, with no order behind it and no Bench minute, and the four-day window costs a path a building with about nine people in it uses, and the certificate he needs is bought with a foreman's eleven days at full rate and the resetting of that foreman's review. He has not been softened, made wrong, suspended, or made a final antagonist. **He is still right about the mechanics and nobody has answered him.**

## Rennick, the works clerk, Danner, and the works

- **Rennick** — the contractor's site manager. He gave the consent in eleven minutes, in a form, for a reason he said once to a door. **He refused to take the employer's head off the sheet** in nine words and then four hundred more, because he has been sued twice for a document that left the compound without it and cannot take it off a document he did not type. He brought Danner back at full rate for eleven days, which resets the review, and initialled Danner's note that he is doing it for the certificate and not the money, and put it in his employer's file, and said that is the first sensible thing a foreman has written on one of his forms in two years. **The head is on the sheet and it is the most useful thing about it, and it is also the reason a man with four rooms to let thought it was worth copying.**
- **The works clerk** — thirty-one, two years in the job, no first name on the page. He typed the turn sheet on the machine in the eleven minutes between the half-hour and the quarter to on the Monday the third, on the employer's stationery, and let a stranger use the machine for four minutes the same afternoon for a shilling. Nobody in that building is going to be disciplined for either thing and he is not a criminal and has not been one.
- **Danner** — a foreman on a district flood levy, thirty years in, on half rate under a review with no date, **brought back at full rate for eleven days**, and certifying a level over a trench with four feet in it that he has already said he cannot stand behind. On the tenth he put a variation of the second stage on the trestle table with the hours filled in his own hand and the *alternative considered* box filled in because he will not leave a space on a district form: reach to schedule level on or before the fifth of next month, certified on ground recorded as settled on the first of next month, no alternative available in the period, and then — *I record that the certification is made in the fourteenth day of a standard deviation in the readings at the trench, and I will not be able to say in a room that I stood behind it, and I am certifying it because the level has to be certified by a man and the only man is me.* **He will be asked about it and the paragraph of the joint finding that says a surface was made level across a place which had been two surfaces for six years went to a Bench on the twenty-first of October and is about him.**
- **Petts** — still saying *nobody told us*, every working day, and nobody has asked him to stop and Milo Rook has not asked and has written down that he has not asked.
- **Five pumps** on Bridge Street, the Terrace and Sazur Row; the gully has come up on the low end for the eleventh day in a row in a month with under an inch of rain; the district flood line is nine inches and the street was at six on the sixteenth.
- **The plate is on the only door in the flood-control wall and it is there to stay. The order stands. The name is not restored.**

## Verrick Assal

## Jo Lask

- **Where he is:** driving on the diversion. **His four weeks ran out on the twentieth of the month and his hearing still has no date on it**, and the interval guarantee he would extract from the tram authority in exchange for keeping the extra stop off the public timetable is **Chapters 51–53 and has not been asked for.**

That is the thing. I have been free at four for a week and I have been told nothing... and if I put four hours a week on a thing that has no name on it then in six weeks there will be a Thursday when there is a person there and a Thursday when there is not, and somebody will write down which, and it will be a thing about me instead of a thing I did.* He is the same man as on the tenth: **a man who is available at a time is a man who can be asked at a time.** He asked Milo to tell the woman he said the same eleven words he said on the tenth. **Milo was not asked; he was told, and has written that down.**

## Hettie Brann

- **Who she is:** the water clinic counter, formerly a laundry hand at Ada Fenn's laundry, nineteen years on it. She now holds the turn sheet.
- **What she said, in a queue, in front of six people, and it is the whole of her terms:** she will spike it behind the counter and **will not look at it and will not enter it in anything**; if somebody asks what is on the spike she will say it is a sheet of paper; if they ask what is on the sheet, or come with a badge, she will say *I do not read it*, to a badge the same as to anybody, **because the day I read it is the day it is in a book and the day it is in a book is the day somebody can be told to produce it.** And to Milo, who had said he was not bringing it to her: *I have been on this counter nineteen years and I have watched two of you, so you may take that as free.*
- **What she does with the district's paper:** she took Lena Marr's card recording the fourth Thursday as not worked, read it, and said *I am not reading this and I am taking it, and I am going to hand it to the clerk at the gate, and if a clerk asks me what it is I am going to say it is a piece of paper.* **She is now holding the sheet on alternate weeks with Advocate Quill and reading neither.**
- **What she said about the yard on the fifteenth:** the few people in it were because of the rain. Nell Crale says it was not the rain.

## The woman who keeps a list in Wragh Street

- **Who she is:** she keeps a sheet for three rooms over a chandler's, and has kept it for **nineteen years.** A column for the room, a column for the name, a column for what is owed, **and a fourth column with a tick in it in every third line.**
- **What Pell Harrow established about it and no further:** the tick is in a different, copying hand from the rest of the sheet; **he has seen about six of them in four months in four streets**; she cannot tell him what the tick is for and says her husband did it; **her husband has been dead four years.**
- **What is deliberately not done with it:** he will not go back and look. **Milo Rook does not join it to the copied turn sheet**, in the same paragraph, and has written down why — *joining them would be me deciding that they are the same, and a man who decides two things are the same in a book is a man who has started making the case instead of the record.* **Ostrow's list in a hut and a tick in a fourth column are the same kind of thing and are not connected on the page, and it is the next volume's question planted once and not answered. It does not touch the Quieting, the First Atlas, or the One Survey.**
