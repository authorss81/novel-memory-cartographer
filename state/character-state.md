# Character State

Updated after Volume 01 Batch 0002 (Chapters 11–20). Keep this file to immediate state, wants, and known costs. Long-range arcs live in `bible/characters.md`; canon rules live in `state/continuity.md`.

## Milo Rook

- **Where he is:** Alderquay, at the works gate on the morning of the twenty-second of Sazur and then on a tram home, having been walked to the works by Rue and walked the last four hundred metres by the woman in the pie shop. He asks, in writing, at ten past five each morning, who is taking him where.
- **Stage:** 1 — trace-sight. Unchanged and not advanced. He can identify a likely seam, draw a rough threshold and return route, and make a short-lived crossing with a witness. He cannot stabilise, name a grammar, or hold a place open. **He drew one map in this batch that other people could not follow and it put a woman through a wall.**
- **Crossings: four, all in Volume 1, and he says the number in a yard on the twenty-second because Iona Sen made him.** The fourth of Sazur (the lane, about sixty metres south of the disused platform end on the west face of the hoarding), the sixth (the loop, from the arch foot), the eighteenth (from the ground-floor door of Cedar Court, out at the coal hatch side), the twenty-first (the same door, in and out the same night). **Chapters 1–10's two are not inflated and must not be.**
- **Cost paid so far:** the **orientation blank**, first stage only, and it has widened to a list. Lost: the route to the office; a corridor of six years' use; his own front door; the four hundred metres between the pie shop and the chandler's; and the way from Iona's consulting room to the dispensary, taught to him on the sixteenth and gone by the morning of the twenty-second. **All recoverable when marked, all marked by other people.** The **Field Book is intact**, the written route home is complete, correct and legible to anyone asked to walk it, and the written-line loss is still booked for Chapters 51–53. No sensory failure, personal blank, seam-scar, record blank, or authorial blank has appeared and none may.
- **The Iona Sen conditions, signed in the Field Book on the sixteenth of Sazur, and binding:** a named person outside holding a count and a stated number of minutes; walked in and walked out by somebody who is not his chalk and who can stop the work, with no argument about it on the way back; and the clinic the morning after, every time. He also promised her he would not start a crossing while he is inside the decision.
- **Method, changed and not cured.** He has agreed in writing to a new rule: a route is only a route if another person can say it in words and walk it without him. He got there by failing. He writes directions in the reader's units now — *turn where the paint stops; if the paint has gone, you are at the wrong end* — and he understands that the door-check and a good route card are the same discipline, which is the deepest respect he has yet shown Ada Fenn's practice.
- **The false route, Chapter 15, and it is not deleted.** Nineteen inches by eleven, filed **as false**, with a page and a half of his own confession attached, including: *I knew that, and there was no way to put one in that did not require me to admit what I was doing.* He took Owen's third-floor labour and Jo Lask's live interval as survey data without asking either of them, on purpose, knowing what he was doing, and Owen predicted exactly that on the tenth.
- **Flaw, intact.** He told Mara the truth about the Lark token on the eighteenth and then **refused her offer to file the disclosure**, which is the same man who read his own book aloud in a corridor on the sixth. He records the refusal with her, dated, signed by both. He also wants the next copy-register entry and has written down on the twenty-second that he has lost the ability to tell the difference between a document and a habit.
- **The standing condition he set himself, in writing, in a yard:** no further passage in this district before the timetable review on the twenty-eighth, and none after it until somebody who is not him says in public that they want one. He also said on the eighteenth, in a yard of thirty-one, that Officer Sable's argument is right about the mechanism and that he is not going to pretend otherwise.
- **Object:** the brass Lark route-token has been **cold through the whole batch** and has produced nothing. His note on that is that he does not know what he expected and is not going to write down what he expected.

## Mara Quill

- **Where she is:** the river core, at the works bank, in corridors; still barred from Crown Heights unaccompanied and still under review, which now runs to the hearing on the order's own outcome and does not conclude.
- **Position:** Continuity Records advocate. The filing of a temporary stay to the twenty-eighth survived the hearing, which is the smallest thing she has ever filed and the first she believes in. She has **not** filed the objection that ends Volume 1. She has two live institutional commitments off the page: the additional schedule of the flood levy goes to a rates authority before the week is out, and the answer to *who asked for the withdrawn page* goes to a Bench in October.
- **Accountability, deeper than Chapter 6.** The intake routing slip's annex box has **two sets of initials** — hers, and one above it in a different pen, undated, ticked before hers. She has read it four times at night, in a records alcove, on her own hours, and has not told the review panel, and she has decided to hold that with a bad argument she admits is bad. She says plainly that she has not been doing her job for six years, she has been doing this case.
- **What she was told and what she did with it (Chapters 15 and 19).** Milo disclosed that this district is his mother's erased neighbourhood and that the brass token is a Lark Street conductor's token. She offered to file the disclosure as a protection for his measurements. He refused. She wrote the refusal down with the date and the words *offer made, refused, and recorded*, made him sign it, and then went on working the case. **She has not told anybody about it, and it is not in any Registry file.**
- **Flaw, still active:** she reaches for the defensible filing. She advised the block to come before the Bench as individual households, one at a time, which is lawful and gets nobody a block, and Lena Marr told her so to her face and she did not apologise, because apologising would be worse.
- **Relationship with Milo:** past mutual competence and into disclosure. He has told her the thing that matters most to him and she is not going to let him put it back. She has told him the thing she has carried alone. Neither of them has written a sentence about how they feel and neither is going to.

## Pell Harrow

- **Where he is:** still gone south, still on the lead about a village with no official address and a list with his brother's name on it, one of two sources being a liar. He has not appeared in Chapters 11–20 and has not been written out of the world: **Rue and Idar are running his round without him and it is thin.**
- **What has been said about him in his absence, on the page:** Rue cleared the post-slot in the works hoarding for eleven years and nobody has cleared it since he left; Idar does the Sunday and the Wednesday and has done them since Pell went south; the round is two of four; and the school pack that sat nine days in the slot is a direct consequence of his absence.
- **Boundary, unchanged and respected:** he is not Milo's chalk. His hour was spent in Chapter 8 and he took no credit. **He has not been asked for anything in this batch and must not be used again without a beat that shows the cost.**

## Rue and Idar

- **Rue** is a Streetkeeper in Pell's round, a woman, the reason the west side of the river core still got its post, and the reason anything at all is known about the ground under the low streets. She is not a friend of Milo's and does not become one. She has told him four things and shown him two, and she has set conditions he must keep: the five cuts out of the service court are not to be drawn or written, the dead lamp past the second hatch is not to go in a report or a conversation or a yard, and the round is not to be reported. She walked him to the works on the twenty-second because he asked in writing.
- **Idar** does the Sunday and the Wednesday and has held the round at two since Pell left. He has not had a chapter of his own in this batch and is not a device.
- **The rota belongs to them.** A sheet of accountancy paper, folded in three, in Rue's satchel, with the rule *anyone can cross their own name off, nobody else crosses it for them.* Its fourth line is Owen Fitch's name in his own capitals with *not asked to* beside it in his own hand. **By the twenty-first it had two names on it, one of them struck through by the woman who struck it, and three floors of a five-storey building with nothing against them.** It did not run on the twenty-first or the twenty-second because the works footprint was shut.
- **Rue's knowledge, now on the page and load-bearing:** the post-slot in the hoarding is theirs and the district is not; the drain opposite the old painted wall comes up in a dry week and has for eleven years; the ground west of Bridge Street is packed tip fill eleven years old; the round notices things that stop. She would like it noted that a courier's complaint and a surveyor's rod are the same information eleven years apart.

## Ada Fenn

- **Where she is:** Cedar Court, third floor, recovering. She was well enough to attend the hearing on the fourteenth and to do the third floor of the drill in forty minutes on the twenty-first in her own way, and she is not cured. She is sixty-eight.
- **What she has given in this batch:** she walked Milo to the tram on the fourteenth because he asked and it was a practical judgement and not a favour, and she said so. She spent an afternoon talking Ilse Oller out of a lane with the door-check. She asked for the fifth floor to be kept out of the drill. She is doing her own floors again and will not say how long that lasts.
- **What she has refused, this batch:** to have anything on her wall — not a card, not a rota, not a note, not a chalk mark with a date — because a sentence she said in her own kitchen turned up in a document in her own kitchen eleven months after a polite young man from Crown Heights asked her a question and wrote the answer down; to be thanked for anything; and, at the end of the drill, to let anybody help. She has **not** interpreted the third bell and has said so again.
- **Her standing refusal, restated in her own words in Chapter 11:** *a thing that can't say who's in it isn't a thing that can be argued about, it's a thing that gets decided about you.* She went to a hearing at which she had no standing to watch forty people say things about a lane, and this is the price of that.
- **Third source of witnessing, built and not enough:** the rota in Rue's satchel, born from the round, sent to her by her own instruction, refused a wall. She remains structurally load-bearing and revocable, and the batch does not resolve that.

## Lena Marr

- **Where she is:** Cedar Court, the public record, and the low streets, where she has now been to a house and back.
- **What she wants, made exact:** a usable address for the block, a school place, clinic access, the money, and a route that recognises the households now living there — and she has now worked out that the first and the third cannot both be had, because the relocation payment is assessed on displacement from a named place.
- **What she has done in this batch:** walked Milo to the school and told him the fault in his own route card; identified the trap before anyone explained it; refused the third source of witnessing on Ada's stairwell; supported the drill and got the floor order written down; went to Jonas Venn's widow on the twenty-first and told her, and could not answer her third question.
- **Her argument, unresolved:** she and Owen are not opposites, they are two correct answers, and the block cannot speak for itself because the only person the register recognises as able to speak for it is asleep on the third floor. She will not be the woman who has a correct opinion and no school place. **She told Milo to his face that he has been deciding since Tuesday.**
- **Her third-turn reading survives untouched:** a count of who is still on the street, which is her mother's list of forty-one names.

## Owen Fitch

- **Where he is:** Cedar Court, fourth floor, and the stairwell.
- **The second source of witnessing, on the page and unchanged in its terms:** once a night, going up, the third floor, stopping when he likes, not the ground floor, not the second, and **unwritten** — agreed with Milo on the tenth on condition that Milo tells Ada and not the yard.
- **The third source, and this is new:** in Chapter 14 he wrote his own name on the third-floor line of the accountancy-paper rota and, beside it, in his own hand, **four words: *not asked to.*** Nobody wrote them for him. He wrote them because the terms are his and a rota that recorded the terms would turn his labour into a job a landlord could ask about. **Milo supplied the paper and was not in the room.** He told the yard not to thank him for the hole in the drill order, because a thanked man's hole becomes his own.
- **What he has refused, this batch:** to be entered in the appeal; to speak for the block; to let a Continuity referral carry nine children's names; to have the fourth floor put in the drill; to have a name crossed off the rota by anybody but the person it belongs to. He is not to be thanked and he is not to be argued with.
- **His initials, still a hole and an addition at once:** the refusal line was read into the record of the fourteenth hearing in these words — *One household has declined to be entered. Initials already held on the instrument of the fifth. Said no with knowledge of them.* A clerk tried to tidy it and Mara stopped him in open court. **Do not let a later batch treat that line as clean paper or let him be scrubbed.**
- **What he did in Chapters 18–20:** enforced the drill's own clause that a person who changes their mind stops everybody, went up the second floor nobody had told him he was going up, went up the fifth floor at four in the morning unasked with a millimetre tape, and answered the only question he was asked with *I do not know, and I will be in the yard if you want to ask me again.* Iona Sen called that the first correct answer anybody has given that woman in six years.

## Jo Lask

- **Where he is:** suspended from driving for four weeks and half pay, on a Thursday in four weeks' time, pending an inquiry into the ninth and the twenty-first. He is not suspended from working and he went out on the tram on the twenty-second and watched a replacement take his run past the disused platform at the speed of a stop that is not there.
- **What it cost and what he said about it:** both days were him doing his job on a public instruction from a man with a bandage, and the minute is his defence and his charge. *The truth is cheaper when it's a person than when it's a street, and the only reason I have been able to be that person is that a stranger with a book was standing on an embankment on the fourth of Sazur and believed me.*
- **What happened to his interval:** Milo used Jo Lask's live interval as a datum on a private map without asking. Jo is not a rival in this; he is precise about it. *You took my live interval. It's the only thing in this city that's true at a given second and you laid it on a piece of paper like a ruler.* **Nobody inside a seam may call the count from outside it**, and Jo holds that rule as a professional standard, not a mystical one.
- **His third-turn reading survives untouched:** a handover, door to front, at the third call, and the tram does not move until it has been handed. He still holds the argument that there was never a bell at that end of the line.

## Tomas Rusk

- **Where he is:** the works, second-shift knowledge, thirty years of it, and a bad knee. He gave the book away on the twenty-first at ten to seven in the morning, in his own front room, with Mara Quill writing verbatim and Milo turning away to look at the washing.
- **What the book contains, and what it settled:** the works bell schedule with the reach and the interval, which proves the third call was a works shift interval at ten to two and settles **nothing**; the maintenance ledger number cited on the withdrawn replacement page, which means whoever wrote that page had his book in front of them; and the second-shift roll for that week, on which Jonas Venn's line is not empty.
- **What it cost him, in his own words:** *I have had six years and a first-class reason and I have not done it, and I want that on whatever page you people are keeping, and I am not going to be thanked for handing it over, and I am not going to be forgiven for not handing it over, and both of those are correct and I'd like the yard to hear the second one.* He would not say the widow's name out loud on a set of steps.
- **His condition is paid:** the road shut in the middle of a run. He has not been given the answer and the batch does not supply one. That is the Chapters 21–30 hinge.

## Verrick Assal

- **Where he is:** the works compound, mending chain, alive, on a wage, fifty-eight.
- **What he said, on the nineteenth, for the first time in six years:** he was off shift on the relief and came out at ten past one for his coat; the three calls go at ten to two; he went up with the second call because you do not stand in a doorway in that reach; a woman in a blue coat was at the door and he said *clear* to her, which is the word, and she ticked him off, which is the line through his name on Lena's mother's list; and when he got to the other end **the door was shut and there was nobody at it, and he cannot produce one thing that happened between the door and the frontage.** He does not know what the third bell was for. He has never been asked and he would like that noticed.
- **He confirms no reading of the third turn and must not be used as a device.** He is a man who came back for a coat.

## Jonas Venn

- **Where he is:** a name in a compensation ledger with *relocation due, pending* and no cause of death and no next of kin — until the twenty-first.
- **What changed:** Tomas Rusk's second-shift roll has a next-of-kin line under his name and it is not empty. **Lena Marr found the woman on the twenty-first at about half past ten and told her in four sentences.** She is fifty-four, she mends other people's clothes, she has a daughter in Wragg Street, and nobody had been to see him. **Where he is buried cannot be answered:** the disposal authority names a cemetery whose register for that district was not kept after the closure. The death record is **not** reopened in this batch. What exists now is a person who can be asked, and a debt owed to her for six years, and a place of burial that nobody in Alderquay can name.
- **He is a real death and a concealed civic crime, and he did not create Bellwether.**

## Orrin Sable

- **Where he is:** the works, a seal kit he still cannot use and does not need on the twenty-second, and a variation he will not sign.
- **What the batch settled about him:** his best argument is **proved**. The drill made the route used, the water went down the alignment, and forty feet of the service court settled nine inches. He is right about the mechanics and was right independently arrived at by his own foreman.
- **What he refused and why, and it is good:** he would not have a steel plate put over a personnel door on the morning of a floodgate test on the word of a surveyor's rod, without a variation, without a hearing, and without telling the district works office why — because on the twenty-fourth a man with a lever will be able to plate the evidence locker, on the precedent. *I have been the local convenience for six years and I am not going to be the local convenience in the other direction on a Monday morning, because that is the same job with a nicer coat on it.*
- **Pressure, still concrete and still dated:** a standing instruction from the Prefect's office, a bond that lapsed on the eleventh and put the works on a flood levy that lands on the poorest people in the district, and a test that has now been run.
- **Unanswered:** both men are right and something has to give, and on the twenty-second it gave the door, and neither of them said the thing that matters. He is legible, unexcused, and not a final antagonist.

## Danner, foreman, Floodgate Four

- **What he did in this batch:** put a rod through the personnel door on the twenty-first and found fourteen feet of clay where the drawing says, better than the drawing, and said out loud that it changed nothing because the door stands eleven inches over the low point of the old alignment. Broke down the winch for the test. Wrote the gauge reading in his own hand. Said the water coming up to a door and stopping at it is not luck and is a man in nineteen-something being right for a hundred and ten years.
- **His price, unchanged and unexcused:** a variation takes six days, the test is on the twenty-second, and the eleven per cent is now a levy on the low streets. **First name still unused on the page.**

## Dr Iona Sen

- **Where she is:** the clinic, and in a yard on the twenty-second with a bag of plasters, having been in the district since half past six without being asked.
- **What she did in this batch, which is a real change in the relationship:** she examined Milo for an hour on the sixteenth, timed him, made him learn a route in an afternoon, gave him a clinic card with instructions for a constable and a word to say at the ferry steps, and extracted **four conditions in writing that he signed in his own Field Book**, including that the person who walks him can stop the work and that he does not get to argue about it on the way back. She reads out his count in a yard when he will not.
- **What she still has not said:** anything about the Atlas, or about her own past, or about the man who taught Milo's hand to let the pen do the work. She has noticed it. She has not followed it.
- **Her principle, unchanged:** a witness has to be able to stop, and the man holding the pattern has to be the one who is allowed to put it down. In Chapter 20 she endorses Owen's *I do not know* as the correct answer.

## Hettie Brann, Ola Petrossian, Ilse Oller, the marshal, the woman in the pie shop

- **Hettie Brann** runs the counter at the water clinic and was a laundry hand at Ada Fenn's laundry. She states the postal queue without sentiment — a letter to a closed street goes to Crown Heights, comes back stamped *no registered location*, and kills the appointment — and she says she has done it about four hundred times and that the people who need the counter cannot read the board and the people who can read the board do not need it.
- **Ola Petrossian** is a clinic nurse of twenty-three, four months in the job, who has never been past the tram steps and who walks Milo's route card and finds its two faults, one of which is a fence.
- **Ilse Oller** is sixty, on the second landing of Cedar Court, said one sentence in a ground-floor kitchen on the night of the fourth of Sazur about stairwells and was not spoken to again until the fifteenth. The false route takes her through a wall, Ada gets her out, she will not come down for days, and she strikes her own name off the rota because she would rather be one of the people who says no. **She is not a device and not a casualty; she is the third source of witnessing demonstrating that it is revocable.**
- **The marshal** at the works is nine years on that site, has never had a resident in a yard at eight at night, and writes the drill's success and its nine seconds into one minute.
- **The woman in the pie shop** has walked Milo round the corner for nine days and refuses to talk about it, because he told her on the sixth that there was nothing to say, and she listened.
