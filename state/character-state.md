# Character State

Updated after Volume 01 Batch 0001 (Chapters 1–10). Keep this file to immediate state, wants, and known costs. Long-range arcs live in `bible/characters.md`; canon rules live in `state/continuity.md`.

## Milo Rook

- **Where he is:** Alderquay. Recovering at Iona Sen's clinic after **two** crossings — Chapter 4's lane and Chapter 8's loop — sleeping badly, and unable to find familiar rooms unaided. He does not cross in Chapter 9 and says so in his own signed report.
- **Stage:** 1 — trace-sight. He can identify a likely seam, draw a rough threshold and return route, and make a short-lived crossing with a witness. He cannot stabilise a seam, identify a grammar with certainty, or hold a place open by drawing.
- **Cost paid so far:** the **orientation blank**, first stage only. The *remembered* routes have gone: the route to the Civic Atlas Office, a corridor he has used for six years, the way to the hearing room, and his own front door. They return only when another person marks them. The **Field Book is intact** and the written route home is complete, correct, and legible to anyone else; it simply no longer tells him which way to turn. The written-line loss belongs to Chapters 51–53 and must not be pre-empted. No sensory failure, personal blank, seam-scar, record blank, or authorial blank has appeared.
- **Working rule he can state:** Bellwether carries people through a changed street while the interval is kept, and the third turn is a place to yield rather than a position to fill. Both halves are earned by work, not by a system prompt. He does **not** know what the third bell originally was.
- **Object:** the brass Lark route-token has produced exactly one thing in ten chapters — his mother's remembered voice giving a tram instruction. It pulls toward private memory, not toward a location. It is not a key and has never opened anything.
- **Private motive, and its cost:** he wants Lark Street to exist in a record. He has begun to write *do not use a person as a condition* on the back of his hand. The flaw is intact — he still hoards, still works through injury, and still wants a clean route. Chapter 9 is the one time he chose the public record over the private map, and he paid for it by signing an incomplete result.
- **Method change, earned:** he now refuses to supply a new turn, and he has asked a man to stop him. He cannot be trusted to do that reliably.
- **Precedent he has set for himself:** in Chapter 9 he struck five working routes out of the official inspection sheet on his own motion, because they belonged to residents who had not consented and he had no authority to publish them. He reasoned that a man with no authority has no business publishing a route that belongs to somebody else, and he left the line-through in his own hand under his own name. He has now made a document weaker on purpose, in public, and it was not received as a good idea.
- **Practical dependency:** he needs someone to take him where he is going. Pell has said no; Ada and Jo are the current options. The next batch should show him asking.

## Mara Quill

- **Where she is:** Crown Heights, in corridors, under a procedural review for invoking a protective finding against its date.
- **Position:** Continuity Records advocate. Has lost preferred access — she may no longer enter a file in Crown Heights unaccompanied — and is under review until the hearing on the 14th. She has filed a temporary stay that expires at the timetable review on the 28th, which is the smallest filing of her career and the first she believes will hold. **She has not filed the objection that ends Volume 1.**
- **New accountability fact, in her own hand:** at twenty-five she countersigned the intake routing slip on the Bellwether file in block capitals, ticking the annex box. She saw that an address was affected and passed it on. She found the swapped replacement page and the *pending* ledger line herself.
- **Relationship with Milo:** moved from nuisance to possible witness and future liability. She declined to seize his Field Book, made him read it aloud instead, and has now watched him protect a refusal she would previously have logged as an obstruction. She has admitted to him that she has chosen a side. She still does not know about the Lark token's purpose and he has not told her.
- **Flaw, still active:** she reaches for the defensible filing. Her flaw has not been cured, only aimed harder.

## Pell Harrow

- **Where he is:** gone south, possibly for some weeks, following a lead that puts Tern's name on a list at a village with no official address. He has said one of his two sources is a liar and he does not know which.
- **What he did:** held the count from outside the seam in Chapter 4 (he reported "most of an hour… I counted forty-two and then I stopped counting, because I started thinking about my round"), chalked the walk line and held the road with Rue and Idar in Chapter 7, took his marks up again when the water went down because he does not leave marks in ground people will walk back through, and in Chapter 8 called the interval from the tram side with the lamp and a bicycle bell. He marked the bollard on Ferry Road on the tenth, not the route to the route room. He has been Milo's chalk twice, the ninth and the tenth, and has said in terms that it will not be a third time.
- **Boundary set:** one shift, then his own search. He has told Milo plainly that he is not Milo's chalk, and that "not gone" and "not here" are different.
- **He is not recruited and not Milo's assistant.** He has a separate goal, and Milo's case has delayed it twice.

**Rue** and **Idar** — Streetkeepers in Pell Harrow's delivery round. In Chapter 7 they hold the road at the works approach with lamps. In Chapter 9 they mark their own ways in and out of the service court on the fourth column of the official inspection sheet, and Idar is the one who makes Milo strike them out. He can name the only way out of a court that a woman with a pram can use before seven in the morning, and he can name what happens to a route once it is in a file. They are named so a later batch does not lose them.

## Ada Fenn

- **Where she is:** Cedar Court, third floor, ill with the flu for part of the batch.
- **What she has given:** the fact that the third strike still comes through her hand; the door-check, in full, as a practice; the standing agreement to share only what she is willing to share; the account of a polite young man from Crown Heights who asked her what the third bell was for, nine years ago, and whose answer turned up eleven months later in a document she never showed him.
- **What she has refused:** to prove the old route, to speak for the Registry, to be read into a file, to stand in the third turn for anyone including Milo, and to be used as a condition of a map. The lane has not punished her for refusing, and the volume must not treat her refusal as a problem to solve.
- **Revocable, structurally load-bearing, and known to be.** Owen Fitch has taken the third-floor check off her while she is ill, once a night, on his own terms. Later batches must build a rota and a written schedule, not a dependency.

## Lena Marr

- **Where she is:** Cedar Court, and the public record. She filed the housing case, she walked out through the service door on the ninth, and she is named in full on Milo's provisional plan with her tenancy and her address in her own spelling.
- **What she wants:** a usable address, school and clinic access, and a route that recognises the households now living there. She does not want Bellwether turned into a memorial that excludes the people in it.
- **Her third-turn reading:** the third call is a count of who is still on the street. She still has her mother's rent-book list, forty-one names, eleven ruled through with a reason, one of them *came back at the third*. She reads it aloud from the landing and she reads it while Owen refuses to be on it, and the two of them are not reconciled.
- **She is not grateful, and she is not a symbol.** She asks practical questions about the school route and writes down *I don't know* in her own margin.

## Owen Fitch

- **Where he is:** Cedar Court, fourth floor, and the ordinary road.
- **What he has done:** refused the order, the waiver, the count, the map, the report, and the third door. He walked home the ordinary way in front of eighty witnesses. He has agreed to one thing: the *refusal itself* being recorded, as *one household has declined to be entered*, with no name, no address, no initials — on the condition that the refusal exists in more than one book so it cannot be got at. He has agreed to check the third-floor stairwell once a night while Ada is ill, on his own terms, and has said he is not doing it for Milo or for Mara.
- **His standing counterweight:** Lena's answer and Owen's answer are both correct and neither will accept the other. The Volume 1 final map has to hold both.
- **His cost is real:** the report has a hole in it where his address should be, and that hole is what makes the refusal defensible.

## Jo Lask

- **Where he is:** back on the road on Line 7, on the detour timetable. In Chapter 9 he closed his tram’s doors on a public instruction from a man with a bandaged hand and held the interval against a pressure surge, and the marshal wrote that down. He has not asked for his job back and has not been suspended; the cost of the Chapter 9 hour is the hearing on the 14th, not a disciplinary record.
- **What he did:** held a tram with a hand signal while the works ran the diversion in Chapter 7; walked the line; carried a bar in a sluice arch; closed his tram's doors on Milo's public instruction during the Chapter 9 surge; and in Chapter 8 stopped the loop himself, on his own, and counted himself out over 219 paces. He has a damaged ear for the interval, admitted it in front of a room, and did not report it.
- **His third-turn reading:** the third call is a handover — whoever is holding the door hands the count on. He also holds the strongest argument that the bell at that end of the line was always a works bell and Bellwether never had one.
- **What he wants:** the timetable and the workers' routes made truthful, even where that means admitting nobody was given a safe way home during the original closure. Milo's provisional plan says *the live interval only, not a standing duty* — the first time in eleven years anyone has put that in writing for him.

## Tomas Rusk

- **Where he is:** the works, still on second-shift knowledge, thirty years of it.
- **What he has offered and withheld:** the pump-house maintenance ledger with the works bell schedule on it, which proves the interval is a works shift interval and not an invented signal. He will not release the page until the team can say why the road shut in the middle of a run. That is the Chapter 21–30 hinge and it is not paid.
- **His fear:** that his own silence made the danger worse, and that giving the page will prove it.
- **His account of the original emergency:** Verrick came back through the door Ada was holding at the third call and said *clear* to her, and then another hand hit the door three times out of rhythm, and the works bell went at the third, and the frontage came down eleven minutes later. This is a fifth voice on the third turn and it is currently the only one with a time attached to it.

## Orrin Sable

- **Where he is:** the district works office, with a seal kit, a crane booked for the morning of the 22nd, and no window.
- **Pressure, made concrete:** a standing instruction from the Prefect's office that flood-season orders be executed inside the season, and a contractor's bond that lapses on the 11th of Sazur. He is legible and unexcused.
- **What he has conceded:** he stopped the gate at eighteen inches rather than sealing it, in public, and told the room he was about to do something half of them would call a cover-up. He will bring the file to the hearing himself if the culvert turns something up in the next three days.
- **His method is not sympathetic:** the waiver, the request for the notes, the insistence that the hazard is the only fact in the district.
- **His best argument, given to Milo face to face in the Chapter 10 corridor and not answered:** *your map did not make the seam coherent, it made it legible; a believed route is a used route, and on the twenty-second it will be standing in the way of a river.* He demanded Milo say it to the yard **before** the test and not after. Milo wrote the argument down in Orrin's own words without editing it. This is the batch's sharpest open problem and the next batch should not let it be resolved by reassurance.
- **His pressure has just become public:** the floodgate test is on the 22nd and the gate will be driven out to full flow.
