**Where the district is.** Cedar Court and eleven streets in the river core, on the **fifteenth of December**, with the order **suspended** at ten o'clock on the twelfth, an investigation open, a public hearing to come, a hundred and forty feet of named frontage behind a fence with a settlement plate on it, and **two hundred and fifty-two people on a per-head schedule that one woman has signed for.** Milo Rook's evening entry for the fifteenth is the last thing on the page.

## Milo Rook

- **Where he is:** the Civic Atlas Office, the route room, the second of the two tables under the north window, at about half past four on the afternoon of the fifteenth, with the Field Book open at a blank.
- **Stage:** 1 — trace-sight. Unchanged and not advanced. **Volume 1 ends at Stage 1.**
- **Crossings: eight, all in Volume 1.** The fourth, sixth, eighteenth, twenty-first and twenty-sixth of Sazur, the tenth of the month at 14.06, **the seventh of December at 16.06** and **the eighth of December at 09.06**. Both new ones read out in a yard with Dr Sen's tally present, all four signed conditions satisfied, and **each cost a route.** The seventh is in the book on page four of the counting; the eighth on the fifth.
- **Cost paid: both stages of the orientation blank, and they are two costs and not one.** **First stage: ten named remembered routes**, the original eight intact and none removed, plus **the hundred and one steps of the stair tower at the Alder Quay interchange** (given by Ola Petrossian on a card at the door, the seventh of December) and **the walk of the eighteenth of October with a lamp and a cat in a laundry bag** (marked on the ninth by a woman of sixty from the second landing, at Rue's insistence and Milo's not asking). All ten recoverable when marked, **all ten marked by somebody who is not him.** **Second stage: the written line home, which went on Monday the twenty-fourth of November at about ten past eight in the morning** — nine lines, his own hand, the oldest entry in the book, gone clean, the page there and the ink on either side the same. **It is not persistent inside the volume: he can still find home when it is marked, and the marking comes from somebody who is not him.** He chalked his own door on the twenty-eighth, was given three chalk arrows on his own building by the fourteenth of December, **asked two people to write the route for him in the last chapter and was refused twice by people who had no reason to refuse him, and the blank is still blank.** He has written down in order that it might be a cost, or a bad memory, or a page that needs another person's mark, and that he does not know which, and has not decided. **He is not cured and the loss is not a wound he closes.**
- **The token: cold, cold, and once not.** Cold on the seventeenth of November, and on the fourteenth and fifteenth of December, and he did not take it out. **On the seventh of December, on the reach, it was warm for about four minutes and the negative space in the front of it lined up with a hundred and forty feet of made ground, exactly, and he could have opened something and did not.** The reason is written on the facing page and is the whole of the batch: *if a street is open because of something I can take out of my coat, then the three answers in this district stop being three answers the moment one of them is a thing I can put back.* **He did not use it as a key, did not summon anybody, did not replace a witness, and did not make the old lane into a replica.**
- **What he gave away, and what it cost.** The sheet, four versions, the rule at the foot, the design, the authorship, the custody, and finally **the crossing itself**: the map he entered the reach with is Rue's two-column sheet with a rule at the foot, and he read the rule out loud on the reach because *the whole of the argument of six weeks is that a map with no rule at the foot is an invitation to whoever has got a pen.* He calls himself **the one who can be replaced.** He was in a corridor for ninety minutes on the fifteenth while Lena Marr stood up in a hall, and he has written that down as the sum of three months and has not been able to add a line to it.
- **His part, as he now names it:** the columns, the rule at the foot, the versions, the arithmetic, the refusal, and the map. **He is not the author, the holder, the clerk, or the custodian of any of it.**
- **What he said on the reach, in daylight, with four hundred people inside a quarter of a mile, and it is in a hearing transcript:** the order's public safety claim, the missing worker record, the current residents' presence while the crossing is open, and — because Doreen Sankey made him — *a cartographer went over the low end of this street on the eighteenth of October with a lamp and a cat in a laundry bag, and I am not going in as a man with a piece of paper in my hand and I am not going in for Lark Street.*
- **The clue chain, carried and not advanced.** Two documents carry a pair of marks, not three. The reopened order file produced a **fourth document with one mark in it** — a carbon relocation schedule, a destination with no official address, a question about the destination and not about the order — and he has written on the page that it is **not a third pair**, because a pair is two marks in two hands on one page. **He has twice, in his own book, recorded that he is not joining the carbon to the turn-sheet copy or to Pell's copying tick, and why: joining them would be him deciding that they are the same.**
- **What is on his page and is not his:** a community and memorial register entry marked contested, a district drainage book with a name and a date in it, a works maintenance schedule, a printed tram rule book, three chalk arrows on his own building, and a nine-line blank in his own book.
- **His standing in the file:** his interpretation remains excluded; the level remains under a foreman's signature and **is not back in his file and may not be put back**; the joint finding of the twentieth of October and the fourth of December are both in it; and the classification of the eleventh of the current year, which was his colleague's, is withdrawn and was made correctly on the day it was made.

## Mara Quill

- **Where she is:** the Registry, with a two-year note on her conduct, a public safety appeal of nineteen paragraphs on a Crown Heights envelope with a division mark on it, a hearing to come, and an attachment in a file that will be produced against her for the rest of her working life.
- **What the review on the third of December found:** that she worked the file off her own hours for six years in a records alcove without telling her review panel, and that **the fourth set of initials in the annex box of an intake routing slip of the sixth year back** was a matter she ought to have declared and did not. **She read the finding out in a corridor to about nine people and about six of them did not understand a word of it. She lost preferred access, and appealed on the fourth in one line, and it is not stayed.**
- **What she did that was the mechanism, and it cost her nothing she can get back:** on the fourth of the month she refused the department's request for a schedule of persons resident at the withdrawn alignment. **The answer is NO and it is on the file in her own hand with her own date, and it says in terms that a nil return is capable of being read as a representation that no persons are resident there, and that she does not know that, and that she knows the schedule is short by twenty-one.** The empty box in the appeal of the eighth of December is the only reason she can stand in a room and ask for an order to be suspended. **She certified the head count of two hundred and fifty-two on the fifteenth with a declaration of interest at the top of it, which is the third thing in a file about her own hand and the thing that finished the review.**
- **What she refused, and did not explain to anybody or get thanked for:** to be kind to him about the line going out of his book (*you have had four people be kind to you in two days and you are going into a room on Monday week with a folder under your arm, and if you go in there having been comforted you will lose it*); to speak for a household; to produce the turn sheet in any room (*I know of no residents' document*); to call an unsworn mechanic as a man rather than as a document; to go to the Bench before the event; and to ask a clerk in a hall of thirty people to look up a file number.
- **What she said that nobody argued with:** *on the sixth of December there is going to be an event, and an event makes a record that has to be written down by somebody, and if I am standing in front of the only record anybody has of a man who died in a pump house six years ago, then the record gets written, and it gets written in a room I am in. That is why we are going on the eighth of December and not on the sixteenth of November.*
- **The relationship, one step and no warmth, and the step is standing next to him and not being able to do anything about it.** She said the thing she has not said — *on the twenty-ninth of Sazur a man promised me that nothing of his bearing on this file would reach me in writing with a date on it, and I held that for forty-four days and then I made him give away the design instead, and he gave it away in about nine seconds and he did not complain, and that is the most decent thing anybody has done for me in six years and I have not said so to him and I am not going to* — in a yard of about eleven, and about nine of them looked at him and he looked at the ground. **Nothing romantic has happened and nothing is going to. She is not going to write his return route for him in Volume 1 and it was never hers to write, and he has not asked. The promise stands and is hers and does not get wider.**

## Pell Harrow

- **Where he is:** the round, out at eight and in at four, and in a yard in the rain. Seventeen. A courier and a Streetkeeper. **He has no power of any kind and is not Milo's assistant.**
- **Tern: still no answer.** He has not seen him. He has not been asked about it in this batch and did not raise it, and nothing is resolved, and the list in the tin is not a finding and Ostrow confirms nothing.
- **What he did:** held the count on the **eighth crossing** with a counter and no words for twenty-seven minutes, and *that was the quietest half hour of my year and I am not going to say anything else about it*; **came back at about four miles an hour and said *Your mark is off* and then four words — *it was me on Saturday at eleven… I am not going to do it again tonight because it does not do anything at two o'clock in the morning and I have not seen it do anything at two o'clock in the morning* — and refused, unprompted, to have a mark asked of him overnight**; **chalked an arrow on the outside of Milo's own building at about five feet on the Sunday night of the fourteenth, dated, and said nothing**; and a third arrow on the wall of the second-floor landing on the same night, **with no date on it, and nobody has ever asked who put it there and nobody is going to.**
- **The limit, now tested and stated on the page:** a mark holds for a **working shift**, on the way out and on the way back in, and **does not hold overnight**, and nobody in the district is going to be asked to hold one overnight, **and that includes the boy.** Rue said it in a yard and it is the first rule anybody drops under pressure.
- **What he still refuses:** to be the reason a document is made; to make the round into a service; to be leaned on; to go and look at the tick in the fourth column; and **to walk a route for a man who cannot find his own door, on the grounds that a person who is passing is not a person who has to be there.**

## Rue

- **Where she is:** the round, the trestle table, the porch, and the wall on the north path.
- **What she did in this batch, and each of it is now a rule somebody else has to keep:** **refused to be handed Lena Marr's notice**, and put the passage on her round instead, out at eight and in at four, *and if you say it to anybody else then you are on somebody else's thing, and I do not want it*; **refused to be the count on the seventh** and said so at a table with thirty people in it and did not fix it in advance; **stated the overnight rule in a yard without softening it**; **made a rule in four seconds on the Sunday morning — thirty people behind a woman at eleven feet and not one of them touching her arm — and did not explain it**; **crossed her own line out of the return map** because *if I am on this then anybody can lean on me*; and **told him, in the rain at a gate, that nobody is going to ask him for a passage, and that on the sixth there would not be one person at a door who had agreed to be there** — which is the reason the district is built around other people and not around him.
- **What she refused twice in the last chapter:** to write his route home, *that is mine, and mine is three streets and a chandler's yard and a pie shop, and I do not know a step of yours, and write it down. Ask somebody who has to write it down.*
- **What she still will not do:** name the fourth-floor or the fifth-floor person, go up, report the round, read a sheet, or write a thing down that is hers. She read a card recording a Thursday as not worked and a four-page return document aloud, and **she read the woman's three lines from the frontage walk aloud and the woman had asked not to be read aloud, and Rue read them.**

## The woman on the fourth floor

- **Not named on the page, and this remains structural.** Rue has carried her name on the accountancy-paper rota since the twenty-fourth of last month and has not said it in a yard and is not going to.
- **What she did, and it is the batch's largest single choice:** **she came down on the Sunday morning of the seventh at about eight, before anybody was on the path, on her own, with her coat on and a bag, and nobody had gone up for her and nobody had sent for her, and she walked the frontage once, in daylight, front to back, ninety minutes, at about two miles an hour, with about thirty people behind her at eleven feet.** She did not go in. **She chose a memorial route rather than a home, and she did not go in, and she has not gone up since.** Her three lines, in her own hand, which she would not read out: *I walked it. Once, in daylight, front to back, and I did not go in and I am not going in. / I am not coming down again for anybody. Not for the sheet, not for the door, not for a turn, not for a hearing, and not for a thing that is in a good coat. I have said that four times now and this is the fourth and I am not saying it a fifth. / The road is not mine and I have not claimed it and nobody is to put my name on it. If it is on anything that is my name on it, take it off in front of me.*
- **Nobody thanked her. Milo Rook was about four hundred yards away and has written down twice that he was about four hundred yards away. She is not healed, she is not on anything, and the deliberate space at the foot of the turn sheet went through the seventh and the eighth empty and about four people have checked it.**

## Ada Fenn

- **Where she is:** Cedar Court, third floor. **She does not appear in Chapters 51–60 and her name is not written once in them. She is not named, thanked, replaced, argued back, or put on anything, and nobody goes up three flights of stairs about her.**
- **She is referred to once, and not by name:** in Chapter 54's account of the four hundred answers, as **a woman of sixty-eight** who was not in the district on the sixth of December and did not say anything on the sixth of December, in a sentence written by somebody who is not Milo Rook. That is the whole of her presence in the batch.
- **Chapter 57 stages the moment she was nearly reached for and was not.** A man said *then go and ask the old woman on the third floor* in a yard of about twenty-nine, and Rue said **no** first, before anybody else could, and Lena Marr gave the real reason in about sixty words: *She is not ours. If we go up and ask her, then the district's answer on the eighth of December is a woman of sixty-eight, and every one of us in this yard goes back to being a thing that happens next to a woman of sixty-eight… and the report will be true, and it will be the end of it, because a report that says a person remained is a report that says a place is kept by one person, and one person is not a district and one person is what this whole street was closed over. And I have not been up those stairs either, and I live in that building, and that is not a thing I am proud of and I am not going to pretend it is.* **Nobody went up. It is on the page that nobody went up.**
- **Unchanged and binding:** she has refused to interpret the third bell thirteen times and will not be asked a fourteenth; she is not on the rota and will not be, including by way of signing herself off it; her own name for what she is remains *a woman with a habit, and those are not the same.*
- **The district did the crossing with the three sources it built itself.** **Her withdrawal is load-bearing and it is not a problem, and Volume 1 does not solve it, thank her, or replace her.**

## Owen Fitch

- **Where he is:** Cedar Court, fourth floor, the stairwell, and the foot of the outside stairs in the rain.
- **The refusal, and it is a real refusal and it is not a wound that is healed.** On Saturday the twenty-second of November he refused the job at the foot of the outside stairs, to four people, in the rain. **He will not be the turn, hold the sheet, train anybody, be in charge of a table, sign anything, keep a list, or be the reason a thing works.** *If the return happens and it wants a turn, it goes without one, and a return without a turn is a return with a hole in it, and the hole is where I am, and it will look like a mistake and it will be deliberate, and I will not be asked about it afterwards.* **He also disclosed, for the first time in six years, that he has not been on that street since the night of the closure and did not go back when the order came.**
- **The one thing he offered, and the reason it was not a role:** on the sixth of December, in daylight, once, at the ground-floor door, he stood there for about four minutes, **said nothing, did not go in, was not a turn, and was not counted**, and he was gone before the round was in. *I have not been on that street since the night of the closure and I did not go back when the order, and I have never once said so out loud and I am saying it now because a piece of paper I did not write has got my name and my floor in it and somebody has to be somewhere near the middle of this, and it is not going to be me.*
- **His fourth position, which nobody knew was there, given in a yard on the nineteenth of November:** **nobody should go in, and the reason should be written down in the district's own book** — *a paper that says a person went in can be used against them and a paper that says a person did not go in can be used for them, and there is one of those and there is not one of the other, and every one of us has spent six weeks only ever writing down the second kind.*
- **Nobody thanked him, nobody healed him, nobody argued with him, and no character in this batch explains his position to him.** The woman of sixty said in a yard that he had not been asked, and asked for that to be written down, and nobody wrote it down.
- **The record, for the sixth time:** one household has declined to be entered; his initials are on the instrument of the fifth of Sazur; a refusal line has been read into the record against them at a hearing; his own capitals and three words are on the third-floor line of the accountancy-paper rota; his third-floor check is unchanged, once a night, going up, unwritten, revocable at will; and **his name is still on five copies of a sheet of paper with a contractor's head on it, in the space that was left empty on purpose, and it has not been taken off anything in this batch and no clerk took it off for him.**

## Lena Marr

- **Where she is:** Cedar Court, the Board school, the Hall of Wards, and four rooms behind a boatyard on the Ferry Road side.
- **The decision that is the batch's bill.** On the fifteenth of December, at about ten to twelve, in a hall of about thirty people, **she stood up and signed as the named head of a temporary accommodation household, which puts the per-head additional schedule for every person in the block onto her instead of onto the district rate — two and four a head a week, in advance, for as long as that building is a building, with no term.** She wrote both her questions down that morning and showed them to a woman of sixty-three on a step, and the woman read them and is not in the room and said she would come on Wednesday if either was wrong. **The same signature in the same minute opens the school intake for the seven**, on a Continuity referral, because a referral asks for a place and a place needs a person. **The two children lost in October are still two, and she said so twice in the room, unasked.** Nobody thanked her.
- **What she refused:** a fifth Thursday in any form; to be the one who chooses what the district gets; to hand a schedule of rooms to a rates authority; **to be the centre of the supervised return**, which she wrote a notice for on the nineteenth of November and handed to Rue inside four minutes, saying *if I run it then in three weeks there is one woman with a plastic wallet and everybody knows where to find her, and that is how this district got closed once*; and to apologise to a widow for something a department did.
- **Her three-part answer, still standing:** no fifth Thursday, no going to a room before the event, and the count. And the reason for the second: *if we go in now and say the word, then for the rest of this case the district is the people who said the word, and the department will find the classification in the file in about ninety seconds… after that the only two sentences anybody hears are ours and his.*
- **And the sentence about the woman on the third floor, in a yard, which is the reason nobody went up** — see Ada Fenn above. **And Lena's own admission: *And I have not been up those stairs either, and I live in that building, and that is not a thing I am proud of and I am not going to pretend it is.***
- **The widow:** visited on the Sunday, in a taxi, at nine pounds of her own money, with Doreen Sankey on the step. She read the six words off the disposal return and **did not make her ask one question**, and did not make her ask where to go, and did not say sorry. **The woman is fifty-four, mends other people's clothes, is alive, and is not named on the page and was not named in this batch.**

## Tomas Rusk

- **Where he is:** his front room, sixty-one, a bad knee, four years of a district works pension, and a taxi from the low end at three and six a trip.
- **His condition, stated once on the twentieth of November and not asked about twice.** It is not for him and it is not for his oath, and he will not swear. **The road is not the lane.** A true sentence about a place that does not exist can be used against a man who did not know it did not exist, and the frontage has a name in the district's own drainage book and in the works' own schedule and has been a maintained frontage since before he was at the pump house: **Bridge Reach.** He wanted the record put in before the sixth. **What it cost him is his name** — a page with his name at the foot of it, in the reopened order file, as a mechanic of sixty-one who sat on a dead man's next-of-kin line for six years and has asked for a road. *Once a road is in the district's book with a name and a maintenance schedule and a man attached to it, there is a road, and there is no version of this that ends with me being a man in a kitchen saying a true thing to about nine people.*
- **And it worked.** On the Thursday morning of the ninth the district drainage section came out to the low end and unstruck the 1904 entry and wrote the date and *maintained frontage, 140 ft, reverting on the suspension of CO/6/BELL*, and said four words to Rue: **Requested by a mechanic.** The works maintenance schedule carries the same reach with a gang and a fortnightly inspection.
- **The instrument, again and again, from a telephone at seven in the morning with a clock on the table:** on the second of that June the crew came off the frontage to the pump house, and that is the whole of it, and **from the moment the crew came off, that length of ground was not a road, it was a gap, and a gap is not a road and it is not a hazard and it is not a street, it is a place where a road used to be, and the difference between those is a crew.** And then, on the sixth of December, in a trench, with a crew on the reach at ten to two: *that is exactly the condition I have been describing to you since June six years ago, and paragraph six says the crew change is sounded from the gantry and the paragraph above the one that was taken out says the frontage is at all times a service access and not a public route.* He said it once, refused to say it again, and hung up. **He still will not swear. Nobody argued him out of it and nobody tried.**
- **What he asked for and did not get:** to be at a door. He is on the round's sheet with **I cannot get there** in his own hand, and he is walked to the door by whoever is going.

## Doreen Sankey

- **Who he is:** forty-one, two rooms behind a boatyard on the Ferry Road side, nine years, paid nobody anything, on no rate book, no index, no tenancy and no schedule, and never has been. One of the thirty-three rooms on no list.
- **What he did in this batch, and it is the most reliable labour anybody did in it:** **walked Milo Rook in and out of the reach on the seventh**, in about eleven words of terms, *You have a man on a platform and I have no place to be, and you want somebody who is not your chalk, and I am the only one in this district who can turn round and come back up this and I do it eleven times a day and I will do it in front of four hundred people and then nobody can say a man from a boatyard stood in a hole.* **Ran the way in and out for the passage on the seventh and the eighth.** **Took the check at a door on the twenty-eighth of November, the first with no named person, and told Rue the terms in his own hand.** **Stood at the foot of the made reach at eleven feet in front of a woman for ninety minutes** so that nobody touched her arm. **Sat on a step in the rain on Sunday the fourteenth and would not come past the first room in nine years**, and said so, because he has not been asked and he is not going.
- **What he says, and it is the argument for the whole batch:** the count has to be a person saying a number to a person, and a photograph of him is a document and a document can be produced, *and I would rather the document be one I chose and stood in deliberately and where there is a foreman's name on it as well as mine.*
- **What he refused, in the last chapter, and it is a refusal with a reason:** to write a man's route home. *I have been asked for that route in about four houses this month and I have said no in all four, and one of them was a man with a good coat and one of them was a man with a worse coat, and if I walk it once to write it down then in a month somebody will be able to say the boatyard man keeps the surveyor's way home, and the second it is written down it is a job… Ask a person who has to be there. Not a person who is passing.*
- **What he still wants:** not a street. **Connected.** A way to walk from his door to the tram steps in the rain that about forty people in this building have never heard of, and he now has one, on a works frontage, and he has said that a works frontage is the only kind of street anybody in this district is ever going to get and that he is not complaining.

## The woman of sixty from the second landing of Cedar Court

- **Not named, by the same rule and for the same reason.** Rue did not tell Milo Rook her name after the count of the seventeenth and he did not ask.
- **What she holds: a street.** And what she got: **a contested community and memorial name** on a register that is not the street index, capable of being produced against any person who uses it, and she has read that sentence four times and said, in a hall, *I asked for a street for nine years and I have been given a thing that can be produced against me, and I would like the register to know that I know that, and I am going to use it anyway, and I am not going to be one of the women who waits for the perfect thing.* **And she asked for it to be noted on the entry, and it is noted.**
- **What she did:** took the hours of the passage and wrote the word **clear** on Rue's round in her own handwriting; **marked the tenth of Milo's ten lost routes, at Rue's insistence and his not asking, and did not go on a list to do it**; made the department read the contested entry out loud in a hall; and said the sentence that made the room say *good* and go home: *Then there is no way in and I would like somebody to say so out loud, because I have lived on a landing for nine years and I have been told there is no way in by five different people in five different coats and I have never once been told it by anybody who had to.*
- **What she will not do:** be the door-check. She said it and she is right and nobody argued with her. She is not on the rota and did not go on the round's floor sheet.
- **She is one of the five who went out through the ground-floor door to Cedar Court.**

## Hettie Brann

- **Who she is:** the water clinic counter, nineteen years on it, formerly a laundry hand at Ada Fenn's laundry. She holds the turn sheet on alternate weeks with Mara Quill and reads neither.
- **What she did, and it was the last thing the seventh crossing needed:** **she said no three times and then said a different yes**, and held the count on the seventh of December from the path head with a stopwatch on the back of her hand, and would not read the sheet, would not come in, would not be asked about anybody who came out, and told the yard why: **the day this district needs a count and there is nobody in it to keep one, that is the day it stops being a district, and I have been on that counter nineteen years and I have watched it stop twice and I am not watching it stop a third time while I am standing up.** She took the tally in her apron pocket before anybody asked her to, and her tally now has **eight lines** on it and is the record of his crossings.

## Dr Iona Sen

- **Where she is:** the water clinic, the dispensary counter, and the edge of an awning in the rain.
- **What she did in this batch:** **gave him the number that is the batch's whole argument, in a dispensary with four people in the queue: there are eight remembered routes and there is a page, and they are not one thing, and the difference is the only interesting thing about either of them** — and told him on the twenty-fifth of November that somebody else is going to have to write the line, from their own feet, in their own hand, on his page, so that he cannot say afterwards that nobody told him it was coming. **Treated eleven people hurt on the sixth of December and four through the room by half past two, and nine on the eighth, and said the thing that is the point of it: a person going into a place that is not safe is not an injury, it is a decision, and I will not have that turned into an argument about a seam.** **Said the sentence that is the batch's whole warning, in a yard of about nine on the Monday the eighth: it is not fixed, it is quiet, and a fixed thing is one you stop watching, and the day somebody in this district stops watching it is the day it starts again, and it will not start with a noise, it will start with a schedule.** **Made him read the eighth crossing out loud and made him say the number out loud, because in a year he will have to say it in a room.** And she corrected her own October finding out loud in a yard: the condition is not *do not be alone with a document about a person*, it never helped him once and it is not going to; **the condition is that he gets walked to a door and walked out of a door, every time, by a person who is not you, and says so in advance, and if they do not come you do not go** — and that it took a line going out of a book to make a man ask for it, and that that is not a magic cost and not a doctor's opinion, it is just what happened.
- **Unchanged:** she has said nothing about the Atlas, nothing about her own past, and nothing about the man who taught Milo's hand. The fifth finding is not cured and is not signed and cannot be signed, and she said on the twelfth of November that writing it down had made it worse and she would do it again.

## Orrin Sable

It is made because an officer may not investigate his own order.** He is not the final antagonist and he is not softened.
- **What he did, and each of it was right:** **announced the test date and the timetable three weeks early and out loud, and said he was doing it early so that nobody could say afterwards that he did it quietly** — the same move as the plate and the same reason, and the second time in eleven weeks he has been right about something with nothing to do with engineering. **Announced the culvert's true state** — throttled to eighteen inches of sill since the sixth of Sazur, never sealed — and told the district that a man had been making his case easier. **Announced the four-day window, the soft lens stopping on the first, and the trench under the only made path.** **Refused to answer a crossing, changed his mind in public inside four minutes, and said the sentence that stopped it: close the crossing and the reach becomes a road, and a road is kept by somebody, and a road with forty people on it at ten to two is a crew, and I would be putting the district's own sentence into the reach against the order of the district, and I have spent six years watching a man do that to about four hundred people and I am not going to be the second one.** Put a woman of sixty-three on a wall who is not on his payroll. **Said in a yard, at about ten on a Sunday morning, that on the sixth of December the district goes into a room and he is going to be the man in that room who does not argue — and that on Saturday he had said the opposite about a fence, and that he would like both recorded.**
- **His three sentences of account, out of nine, on the evening of the twelfth, to about nine people in a yard that had emptied:** **the safety need was real and is still real and the wall behind this district did not fail on the sixth of December and that is the only thing he has ever wanted to be true**; **the concealment was not required by the safety problem, he has known that since the first week, he did it with a document and not with a lie, which is worse, and he put the true sentence in the box that only takes it**; and **there is a file in the district he has never been able to get at and has stopped trying for, and he is telling nine people about it in a yard because there is a dead man in it and the name is on a ledger with the word pending against it — and he is not going to say the rest of it in December, and he wanted the two words *not* and *not* noticed, and he was aware of both.** Nobody asked him. **His later testimony will expose how the order was made and will not absolve him, and he is not absolved anywhere in this volume.**
